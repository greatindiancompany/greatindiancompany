India’s mid-term logistics trajectory was written down in September 2022, and it is still a target rather than a result. The path from here to 2030 is visible only where later replies say an institution now exists. It is not visible as a year-by-year modal shift.

## The aims, dated to the cabinet note

The [note of 21 September 2022](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/policy-introduces-unified-logistics-interface-platform-standardization-monitoring-framework-and-skill-development-for-greater-efficiency-in-logistics-services/) sets three aims for the National Logistics Policy. Reduce the cost of logistics in India to a level comparable with global benchmarks by 2030. Improve the Logistics Performance Index ranking to the top 25 countries by 2030. Create a data-driven decision system for the logistics ecosystem. The same note says the policy complements PM Gati Shakti: the master plan integrates infrastructure; the policy works on services, regulation, skills, and technology.

The launch speech four days earlier asked for single-digit logistics cost, set against a 13–14 percent description. The cabinet note is the one that attaches a year, 2030, and a benchmark, the top 25. Use the note when you need a deadline. Use the speech when you need the political phrasing. Do not merge them into a promise that cost “will be” 7 point something by a given Diwali.

## Where August 2026 says the trajectory stands

The [reply of 11 August 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS327_tYLfn2.pdf) restates the policy’s aims as lower cost, a better index rank, and data-driven support. It then offers a position, not a glide path.

The rank it cites is 38th, six places up. The top-25 line from 2022 is unmet on that citation. This page will not assign the remaining climb to customs reform, port dwell, or rail share. The reply does not.

The cost position it cites is about 7.9 percent of GDP, from the 2025 DPIIT–NCAER assessment, described as comparable to advanced economies. If that method is accepted, the single-digit phrasing of 2022 has a government number under it. Acceptance is the reader’s job. The reply does not show the method, and it does not say the 2030 benchmark exercise is finished.

What the reply does treat as stood up, and therefore as the trajectory’s machinery, is a list of institutions. ULIP operational. The Logistics Data Bank tracking export-import containers. LEADS institutionalized. A Service Improvement Group in place. Sectoral plans initiated for coal, steel, fertilizers, cement, pharmaceuticals, and food. State logistics policies formulated in 29 states and Union Territories. Industry status in 32. Courses in more than 100 universities and institutes. Sixty-eight qualification packs operational. Process standards at ports, land ports, and airports. A warehousing handbook, city logistics guidelines, and NLP-Marine named as work undertaken.

Machinery is not the 2030 outcome. It is the reason a mid-term review can be specific. The question for the rest of the decade is whether those machines change the index pillars and the shipper’s clock, not whether another platform can be launched.

## What is explicitly not on a trajectory

The [written reply the same day](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297818) says Gati Shakti has no quantified targets and no expenditure requirement of its own. There is no official multi-year spending path called “the Gati Shakti trajectory.” There is a pipeline: 396 projects evaluated, 256 sanctioned, 198 under implementation, about ₹18.66 lakh crore estimated. The mid-term build is whatever the roads, rail, and ports ministries fund inside that pipeline and outside it.

[Bharatmala](https://morth.nic.in/en/bharatmala) remains the roads corridor programme. Dedicated freight corridors and Sagarmala remain the rail and port programmes the August text names. None of those pages, in the material cited here, commits India to a rail share of freight by 2030. If you need a mode-share target, find the railway or planning document that states it. Do not borrow one from a logistics slogan.

A sober mid-term sentence fits in a line. By 2030 the 2022 policy asked for a top-25 rank and a globally comparable cost. In August 2026 the government cited 38th and about 7.9 percent of GDP, and pointed to a set of platforms and state policies as the work in between. The distance left is the trajectory.

[Great Indian Company](https://greatindiancompany.com/) will update that sentence when a later primary note moves the rank or shows the method.
