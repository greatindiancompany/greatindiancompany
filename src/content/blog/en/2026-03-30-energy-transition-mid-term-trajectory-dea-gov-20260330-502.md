# India's Energy Transition to 2032

The mid-term path that Indian power planning actually uses is the National Electricity Plan (Volume I, Generation), notified by the Central Electricity Authority on 18 May 2023. It reviews 2017-22, plans 2022-27 in detail, and gives a perspective for 2027-32. It is a planning study, not a forecast that updates itself. Later official statements have added nuclear and hydrogen aims that the 2023 tables do not fully absorb. A trajectory note has to keep those layers dated.

## Demand the plan was built to meet

The plan takes the 20th Electric Power Survey as its demand anchor. All-India peak demand and electrical energy requirement are 277.2 GW and 1,907.8 billion units for 2026-27, and 366.4 GW and 2,473.8 billion units for 2031-32. Those figures already include the survey's view of electric vehicles, rooftop solar, green hydrogen, and household electrification. FY 2025-26 generation, reported by MNRE's ministerial briefing, was 1,845.921 billion units. That is one observed year, sitting under the survey's 2026-27 energy number, not a revision of it.

## Capacity the plan expects, and capacity now

For the end of 2026-27 the plan's likely installed capacity is 609,591 MW. Conventional capacity is 273,038 MW, including coal of 235,133 MW, gas of 24,824 MW, and nuclear of 13,080 MW. Renewable-based capacity is 336,553 MW: large hydro 52,446 MW, solar 185,566 MW, wind 72,895 MW, small hydro 5,200 MW, biomass 13,000 MW, and pumped storage 7,446 MW. Batteries are additional, at 8,680 MW / 34,720 MWh. The non-fossil share of capacity is put at 57.4 percent by the end of 2026-27, from about 42.5 percent in April 2023.

On 31 July 2026 the observed system is earlier and, on share, already close. PIB describes total capacity as around 552 GW and the non-fossil share as over 54 percent, with non-fossil capacity at 300.50 GW. Solar is 164.59 GW against the plan's end-2026-27 figure of 185.6 GW. Wind is 58.14 GW against 72.9 GW. Pumped storage inside MNRE's large-hydro line is 7,425.6 MW, essentially at the plan's 7,446 MW marker for the end of 2026-27. The share has moved faster than some of the technology rows. Wind, not the percentage, is the row with the most ground to cover before March 2027.

For 2031-32 the plan's likely capacity is 900,422 MW. Conventional capacity is 304,147 MW, including coal of 259,643 MW and nuclear of 19,680 MW. Renewable-based capacity is 596,275 MW, including solar of 364,566 MW, wind of 121,895 MW, large hydro of 62,178 MW, and pumped storage of 26,686 MW, plus batteries of 47,244 MW / 236,220 MWh. The non-fossil share is put at 68.4 percent. The plan says this path lines up with about 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2029-30. India was at 300.50 GW of non-fossil capacity on 31 July 2026. The remaining distance to 500 GW is about 200 GW, to be read against a recent year, FY 2025-26, in which non-fossil addition was 55.29 GW.

Storage is the steep part of the second half. Pumped storage has to go from roughly 7.4 GW now to 26.7 GW in the 2031-32 table. Batteries have to go from a planning requirement of 8.7 GW in 2026-27, which is not a row on today's MNRE sheet, to 47.2 GW. The average emission factor in the plan falls from 0.548 kg CO2 per kWh in 2026-27 to 0.430 in 2031-32. That is an output of the capacity mix, not a separately enforced limit.

Coal does not leave the trajectory. The plan expects the coal fleet to be used at about 58 percent plant load factor in both horizon years, and it estimates domestic coal requirement at 866.4 million tonnes in 2026-27 and 1,025.8 million tonnes in 2031-32, plus 28.9 million tonnes of imported coal for plants designed to burn imports. A mid-term transition that deletes those lines is a different plan from the one that was notified.

## Aims dated after the plan

Later Government statements stretch past the 2023 nuclear line. The PIB factsheet of 13 August 2026 says nuclear capacity, 8.78 GW on 31 July 2026, is projected to reach 22.38 GW by 2031-32, above the plan's 19,680 MW, on the way to an aim of 100 GW by 2047. It also records ₹20,000 crore for small modular reactors, with at least five indigenous reactors aimed at by 2033, and first criticality of the 500 MWe prototype fast breeder at Kalpakkam on 6 April 2026. Those are programme statements. They update the story. They do not replace the notified Electricity Plan until CEA notifies a new one.

Green hydrogen is outside the capacity table and inside the demand footnote. The Mission aims at at least 5 million tonnes a year by 2030 and about 125 GW of associated renewable capacity. The Electricity Plan's demand figures already say they include a hydrogen effect. They do not show 125 GW as a separate line item a reader can tick off.

## How to hold the path

Use 31 July 2026 as the pin: 300.5 GW non-fossil, 164.6 GW solar, 58.1 GW wind, 7.4 GW pumped storage, generation still mostly fossil over a full year. Use March 2027 as the plan's first gate, especially for wind and for whether a battery stock becomes visible. Use 2029-30 for the 500 GW non-fossil planning mark, and 2031-32 for the full Electricity Plan mix, including a larger coal fleet and a much larger storage fleet. Anything presented as a single smooth line through those dates is smoother than the documents.
