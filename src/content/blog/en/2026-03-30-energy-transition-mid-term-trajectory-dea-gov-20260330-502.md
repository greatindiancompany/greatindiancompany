# The Mid-Term Shape Of India's Energy Transition

Over the next several years, India's power system is set up to add a great deal of non-fossil capacity while coal continues to supply a large share of electrical energy. That is the mid-term shape. It is not a straight line down for coal and a straight line up for every green technology at once. Demand growth, the speed of wires and storage, and the finances of distribution companies decide how close the energy mix gets to the capacity mix.

## Capacity can move faster than energy

India has stated an aim to raise the non-fossil share of installed electricity capacity, and a net-zero aim for 2070. Solar is the main near-term instrument, with wind, hydro, and nuclear as the other non-fossil contributors. Those additions change the installed fleet faster than they change annual energy, because solar and wind run fewer hours than a coal unit, and because new demand absorbs part of the new generation. A mid-term plan that equates a capacity goal with a coal exit date has compressed a long arc into one slogan.

Coal's mid-term role is energy plus flexibility. Plants that ramp for the evening remain relevant until storage, hydro, and demand shifting cover that ramp. Some plants will run less in the afternoon as solar grows. That is a loading change, not an announcement that mining regions are finished.

## Flexibility is the slow layer

Transmission corridors, interstate access, batteries, and pumped storage are the layer that lets the next wave of renewables count as delivered energy. The Ministry of Power's grid and distribution agenda is as mid-term as the renewable ministry's capacity agenda. If this layer slips, the trajectory shows up as curtailment and as coal plants that were supposed to be residual and are still busy. If it holds, more of the solar day becomes usable and the evening is less of a thermal monopoly.

Hydrogen and other industrial fuels are a later load, not the main mid-term substitute for coal power. They matter where industry needs molecules. They do not balance tonight's peak.

## Demand is part of the trajectory

Buildings, industry, and electric transport add demand that is not shaped like a solar curve. Air-conditioning already pulls the evening in hot states. A mid-term view that holds demand constant will overstate how quickly the coal share falls. This page does not publish a year-by-year path or a capacity integer. The honest mid-term statement is directional: more non-fossil plant, a coal fleet that is still large in energy terms, and a flexibility build that is the constraint to watch.
