# The retail permissions that are actually open

An opportunity landscape for Indian retail should list permissions and measured behaviour. A market-size slogan is not a permission.

## What the FDI circular leaves open

Paragraph 5.2.5.1 of the Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2020 says a manufacturer may sell products manufactured in India through wholesale or retail, including e-commerce, without government approval. That is the cleanest official door for a firm that makes the good in India and wants to sell it itself.

Paragraph 5.2.15.3 leaves single-brand product retail open at 100% on the automatic route, including e-commerce, if stores follow within two years of the start of online retail and if the sourcing rule is met once foreign investment goes beyond 51%: 30% of the value of goods purchased, from India, first as a five-year average and then every year. Procurements for export and sourcing for global operations can count, on the circular’s terms.

Paragraph 5.2.5.2 leaves a food-specific door: 100% FDI on the government route for retail trading, including e-commerce, of food products manufactured or produced in India. It is not the multi-brand route, and it is not a licence for a general merchandise inventory model.

Paragraph 5.2.15.2 leaves the marketplace door open at 100% automatic FDI, for a platform that does not own or control the inventory. The opportunity is the platform fee and the allowed support services (warehousing, logistics, payments), subject to arm’s-length and non-discrimination conditions. It is not an opportunity to be the seller of record.

## What the enterprise survey says is already happening

ASUSE 2025 (January–December 2025) reports that more than half of trading establishments used the internet for entrepreneurial purposes. Across the unincorporated sector, internet use rose from 26.68% in the 2023-24 round to 39.37%. Other retail trade remained the largest activity category, at 26.49% of establishments. The opportunity inside that fact is digitisation of an existing unincorporated trade base, not the creation of a new corporate sector. Companies and LLPs are outside ASUSE by design.

## What the tax recast changed for the shelf

The 56th GST Council, recorded by the Press Information Bureau, replaced the four-rate structure with a 5% merit rate, an 18% standard rate, and a 40% de-merit rate for a select few goods and services, effective 22 September 2025 for services and for goods other than the specified tobacco products. A lower rate on a listed common-use good is a real change in the consumer price wedge. It is an opportunity only for the items in the annex, and only after the notification. It is not a general consumption boom declared by the Council.
