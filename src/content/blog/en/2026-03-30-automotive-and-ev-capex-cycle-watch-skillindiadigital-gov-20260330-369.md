# Where Automotive Capex Shows Up in PLI Auto and the Cell Programme

The capex cycle in India's official auto record is two programmes with very different ratios of money spent to capacity standing. PLI Auto already shows tens of thousands of crores of reported investment. The cell programme shows a few thousand crores and a 1 GWh pilot against a 50 GWh target. Demand subsidies are not this cycle. They are a separate cheque, paid to OEMs for vehicles already sold.

## PLI Auto: investment has moved, determined sales are earlier in the clock

The [13 January 2026 review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2214039) puts the PLI Auto outlay at ₹25,938 crore. It counts 82 approved applicants and an estimated investment of ₹42,500 crore over five years. Cumulative investment reported till 30 September 2025 was ₹35,657 crore. That is the capex line.

The sales line beside it is cumulative determined sales of ₹32,879 crore till the same date, against estimated incremental sales of ₹2,31,500 crore over five years. FY 2023-24 was the first performance year. Claims of ₹322 crore for that year were paid in FY 2024-25. Claims of ₹1,999.94 crore were paid for performance year FY 2024-25. Incentive follows determined sales by a year, and determined sales follow a 50 percent domestic-value-addition certificate.

The certificate book, as of 31 December 2025, is narrower than the applicant book: 94 variants across eight Champion OEM applicants, and 37 variants across ten Component Champion applicants. Units carrying an incentive till that date were 13,61,488, of which 10,42,172 were electric two-wheelers. The plant investment can be real while the payable product list is still a subset of what the applicants make. The [ministry page](https://www.heavyindustries.gov.in/en/pli-scheme-automobile-and-auto-component-industry) dates the scheme's incentive window from FY 2022-23 to FY 2026-27, with disbursement in the next year. The January review dates the first performance year to FY 2023-24. Capex watchers should keep both clocks and follow the disbursement facts.

## Cells: capacity awarded, little of it described as installed

The same review describes the National Programme on Advanced Chemistry Cell Battery Storage, approved in May 2021 with an outlay of ₹18,100 crore to establish 50 GWh. The [28 July 2026 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696) repeats that outlay and the 12 May 2021 start, and the 50 GWh aim. The scheme period is seven years: two of gestation and five of performance.

Awarded capacity in the January review: 30 GWh in round one, to ACC Energy Storage (5 GWh), Ola Cell Technologies (20 GWh), and Reliance New Energy Battery Storage (5 GWh). Round two awarded 10 GWh to Reliance New Energy Battery Ltd. The remaining 10 GWh is earmarked for grid-scale stationary storage, which is a different end use from a vehicle pack.

The installed fact in that review is specific and small next to the awards. Ola Cell Technologies had set up a plant with installed capacity of 1 GWh, started pilot production in March 2024, and was working toward stable commercial production. Investment attracted under the cell scheme was ₹2,878 crore till 30 October 2025, with employment of 1,118. A capex cycle that quotes "50 GWh" without the 1 GWh pilot and the ₹2,878 crore is quoting the target.

## The car-scheme threshold is a minimum, not a reported spend

The Scheme to Promote Manufacturing of Electric Passenger Cars was notified on 15 March 2024. An approved applicant must invest at least ₹4,150 crore within three years, reach 25 percent domestic value addition in that period and 50 percent within five years, and may import a capped 8,000 electric cars a year at reduced duty. Duty foregone per applicant is limited to ₹6,484 crore or the committed investment. The January review states the rule. It does not, in the passage used here, report how much has been invested under it. Until a later review does, this threshold is a condition for entry, not a capex outcome.

Testing-agency modernisation is another heavy-industries outlay on the same map: ₹780 crore inside PM E-DRIVE, carried in the portal's allocation table. It is public capex for certification capacity, which is what makes private vehicle capex eligible for the demand incentive.
