# What CDSCO Licenses and What the State Drug Controller Still Does

The regulatory outlook for an Indian drug project is a split of authority under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, and the Drugs Rules, 1945. The Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation describes that split on its own portal. Manufacturing incentives from the Department of Pharmaceuticals do not move a file from one side of the split to the other.

## The centre’s stated job

CDSCO sits in the Directorate General of Health Services, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, at FDA Bhavan in New Delhi. Its SUGAM portal states the statutory design in plain language. The Act and Rules entrust responsibilities to central and state regulators. CDSCO is responsible for approval of new drugs, conduct of clinical trials, laying down standards for drugs, control over the quality of imported drugs, and coordination of state drug control organisations so enforcement is more uniform.

The same portal says applicants use SUGAM for permission to manufacture, import, or conduct clinical trials under the clinical-trial rules. Biologicals, including vaccines and recombinant-DNA products, have an online process. Manufacturers can add formulation data. Newly approved drugs are published in the drugs@CDSCO section. Firms can request vaccine-sample testing at the Central Drugs Laboratory, Kasauli, and receive a batch-release certificate online. They can also add licence details issued by state FDAs. SUGAM is described as a database of permissions and licences issued by those state authorities, including manufacturer, site, and formulation.

A founder who needs a new-drug permission, an import licence, or a clinical-trial permission is in this central lane. A founder who needs a manufacturing licence for a drug the state already licenses is in the state lane, with CDSCO in a coordinating and, for some categories, approving role. The correct form follows the product and the rule, not the size of the PLI headline.

## Clinical trials have their own rules

The New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019, were notified as G.S.R. 227(E). CDSCO’s new-drugs pages host the rules, a common technical document guidance, checklists, and FAQs, including an FAQ set dated 24 September 2025. The rules apply to new drugs, investigational new drugs for human use, clinical trials, bioavailability and bioequivalence studies, and ethics committees. They came into force on publication, with Chapter IV following 180 days later. The text requires a six-monthly status report of each clinical trial — ongoing, completed, or terminated — to be submitted to the Central Licensing Authority on SUGAM.

That reporting duty is the outlook for a sponsor: the trial file stays electronic and periodic, and the ethics committee is inside the rule, not beside it. A biotech company running a first-in-human study and a formulation company running a bioequivalence study for an export dossier both need this rulebook. They do not need the bulk-drug park guidelines for the trial permission itself.

## Manufacturing practice is a 2023 substitution of Schedule M

On 28 December 2023 the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare notified G.S.R. 922(E). It amends the Drugs Rules so that the phrase in the relevant licence conditions becomes Good Manufacturing Practices and Requirements of Premises, Plant and Equipment for Pharmaceutical Products, and it substitutes Schedule M. The schedule is what a licensee’s premises, plant, and quality system are inspected against. Compliance dates and any later extension sit in the gazette and in subsequent health-ministry or CDSCO notices. Read those notices before you publish a deadline. The existence of the substituted schedule is already enough to change a plant design: documentation, validation, and premises requirements are part of the licence, not a voluntary export extra.

## What this outlook refuses to predict

SUGAM and the National Single Window System, which CDSCO’s home page points applicants toward, are filing routes. They are not published service-level guarantees of a decision in a fixed number of days. An outlook memo can say where the file goes. It starts inventing when it promises a median approval time the organisation has not published in the page you are citing.

Department of Pharmaceuticals schemes — the two PLIs, the three bulk-drug parks, PRIP — can change whether a plant is financeable. CDSCO and the state drug controller still decide whether the product may be studied, made, imported, or sold. Keep both files.
