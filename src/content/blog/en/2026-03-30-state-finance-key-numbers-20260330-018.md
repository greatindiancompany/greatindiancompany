# The state fiscal ratios that survive a headline

A state-budget headline usually quotes one percentage. The Reserve Bank's study of state budgets for 2025-26, released on 23 January 2026, prints several, and they answer different questions. The figures below are the ones in that press release (2025-2026/1982). They are consolidated ratios. State-wise tables in the same study can tell a different story, and later accounts can revise a budget estimate.

## Gross fiscal deficit, 3.3 per cent of GDP

The consolidated gross fiscal deficit was 3.3 per cent of GDP in 2024-25, after three years below 3.0 per cent. States budgeted 3.3 per cent of GDP again for 2025-26.

Gross fiscal deficit is the gap the government must finance after both revenue and capital spending. It is wider than the revenue deficit, which counts only the current account. A state can show a revenue surplus and still borrow because it is building assets. Statement 2 of the study is the revenue deficit or surplus. Statement 3 is the gross fiscal deficit. Statement 4 decomposes the deficit, which is where a reader sees how much of the gap is capital.

The press release attaches a cause to the move through 3 per cent: it mainly reflects 50-year interest-free loans under Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment, and those loans sit above the normal net borrowing ceiling. The 3.3 is a financing number. It is also a classification number. Chart II.18 is the study's picture of the deficit next to those loans.

Two denominators are in use. The press-release ratios are shares of national GDP. Chart II.2 and Statement 20 use each state's GSDP. A state's deficit can look moderate against India's GDP and tight against its own GSDP, or the reverse. Quote the denominator with the ratio.

## Capital expenditure, 2.7 then a budgeted 3.2

Capital expenditure was 2.7 per cent of GDP in 2023-24 and 2.7 per cent in 2024-25. The 2025-26 budgets put it at 3.2 per cent of GDP. Capital outlay is the piece of spending that creates assets. Revenue expenditure pays salaries, pensions, interest, subsidies, and operations. Chart II.7 is major components of capital outlay. Chart II.8 is the composition of expenditure. A rise in the capital ratio is a quality signal only after you check that revenue spending has a matching receipt, and that the capital line is an account and a cash outflow.

## Outstanding liabilities, 29.2 per cent of GDP

The budget estimate of consolidated outstanding liabilities is 29.2 per cent of GDP at end-March 2026. The Bank describes the stock as elevated in the post-pandemic period. A flow (this year's deficit) and a stock (debt already issued) move on different clocks. Interest on the stock is committed expenditure in later years. Statement 13 is interest payments. Statements 18 to 20 are the composition and the GSDP ratios of the stock.

The Sixteenth Finance Commission memorandum, accepted with the Union Budget, treats 3 per cent of GSDP as the ordinary state deficit cap and leaves SASCI loans outside that cap. The government accepted that borrowing quantum in principle and left amendments to state fiscal-responsibility laws for separate examination. The 3.3 per cent of GDP in the Reserve Bank release and the 3 per cent of GSDP in the memorandum are related rules with different bases. They are easy to mash into one sentence.

## Own tax, devolution, and grants are receipt ratios

The study's receipt tables separate what a state raises from what the Union transfers. Chart II.4 is states' own tax revenue. Statement 14 is tax revenue. Statement 17 is devolution and other transfers. The Finance Commission memorandum keeps the states' share of the divisible pool at 41 per cent for 2026-27 to 2030-31, and the government accepted that share. Own tax plus that devolution plus grants is the receipt side. A deficit ratio without the receipt mix hides whether the state is living on its own base.

## A short order of reading

1. Name the vintage: account, revised estimate, or budget estimate.
2. Name the denominator: GDP or GSDP.
3. Read revenue deficit and gross fiscal deficit as a pair (Statements 2 and 3).
4. See how much of the deficit is capital, and how much is the interest-free capital loan.
5. Then read the debt stock and the interest line.

The study's contents page lists every statement. The all-state press-release ratios are the orientation. They are the start of a state file.
