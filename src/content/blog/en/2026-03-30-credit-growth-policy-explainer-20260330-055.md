# What the policy rate does, and does not do, to bank credit

The policy rate explains the price of a large part of the loan book. It does not explain why the book is growing this fast.

## The rate path

During 2025-26 the Monetary Policy Committee cut the repo rate by **100 basis points** and kept a neutral stance from June 2025. Liquidity sat in surplus. Average daily net absorption under the liquidity adjustment facility rose to **₹1.86 lakh crore**, from ₹1,605 crore the year before. The Reserve Bank added durable liquidity with open-market purchases, dollar-rupee buy-sell swaps, longer-tenor variable-rate repos, and four cash-reserve-ratio cuts that totalled **100 basis points**. The weighted average call rate averaged 7 basis points under the repo rate during the year.

In April 2026 the committee left the repo rate at **5.25%** and stayed neutral. It did the same on 3–5 June 2026 and again on 3–5 August 2026. The standing deposit facility rate is **5.00%**. The marginal standing facility rate and the Bank Rate are **5.50%**. The August resolution lifted the 2026-27 real GDP projection to **6.7%**, from 6.6% in June, and said investment indicators, including bank credit, were still resilient. The inflation target remains 4%, with a band of 2 percentage points either side, for April 2026 through March 2031.

## Transmission has been real, and lately uneven

The Annual Report says the pass-through of the easing cycle that began in February 2025 was strong, helped by surplus liquidity. A larger share of floating-rate loans is tied to an external benchmark, and a smaller share to the marginal cost of funds.

The August 2026 policy statement measures the cumulative result through June 2026. Against **125 basis points** of repo cuts since February 2025, the weighted average lending rate on fresh rupee loans was down **80 basis points**, and the rate on outstanding rupee loans was down **91 basis points**. On deposits, the weighted average domestic term-deposit rate was down **63 basis points** on fresh deposits and **51 basis points** on outstanding deposits. The statement says pass-through to fresh lending rates has moderated in recent months because credit demand is strong. In May and June, deposit and lending rates hardened even though the repo rate was unchanged.

The June statement, covering the period through April 2026, had shown a larger pass-through on fresh deposits (85 basis points) and a slightly larger one on fresh loans (83 basis points). The later, smaller fresh-deposit figure is the one to use. Transmission is not a ratchet.

An earlier checkpoint, in the Trend and Progress chapter, stopped at October 2025: 69 basis points of pass-through on fresh loans against the 100 basis point cut from February to June 2025, after banks had passed through 182 of the 250 basis points of tightening from May 2022 to January 2025. The 2026 statements supersede that checkpoint. They also show the asymmetry the chapter flagged between public-sector and private-sector banks is still the right question. The national average hides it.

## Credit growth did not wait for a perfect pass-through

On 15 July 2026, bank credit was up **17.7%** year on year, against 9.9% a year earlier. Credit from all sources was up **16.3%** in 2026-27 so far, against 11.5% a year earlier. The Governor’s June statement had put bank credit at **16.2%** on 15 May 2026, against 9.8%, and credit from all sources at **15.4%** for 2025-26, against 12.1%. The August text says the sector pattern is still broad: retail and services are buoyant, industry is stronger because MSME credit has held up and large industry has picked up, and agriculture is steady.

That combination — a paused repo rate, incomplete pass-through on fresh loans, and high-teens credit growth — is the policy fact. Demand is firm enough that fresh-rate transmission can stall without the book stalling.

## Rules that sit beside the rate

Two non-rate decisions belong in the same reading. From 1 April 2026, collateral-free loans to micro and small enterprises go up to **₹20 lakh**, from ₹10 lakh. The Bank’s stated regulatory agenda pairs customer protection with the credit system: a review of directions on interest rates and credit-risk management, market mechanisms for transferring credit risk, and a framework for shared loan arrangements.

For a borrower, the repo rate at 5.25% is the anchor, the fresh-loan rate is about 80 basis points lower than at the start of the easing cycle rather than 125, and the availability of credit is a separate fact from that discount.
