India's semiconductor headlines add together numbers that sit in different accounts. One account is the money companies have committed to approved plants. Another is the fiscal outlay the Cabinet has set aside. A third is a single year's budget provision. A fourth is an expected outcome written into a scheme on the day it was approved. This brief keeps them in separate columns. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## Project commitments

These are the figures in Cabinet approvals. They describe the investment attached to a project. They are not a report of cash already spent, and the releases used here do not publish a disbursement against them.

| --- | --- | --- |

| 29 February 2024 | Tata Electronics with PSMC, Dholera | ₹91,000 crore |

The factsheet splits the August 2025 four into ₹2,066 crore, ₹1,943 crore, ₹117 crore, and ₹468 crore. The approval release itself publishes the group total. Where a later paragraph needs one number for all 12, use Parliament's figure, not a fresh sum. The [22 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) says the 12 projects carry investment commitments of about ₹1.64 lakh crore. The [15 July 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) says over ₹1.64 lakh crore. Quote one of those sentences. Do not add the rows above and present the addition as an official total. Micron's rupee figure, Kaynes, and HCL-Foxconn are factsheet rows, and the May 2026 pair is a combined figure.

## Fiscal outlays

The 2021 programme outlay is ₹76,000 crore. That is the public framework notified on 21 December 2021, as restated in the February 2024 release. A [7 February 2026 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) describes fiscal support of up to 50 percent inside that framework for silicon fabs, compound facilities, assembly and test, and chip design. The support is a share of eligible project cost. It is not a second ₹76,000 crore on top of the companies' commitments, and it is not automatically equal to half of ₹1.64 lakh crore. Each project's fiscal support is set at appraisal.

Semicon 2.0, approved on 15 July 2026, has a budget outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore. That is a new Cabinet decision. The 15 July release does not publish a revised percentage of project cost. Until the implementing notification is on the MeitY site, an investor cannot treat ₹1,27,500 crore as a rate card.

Two smaller official numbers belong to the budget year, not to the multi-year outlay. The 7 February note records a provision of ₹1,000 crore for ISM 2.0 in FY 2026-27, and a financial outlay of ₹8,000 crore for the modified semiconductor and display programme in that same year. Those are annual figures. Placing them in the same sentence as ₹1,27,500 crore without the dates makes the programme look either underfunded or triple-counted.

## Money that is not a fab commitment

Design is a separate pool. The Design Linked Incentive, notified on 21 December 2021, caps product-design reimbursement at ₹15 crore per application and deployment support at ₹30 crore per application. The February 2026 budget note says that, as of January 2026, startups under the scheme had attracted nearly ₹430 crore of venture capital. That venture figure is private capital reported in the note. It is not a government disbursement, and it should not be added to the ₹1.64 lakh crore manufacturing commitments.

Adjacent schemes publish expected investment, which is a target written at approval:

- PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware, [Cabinet approval 17 May 2023](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1924766): budgetary outlay ₹17,000 crore, expected incremental investment ₹2,430 crore, expected incremental production ₹3.35 lakh crore.
- Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme, in the [26 April 2025 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620): outlay ₹22,919 crore, envisaged investment ₹59,350 crore, envisaged production ₹4,56,500 crore.

"Expected" and "envisaged" are the words in those releases. They become useful to an investor when a company shows the approval letter and the claim milestones. They are not a measure of capital already on the ground.

The February 2026 note also sets FY 2026-27 projections inside the modified programme: ₹4,000 crore of investment during the year under the semiconductor-fab scheme, and ₹11,000 crore under the compound, sensor, and packaging scheme. Those are year-ahead projections for support during 2026-27. They are not a revision of the ₹1.64 lakh crore already attached to approved projects.

## What a commitment still leaves open

The releases do not report cumulative expenditure against the approved projects. Commercial production at Micron, Kaynes, and CG Semi, as stated on 22 July 2026, is the strongest public sign that some of the capital has been installed. The silicon fab at Dholera is scheduled, in the 15 July 2026 Cabinet text, for commissioning in 2028. For that project, ₹91,000 crore remains an approved investment, and the public record used here does not convert it into a percentage completed.

A term sheet can cite the approval date, the partner, the capacity, and the commitment. It should cite the fiscal outlay in a different sentence, and it should mark scheme-level "expected investment" as a target.
