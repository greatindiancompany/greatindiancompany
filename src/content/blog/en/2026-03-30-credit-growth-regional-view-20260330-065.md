# Most of India’s bank credit is used in metropolitan centres

The Reserve Bank’s March 2026 credit release does not rank states in its press note. It does say where the loan is used, by the population of the place, and that cut is the official regional picture a reader can quote without inventing a state table.

## Four population groups, one date

The annual Basic Statistical Return for March 2026 classifies outstanding credit by the population of the place where the credit is utilised, using Census 2011. Rural means under 10,000. Semi-urban means 10,000 to under 1 lakh. Urban means 1 lakh to under 10 lakh. Metropolitan means 10 lakh and above.

At end-March 2026 the shares were:

- Rural: **9.4%**
- Semi-urban: **14.5%**
- Urban: **17.9%**
- Metropolitan: **58.2%**

Metropolitan places of utilisation hold a clear majority of the stock. Rural and semi-urban places together are under a quarter. Every one of the four groups grew in double digits year on year. The release does not print a separate growth rate for each group, so a precise rural-versus-metro race is not in the press note. The full state, district, and centre tables are on the Database on Indian Economy with the BSR publication. Until those cells are read, a national brief should not name a fastest state.

The return follows the place of utilisation, not the city on the bank’s letterhead. A metro-booked limit used in a smaller centre is supposed to sit with that centre. That is the right concept for “where credit lands.” It is still a 2011 population map, so towns that have grown past a threshold since the census can be classified below their present size.

## Who is lending into that map

On the same return, private-sector bank credit growth rose to **12.3%** at end-March 2026, from **9.5%** a year earlier. Public-sector banks grew faster than the 14.1% headline for all scheduled commercial banks, including regional rural banks. The release does not give the public-sector percentage in the highlight. Small finance banks kept growing faster than the system and lifted their share of total credit to **1.6%**, from **1.0%** in March 2021. Their share is still small. Their direction is the opposite of a metro-only system, because small finance banks are licensed toward smaller borrowers, but the press note does not split their book by population group.

A new district-level series in this round shows credit to individuals by gender. Nationally, women were **24.7%** of credit to individuals, against 23.8% a year earlier. The district file is the place to see whether that share is a metro fact or a broader one. The highlight is only the national number.

## Rural credit delivery is a second series

Population-group shares are not the same thing as agricultural credit schemes. Operative Kisan Credit Cards fell **11.7%** in 2025-26, and the outstanding amount on the scheme fell **4.9%**. The Annual Report’s table puts operative cards at **256.2 lakh** and total outstanding at **₹5,71,444 crore**, against 290.2 lakh cards and ₹6,00,975 crore in 2024-25. The Bank says the decline comes mainly from some private-sector banks reclassifying exposures that did not fit the card guidelines. The count of operative cards also excludes non-performing accounts. A lower KCC stock is not, on the Bank’s own note, evidence that rural places of utilisation lost credit. Those places were still inside a double-digit national expansion.

Lead-bank responsibility covers **778 districts**, through 12 public-sector banks and two private-sector banks, Jammu & Kashmir Bank and ICICI Bank. That is the administrative map for priority-sector credit planning. It is not a growth ranking.

## How to use this without a false state story

An investor or a state official who wants “credit growth in this state” has to open the BSR state table. The sentence the press note will support is narrower and still important: almost three-fifths of outstanding bank credit is used in metropolitan centres, the other three groups are not shrinking, and a fall in Kisan Credit Card balances has a stated classification cause. Great Indian Company stops at that sentence on purpose. A made-up state league table would be easier to search for and worse to trust.

The national rates those local tables should be compared with are in [the key figures](/blog/credit-growth-key-numbers-20260330-015). The priority-sector and MSME stocks that lead banks actually plan against are in [the MSME note](/blog/credit-growth-impact-on-smes-20260330-025).
