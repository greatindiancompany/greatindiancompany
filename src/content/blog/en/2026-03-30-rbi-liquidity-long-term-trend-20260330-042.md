The useful long comparison in the Reserve Bank’s own tables is one year, not a decade reconstructed from memory. The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 18 September 2026 places the week ended 11 September 2026 next to the week ended 12 September 2025, and the fortnight ended 31 August 2026 next to the fortnight ended 5 September 2025. The ratios moved in one direction. The yield curve moved in another.

## Cash fell as a share of deposits, credit rose

For the fortnight ended 31 August 2026 the cash-deposit ratio is 3.22 per cent. A year earlier, for the fortnight ended 5 September 2025, it was 4.27 per cent. The cash reserve ratio in the weekly block fell over the same span from 3.75 per cent to 3.00 per cent. The notification of 6 June 2025 had set that destination: 3.0 per cent of net demand and time liabilities from the fortnight beginning 29 November 2025, after steps at 3.75, 3.50 and 3.25. The lower cash-deposit ratio sits beside that completed cut. The supplement also records, in a footnote, that from 15 December 2025 a reporting fortnight ends on the 15th and the last day of the month. The year-ago fortnight and the latest fortnight are not drawn on the old Friday calendar.

The credit-deposit ratio for the fortnight ended 31 August 2026 is 80.32 per cent, against 79.43 a year earlier. The investment-deposit ratio is 26.06 per cent, against 28.81. Incremental ratios for the latest fortnight are 62.56 for credit and 18.40 for investment, against 51.15 and 11.22 a year earlier. Both incremental ratios are higher than a year ago. The level of investments relative to deposits is lower, and the level of credit relative to deposits is higher.

The Bulletin extract dated 11 September 2026 gives the stocks behind those ratios, as on 31 August. Aggregate deposits of scheduled commercial banks are ₹2,78,71,530 crore, with year-on-year growth of 17.8 per cent. Bank credit is ₹2,23,87,567 crore, with year-on-year growth of 19.1 per cent. In the money-stock table, time deposits are up 17.1 per cent year on year and demand deposits are up 18.7 per cent year on year, while demand deposits are down 6.3 per cent in 2026–27 so far. The drift inside the year is toward time deposits and toward credit, with a smaller share of the deposit base held as cash and as investments.

## The policy rate eased, and the long yield did not

In the week ended 12 September 2025 the repo rate was 5.50 per cent, the standing deposit facility 5.25 per cent and the marginal standing facility 5.75 per cent. In the week ended 11 September 2026 those rates are 5.25, 5.00 and 5.50. The last step of that move was the 25 basis point cut on 5 December 2025. The 2026 resolutions left the corridor there. The fixed reverse repo rate is 3.35 per cent in both weeks. The statutory liquidity ratio is 18.00 per cent in both weeks.

The call rate did follow the policy rate down, and then went further. It was 5.36 per cent in the week ended 12 September 2025 and 4.97 per cent in the week ended 11 September 2026. The 91-day treasury bill primary yield went from 5.51 per cent to 5.21 per cent. The 364-day yield went from 5.67 per cent to 5.91 per cent. The 10-year par yield went from 6.57 per cent to 7.05 per cent, which is 48 basis points higher against a repo rate that is 25 basis points lower. The one-year bill and the 10-year par yield rose over a year in which the overnight policy rate fell.

The rupee spot rate in the same table went from ₹88.28 per US dollar to ₹95.72. One-month forward premia went from 1.85 per cent to 3.05 per cent. A liquidity series and an exchange-rate series are printed together because foreign-exchange operations are one of the durable liquidity tools. The table itself is the year-on-year record.

## The surplus through 2026 got smaller, and it stayed a surplus

The Governor’s statements give the net liquidity adjustment facility position as an average daily surplus, in lakh crore of rupees. The June statement’s footnote records average daily net absorption of ₹1.7 lakh crore in March 2026, ₹3.9 lakh crore in April and ₹1.7 lakh crore in May, with the average up to 3 June at ₹1.3 lakh crore. The August statement’s footnote records ₹1.7 lakh crore in May, ₹0.9 lakh crore in June and ₹1.1 lakh crore in July. Since the June meeting, the August statement puts the average daily surplus at ₹1.0 lakh crore.

The peak in that published run is April. The later months are still absorption, at a smaller average. The money-market release of 21 September then reports a different concept, net durable liquidity, as a surplus of ₹10,66,303 crore as on 31 August 2026. The daily facility average and the durable stock are both surplus readings. The durable stock is an order of magnitude larger than the ₹1.0 lakh crore average facility surplus cited for the period after the June meeting. The trend to keep is that the facility surplus narrowed after April and remained a surplus, while the 31 August durable figure was still a large positive stock.

Currency with the public rose 12.8 per cent year on year to ₹41,94,110 crore as on 31 August, and money supply rose 16.7 per cent to ₹3,32,21,648 crore. Net Reserve Bank credit to government in that table is ₹1,89,819 crore lower in 2026–27 so far. Those are the slow variables. The fast variable is the call rate, which in the latest week printed under the floor after a summer in which, from 6 June to 31 July, it had averaged 6 basis points above the repo rate.
