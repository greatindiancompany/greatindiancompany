An investment implication is only as good as the instrument it is tied to. For India AI and data governance, three numbers get used as if they were the same kind of money. They are not.

## The number that is a government outlay

The Lok Sabha reply of 5 August 2026, published by PIB on 6 August, says the Government approved the IndiaAI Mission on 7 March 2024 with a total outlay of Rs. 10,371.92 crore over five years. That is the figure to underwrite as the Cabinet-approved mission budget. Other notes round it. The Rajya Sabha reply of 24 July 2026 says ₹10,371 crore. The 13 August 2026 backgrounder says "about ₹10,372 crore." The 6 March 2025 anniversary note says "Rs. 10,372 crore." Cite Rs. 10,371.92 crore when precision matters, and cite the rounded line only as that later note's rounding.

On 6 March 2025 the MeitY Secretary said the compute portal was the largest component, with nearly 45 percent of the Mission's funding allocated to it. Treat that as a ministerial allocation statement on that day. The reply of 5 August 2026 does not republish a pillar-wise split, so this brief does not invent one.

The outlay is spending authority for a public mission. It is not the revenue of companies that use the compute, and it is not a fund an outside investor can subscribe.

## Capacity that the state does not necessarily own

The GPUs in the IndiaAI pool are described as empaneled. The 25 March 2026 Lok Sabha material says 38,231 GPUs from 14 service providers. The 13 August 2026 backgrounder says shared capacity of 45,000+ GPUs as of June 2026. Empanelment is access contracted from providers, at a subsidised rate. It is not a line that says the Government bought 45,000 GPUs onto its own balance sheet.

The India AI Governance Guidelines of November 2025 separately say a secure cluster "is also being constructed" for 3,000 next-generation GPUs for sovereign and strategic uses. That sentence is a construction statement in the guidelines. It is not an addition to the empaneled pool, and this brief has no later note that says those 3,000 are commissioned.

Hours consumed are a third quantity. By August 2026, 237 projects had used 93.18 lakh GPU hours. That is demand on the subsidy, not capital expenditure.

## A summit sentence that is not a cap table

PIB's note of 13 March 2026 says the India AI Impact Summit 2026 saw investment commitments of around $250 billion in AI infrastructure. The note calls them commitments. It does not publish a list of contracted amounts, a closing date, or a split between Indian public capital and foreign private capital. An investor who adds $250 billion to Rs. 10,371.92 crore is adding a press sentence to a Cabinet outlay.

The same note says the Union Budget 2026–27 announced a long-term tax holiday for data-centre and cloud investment. The holiday is a budget announcement in that note. The conditions sit in the Finance Act and the relevant notification, which this brief does not restate because they are tax law, not the DPDP Act.

## What the penalty schedule is not

The DPDP Schedule ceilings, up to ₹250 crore for a security failure, are maximum penalties once the penalty provisions are in force. Those provisions are in the eighteen-month set and, as of 24 September 2026, start on 13 May 2027. They are not a present cash cost, and they are not a market size for "compliance." A business that sells readiness can describe the future duty. It cannot book the ceiling as a fine already levied.

The duty that can already bind a payments investment is the Reserve Bank's storage rule of 6 April 2018. That is a condition of operating a payment system, in force for years. It is discussed in the [cross-border note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-india-vs-global-benchmark-meity-gov-20260330-270). The [investor checklist](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-for-investors-meity-gov-20260330-790) puts the same distinction into a reading order.
