The medium-term path that can be defended is a set of dates already written down. Some are commissioning schedules. Some are scheme tenures. Some are aspirations in a budget backgrounder that a later Cabinet note did not repeat. This brief sorts them. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## Dates that are schedules

The silicon fab has the clearest future date in the manufacturing book. On [15 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) the Cabinet said the first fab is scheduled to be commissioned in 2028. The fab that matches "first" in the approval record is Tata Electronics with PSMC at Dholera, approved on [29 February 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2010132) at 50,000 wafer starts a month, including 28 nm high-performance compute. The same July note places the technology journey so far at 28 nm to 110 nm, and puts more advanced nodes under the research pillar, as work with research centres, rather than as a second fab with its own year.

Packaging is already on the near side of that date. The [Lok Sabha reply of 22 July 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) says commercial production had commenced at Micron, Kaynes Semicon, and CG Semi. The July Cabinet note added that one more unit was expected to start in 2026, without naming it. Between late 2026 and 2028, the trajectory the texts support is: three packaging plants already producing, a fourth production start still expected inside 2026, and the silicon fab on a 2028 commissioning schedule. Compound, glass packaging, discrete, and the gallium-nitride display plant are approved and, in these texts, not given their own commissioning years.

## Dates that are scheme tenures

PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware was notified on 29 May 2023 and runs for six years, with an average incentive around 5 percent of incremental sales of laptops, tablets, all-in-one PCs, servers, and ultra-small-form-factor devices. A six-year tenure from a 2023 notification is still open through the back half of this decade. The [scheme page](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/production-linked-incentive-scheme-pli-2-0-for-it-hardware-wM0MDOtQWa) is the tenure to cite. The Cabinet's expected incremental production of ₹3.35 lakh crore was an expectation at approval in May 2023, not a milestone dated to a later year.

The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme runs from 2025-26 to 2031-32, six years with one year of gestation, on an outlay of ₹22,919 crore. That is the longest dated instrument in the electronics stack this brief uses. Its envisaged production of ₹4,56,500 crore and envisaged investment of ₹59,350 crore are tenure-long targets in the [26 April 2025 note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620), not annual run-rates.

The Design Linked Incentive was notified for five years of support, with applications originally open for three years from 1 January 2022 and an extension left to the Minister. Approved design projects continue against their milestones even after a reader has started talking about Semicon 2.0. The design pillar of the new programme is additional policy. It does not, in the 15 July text, close or replace the 2021 scheme's ceilings of ₹15 crore and ₹30 crore.

## The budget year inside the longer path

FY 2026-27 has its own official targets, and they are one year wide. The [7 February 2026 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) records a ₹1,000 crore provision for ISM 2.0 in that year and an ₹8,000 crore outlay for the modified semiconductor and display programme. It projects support for one fab (₹4,000 crore of investment and 1,500 jobs during the year), nine compound or packaging units (₹11,000 crore and 3,000 jobs), and 30 design companies (10 IP cores and 200 design jobs). Those are the in-year waypoints. The multi-year outlay decided later, on 15 July, is ₹1,27,500 crore. A trajectory chart should show the annual provision and the multi-year outlay on separate lines, because the July decision did not describe itself as a replacement sentence for the February annual figures.

## Aspirations the Cabinet note did not restate

The February budget note also says that by 2029 India is expected to be able to design and manufacture chips for nearly 70 to 75 percent of domestic applications, that the next phase has a roadmap toward 3-nanometre and 2-nanometre nodes, and that by 2035 India aims to be among the top semiconductor nations. It attributes a market path of about $38 billion in 2023 and $100 billion to $110 billion by 2030 to industry estimates.

The 15 July Cabinet decision, which is the binding approval for Semicon 2.0, does not repeat the 2029 percentage, the 3 nm and 2 nm years, or the 2035 rank. It does say the research pillar will pursue more advanced nodes than 28 nm to 110 nm, and that the aim of the design pillar is Indian intellectual property. A mid-term plan can report the February sentences as the budget backgrounder's language, dated 7 February 2026. The schedule a board can underwrite from the later decision is the 2028 fab commissioning and the six-pillar mandate, with rates still to be notified.

Academic 180 nm fabrication at the Semiconductor Laboratory in Mohali, which the February note records as 56 chips from university designs, is a capability that already exists on a research line. It is the wrong line to extend into a 2028 commercial forecast. The commercial silicon commitment remains Dholera at the node and capacity in the 2024 approval.
