The surplus is real on the published pages, and it is not a single number. Three dated facts tell a reader where it could change shape before the next policy meeting: the call rate has slipped under the floor, a large variable rate reverse repo book matures on 7 October, and the Monetary Policy Committee expects inflation to peak in the quarter now under way. The stance that governs the response is still neutral.

## The operating target left the corridor in the latest week

From 6 June to 31 July 2026 the weighted average call rate traded inside the corridor and, on the August Governor’s statement, averaged 6 basis points above the repo rate, at 5.31 per cent. The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 18 September 2026 shows what happened after that window. The weighted average call rate was 5.20 per cent in the week ended 28 August, 5.07 per cent in the week ended 4 September, and 4.97 per cent in the week ended 11 September. The standing deposit facility on that page is 5.00 per cent. The repo rate is 5.25 per cent. The latest weekly average is 3 basis points under the floor and 28 basis points under the policy rate.

A print under the floor means the overnight average cleared below the rate at which banks can place funds with the Reserve Bank. The August statement says the Bank’s two-way operations are guided by aligning the call rate with the repo rate. The distance from 4.97 to 5.25 is the gap that instruction is aimed at. It opened up after a summer in which the average had been slightly above the repo rate, not below the floor.

The first week of September shows the desk already absorbing. The Bulletin extract of 11 September records net absorption of ₹8,30,561 crore on 4 September, of which ₹6,02,394 crore was variable rate reverse repo and ₹2,28,290 crore was the standing deposit facility. Outright open market sales and purchases are blank from 31 August through 6 September. On 31 August the Bank had also announced a second overnight variable rate reverse repo for that day, notified amount ₹4,00,000 crore. The announcement is the intention. The result of that auction is a separate release. The daily row in the extract is the absorption that was booked.

## A book of ₹2.9 lakh crore matures on one October day

The money-market release dated 21 September, for 20 September, lists outstanding variable rate reverse repos at a 5.24 per cent cut-off:

- ₹2,22,629 crore from 18 September, 3-day, maturing 21 September
- ₹52,449 crore from 11 September, 26-day, maturing 7 October
- ₹2,39,276 crore from 7 September, 30-day, maturing 7 October

The two October lines sum to ₹2,91,725 crore. When they mature, that absorption rolls off unless the Bank replaces it. The 3-day line was already due the next working day. On 18 September the Bank had announced a further overnight variable rate reverse repo for 21 September, notified amount ₹1,00,000 crore, to be reversed on 22 September. Replacement is a choice the desk makes auction by auction. The published maturity schedule says the choice on the October book is about ₹2.92 lakh crore on a single date, at a cut-off one basis point under the repo rate.

Net durable liquidity as on 31 August is a surplus of ₹10,66,303 crore on the same release. The Governor’s average daily facility surplus since the June meeting is ₹1.0 lakh crore. A large durable surplus can coexist with a call rate that the Bank still has to pull up toward the repo, which is what the June-to-July average of 5.31 per cent already showed, and it can coexist with a call rate that has since fallen through the floor. The risk is a swing between those two prints when government cash, currency and the reverse repo book move together. The Centre’s surplus cash balance reckoned for auction was zero on 18 September. State government loans and advances had risen by ₹24,824 crore in the week to 4 September, to ₹27,940 crore. Neither line is a forecast. Both are recent frictional moves large enough to matter next to a ₹1 lakh crore overnight auction.

## The inflation path is the reason the stance can tighten

The resolution of 3–5 August 2026 holds the repo rate at 5.25 per cent and retains the neutral stance so the Committee can respond to the macroeconomy. CPI inflation was 4.4 per cent in June 2026. The projection for 2026–27 is 5.0 per cent, with the third quarter at 5.9 per cent and the fourth at 5.5 per cent. Core inflation is projected at 4.3 per cent for the year. The resolution says the rise is food and fuel, that underlying inflation excluding precious metals has been benign, and that the outlook is hazy because of the monsoon, El Niño, geopolitics and trade policy. It also says any later action would have to reckon with underlying inflation moving up from the low levels seen so far.

A neutral stance is permission to absorb, to inject, or to move the repo rate when the Committee has the clarity it says it is waiting for. The next meeting is 5–7 October 2026, the same week the ₹2,91,725 crore reverse repo book matures. The liquidity risk into that week is mechanical: a call rate under the floor, a maturation that returns funds unless it is rolled, and an inflation projection that the Committee has already marked as peaking in this quarter. The durable surplus of ₹10,66,303 crore is the stock underneath. It does not, by itself, fix the call rate to 5.25 per cent. The August statement’s own objective is that alignment, pursued with two-way operations.
