# The 4% CPI target and the August hold at 5.25%

India’s monetary policy target is a retail index, not a wholesale index and not a core index. The Reserve Bank’s Annual Report, dated 29 May 2026, records the Gazette notification of 25 March 2026: the central government kept the target at **headline consumer-price inflation of 4%**, with an upper tolerance of **6%** and a lower tolerance of **2%**, for 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031. [Annual Report chapter](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1463).

Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, as the report restates it, requires the government, in consultation with the Reserve Bank, to set that CPI target once every five years. The same chapter says the 4% headline target with a 2–6% band was notified in 2016, retained in 2021 through March 2026, and retained again through March 2031. A discussion paper on 21 August 2025 had asked whether policy should aim at headline or at core. The notified choice, as the Annual Report describes the Gazette, is headline CPI.

Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta’s speech on 5 May 2026 describes the same renewal: 4% with a tolerance of plus or minus 2 percentage points, through March 2031, with the existing features of the framework kept. [Speech](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1557).

## What the committee did on 5 August

The Monetary Policy Committee held its 62nd meeting from 3 to 5 August 2026. It voted unanimously to leave the policy repo rate at **5.25%**. The standing deposit facility rate stays at 5.00%. The marginal standing facility rate and the Bank Rate stay at 5.50%. The stance stays **neutral**. [Resolution](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63287).

The resolution’s inflation projection for 2026–27 is **5.0%**:

| Period | CPI projection |
| --- | --- |
| Q2, 2026–27 (July–September) | 4.7% |
| Q3 (October–December) | 5.9% |
| Q4 (January–March) | 5.5% |
| Full year 2026–27 | 5.0% |
| Q1, 2027–28 | 5.3% |

Risks are described as evenly balanced. Core inflation, CPI excluding food and fuel, is projected at **4.3%** for 2026–27. Core excluding precious metals is expected to be lower in the near term and to move toward core inflation later in the year. Real GDP growth for 2026–27 is projected at 6.7%.

The committee’s reasons, in its own order: headline inflation had moved above the target, as expected; the Q1 outcome was still a bit under the projection; the rise was mostly food and fuel; pass-through of costs looked limited; core excluding precious metals was still benign; headline inflation was expected to peak in Q3 and ease after that. Growth was described as resilient, and as expected to be lower in 2026–27. The resolution says the committee wanted more clarity on the inflation path before acting, and that any later action would also have to reckon with underlying inflation moving up from the very low levels of the previous year.

Minutes were due on 19 August 2026. The resolution is also in the [August 2026 Bulletin](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24366), released on 25 August. The next meeting is **5–7 October 2026**.

## Headline, core, and the August print

“Above the target” in the August resolution means above 4%, the point target. It does not mean outside the 2–6% band. The data the resolution discusses stop at June, which it rounds to 4.4%. MoSPI’s combined June rate on the 2024 base is 4.38%. Core in May and June is given as 3.9%, and core excluding precious metals as 2.3–2.5%.

MoSPI’s later release, on 14 September, put August combined CPI at **4.82%** provisional, July at 4.45% final, and food at 5.95%. August is above 4% and below 6%. The August CPI release does not publish a core rate, so the 3.9% figure should stay attached to May–June. The personal-care division at 15.17% in August, and jewellery item rates above 35%, are the retail trace of the precious-metals point the committee separated from underlying inflation.

The Q3 projection of 5.9% is still inside the 6% upper tolerance. It is also close to it. That is a projection from 5 August, not a realised quarter. September CPI had not been released when this note was written. It is scheduled for 12 October, after the October meeting.

## What an operator should take from the framework

The rate that anchors policy is combined headline CPI. Wholesale inflation at 9.92% in August is real for costs and is not the target variable. Core is the measure the committee uses to judge whether food and fuel are spreading. The published stance is neutral, which the resolution says is there so the committee can respond as the picture clears. The published repo rate is 5.25%.

The risks the committee named for the next few prints are set out in the note on [food, fuel, and the October calendar](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/inflation-trends-risk-watch-20260330-074).
