# Three Different Rupee Figures in India’s Green Hydrogen Record

Public talk often stacks every green hydrogen rupee into one pool. The documents separate at least three pools, and an investor who mixes them will underwrite the wrong thing.

## The programme envelope

The [MNRE mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) states an initial outlay of ₹19,744 crore. Inside it, the Strategic Interventions for Green Hydrogen Transition programme is ₹17,490 crore through 2029-30, pilot projects are ₹1,466 crore, research and development is ₹400 crore, and other components are ₹388 crore. The same page lists narrower ceilings: ₹455 crore for low-carbon steel pilots through 2029-30, ₹496 crore for mobility pilots through 2025-26, ₹115 crore for shipping pilots through 2025-26, and ₹400 crore through 2025-26 for hubs and other projects. This is the Union government’s scheme envelope. It is administered through guidelines MNRE publishes, including the [schemes list](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/). It is not the capital expenditure of every private plant.

## The catalysed-investment sentence

A second figure is an expectation. The Press Information Bureau note of 12 November 2025 says the Mission is expected to attract investments exceeding ₹8 lakh crore, alongside about 125 GW of renewable capacity for green hydrogen and a production aim of 5 million tonnes a year by 2030. On 17 June 2026, Minister Pralhad Joshi repeated the investment expectation, the 5 million tonne aim, the 125 GW figure, an expectation of over 6 lakh jobs, and a reduction of about 50 million tonnes a year in carbon emissions. “Expected to catalyse” and “aims to establish” are outcome language. They are not a register of sanctioned private capex.

## Figures that name a place or a firm

The 5 August 2026 Lok Sabha annex on hubs is where committed and contracted amounts become local. It says the NGEL proposal at Pudimadaka has investment committed of ₹85,000 crore, excluding renewable projects, with green ammonia, green methanol, sustainable aviation fuel, and jetty works on the list. It prices a Paradip handling jetty at ₹797.17 crore, approved for public-private partnership. It prices a Kandla 5 MW plant, scalable to 10 MW, at ₹62.80 crore, with contractual completion on 31 December 2026, and a completed 1 MW demonstration plant at ₹14.1 crore. Deendayal Port has allotted 3,400 acres on a 30-year lease. A lease and a contract are assets a diligence team can ask to see. A national “over ₹8 lakh crore” line is not that asset.

Incentive awards are a fourth record, closer to the programme envelope than to a plant’s revenue. On 17 June 2026 the minister said SIGHT incentives had been awarded for 8,62,000 tonnes a year of green hydrogen production, and that 15 companies had financial incentives to establish 3,000 MW a year of electrolyser manufacturing. An award makes a firm eligible for support if it performs against the scheme. It does not book the factory’s sales.

Private capital is explicit where the scheme says the developer pays. The 11 August 2026 reply on refinery projects says they are build-own-operate, with project-specific investment by the developers. Hub recognition can be thinner still. The 10 October 2025 PIB release on the three port hubs says Component B2 allows recognition without direct financial assistance, so that projects seek other central or state benefits. A project inside a recognised port still has to raise its own capital.

JSW’s 3,600 tonne-a-year plant, which the minister said was commissioned in November 2025, is one of the few operating production figures in that June speech. Commissioned capacity is the column that should be smallest until more plants report the same way.

Great Indian Company separates these records so a term sheet does not inherit the largest number in a press note.
