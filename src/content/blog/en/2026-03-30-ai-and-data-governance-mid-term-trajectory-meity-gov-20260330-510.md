"Mid-term" only helps if the reader knows which clock. India is running at least two, and they do not expire together.

## Clock one: the Mission

The Lok Sabha reply of 5 August 2026 dates the IndiaAI approval to 7 March 2024 and the outlay to Rs. 10,371.92 crore over five years. Five years from March 2024 runs into 2029. The reply does not publish year-wise releases or a remaining balance, so this brief will not invent a burn rate.

What the notes do show, inside that five-year window, is a build that has already moved:

- Compute portal opened 6 March 2025, with an initial access figure of 10,000 GPUs and 8,693 more then to be added.
- Empanelled stock reported as 38,231 GPUs on 25 March 2026, and as 45,000+ GPUs as of June 2026.
- AIKosh reported at over 14,000 datasets and 331 models as of July 2026.
- 20 foundation-model proposals selected from 506 applications, in the 13 August 2026 backgrounder.
- 13 Responsible AI projects under the Safe and Trusted AI pillar, as of July 2026.

The pillar names themselves shifted in official wording. The 6 March 2025 note lists Compute, a Datasets Platform, Application Development, FutureSkills, an Innovation Centre, Startup Financing, and Safe and Trusted AI. The 24 July 2026 Rajya Sabha reply lists Compute, Foundation Models, AIKosh, the Application Development Initiative, FutureSkills, Startup Financing, and Safe and Trusted AI. Use the later list for "what the ministry calls them now," and do not treat the rename as a new Cabinet outlay.

A further 20,000 GPUs were "under process" on 13 March 2026. The June snapshot of 45,000+ is the later stock. The trajectory is "the pool grew." It is not a promise, in these notes, of a dated GPU total for 2029.

## Clock two: the Act

From the gazette of 13 November 2025:

- Immediate: definitions, Board machinery, rule-making, TDSAT as the appellate forum, and the substituted RTI clause.
- 13 November 2026: Consent Manager registration, and the Board's function of inquiring into a breach of those registration conditions.
- 13 May 2027: sections 3 to 5, most of section 6, sections 7 to 17, the rest of the Board's inquiry powers, the penalty chapters, and section 44(2).

Section 44(2) is the one that omits section 43A of the IT Act. The mid-term legal fact is that the old compensation section and the new fiduciary duties are scheduled to change on the same day, 13 May 2027. Anyone planning a 2026 compliance programme as if both had already happened is a year early on the duties and early on the repeal.

Section 43 of the DPDP Act limits the government's power to issue difficulty-removal orders to three years from the commencement of the Act. Because commencement is staggered, "the commencement" for that section is itself a reading of the gazette, not a slogan. This brief does not pick a difficulty-order deadline the notification does not compute in words. It does say the power in section 43 is among the provisions brought in on 13 November 2025.

## Clock three: a guideline, not a date in the gazette

The India AI Governance Guidelines of 5 November 2025 include an action plan mapped to short, medium, and long term. PIB describes that plan. It does not, in the press note, turn the plan into notified obligations. The guidelines also propose institutional pieces, including an AI safety institute. The Rajya Sabha reply of 24 July 2026 says the IndiaAI Safety Institute was announced in January 2025 to do science-based work on safety and to oversee projects under the Safe and Trusted AI pillar. An announced institute and a statutory Board are different bodies. The Data Protection Board is the one in the Act.

The [ninety-day checklist](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-next-90-days-checklist-meity-gov-20260330-470) is clock two's next tick. The [capex note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-capex-cycle-watch-meity-gov-20260330-350) is clock one's GPU path.
