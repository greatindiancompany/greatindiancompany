# What Parliament has said about India's industrial position

A global benchmark is only as good as the comparison the government actually published. On 28 July 2026, Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423 asked for India's industrial position in the global market. The reply from the Minister of State, Jitin Prasada, is the document to use. It is [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf). This note does not add a country ranking the reply does not contain.

## Comparisons the reply does make

The reply says total FDI inflows over the eleven years 2014–25 were USD 748.78 billion, against USD 308.38 billion in 2003–14, and describes the later period as 143 percent higher. It says gross FDI inflows in FY 2025–26 were USD 94.53 billion, up 17 percent year on year. Manufacturing FDI is stated separately: USD 19.04 billion in FY 2024–25, up 18 percent from USD 16.12 billion in FY 2023–24.

On electronics, the reply says production rose sixfold and exports eightfold over eleven years. It does not name the start year beyond "the past 11 years," and it does not give the underlying dollar or rupee levels in that paragraph. Quote the multiple as the reply's multiple. Do not back-solve a base year.

On pharmaceuticals, the reply says the industry ranks third in the world by volume and fourteenth by value of production, supplies over 50 percent of global vaccine demand, and supplies nearly 40 percent of generics to the United States. Those are the global comparisons in the answer. They are not PLI results by themselves. The PLI paragraph earlier in the same reply is a separate set of scheme figures.

On textiles and apparel, the reply cites the Economic Survey 2024–25: the industry contributes around 2.3 percent of GDP, 13 percent of industrial production, and 12 percent of total exports. That is an official citation of the Survey, not a new survey in this brief.

## What these documents do not compare

The reply does not publish manufacturing as a share of GDP, and it does not place that share next to China, Vietnam, or Mexico. DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 does give a domestic production index, which is a different benchmark. With base year 2011–12, it says industrial production growth moved from -1.4 percent in FY 2019–20 to 9.5 percent in FY 2023–24, and manufacturing growth from -3 percent to 9.9 percent. Growth rates over those two years are not a global rank. The report is the [DPIIT Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/3d9c9c2daeefb97bb9ce964370938b71.pdf).

The August 2024 corridor note, [PIB PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136), says industrial nodes are meant to support a target of USD 2 trillion in exports by 2030, and it estimates employment from the corridor programme. A target and an estimate are not a current global standing.

Ease-of-doing-business ranks are also absent from the July 2026 reply. The annual report discusses the India Industrial Land Bank and the Industrial Park Rating System as domestic tools for investors choosing a park. IPRS 2.0 was released on 5 October 2021. The report says an IPRS 3.0 exercise has been initiated. It does not, in the passage used here, publish a new league table.

## How to use this in a comparison slide

If the slide says "India versus the world," the honest official lines are the ones above: FDI against India's own earlier decade, pharma volume and value ranks, vaccine and US generic shares, textile shares of India's own GDP and exports, and an eleven-year electronics multiple. Anything else on that slide needs a different official document, named in full.
