# A field guide to the official numbers on work in India

Use this page as a lookup. Every figure below is tied to the release that printed it and to the question it can answer. If a sentence needs a different question, it needs a different figure. Mixing them is how confident and wrong labour commentary gets written.

## Household survey, year-long status

Source: Periodic Labour Force Survey annual press note for July 2023–June 2024, National Sample Survey Office. Persons aged 15 and above. Usual status means principal and subsidiary status over the year.

| Question | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| What share of people were in the labour force? | 60.1 percent. Men 78.8. Women 41.7. |
| How did that change in one year? | From 57.9 percent in July 2022–June 2023. Women from 37.0 to 41.7. Men from 78.5 to 78.8. |
| What share were working? | Worker population ratio 58.2 percent. Men 76.3. Women 40.3. Up from 56.0 percent a year earlier. |
| What share of the labour force was unemployed? | 3.2 percent for men and for women. Men were 3.3 percent a year earlier. Women were 2.9 percent. |
| What changed since 2017–18? | Rural participation 50.7 to 63.7 percent. Urban participation 47.6 to 52.0. Women's participation 23.3 to 41.7. Men's 75.8 to 78.8. Rural unemployment 5.3 to 2.5 percent. Urban unemployment 7.7 to 5.1. |

The Directorate General of Employment's note on the employment situation restates the long window in one place: participation 49.8 percent in 2017–18 to 60.1 percent in 2023–24, worker ratio 46.8 to 58.2 percent, unemployment 6.0 to 3.2 percent, and women's worker ratio 22 to 40.3 percent.

These figures answer "over the year, in the household survey, what was this person's main attachment to work." They do not answer "did this person have work last week," and they do not count gig workers as a class.

## Household survey, reference week

Source: MoSPI press note on key employment-unemployment indicators for January–December 2024. Current weekly status.

| Question | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| All-India participation in the reference week, calendar 2024? | 56.2 percent, the same as calendar 2023. Men 78.1. Women 34.7. |
| All-India weekly unemployment, calendar 2024? | 4.9 percent. |
| Urban weekly unemployment, calendar 2024? | 6.7 percent. Urban women 8.2 percent. |
| Urban weekly participation, calendar 2024? | 51.0 percent, up from 50.3. Urban women 25.8, up from 25.5. |

The July–September 2024 quarterly bulletin, urban only, put weekly participation at 50.4 percent (men 75.0, women 25.5), against 49.3 percent in July–September 2023. The Directorate General of Employment records April–June 2024 urban weekly unemployment at 6.6 percent, and the urban worker ratio at 46.8 percent, up from 45.5 percent a year earlier.

Use this block when the question is about current joblessness, especially in cities. Do not average it with the 3.2 percent usual-status rate.

## Covered formal employment

Source: EPFO provisional payroll for June 2025, released through the Press Information Bureau.

Net addition was 21.89 lakh members, described as the highest since payroll data began in April 2018. That was 9.14 percent above May 2025 and 13.46 percent above June 2024. New subscribers were about 10.62 lakh. Net means new members minus exits plus rejoins, inside establishments covered by the provident fund. The file is provisional and is revised. It does not measure the unorganised workforce, and the months of a year are not a year-end stock.

## Unorganised registration

Source: Ministry of Labour and Employment, via PIB.

e-Shram launched on 26 August 2021. Registrations were 31,78,31,749 as on 14 July 2026 and 31.89 crore as on 20 August 2026. On the August date, women were 54.28 percent and men 45.72 percent. Ages: 0.08 percent were 16–18, 55.21 percent were 18–40, 24.18 percent were 40–50, and 20.53 percent were over 50.

The 14 July annexure also reported registrants enrolled in linked schemes, including 24,46,15,089 under One Nation One Ration Card and 15,10,06,017 under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY. Those counts overlap. The number answers "how many self-declared unorganised workers have a Universal Account Number," not "how many jobs exist."

## Gig workforce, as an estimate

Source: NITI Aayog, *India's Booming Gig and Platform Economy*, June 2022, and the PIB launch note of 27 June 2022.

| Question | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Estimated gig workers, 2019–20? | 68 lakh. |
| Estimated gig workers, 2020–21? | 77 lakh. 2.6 percent of the non-agricultural workforce. 1.5 percent of the total workforce. |
| Extension to 2029–30? | 2.35 crore. 6.7 percent of the non-agricultural workforce. 4.1 percent of total livelihood. |
| Skill split in the estimate? | About 47 percent medium-skilled, 22 percent high-skilled, 31 percent low-skilled. |

This block answers "what did the 2022 model say." It does not answer "how many platform workers were there in 2026."

## One coverage sentence that is not a survey result

On 21 November 2025 the Ministry of Labour and Employment said social-security coverage had risen from about 19 percent of the workforce in 2015 to more than 64 percent in 2025. Quote it as that sentence. Do not place it in the PLFS table.

## Three mixing errors

Do not call 3.2 percent and 4.9 percent a disagreement. One is usual status for 2023–24. The other is weekly status for calendar 2024.

Do not add EPFO's 21.89 lakh to e-Shram's 31.89 crore. One is a monthly flow of covered members. The other is a stock of self-declarations. Their account numbers are both called universal account numbers. They are not the same account.

Do not cite 2.35 crore gig workers as a present headcount. The present-tense figure in that report is the 2020–21 estimate of 77 lakh, and it was never a census.
