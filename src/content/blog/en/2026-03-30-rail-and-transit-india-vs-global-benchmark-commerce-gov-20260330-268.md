# How India Compares on Rail Freight, Metro Scale, and Vande Bharat Fares

A useful comparison is one a ministry has already made. This brief stays inside three of those sentences. It does not invent network lengths for other countries.

## Freight: second-largest, on India’s own tonne series

On 7 August 2026 the Ministry of Railways said Indian Railways had become the second-largest freight-carrying railway in the world, after loading rose to 1,670 million tonnes in 2025-26. The same reply prints the series behind that claim, in million tonnes: 1,098 in 2014-15, 1,104 in 2015-16, 1,109 in 2016-17, 1,162 in 2017-18, 1,223 in 2018-19, 1,210 in 2019-20, 1,233 in 2020-21, 1,418 in 2021-22, 1,512 in 2022-23, 1,591 in 2023-24, 1,617 in 2024-25, and 1,670 in 2025-26. The rank is the ministry’s. The series is what a reader can check without a second country’s yearbook.

The 1 April 2026 release is consistent on the latest year: 1,670 million tonnes, up 3.25 percent. It also shows freight earnings up only 1.44 percent. Rank and yield are different questions. The [market-signal brief](/blog/rail-and-transit-market-signal-brief-commerce-gov-20260330-188) stays with yield.

## Metro: third-largest network, with two dated snapshots

PIB notes describe India as having the world’s third-largest operational metro network. The note of 9 August 2025 put the network at 1,013 km across 23 cities by May 2025, up from 248 km across 5 cities in 2014, and put average daily ridership above 1.12 crore, from 28 lakh in 2013-14. The later note, on 15 March 2026, put the operational network at about 1,095 km across 26 cities, including 55 km of the Delhi–Meerut regional rapid transit system, and repeated the third-largest description. Use the March 2026 length. Use the August 2025 note for the ridership comparison, because the March note does not restate daily ridership.

Neither note prints the length of the two larger networks. “Third-largest” is the claim PIB makes. A kilometre gap versus another country is not in these pages, so it is not in this brief.

## Fares: a ministry comparison, plus the subsidy behind it

On 11 March 2026 the railways minister told the Lok Sabha that the Vande Bharat Sleeper fare ranges from ₹2.40 to ₹3.80 per passenger-kilometre, and that similar services in China, Japan, and France range from ₹7 to ₹20 per passenger-kilometre. The 3AC rate cited is ₹2.40 excluding GST, and the indicative fare for 1,000 km in 3AC is ₹2,400 excluding GST. That is the official fare comparison. It is not a comparison of cost recovery.

The same reply said Indian Railways gave a subsidy of ₹60,239 crore on passenger tickets in 2024-25, described as an average concession of 43 percent: if the cost of providing the service is ₹100, the ticket price is ₹57. Low fares and a large passenger subsidy are one policy, stated together. Quoting only the foreign fare range leaves out the domestic cross-subsidy the ministry disclosed in the same answer.

Vande Bharat chair-car scale is a fleet fact, not a foreign benchmark. As of the 23 July 2026 release, 162 chair-car services and 2 sleeper services were operating. The sleeper comparison above is a fare fact about a very small fleet.

Operators pricing a service against this should read the [founder note](/blog/rail-and-transit-for-founders-and-operators-commerce-gov-20260330-708) before treating the foreign range as a market price in India.

Great Indian Company publishes the comparison the ministries have signed, and leaves out league tables they have not.
