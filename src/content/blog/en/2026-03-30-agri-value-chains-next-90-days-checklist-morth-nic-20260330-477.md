# A ninety-day reading list for the kharif market

From 24 September 2026, ninety days runs to late December. Kharif sowing is largely on the record. Kharif buying is about to become the record. The useful work in that window is to know which document replaces which, and which older sentence is still the latest one.

## Start from the area print you already have

The Crop Weather Watch Group report published on 8 September, as on 4 September, is the sowing baseline. All-India kharif area was 1,086.31 lakh hectares. Rice was 421.82 lakh hectares, down 16.37 from the same week last year and above its normal of 412.00. Pulses were 117.13, below a normal of 123.63. Cotton was 109.17, against a normal of 125.51. Urad was the pulse that moved most, up 2.68 lakh hectares to 25.15, still under its normal of 29.60.

Carry those rows forward. A later weekly area note matters only where sowing is still open. For rice, the next number that changes a decision is arrivals and procurement, then an advance estimate of production for 2026-27. The Third Advance Estimate released on 27 May 2026, foodgrain at 376.563 million tonnes, is the 2025-26 crop. Leave it in last year's column.

## Read procurement against a known price, not against a hope

The price list is already fixed. On 13 May 2026 the Cabinet set common paddy at Rs 2,441 a quintal for marketing season 2026-27, grade A at Rs 2,461, tur at Rs 8,450, moong at Rs 8,780, urad at Rs 8,200, and maize at Rs 2,410. The largest absolute rises were sunflower seed, cotton, nigerseed, and sesamum.

Over the next ninety days, the check is whether public procurement reports arrivals against those prices, and for which crops. The May release itself shows that past buying was overwhelmingly paddy: 8,418 lakh metric tonnes of paddy during 2014-15 to 2025-26, inside 8,746 lakh metric tonnes for 14 kharif crops. If the new season's first procurement notes are silent on nigerseed and loud on paddy, that matches the history. It is still a fact to record, not a fact to assume before the note appears.

## Check the transfer, and do not guess the date

PM-KISAN pays three instalments of Rs 2,000 a year. The Cabinet note of 31 July 2026 is the latest published count: more than Rs 4.47 lakh crore through 23 instalments, and a 23rd instalment of over Rs 18,984 crore to more than 9.49 crore farmers. The scheme now runs to 2030-31, with an outlay of Rs 3.15 lakh crore. The next instalment will be announced. The design says there are three a year. It does not give the December date, so this list does not invent one.

Until a later reply says otherwise, new registrations still follow the 10 March 2026 rule: Farmer ID is mandatory for new PM-KISAN entries in the 19 states where the farmer registry has started, and is not required where that work has not begun.

## Treat the loan window as a question, not as a rumour

The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund note of 9 August 2025 said loan disbursement would finish by the end of 2025-26, while the scheme itself runs to 2032-33 and subvention on existing loans can run for seven years. The latest stock in hand is the 15 July 2026 reply: Rs 1,61,537.22 crore of capital investment leveraged, including Rs 50,332.66 crore for farm-gate storage and logistics. A stock dated July does not prove that new sanctions continued past March 2026, and it does not prove they stopped. In this ninety-day window, an operator with a file in a bank should read the AIF portal and any reply newer than 15 July. The guarantee names on the file remain CGTMSE and NABSanrakshan, for loans up to Rs 2 crore.

## Two scheme clocks that have already struck

APEDA's notice of 1 September 2026 for a project-management unit under Bharat Krishi Niryat Yojana set the last date for bids at 15 September 2026, with a note that bids to an earlier notice of 20 July 2026 would still be considered. That date has passed. The ninety-day question is whether the unit is awarded, and how exporters, FPOs, and laboratories use the three assistance heads the notice describes: export infrastructure, quality development, and market development. The notice covers 2026-27 to 2030-31. It does not print the scheme's outlay.

On the food-processing side, 98 of 409 approved cold-chain projects were still ongoing as of 30 June 2026, and Rs 2,540.89 crore of a sanctioned Rs 3,089.08 crore had been released. The rate card to check them against is the 6 August 2026 reply: 35 per cent or 50 per cent, caps of Rs 10 crore or Rs 15 crore depending on the component. A completion or a release after 30 June is the update. The 30 June stock is the baseline.
