# How India's cyber institutions divide the work

Policy teams inherit a set of institutions that were built under different Acts. The work is to keep their triggers separate, and to write the hand-off when one incident meets more than one trigger. Merging them on a slide is how reports go to the wrong place.

## MeitY's two cyber institutions are not the same office

CERT-In, under section 70B of the Information Technology Act, is the national incident-response agency. It has operated since January 2004. It issues directions, alerts, advisories and vulnerability notes, runs a 24x7 help desk, empanels auditors, operates the National Cyber Coordination Centre, and shares indicators through a threat-exchange platform. The directions of 28 April 2022 are its standing order to body corporates and the other entities named there.

The Data Protection Board is a creature of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, Chapter V, as the demand for grants describes it. The rules of 13 November 2025 commence the Board's own provisions earlier than they commence breach-notice duties of data fiduciaries. PIB says appeals from the Board lie with TDSAT. The appeal rule itself is in the later commencement group. A policy note that sends a personal-data complaint to CERT-In, or a vulnerable-service incident to the Board, has mixed the two MeitY bodies.

## Critical infrastructure sits under a different parent

NCIIPC is a unit of the National Technical Research Organisation, designated under section 70A by gazette notification of 16 January 2014 as the national nodal agency for critical information infrastructure. It coordinates with CERT-In. It is not a department of MeitY. Protected systems are notified under section 70 by the appropriate government. NCIIPC's identification guidance places basic protection responsibility on the agency that runs the system. Policy teams in a ministry that operates citizen platforms need a list of what, if anything, has actually been notified, and a path to NCIIPC that is not "we copied CERT-In."

## Sector regulators keep their own rulebooks

The Reserve Bank, SEBI and IRDAI have each written cyber directions for the firms they already license. The Reserve Bank directions of 2023 explicitly say other laws are not barred. A bank's incident can be owed to the Reserve Bank and to CERT-In. SEBI's framework superseded SEBI's earlier cyber circulars; it did not supersede CERT-In. IRDAI's 2023 guidelines superseded IRDAI's own earlier cyber circulars. Policy for a financial conglomerate has to say which licence pulls which framework, using SEBI's rule that the highest applicable category governs an entity registered in more than one SEBI category.

## Telecom security is DoT's statute

The Telecommunications Act, 2023, section 22, is the power behind the Telecom Cyber Security Rules, 2024. The Department of Telecommunications is the line ministry. The rules create a Chief Telecommunication Security Officer, a portal, certified-agency audit, and a government power to seek traffic data other than message content, and to suspend or disconnect a telecommunication identifier after the notice process the rules set out. The October 2025 amendment pulls in entities that use telecom identifiers for customer identification even when they are not licensees. A policy team in a non-telecom firm should test that definition before it files the rules under "telecom companies only."

## Crime and victim support are Home Ministry and the states

I4C is an attached office of the Ministry of Home Affairs. The reporting portal, the 1930 helpline, the financial-fraud reporting system, the fraud-mitigation centre, the suspect registry, joint cyber coordination teams, Samanvaya and Pratibimb are its tools. The 2 January 2026 standard operating procedure is about handling complaints on the portal and the fraud-reporting system, with states as integral stakeholders.

The constitutional line in the 24 March 2026 reply is the one policy teams most often skip: police and public order are state subjects. Central advisories and the ₹132.93 crore CCPWC assistance do not move investigation out of the state police. A national cyber strategy that measures only CERT-In totals will miss whether states are converting portal complaints into cases. NCRB's series is the published window onto that question, and it is not the CERT-In series.

## A hand-off worth writing down

For one incident, the policy sheet can be five lines. Technical cyber incident: CERT-In, six hours, with logs. Sector licence: the regulator's addressee and clock. Personal data, once Rule 7 has commenced: the person without delay, the Board without delay, the detailed report in seventy-two hours. Telecom network: Central Government in six hours and the fuller report in twenty-four. Crime against a victim: the portal or 1930, then the state police. Protected system: the notified-system path and NCIIPC.

No current instrument collapses those five lines into one form. Writing the sheet is the policy task.
