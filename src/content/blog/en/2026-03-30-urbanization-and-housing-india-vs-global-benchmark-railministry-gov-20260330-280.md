# India’s Urban Share Is a Census Definition, Not a Global Rate

## The only national rate MoHUA puts on its urban page

MoHUA’s urban-growth page says that, of a total population of 1,210.2 million as on 1 March 2011, about 377.1 million people were in urban areas. The net addition in urban areas over the previous decade was 91.0 million. The prose on that page says the urban share stands at 31.6 percent, up 3.35 percentage points during 2001-2011. The table on the same page prints the urban share as 31.16 percent in 2011 and 27.81 percent in 2001, and the urban population as 377 million against 286 million. Decadal urban growth is printed as 31.8 percent for 2001-2011, against 12.2 percent in rural areas and 17.6 percent for the country.

Use the table’s 31.16 percent when a document needs one Census figure, and note that the ministry’s own sentence rounds to 31.6. Do not average them into a third number.

A Ministry of Home Affairs Lok Sabha reply of 7 May 2013, reproducing Census 2011, gives the count behind the round number: 377,106,125 urban residents in a population of 1,210,569,573. That reply is the place for state shares. It is still a 2011 count.

## What “urban” means in that count

The same MoHUA page says provisional Census 2011 results showed 2,774 additional towns: 242 statutory towns and 2,532 census towns. A statutory town is a place with an urban local body. A census town is a place the Census classifies as urban on its criteria even without that body. Most of the new towns in that decade were census towns. A comparison that treats every Indian urban resident as a resident of a municipal corporation is reading past the table.

Million-plus cities and urban agglomerations rose from 35 in 2001 to 53 in 2011. Kerala moved from one million-plus urban agglomeration, Kochi, to seven. The ministry page lists the new agglomerations. The list is part of the Indian definition of concentrated urban population. It is not a global city ranking.

## Why a foreign percentage does not belong on this page

International series often classify settlements with a single population cutoff, or they use a built-up-area measure. India’s official share uses statutory towns plus census towns, as the Registrar General drew the frame for 2011. Until both sides use the same rule, a gap between “India” and “the world” is a gap in definitions. This brief does not import a United Nations or World Bank urbanisation rate to fill the silence. Those institutions are outside the official record this series uses, and a borrowed rate would look like a Census update.

There is no published Census urban share for 2021. The exercise was not completed on the original calendar. The replacement, Census 2027, is described by PIB as India’s first digital census, with self-enumeration. Phase I, between April and September 2026, is a housing census: condition, amenities, and assets. It rebuilds the frame. It does not, by itself, publish the new urban percentage. That percentage waits on Population Enumeration, scheduled for February 2027, with a reference date of 1 March 2027 except in the notified snow-bound areas.

## How to benchmark in the meantime

Benchmark a city against another Indian city on a common official table: the Census 2011 urban share of its state, or the Smart Cities project completion in the August 2026 Lok Sabha annex, or the PMAY vertical mix in the December 2024 note. Benchmark a reform, such as non-revenue water below 20 percent under the AMRUT 2.0 guidelines, against the guideline text. Leave the cross-country league table until the Registrar General publishes the 2027 distribution and the definition is stated beside it.
