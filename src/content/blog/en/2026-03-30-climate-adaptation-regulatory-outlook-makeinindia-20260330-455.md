# The Regulatory Outlook for Climate Adaptation in India

Climate adaptation in India is not one statute. Four instruments do four jobs. A regulatory note that merges them cannot be complied with.

## Plans set direction

The National Action Plan on Climate Change is the national strategy, released on 30 June 2008. On 21 July 2025, the Press Information Bureau reported that the plan comprises nine missions, including human health, revised to align with India's Nationally Determined Contribution, and at various stages of implementation. The same reply says 34 States and Union Territories have State Action Plans on Climate Change. Those plans are posted by the environment ministry. A state plan does not, by itself, replace a coastal clearance or a disaster plan.

## The coast is a notification

The Coastal Regulation Zone Notification, 2019, is the instrument a PIB release of 3 February 2022 cites for conserving coastal stretches and marine areas and for sustainable development. That release says the ministry has demarcated the hazard line for the entire mainland coast under the Integrated Coastal Zone Management project. The notification uses the line, mapped by the Survey of India and shared through the National Centre for Sustainable Coastal Management, as a tool for coastal disaster-management planning and for land use between the hazard line and the high-tide line, taking sea-level rise and shoreline change into account. A mainland coastal project that ignores the line is arguing with the notification.

## Disaster guidelines are the preparedness rulebook

The National Disaster Management Authority's [guideline index](https://ndma.gov.in/Governance/Guidelines) is a set of separate documents: floods (January 2008), cyclones (April 2008), drought (September 2010), urban flooding (September 2010), heat-wave action plans (October 2019), and glacial-lake outburst floods (October 2020). They guide state and local plans. They are not an environmental clearance.

## Warnings make a day legally meaningful

IMD's heat criteria, including the version printed with the 6 April 2021 bulletin, turn "heat alert" into a defined test rather than a slogan. The follow-through the disaster authority asks for is a state or city action plan under the October 2019 guideline.

## What would count as a change in the outlook

Watch PIB for a change in the mission count, the state-plan coverage, the adaptation fund's status, or the coastal notification. Watch the NDMA index for a revised hazard guide. Watch IMD for the warning in force. A renewable-capacity headline does not amend any of the four.
