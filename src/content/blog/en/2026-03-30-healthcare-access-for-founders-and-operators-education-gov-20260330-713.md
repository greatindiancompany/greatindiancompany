## Three buyers, not one health system

A founder selling “healthcare access” in India is usually selling to one of three buyers, and the product breaks when the pitch mixes them.

The first buyer is the State Health Agency or, in insurance mode, the insurer that agency has tendered. They pay empanelled hospitals for listed PM-JAY packages. In trust mode the agency pays and may hire an implementation support agency for claims. Cashless care means the eligible patient is not your collection channel for a covered admission. If your workflow needs the patient to pay and seek reimbursement, it fights the scheme.

The second buyer is the state health department and the district, through the National Health Mission structures that still run primary and secondary care. Ayushman Arogya Mandirs are free to the user for an expanded set of services, with essential drugs and diagnostics included in the design. A consumer subscription for the same antenatal visit or the same blood-pressure check is competing with a public price of zero. The opening the mandir page actually writes down is gap-filling with non-profits and private partners, inside public protocols.

The third buyer is the hospital, public or private. Public hospitals are reimbursed at par with private hospitals for scheme packages. A product that only fits a corporate hospital misses the public provider on the same rate card. States may reserve procedures for public facilities. Your private-hospital customer can lose a service line by a state order that is allowed in the design.

## What you have to know before a roadmap

Pick one district before you write a national rollout. Name a mandir, the primary health centre that is supposed to receive its referrals, the district hospital, and one empanelled private hospital. Note whether that state pays hospitals through a trust or an insurer. Note which packages are reserved. The [ninety-day list](/blog/healthcare-access-next-90-days-checklist-education-gov-20260330-473) is a short version of this homework. The [execution note](/blog/healthcare-access-execution-bottleneck-review-education-gov-20260330-593) explains why the clinic’s family register and the hospital’s empanelment are different events.

Read the benefit as a contract. ₹5 lakh a year per family. No family-size cap. Pre-existing disease from day one. Three days before admission and 15 days after. Portability across empanelled hospitals. People aged 70 and above covered irrespective of income since the Cabinet decision of 12 September 2024, on a shared family amount described in the Authority’s FAQ. If your case mix is elective outpatient care for paying urban professionals, this contract is not your demand engine.

## Identity, data, and payment timing

The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, launched in September 2021, provides a 14-digit Ayushman Bharat Health Account and registries of facilities and professionals. Integrating with those rails is a plausible product. Treating the account number as proof of PM-JAY eligibility is a product defect. Eligibility is a beneficiary-system check against SECC-linked records and any state addition.

Payment timing follows the mode. Trust-mode cash flows depend on the state agency and its support vendor. Insurance-mode cash flows depend on the insurer’s settlement practice under a public tender. The Authority’s about-page caps insurer margins and requires surplus to return to the agency. Those rules tell you the insurer is not a free commercial payer. Build working-capital assumptions from the state you chose, not from a national average.

## A credible sequence

Start with the official page that matches the buyer: the PM-JAY about-page for hospital cover, the mandir about-page for primary care, the standards portal for what a district hospital is supposed to be. Then sit in one facility and watch a screening become, or fail to become, a referral and a claim. The [opportunity note](/blog/healthcare-access-opportunity-landscape-education-gov-20260330-633) lists functions that already exist in that chain. Build for one of them.
