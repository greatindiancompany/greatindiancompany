An investment in Indian education or skilling is usually a claim about public budgets, regulated seats, or employer contracts. It is rarely a claim that can be checked against a single official "market size." The Budget and the surveys do not publish one. What they publish is enough to tell a weak memo from a serious one. The questions below are the ones those documents can actually discipline.

## Is the revenue a budget estimate or a revised estimate?

The skill ministry's net provision for 2025-26 was estimated at ₹6,100.10 crore and revised to ₹2,703.54 crore. Centrally sponsored schemes inside it went from ₹3,049.90 crore to ₹367 crore. The Skill India Programme line, covering PMKVY 4.0, apprenticeship promotion, and Jan Shikshan Sansthan, went from ₹2,700 crore to ₹2,000 crore. The 2026-27 budget estimate puts the ministry back at ₹9,885.80 crore net, with PM-SETU at ₹6,140.50 crore.

Ask which of those columns the company's revenue sits on. A contract signed against a budget speech, for ITI equipment or a training target, can be real and still unpaid if the revised estimate and the release condition do not follow. PM-SETU's release condition is a special purpose vehicle, and as of 1 April 2026 state proposals had not reached national approval. A revenue forecast that books a share of ₹6,140.50 crore in the year before that condition is met is booking an estimate. Ask for the purchase order or the sanctioned target, and for what happened to the same line in 2025-26.

School schemes rhyme. Samagra Shiksha was revised from ₹41,250 crore to ₹38,000 crore. PM SHRI was revised from ₹7,500 crore to ₹4,500 crore, then restored to a ₹7,500 crore budget estimate for 2026-27. A company selling into exemplar schools should show the schools named in a state list, not the national ceiling. Kendriya Vidyalaya and Navodaya allocations, ₹10,129.41 crore and ₹6,025 crore, are steadier institutional buyers. They are also public and tendered.

Gross and net differ. The skill ministry's gross 2026-27 figure is higher than ₹9,885.80 crore because of recoveries. If a pitch uses the gross number, ask why.

## What outcome is official, and what is the company's own?

PMKVY tracked placements for short-term training in its first three versions. PMKVY 4.0, the current one, does not do so in the same way. The Ministry says candidates are oriented toward varied paths, and that the digital hub is part of how they proceed. If a company still markets a "PMKVY placement rate," ask for the scheme document that defines it for version 4.0. If the rate is the company's own alumni follow-up, ask for the cohort size, the date, and the definition of placed. That can be a good dataset. It is not a government statistic.

Training volume is official and checkable. As on 7 December 2025, 27.08 lakh candidates had been trained under PMKVY 4.0. As on 30 June 2026, more than 5.44 lakh of the future-technology trainees were reported, at a top hourly common-norms cost of ₹49. A centre whose model needs a public rate above that ceiling needs a different payer. Compare the company's hub count with the public picture of more than 15,500 institutions and more than 7,000 skill hubs. Share of a scheme is a fact you can approximate only after you see their sanctioned targets, not from a national press total.

Apprenticeship revenue should be underwritten on completions if the economic buyer cares about finished workers. Engagements are large, 51.30 lakh cumulatively up to 31 December 2025. Final assessments in 2024-25 were 2.33 lakh. A business paid only to register apprentices is a different risk from one paid when assessment is done.

## Which regulator, and which headcount, is the demand?

Higher-education enrolment of 4.50 crore in 2023-24, with a gross enrolment ratio of 30 against a policy aim of 50% by 2035, is the demand backdrop for seats and services. It is not a licence. Private universities and colleges still need the University Grants Commission, the technical-education council, and state acts. The policy's single higher-education regulator is a proposal that requires existing Acts to be repealed. Do not underwrite a timeline that assumes the Higher Education Commission of India already accepts applications.

School demand for foundational products is timely because NIPUN Bharat points at Grade 3 in 2026-27. School demand for "the whole market" is not coherent: 99.3% of schools already report drinking water, while 54.9% report ramps with handrails and 64.7% report computers. The unsaturated official gaps are specific. A product that solves a gap the census says is already closed will not find the tender it expects.

Labour demand is not the same as enrolment. Youth unemployment was 10.2% in 2023-24. Graduate work rates have risen since 2017-18. Neither figure is a vacancy list. A staffing or training investment that cites them should say which one, for which age, on usual status, and should not convert them into a headcount of unfilled jobs.

## What to leave out of the memo

Leave out an education-to-GDP ratio unless you have a sourced year. The policy's 4.43% is from 2017-18. Leave out a current "India versus Germany" vocational percentage. The policy's 5%, 52%, 75%, and 96% figures are 12th Plan estimates. Leave out a sum of school enrolment, college enrolment, and PMKVY trainees. Those series overlap and measure different events. Leave out links to ministries that do not run these schemes. They are a sign the memo was padded.

The investable statement, when it is honest, is narrow. Name the payer, the sanction document, the recognition body, the rate, the cohort, and the last revised estimate that payer actually used. The official record is good enough to support that statement. It is not good enough to support a national market size with a growth rate attached.
