## Read the accounts as accounts

An investor looking at Indian education and skilling will find public-scheme numbers more easily than company numbers. The scheme numbers are real. They are not a revenue pool.

On 7 February 2025 the Cabinet set an overlay outlay of Rs 8,800 crore for the Skill India Programme for 2022-23 to 2025-26. That sum covers PMKVY 4.0, PM-NAPS, and Jan Shikshan Sansthan together. The note’s “more than 2.27 crore beneficiaries till date” is a cumulative count as of that Cabinet date, across the three flagship schemes. It is not annual volume, and it is not a customer base an investor can buy.

A narrower spend line appeared on 12 August 2026: Rs 414.86 crore utilized as on 30 June 2026 for future and new-age job roles under PMKVY 4.0, alongside more than 5.44 lakh candidates trained in artificial intelligence, drones, 5G, solar energy, additive manufacturing, semiconductors, and the Internet of Things. Utilization is money recorded against training. It is not sales of a listed firm. The Rs 49 per hour figure in that reply is the highest Common Norms training cost then prevalent for those courses. It is a cost norm for providers, not a price an investor can treat as a market rate for every skill.

## Public support that is easy to over-read

PM-NAPS pays 25% of an apprentice’s stipend, capped at Rs 1,500 per month, by direct benefit transfer, for ages 14 to 35. A business that hosts apprentices receives that partial support. It does not receive a full wage subsidy, and the Cabinet note does not state a return on the stipend.

Industry names in the 12 August reply are examples of recognised awarding bodies and of organisations consulted since May 2024. HCL Technologies, IBM, Bajaj Finserv, Microsoft, Larsen & Toubro, Swiggy, and CREDAI appear in that official list. Naming them is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. The reply says more than fifteen entities are awarding bodies and that more than 13 consultations involved over 120 stakeholders. It does not disclose contracts, order books, or margins.

## Higher education is a participation story, not a valuation

The education ministry’s 10 August 2026 reply is the participation account: gross enrolment ratio 30 in 2023-24, up from 23.7 in 2014-15, and 4.50 crore students, up from 3.42 crore. NEP 2020’s aim is 50% by 2035, including vocational education. The gap describes a public policy target. It does not describe the addressable market of a company.

This brief does not publish a placement rate, because the replies do not. It does not convert the Rs 8,800 crore outlay into an equity opportunity. Training businesses participate as project implementing agencies, Sector Skill Councils, or recognised awarding bodies, under inspection rules that can suspend a batch and recover payments when biometric attendance and inspection findings diverge.
