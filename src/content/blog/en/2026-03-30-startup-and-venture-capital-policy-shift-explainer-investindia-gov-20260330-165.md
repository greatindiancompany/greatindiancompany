On 4 February 2026, DPIIT notified G.S.R. 108(E). The Press Information Bureau explained it the next evening. The Startup India recognition page now applies that notification. The change is a new legal test for the word “startup” in central schemes. It is not, by itself, a tax holiday or a venture fund.

## The test the portal publishes

The [Startup India recognition page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html) says an entity can seek recognition if it is incorporated as a private limited company or registered as a partnership firm, limited liability partnership, or cooperative society. Turnover should be less than ₹200 crore in any previous financial year, or less than ₹300 crore for a deep-tech startup. The entity is considered a startup for up to 10 years from incorporation, or 20 years for deep tech. It should be working towards innovation or improvement of products, services, or processes and should have the potential to generate employment or create wealth. An entity formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business is outside the definition.

PIB’s note of 5 February 2026 records what moved relative to the previous ceiling: the turnover limit rose from ₹100 crore to ₹200 crore. Deep-tech startups, a new sub-category for cutting-edge and breakthrough technologies, received the longer 20-year age limit and the ₹300 crore turnover limit. PIB describes that category as a response to long gestation, high research intensity, and capital intensity. Cooperative societies registered under state or Union Territory acts, and multi-state cooperative societies under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002, became eligible if the other conditions are met.

Application is digital. The recognition page routes companies, LLPs, registered partnerships, and cooperative societies through the National Single Window System, using the form “Registration as a Startup”. A Lok Sabha reply states that the process uses self-certification and simplified documentation.

## Recognition and the tax holiday are different gates

After recognition, the same Startup India page points a firm toward two further applications: a deduction under section 80-IAC of the Income-tax Act, and an exemption application under section 56, which the page labels angel tax.

The page’s own 80-IAC rules are narrower than recognition. The holiday is three consecutive financial years out of the first ten since incorporation. Only a private limited company or an LLP can apply. The startup should have been incorporated after 1 April 2016. A partnership or a cooperative can be recognised and still fail this tax test. A deep-tech firm can be recognised for 20 years on the portal’s eligibility list; the same page does not extend the 80-IAC window from ten years to twenty. Read the page as the application route. Confirm the charging section in the current Income-tax Act before anyone prices a round around a tax assumption.

## Why older Invest India pages will mislead

Invest India’s startup brochure filed under a June 2019 path still prints an older eligibility line: turnover not exceeding ₹100 crore, and no cooperative limb. That PDF is a dated pre-2026 document. It is the wrong test for a 2026 application. The current test is G.S.R. 108(E), as restated on the Startup India page and in the 5 February 2026 PIB note.

Fund of Funds 2.0 uses this definition on purpose. DPIIT’s operational guidelines, linked from the 25 April 2026 PIB release, tell selected alternative investment funds to finance startups “as recognized by DPIIT as per the latest eligibility criteria notified in the Official Gazette”. The deep-tech segment of those guidelines points private placement memoranda at G.S.R. 108(E). A fund document that still cites the 2019 ₹100 crore notification is out of line with the scheme that is now written down.

## What the wider test changes in the count

A wider age, turnover, and entity test admits firms the old ceiling kept out. The 30 June 2026 stock of 2,40,092 recognised entities, including 16,334 added in April–June, sits after this notification. Growth in the registry from February onward mixes new companies with companies that became eligible because the ceiling moved. DPIIT has not, in the releases used here, split those two groups. Anyone quoting “startup growth” after February 2026 should say the denominator changed.
