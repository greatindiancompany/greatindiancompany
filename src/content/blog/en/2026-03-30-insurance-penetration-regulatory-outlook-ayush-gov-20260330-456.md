# IRDAI's 2024 Rulebook Is Built to Raise Insurance Penetration

The current supervisory frame is the set IRDAI approved on 19 March 2024 and described in its [regulatory revamp press note](https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/366347/Press+Note+on+Regulatory+Revamp.pdf/9ad7286a-b0a7-ea19-d51c-4c942bad43e1?t=1711121865505&version=3.0). The meeting was the 125th, held at the Hyderabad head office. The note says the Authority approved eight principle-based consolidated regulations after consultation. The exercise replaced 34 regulations with six consolidated regulations and introduced two new ones. It followed the expenses-of-management regulation notified in January 2024.

The stated direction is Insurance for All by 2047. The product regulation is the one that names penetration directly.

## What each block is for

Rural, social-sector, and motor third-party obligations consolidate two earlier regulations. Compliance for rural business is measured at the Gram Panchayat. The social-sector definition is extended to cardholders and beneficiaries of schemes. Motor third-party measurement includes renewal of cover for goods vehicles, passenger vehicles, and tractors.

Bima Sugam, under the Insurance Electronic Marketplace regulations, is a digital public infrastructure. IRDAI calls it a one-stop solution for customers, insurers, intermediaries, and agents, meant to universalise insurance and to serve the 2047 vision.

Registration, capital structure, transfer of shares, and amalgamation fold seven regulations into one, covering registration, share transfers, capital, amalgamation, and listing.

Corporate governance for insurers is notified as regulations for the first time, setting board and management duties with policyholders named as the stakeholder the framework has to meet.

Insurance products merge six regulations. IRDAI says the aim is a faster response to market demand, easier business, and higher penetration, with stronger principles on guaranteed surrender value and special surrender value, and room for products aimed at different segments.

Foreign reinsurer branches and Lloyd's India are covered in one regulation, aimed at orderly reinsurance capacity.

Actuarial, finance, and investment functions fold nine regulations into one, including regulatory returns that show the insurer's state of affairs.

Policyholder protection folds eight regulations into one: fair solicitation, servicing, grievance redressal, outsourcing, and the opening or closing of places of business in India and abroad.

## What Parliament added on top

The [PIB note of 23 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) records the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025 as the later legislative layer: FDI at 100 percent, one-time intermediary registration, a 5 percent share-transfer approval threshold, a ₹1,000 crore net owned fund for foreign reinsurers, a policyholder education fund, disgorgement, and a ₹10 crore penalty ceiling. PIB also records health-contract changes already in IRDAI's framework: a health moratorium cut from 8 years to 60 months in 2024, a 30-day free-look period for policies of one year or more, guaranteed renewal except in fraud or misrepresentation, portability that keeps waiting-period credit, and a requirement that insurers offer products across ages, regions, occupations, medical conditions, and systems of medicine.

The 2024-25 penetration outcome of this rulebook is already published. The [annual report](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620) shows the ratio still at 3.7 percent. A shorter regulation book is not, on that evidence, the same thing as a higher ratio in the first full year after the revamp.
