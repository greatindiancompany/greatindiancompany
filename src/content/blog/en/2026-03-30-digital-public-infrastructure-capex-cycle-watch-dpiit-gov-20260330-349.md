# The spending cycle under India's digital rails

Capital expenditure, in a road or a power plant, is a visible stock of concrete and equipment. Digital public infrastructure does not present itself that way. The official accounts split into three different cycles: a ministry umbrella that is thousands of crores a year, two famous citizen platforms whose disclosed budgets are small, and a fibre project whose constraint is still physical.

None of these accounts is the private capital that builds apps on top of UPI or the Account Aggregator framework. NPCI is not a line in the MeitY tables below.

## The umbrella MeitY actually publishes

On 29 July 2026 the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology told the Lok Sabha that the Digital India programme is an umbrella. Projects sit in many ministries, departments, and states, each with its own budget. MeitY itself implements eight central-sector sub-schemes. No state-wise allocation is made for those sub-schemes. The amounts MeitY reported, in crore rupees, are:

| Financial year | Funds allocated | Expenditure |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2021-22 | 6,388.00 | 4,504.36 |
| 2022-23 | 5,400.50 | 3,863.13 |
| 2023-24 | 4,428.01 | 4,174.14 |
| 2024-25 | 4,173.00 | 3,784.93 |
| 2025-26 | 5,144.45 | 4,638.28 |

The allocation fell from ₹6,388 crore in 2021-22 to ₹4,173 crore in 2024-25, then rose to ₹5,144.45 crore in 2025-26. Expenditure did not follow the allocation in a fixed ratio. In 2023-24 expenditure was ₹4,174.14 crore against an allocation of ₹4,428.01 crore. In 2021-22 a larger allocation coincided with expenditure of ₹4,504.36 crore, leaving a wider unspent gap. The answer does not break the eight sub-schemes out inside this table, so a reader cannot see how much of a given year was software, how much was connectivity support, and how much was administration.

This is the cycle to watch if the question is "what did MeitY's Digital India envelope do?" It is the wrong cycle if the question is "what did India spend on UPI?" The payment system is operated by NPCI and funded through the banking system. That funding is not in these rows.

## Two platforms, disclosed because Parliament asked

The same Lok Sabha answer isolates DigiLocker and UMANG, which makes them the cleanest platform-level capital accounts in the set.

DigiLocker, since inception and through the date covered by the answer (described as 2015–2026 till date), had funds allocated of ₹168.58 crore, of which ₹105.14 crore had been spent. Against that, the answer reports over 71.66 crore registered users and more than 936.03 crore documents accessible. A later Rajya Sabha statement, as on 31 July 2026, puts registered users above 72.43 crore. The budget figure was not restated in that later statement. Pair the ₹168.58 crore with the 29 July answer, not with a claim that the allocation changed in August.

UMANG, since inception (2016–2026 till the date of the answer), had ₹149.91 crore allocated and ₹129.15 crore utilised. The answer's prose puts registered users above 11.85 crore, with 2,575 services from 241 departments. The Rajya Sabha statement of 7 August, referring to the position as on 31 July 2026, says more than 11.66 crore registered users and about 798 crore transactions in the last three years. The user counts differ across the two answers. The budget does not. Use the budget from the answer that published it.

The capital point is the ratio, not the reconciliation of user definitions. A national document wallet and a national services app are, on the published accounts, hundred-crore projects, not lakh-crore projects. Their constraint, if they have one, is not showing up as a surge in appropriated funds. The Lok Sabha text describes security work that does cost money without appearing as a separate capex line: government data centres, encryption, periodic security audits, vulnerability assessment and penetration testing, and drills aligned with CERT-In and NIC-CERT. That is operating discipline attached to a small disclosed stock of spend.

## The physical cycle is BharatNet

The Press Information Bureau's 29 July 2026 account of the government's digital-infrastructure note separates two BharatNet facts, and the gap between them is the capital story.

As of June 2026, 2.21 lakh gram panchayats had been made service-ready under BharatNet. During June 2026, BharatNet points of presence were operational at 80,472 gram panchayats. Service-ready and operational are not synonyms in that paragraph. A panchayat can be in the first count and absent from the second. The difference is on the order of 1.4 lakh gram panchayats in that month's pairing. This brief does not convert that gap into a rupee cost, because the release does not give the cost. It does say that the binding public work, for rural reach, is still a network build rather than another rewrite of a mobile app.

Common Service Centres are the staffed layer on top of that connectivity. As of May 2026, the same PIB account says 5.01 lakh CSCs were functional, including 3.91 lakh at gram panchayat level. A Rajya Sabha statement referring to 30 June 2026 says 4,07,122 CSCs were functional at gram panchayat level, 70,069 of them operated by women village-level entrepreneurs. The gram-panchayat count moved between the May and June cuts. That is an installation and entrepreneurship cycle, smaller than BharatNet and more local.

## A commerce-side outlay with a closing year

ONDC's own corporate form is a section 8 company, so it is a poor place to look for a classic capex boom. The public money attached to it in the 4 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply is the MSME Trade Enablement and Marketing initiative. A total outlay of ₹277.35 crore was approved for 2024-25 through 2026-27, as a sub-component of the World Bank-supported RAMP programme, to help micro and small enterprises join the network. Eligible support includes onboarding, cataloguing, account management, logistics, and packaging. Half of the beneficiary enterprises are targeted to be women-owned.

Financial year 2026-27 is the last year of that approved window. For someone watching a spending cycle, the relevant question at the next budget is whether the outlay is extended, not whether ONDC has announced a new warehouse. The network's about page describes participants and orders. The parliamentary answer describes a training and onboarding cheque. Those are different capital goods: one is a specification, the other is a three-year assistance programme.

## What to watch, without pretending it is one cycle

Four clocks are running, and they should stay separate.

- MeitY's eight-scheme envelope, last published as ₹5,144.45 crore allocated and ₹4,638.28 crore spent in 2025-26. The next Union Budget is the update. This page does not guess the bid.
- DigiLocker and UMANG, whose disclosed lifetime allocations are ₹168.58 crore and ₹149.91 crore. A large new appropriation would be a change in kind. Nothing in the July 2026 answer shows one.
- BharatNet's two counts: service-ready gram panchayats versus operational points of presence. The June 2026 pair is 2.21 lakh and 80,472. Closing that gap is physical work.
- The TEAM window, approved through 2026-27 at ₹277.35 crore in total, not per year.

Calling all of this a single capex cycle flattens a ministry budget, a pair of cheap national platforms, a fibre backlog, and a small-enterprise onboarding scheme. The figures are large in different units. The decision each one supports is different too.
