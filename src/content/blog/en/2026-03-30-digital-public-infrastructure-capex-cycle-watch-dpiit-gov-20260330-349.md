# India DPI Capex: The Outlay the March 2026 Note States

## Most large numbers in the note are not capex

Transaction value, transfer value, and procurement value are often placed next to digital public infrastructure as if they were capital spending. In the Press Information Bureau research note of 6 March 2026, they are not. This page does not convert them into a spending cycle.

- UPI's January 2026 figure, over ₹28.33 lakh crore, is the value of payments moving on the rail across 21.70 billion transactions.
- Cumulative Direct Benefit Transfer crossing ₹49.09 lakh crore as of January 2026 is benefits paid, not a construction budget. The note's separate savings figure, more than ₹4.31 lakh crore from 2015 to March 2024, is reported savings from de-duplication and lower leakage.
- Government eMarketplace cumulative gross merchandise value above ₹16.41 lakh crore as of November 2025 is the value of orders on the marketplace.
- PM GatiShakti, launched on 13 October 2021, is a GIS planning platform. The note says that as of 10 February 2026 the Network Planning Group had evaluated 352 infrastructure projects with an estimated cost of ₹16.10 lakh crore. Of these, 201 had been sanctioned and 167 were under implementation. That estimated cost is the cost of the projects being planned. It is not the cost of the digital platform.

## The programme outlay that is stated

e-Courts is the clear capital figure in the note. Phase I, from 2011 to 2015, computerised 14,249 courts and installed local area networks in 13,683 courts. Phase II ran from 2015 to 2023 and moved the project toward citizen-facing services, including the National Judicial Data Grid and online filing and payments.

In September 2023 the Union Cabinet approved Phase III for 2023 to 2027 with an outlay of ₹7,210 crore. The note says this phase covers digital and paperless courts, digitisation of legacy records and pending cases, wider video conferencing across courts, jails, and hospitals, and tools such as optical character recognition for case records.

That ₹7,210 crore is a judicial-digitalisation outlay. The note does not present it as the capital budget for UPI, Aadhaar, or DigiLocker.

## What a capex watch still cannot say

The note does not give annual capital expenditure for MeitY's digital public systems, a public-versus-private split, or a cycle turning up or down. API Setu's March 2026 counts — 8,036 APIs, 6,592 consumers, 2,559 publishers, and 10,530 organisations — measure catalogue size, not spend. Digital India, launched on 1 July 2015, is the programme frame. Its about page does not replace a budget line.

Until a budget document states an outlay, this brief stops at the e-Courts figure.
