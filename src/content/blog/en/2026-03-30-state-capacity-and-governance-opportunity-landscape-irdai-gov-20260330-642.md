# The Opportunity Map Around Public Finance Systems and Local Services

A capacity gap is a market only when a public document names the system a government has to finish, and when a firm can do that work without pretending the incentive cheque is its revenue. Four such gaps are written down for 2026.

## Public finance IT, with the cheque going to the state

Part IV of the SASCI guidelines issued on 27 March 2026 earmarks Rs 4,000 crore so states and UTs will digitise financial processes and join up finance, works and procurement. Each state can earn Rs 50 crore for completing a component during 2026-27. The money, once released, may be used for capital projects in any sector. It is an incentive for the reform, verified by the Comptroller and Auditor General, not a software budget.

The work itself is specified, which is what makes it an opportunity:

- An e-sanction module for online sanctions against delegated powers.
- An e-budget module for preparation, distribution and monitoring.
- An e-payment module integrated with banking systems, including the Reserve Bank's e-Kuber.
- An e-receipt module and an e-voucher module so voucher-level data exist for accounting.
- An e-HRMS covering employee records, payroll, leave and service matters, tied to salary and pension.
- Full Aadhaar-based direct benefit transfer for beneficiary schemes, including schemes the state itself runs.
- A works system from sanction to e-measurement book, bill, payment and monitoring, across public works, forest, and roads and bridges.
- An e-procurement system from bid to award.
- Live integration of the financial system with works and with procurement, so a sanction can be traced to an account.
- Digitisation of every drawing and disbursing officer, treasury and works division.

A firm that already implements treasuries, works billing or procurement can read this list as the 2026-27 specification many finance departments have been paid to meet. A firm that quotes the Rs 50 crore as its contract value has misread the guideline. The state earns the incentive. The vendor earns a procurement, if the state chooses to buy.

Part V, Rs 5,000 crore on a first-come basis, is a narrower opening in mining administration: integration with the Ministry of Mines portal, a pre-auction committee, a chief-secretary coordination committee that actually meets, a published auction calendar, and technology to detect grade misclassification. Again the incentive is the state's, and the operational work is real.

## Local and unified services

The December 2025 NeSDA report, released 22 January 2026, records 8,656 e-services in local governance and utilities out of 24,090. Seven state portals are named as carrying 100 percent of services, and the note spends time on municipal platforms because that is where citizens interact. The NeSDA 2025 round, opened on 25 May 2026, scores city portals as well as state portals, and it asks for integrated delivery, request tracking, open data, security and privacy. The opportunity is last-mile service completion on the mandatory list of 59, and city portals that can survive those parameters. The buyer is the state, the city or the department that owns the portal. DARPG's role is assessment and a monthly dashboard, which is a sales map only in the sense that it shows who is thin.

## Learning content and grievance feedback

DoPT's design for iGOT includes a content marketplace run by Karmayogi Bharat. As on 6 March 2026 the platform had over 4,400 courses and more than 1.51 crore users, and Karmayogi Bharat is an NCVET awarding body for qualifications in public policy, AI-led digital transformation, and public governance. The opening for a content or assessment firm is that marketplace and those credentials. The opening is not a claim on the two-crore design population as a guaranteed licence base. District monitoring of learning is explicitly not on the platform, so a product that assumes a district dashboard is ahead of the published system.

CPGRAMS, in the June 2026 report, collected 75,318 feedbacks that month, 42,226 of them on central ministries, and registered 6,262 grievances through Common Service Centres. The portal is available at more than five lakh Common Service Centres, working with 2.5 lakh village-level entrepreneurs. Grievance categorisation, feedback and last-mile registration are operating workloads. They are also a warning: DARPG already runs the index and the call centre. A product has to fit that machinery.

NITI's Aspirational Districts Programme remains the district collaboration frame for 112 districts, with a public delta ranking. It is a place to see which outcomes a collector is judged on. It is not a procurement programme.
