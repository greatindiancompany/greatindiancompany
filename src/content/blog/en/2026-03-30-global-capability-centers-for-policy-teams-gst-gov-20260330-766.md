# What Policy Teams Should Measure About Captive Hubs

Policy teams are asked for a GCC strategy: jobs, exports, investment, and a spread beyond six cities. The official record can support parts of that strategy. It cannot support a single target that mixes all four, because no statistical office publishes the object the target would measure.

## Separate the goal from the series

If the goal is **exports**, the series is the Reserve Bank's annual survey of computer software and IT-enabled services. The 2025-26 round, released on 18 September 2026, puts exports excluding overseas commercial presence at **US$ 221.4 billion**, up **8.2 percent**. Engineering services were **US$ 12.0 billion**. Off-site delivery was **91.7 percent**. The United States took **54.1 percent**. Say so in the note, including the exclusion of commercial presence. Do not relabel the total as captive-centre revenue. The survey does not ask that question.

If the goal is **foreign investment**, use DPIIT's equity line and the Finance Ministry's gross and net figures side by side. Computer software and hardware took **USD 13,946 million** of equity in 2025-26, after **USD 7,814 million** in 2024-25, on the March 2026 factsheet cited in the Lok Sabha on 20 July 2026. Gross FDI was **USD 94.84 billion** and net FDI **USD 6.95 billion**. A policy paragraph that quotes only the gross number will describe a different external account from the one the Ministry described in the same answer, where repatriation and Indian outward investment explain the net. Outsourcing and R&D sit in DPIIT's services sector (**USD 10,010 million** in 2025-26), not in the computer-software row. A captive can be classified in either place.

If the goal is **places**, use STPI's state table and STPI's centre count as two indicators. Registered IT/ITeS exports in 2024-25 were **₹10,69,270.59 crore**, tentative, with about **94 percent** in six states and Karnataka near **43 percent**. The society operates **67** centres, **59** in Tier II and Tier III cities. A state incentive that claims diversification should be able to show that state's row in the export table. A new centre in the network is an input. The row is the outcome. Both can be published. They should not be substituted for each other. The worksheet is the [state brief](/blog/global-capability-centers-state-wise-patterns-gst-gov-20260330-326).

If the goal is **jobs**, there is no matching statistical series in the RBI survey or in MoSPI's provisional national accounts. PIB Delhi on 25 February 2025 reported a ministerial highlight of **1.9 million** professionals in GCCs. STPI's annual report reproduces a NASSCOM figure of **5.8 million** direct IT-BPM jobs and marks the source. Those are not interchangeable, and neither is a household-survey estimate. A jobs target set on either number cannot be audited from MoSPI as the publications stand. MoSPI's IT activity sits inside a GVA group with finance, real estate, professional services, and ownership of dwellings, which reached **₹77,09,678 crore** at constant prices in the 2025-26 provisional estimates, up **10.4 percent**. Publishing an IT split, or a satellite table, is the measurement reform that would let national accounts speak to this sector. Until then, do not cite 10.4 percent as IT growth.

## What a centre register would have to contain

A policy goal expressed in number of centres needs a frame the February 2025 PIB note does not provide. A usable register would define a captive against a third-party exporter, name the filing or survey that populates it, date the stock, and say whether a legal entity with three sites is one centre or three. The same discipline applies to a 2030 projection. The PIB note states a projection of around **2,400** centres and about **$105 billion**. It does not publish a method. Adopting it as a government target adopts an unnamed method.

STPI has argued, in its own publication on this subject, for a national GCC policy. The documents in this set do not include a notified statute that creates the category. Writing the policy before writing the register will produce targets that cannot be checked. The [evidence snapshot](/blog/global-capability-centers-evidence-snapshot-gst-gov-20260330-686) is the list of cells that are already filled and the cells that should stay empty.

## Data policy is a specification problem

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act and the rules notified on 13 November 2025 already set the structure. Transfer out of India is permitted unless the government restricts it. From **13 May 2027**, a notified Significant Data Fiduciary can be required to keep specified personal data and the traffic data of its flow inside India. The next official act that changes captive operations is that specification, or an order under the cross-border rule, not another general statement that data policy matters. Policy notes should also expand "GCC" when the file is a Government Community Cloud tender. The letters are already in use for a different MeitY programme.
