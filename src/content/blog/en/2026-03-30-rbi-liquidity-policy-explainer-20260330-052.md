The Reserve Bank’s liquidity machinery has a target, a corridor, a set of auctions for short gaps, and a separate set of tools for liquidity that is meant to last. The framework that states this was finalised on 30 September 2025 in Press Release 2025-2026/1201, after an internal working group report had been placed on the Bank’s website on 6 August 2025. The rates inside the corridor were last reset on 5 December 2025 and have been held since.

## The target is the call rate, and the walls are 25 basis points away

The overnight weighted average call rate remains the operating target. The Bank also watches other overnight segments so that money-market rates stay orderly. The corridor is symmetric. The policy repo rate is the middle. The standing deposit facility is the floor, 25 basis points below the repo. The marginal standing facility is the ceiling, 25 basis points above the repo.

The resolution of 3–5 August 2026 states the current setting. The repo rate is 5.25 per cent. The standing deposit facility is 5.00 per cent. The marginal standing facility rate and the Bank Rate are 5.50 per cent. The stance is neutral. The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 18 September 2026 prints the same three operating rates for the week ended 11 September, and it also prints a fixed reverse repo rate of 3.35 per cent. The framework names the standing deposit facility and the marginal standing facility as the floor and the ceiling. The fixed reverse repo rate is a published rate. It lies well below the floor the framework uses.

A bank with spare reserves can place them in the standing deposit facility at 5.00 per cent. A bank that is short can borrow at the marginal standing facility at 5.50 per cent. On 20 September 2026 the money-market release shows ₹91,747 crore in that day’s standing deposit facility and ₹82 crore at the marginal standing facility. The use of the two windows, on that Sunday print, was almost entirely placement, not borrowing.

## Transient auctions were retargeted at seven days

Before the September 2025 revision, the 14-day variable rate repo and variable rate reverse repo were the main operation for short-term liquidity. The revision discontinued that role. Transient liquidity is now managed primarily with 7-day variable rate repos and reverse repos, and with other operations whose tenor runs from overnight up to 14 days, at the Bank’s discretion. The Bank said it would give at least one day’s notice of tenor, amount and timing, and that it would still operate on the same day when circumstances warrant it.

Variable rate repo injects funds. Variable rate reverse repo absorbs them. The rate is discovered at auction, which is why a reverse repo cut-off can sit near the repo rate rather than at the standing deposit facility. On 18 September 2026 a 3-day variable rate reverse repo was accepted for ₹2,22,629 crore at a cut-off of 5.24 per cent, one basis point under the repo rate. The money-market release for 20 September shows that 3-day book still outstanding, plus two longer reverse repos: ₹52,449 crore from 11 September with a 26-day tenor, and ₹2,39,276 crore from 7 September with a 30-day tenor, both at a 5.24 per cent cut-off and both maturing on 7 October.

The annex to the framework puts overnight-to-fortnight operations in the transient bucket, with seven days as the primary tenor, and puts variable rate repo and reverse repo beyond a fortnight with the durable tools. The 3-day auction is inside the transient window. The 26-day and 30-day books are beyond a fortnight.

## Durable tools are open market operations, long auctions and swaps

The same press release keeps three instruments for durable liquidity: outright open market operations, long-term variable rate repo and reverse repo, and foreign-exchange swap auctions. Amounts are discretionary. The Bulletin extract dated 11 September 2026 shows the distinction in a single week. From 31 August to 6 September the columns for outright open market sale and purchase are blank. Absorption on 4 September is ₹6,02,394 crore of variable rate reverse repo and ₹2,28,290 crore at the standing deposit facility, a net absorption of ₹8,30,561 crore. That week’s surplus was handled inside the facility and the auctions, without an outright bond operation on the dates listed.

The Governor’s statement of 5 June 2026 lists one durable action of the preceding weeks by name: a long-term foreign-exchange buy/sell swap of USD 5 billion in May 2026. The August statement says the Bank will keep running two-way operations so that the weighted average call rate lines up with the repo rate.

## Reserve ratios sit underneath the corridor

The cash reserve ratio and the statutory liquidity ratio are requirements, not auction tools. The notification of 6 June 2025 set the cash reserve ratio at 3.0 per cent of net demand and time liabilities from the fortnight beginning 29 November 2025. The supplement dated 18 September 2026 shows 3.00 per cent, and a statutory liquidity ratio of 18.00 per cent. Cash reserve is a balance banks maintain with the Reserve Bank. Statutory liquidity is a holding of liquid assets. On 20 September, scheduled commercial banks’ cash balances with the Reserve Bank were ₹8,15,649.80 crore, against an average daily cash reserve requirement of ₹8,21,989 crore for the fortnight ending 30 September. The requirement is an average for the fortnight. The release publishes both numbers and does not, on its face, call that day’s balance a default.

Net durable liquidity, a separate line on the same money-market release, was a surplus of ₹10,66,303 crore as on 31 August 2026. The Governor’s August statement measured system liquidity the other way, as the net position under the liquidity adjustment facility: an average daily surplus of ₹1.0 lakh crore since the June meeting. Reading the corridor means holding those two definitions apart. One is the stock the Bank labels durable. The other is the net of repos, reverse repos, the standing facilities and the standing liquidity facility on a given day. The operating target that both are supposed to serve is still the call rate, and the walls around it are still 5.00 and 5.50 around a repo rate of 5.25.
