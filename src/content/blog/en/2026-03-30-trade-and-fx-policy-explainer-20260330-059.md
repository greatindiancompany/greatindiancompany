# How to read India's trade and foreign-exchange releases

India's trade and foreign-exchange system is a set of clocks. Customs records the shipment. The Reserve Bank records the payment. FBIL publishes the rupee benchmark the RBI displays. The commerce ministry's promotion schemes are a third apparatus, about support to exporters, and they do not change either statistical clock. Reading them as one "trade number" is how confident mistakes get made.

## Clock one: DGCIS and the Commerce quick estimate

The Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics compiles merchandise trade from customs. The Commerce ministry publishes a monthly quick estimate, usually through PIB, covering merchandise and an estimate of services.

The [13 August 2026 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298878) is the clean example. It says July 2026 merchandise exports were US$44.24 billion and imports US$76.22 billion. It says services exports were an estimated US$35.89 billion. The footnote is the policy fact: the latest services data then available from the RBI were for June, so July services were an estimation. A second footnote says earlier months were revised pro-rata using quarterly balance-of-payments data.

DGCIS also publishes commodity and country quick estimates. The August 2026 tables, provisional, are in million US dollars and carry the line "the figures for August 2026 are provisional." Grand totals in those tables are merchandise exports of US$43,810.92 million and imports of US$70,670.34 million. Commodity lines such as engineering goods and readymade garments live there, not in the RBI current-account statement.

Use this clock for ports, commodities, destinations, and the monthly merchandise deficit the ministry itself states. For April–July 2026-27 that stated merchandise deficit is US$118.60 billion.

## Clock two: the RBI balance of payments

The balance of payments covers goods, services, primary income, secondary income, and the financial account. The [1 September 2026 quarterly release](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_PressRelease.aspx?fn=5&prid=63493) is marked preliminary for April–June 2026, and the year-ago column is marked partially revised.

Goods in that quarter are exports of US$132.0 billion and imports of US$218.0 billion. That goods line will not match a sum of Commerce monthly merchandise exports, because valuation, timing, and coverage differ. July already shows the gap in public: Commerce merchandise exports US$44.24 billion, RBI merchandise exports US$45.1 billion.

Services in the RBI clock are measured, not filled in from the previous month. On 31 August 2026 the RBI published July services receipts of US$38,253 million and payments of US$20,606 million, provisional. On 15 September the monthly balance of payments rounded July services exports to US$38.3 billion. The Commerce estimate of US$35.89 billion was the right number to quote on 13 August. It is the wrong number to quote once the RBI July release exists.

Labels travel with the cells. "P" is preliminary. "PR" is partially revised. A later quarter can move a preliminary cell. Quote the label with the figure.

## Reserve signs, and the older format beside BPM6

Two statements dated 1 September 2026 describe the same quarter's reserves and use different layouts.

In the main statement, reserve assets are shown with a stated convention: a minus sign is an increase in reserves, a plus sign is a decrease. April–June 2026 shows +US$8.1 billion on that line, and the text says reserves were depleted by US$8.1 billion on a balance-of-payments basis.

The companion note, [sources of variation in reserves](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63494), says it follows an older format that can differ from BPM6 in the treatment of transfers and of ADRs and GDRs. In that note, an increase in reserves is a plus. Nominal reserves, including valuation, fell US$22.5 billion. Valuation was a loss of US$14.4 billion. The payments-basis change was −US$8.1 billion. The text attributes the valuation loss mainly to a lower gold price and appreciation of the US dollar against major currencies.

The weekly statistical supplement is a third reserve document. It is a stock, in rupees and US dollars, split into foreign-currency assets, gold, SDRs, and the reserve position in the IMF. As on 11 September 2026 the stock was US$780,782 million. The supplement's footnote excludes, from foreign-currency assets, SDR holdings already counted as SDRs, certain IIFC (UK) bonds, SAARC and ACU swap lending, and the RBI's contribution to Nexus Global Payments. The stock is not "all foreign assets the economy holds."

The July monthly balance of payments uses the same minus-means-increase convention on monetary movements. A print of −US$20.8 billion means reserves rose by US$20.8 billion on a payments basis in July.

## The rupee benchmark

Since 10 July 2018, FBIL has computed and disseminated the rupee reference rate. The RBI's own reference-rate page says so and points to fbil.org.in. The RBI homepage still displays the day's FBIL quotes. On 24 September 2026 at 1:00 pm those quotes included Rs 95.9099 per US dollar. Use the homepage for the displayed level and the stated time. Use FBIL if you need the benchmark series itself.

## The promotion apparatus is not a statistical release

The Export Promotion Mission, approved on 12 November 2025, has an outlay of Rs 25,060 crore from 2025-26 to 2030-31. Niryat Protsahan is trade finance for MSME exporters. Niryat Disha covers quality, compliance, branding, fairs, warehousing, logistics, inland transport, and trade intelligence. That is policy. It does not revise the customs deficit or the current account.

Preferential origin certificates are the operational side of trade agreements. The PIB note of 18 August 2026 on trade agreements says the India-EFTA TEPA and the India-UK CETA allow origin to be established by self-declaration, and that the UK agreement waives origin documents for consignments below £1,000. Those rules change the paperwork of a shipment. They are not a line in the DGCIS grand total.

## A short discipline

Name the clock before you name the number. Keep the preliminary label. Replace a Commerce services estimate when the RBI month arrives. Read reserve plus and minus only with the convention printed on that page. Keep promotion schemes in the policy pile.
