# Where a counted tap connection can still fail

This is a map of failure modes that the official design already admits. It is not a probability model, and it does not assign a chance to any scenario. Each case below is a way the published rules can be met on paper and still miss the service the same rules describe.

## A household is counted, and the village is not certified

The dashboard's first stage is an entry: name and Aadhaar of the head of household, then a village marked 100 percent once every household is entered. The national rates quoted for 10 March 2026 (around 81.61 percent) and 4 August 2026 (82.19 percent) are reported household connections. Certification is a later Gram Sabha act.

The failure mode is a village that looks finished in a household table and is unfinished in the certification table. JJM 2.0 widens that gap on purpose. The Gram Panchayat is not supposed to declare Har Ghar Jal until the state has put in-village maintenance in place. A connection can be real, and the declaration can still be properly refused.

## Maintenance is promised, and the source is not

The Cabinet note names source sustainability as part of the reason for Jal Utsav and for the whole-of-government convergence it hopes to arrange with other departments. It does not publish a village-wise source-adequacy audit in that note. The failure mode is a handed-over scheme whose aquifer or intake cannot support the supply the tap count implies. The mission's own shift toward service delivery is an admission that infrastructure, once built, still has to keep working.

## The state spends, and the central share arrives late

The 6 August 2026 note says several states carried approved schemes on their own funds during the transition from the earlier mission period to JJM 2.0. The Centre then reimbursed ₹5,289.09 crore to ten states. That episode already happened. The scenario it illustrates is still open: a state that pauses when reimbursement lags, or a state that sprints and then waits. Scheme-level release through SNA SPARSH is the control meant to make the next round less lumpy. A control that is new can itself be a bottleneck if schemes are not yet identifiable on the rail.

## A village is ODF Plus, and still has one waste stream missing

As on 11 June 2026, 57,923 SBM villages were Aspiring: open-defecation-free, with either solid or liquid waste arranged, not both. The failure mode is a sanitation claim that stops at the toilet. Liquid waste left unmanaged is the gap the Aspiring definition exists to show. Rising villages, only 3,463 on that date, are the thin middle. The mass of the programme is already Model (5,07,634). The residual risk is concentrated, which makes it easier to ignore in a national sentence and easier to find on the [dashboard](https://sbm.gov.in/sbmgdashboard/).

## An urban project is awarded, and the works are not finished

Against AMRUT 2.0 capex awards of ₹1,37,130.13 crore, physically completed works stood at ₹66,472 crore as on 9 July 2026. Sewerage and septage is the expensive minority of the approved book: 605 projects, ₹71,133.01 crore. The failure mode is a water-tap project that gets completed in the statistics while the sewage project beside it, slower and larger, is still in the ground. City households can gain a functional tap and still depend on an unfinished network for what leaves the house.

## Treatment is sanctioned, and the river still receives the rest

Till June 2026, Namami Gange had completed 4,263 million litres a day of sewage treatment capacity out of 6,610 million litres a day taken up, under the Department of Water Resources ([jalshakti-dowr.gov.in](https://jalshakti-dowr.gov.in/)). The failure mode is treating the sanctioned figure as if it were already intercepting sewage. The unfinished capacity is the load the completed plants do not yet cover. The ministry's parliamentary answer is the right denominator. A slogan about a clean Ganga is not.

None of these cases needs a made-up shock. They are the gaps between two numbers, or between two stages, that the programmes already publish. The main texts are the [Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2237548), the [August fund note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295509), the [JJM dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx), and the [AMRUT answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3022_tT0QXq.pdf).
