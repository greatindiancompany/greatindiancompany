# Why India's payments stack is not a card scheme with a new interface

Comparisons that start with "India versus the world" usually want a single rank. The official record supports a sharper claim. India built a real-time account-to-account system that any participating app can reach, a consent pipe that is not allowed to keep the data, and a central-bank token that is designed to behave like cash. Those are design choices. They are not a claim that every other country copies the same plumbing.

## Interoperability is the benchmark, not a brand

UPI, as MeitY describes it on the [Digital India page](https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/initiative/unified-payment-interface-upi/), lets a person use one participating application to reach accounts at other banks and to pay merchants on the same system. The Press Information Bureau factsheet of 24 August 2026 dates the pilot to April 2016 with 21 member banks. A card scheme can be ubiquitous and still keep acceptance, credentials, and switching inside the scheme's rules. UPI's public design point is that the user's app and the recipient's bank do not have to be the same company.

The same factsheet relays an IMF paper of June 2025, *Growing Retail Digital Payments: The Value of Interoperability*, for a 2024 snapshot: about 49 per cent of global real-time payment transaction volume. Read the noun. Real-time payment volume is not card volume, not cash, and not a measure of credit. PIB's April 2026 note separately says UPI was 81 per cent of retail digital transactions in India, using January 2026 as the month for its 21.70 billion transactions. A global share and a domestic share answer different questions. Neither is a licence to say UPI has "replaced cards" without a Reserve Bank payment-system table that splits instruments.

Cross-border reach is also a design fact, not a volume fact. The August 2026 factsheet says UPI is live in 11 foreign countries for acceptance or remittances, and it records 2026 steps with Cambodia, Greece, and the Maldives. A corridor agreement is not the domestic July 2026 total of 2,365.8 crore transactions.

## Consent is regulated because screen-scraping was the alternative

The [NBFC-Account Aggregator Directions, 2016](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=10598) create a company whose only business is to collect and present specified financial information with a customer's explicit consent. The consent artefact has to name the customer, the information, the purpose, the recipient, a notification address, and the creation and expiry of consent. The customer can revoke it. The aggregator does not see the customer's login credentials at the bank or insurer. The information must not reside with the aggregator. If the aggregator's statement disagrees with the financial information provider, the provider's books prevail.

That is a different bargain from asking a borrower to share a banking password, and from a private bureau that stores the raw feed as its asset. Financial information users have to be regulated by a financial-sector regulator. The list of information runs from bank deposits through securities, insurance, the National Pension System, and GST returns in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, plus anything the Reserve Bank adds. It is broad, and it is still not a right to mine a phone's contact list. That restriction lives in the digital lending directions, not in the aggregator directions.

## The Digital Rupee is public money, not a stablecoin business

The Reserve Bank's [concept note on the Digital Rupee](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationReportDetails.aspx?ID=1218) treats e₹ as another form of the rupee, issued by the central bank. Wholesale e₹ is for restricted use by financial institutions. Retail e₹ is the electronic version of cash. The note's retail model has the Reserve Bank issuing and redeeming tokens, with banks distributing them.

The [retail pilot press release](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=54773) of 29 November 2022 makes the cash-like features explicit. e₹-R is a digital token and legal tender, issued in the same denominations as notes and coins, held in a bank wallet, usable person-to-person and person-to-merchant with a QR code. It does not earn interest. It converts into deposits. Settlement finality is the point. A private wallet balance that is a claim on a company is a different liability. A deposit at a commercial bank is a different liability again. Comparing e₹ adoption with UPI volume mixes a cash substitute still described, at launch, as a pilot with a retail payment system that PIB already counts in crores of transactions per month.
