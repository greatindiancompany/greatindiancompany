# What Operators Should Separate Before Building on Rural Broadband

An operator building on Indian rural broadband inherits three networks that share a map and do not share a service level. The mobile network is wide. The gram-panchayat fibre network is a public build with an uptime problem. The household connection, fibre or 5G radio, is a third deployment with its own crew. Mixing them in one availability promise is how pilots fail after the inauguration.

## Ask which count you are buying

Nearly 2.14 lakh of 2.56 lakh gram panchayats were online under BharatNet Phases I and II, in the 12 February 2026 official reply. Optical fibre with 95 percent uptime covered 42,000 villages in December 2025. In Tamil Nadu, 10,869 gram panchayats were service-ready and 808 homes had fibre through BharatNet. The state plan under the amended programme is 5,27,506 household connections over ten years. If the product needs a household that can hold a session every day, the relevant denominator is the uptime-village count or the fibre-to-the-home count. The service-ready panchayat count answers a different question: whether the public network has reached the building.

The amended design, Rs 1,39,579 crore in the December 2024 note, adds ring fibre, a ten-year service-level agreement, a network operating centre, remote fibre monitoring, and power backup. That is the network a wholesale user will eventually buy. It is under implementation. A product roadmap that assumes the ten-year clause is already true at every lit panchayat is early.

## Pick the access technology the July book supports

Fixed wired broadband was 48.14 million lines at end-July 2026. Mobile wireless broadband was 1,027.50 million. 5G fixed wireless was 13.21 million, and rural lines (6.64 million) slightly exceeded urban lines (6.56 million). Unlicensed-band radio fixed wireless was 5.14 million and mostly urban. Rural wireline tele-density was 0.57 percent. A rural video product that requires fibre into the home is aimed at a thin installed base. A rural product that can ride 5G fixed wireless is aimed at a base TRAI already splits by service area in an annex. A product that only needs a handset is aimed at the mobile book, where rural tele-density was 61.05 percent and where 15.98 million people requested a new operator in July alone.

Portability concentrates in large circles: Uttar Pradesh (East) 2.29 million requests in July, then Uttar Pradesh (West), Madhya Pradesh, and Bihar. Churn assumptions copied from Delhi, where tele-density is 368.33 percent and the licensed area spills into neighbouring cities, will not describe Bihar, where tele-density is 64.19 percent.

## Price the permission where you dig

National right-of-way disposal averaged 30.4 days. Tamil Nadu averaged about 85 days and had not completed adoption of the 2024 rules, nor had West Bengal, in the February 2026 reply. Thirty-three of 36 states and union territories had implemented the rules. A multi-state fibre rollout needs a state clock, not the national mean. Anchor institutions are the nearer public customer: 68.8 percent connected nationally, target 90 percent by 2030, with Tamil Nadu already at 84.74 percent. Schools, anganwadis, health centres, and panchayat offices are named. They are a better first site list than "every village household."

Machine-to-machine SIMs, 137.70 million, led by Airtel, are a device market. Leave them out of a household broadband total. TRAI prints the broadband book both ways: 1,094.48 million with those connections in the mobile mix, and 1,005.72 million without machine-to-machine cellular broadband.
