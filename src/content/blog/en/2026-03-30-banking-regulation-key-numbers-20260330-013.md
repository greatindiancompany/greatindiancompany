# The capital ratios a commercial bank in India must hold

The numbers that decide whether a commercial bank in India is above its minimum capital floor are published as a table, not as a market estimate. They sit in the [Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks - Prudential Norms on Capital Adequacy) Directions, 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13159&Mode=0), issued on 28 November 2025 under section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, and updated as on 1 July 2026.

For these Directions, a commercial bank means a banking company other than a small finance bank, a payment bank, and a local area bank, together with corresponding new banks and the State Bank of India. Small finance banks, payment banks, and local area banks are outside this table. Their capital rules are in their own licensing and operating instructions.

## The minimum table

Risk-weighted assets are the sum of credit-risk, market-risk, and operational-risk risk-weighted assets. Against that base, Table 1 of the Directions sets the minimum:

| Regulatory capital | Percent of risk-weighted assets |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum Common Equity Tier 1 ratio | 5.5 |
| Minimum Tier 1 capital ratio | 7.0 |
| Maximum Additional Tier 1 inside that Tier 1 minimum | 1.5 |
| Minimum total capital ratio | 9.0 |
| Maximum Tier 2 inside that total | 2.0 |
| Capital conservation buffer, in Common Equity Tier 1 | 2.5 |
| Minimum Common Equity Tier 1 plus the buffer | 8.0 |
| Minimum total capital plus the buffer | 11.5 |

Two readings follow directly from the text.

First, 9 percent is the minimum total capital ratio on an ongoing basis, before buffers. Inside it, Additional Tier 1 can count for at most 1.5 percent of risk-weighted assets, and Tier 2 for at most 2 percent. Excess common equity can fill the Tier 1 and total-capital minima. The Directions call Tier 1 going-concern capital and Tier 2 gone-concern capital.

Second, the capital conservation buffer is extra common equity of 2.5 percent of risk-weighted assets. Add it to the 5.5 percent common-equity minimum and the equity floor is 8 percent. Add it to the 9 percent total and the all-in minimum in the table is 11.5 percent. The buffer applies at solo and consolidated level.

## The leverage backstop

The same Directions set a leverage ratio that does not depend on risk weights. The numerator is Tier 1 capital. The denominator is the exposure measure defined in the Directions. A domestic systemically important bank must hold at least 4 percent. Any other bank covered by the Directions must hold at least 3.5 percent. The ratio is disclosed at quarter-end and must be met at all times.

## What the table does not include

A domestic systemically important bank also holds an additional common-equity surcharge, graded by bucket, on top of this table. The Directions describe that surcharge as an extension of the conservation buffer. A countercyclical capital buffer can be mandated separately. Pillar 2 allows the Reserve Bank to set a higher minimum for a particular bank after looking at its risk profile. The Directions also say a bank is expected to operate well above the minimum.

This page states the regulatory floors. It does not report any bank's actual ratio.
