# Four Risks Already Written into India’s API Record

A scenario map is useful when each branch starts from a sentence the government has already published. Probabilities that nobody measured do not make the map more serious. These four risks are named in cabinet, parliamentary, or regulatory texts. What would confirm each one is also a document, not a mood.

## Single-source inputs

The 21 March 2020 cabinet note says that in some specific bulk drugs import dependence is 80 to 100 percent, and that a supply disruption would hit drug security. The 6 February 2026 Lok Sabha reply repeats the operating aim of the bulk-drug PLI: reduce disruption risk for critical ingredients that have no alternatives, where dependence on a single source is the problem. The risk is molecule-specific. A diversified formulation export book can sit on top of a concentrated intermediate.

What confirms it for a given product is the firm’s own bill of materials plus the current notified list: if the intermediate is one of the products the scheme still treats as critical, the official record agrees it is a supply-security item. What does not confirm it is a general remark about “China dependence” with no molecule and no year.

## Common utilities that are still works

The February 2026 reply is the scenario source for park timing. The scheme tenure runs through FY 2026–27. On 6 February 2026, Himachal Pradesh had floated the tender for zero-liquid-discharge facilities, including the effluent plant and steam. Andhra Pradesh was still in land acquisition for an additional parcel, with environmental clearance for that land granted in January 2026, and power-design approval was in process. Gujarat was further along on civil works, with utility execution underway, which is still execution.

The risk branch is a tenant who finances a plant against utilities the reply has not described as commissioned. Confirmation is a later status from the state implementing agency or a later parliamentary reply that changes the stage words: awarded, floated, under installation, advanced stage. Those words are the scenario labels. Replacing them with a completion percentage the reply does not give is how the risk note becomes fiction.

## A product list that moves

The cabinet identified 53 bulk drugs and set 20 percent and 10 percent incentive rates. The February 2026 reply speaks of 41 identified products and 48 approved projects for 33 bulk drugs. The Department’s schemes page lists corrigenda and fresh invitations dated through 2025 and 2026, including a corrigendum dated 4 May 2026. A financing case built on the 2020 list, without the approval letter and the latest guideline, can be describing a product the scheme no longer pays for, or a claim rule the corrigendum changed.

The confirmation step is mechanical. Match the molecule to the approval letter and to the guideline version cited in the company’s claim manual. The pharmaceuticals PLI is the neighbouring trap: it covers inputs other than those notified under the bulk-drug scheme. A product cannot be described as covered by both lists at once on the strength of the February 2026 boundary sentence.

## Permission risk beside incentive risk

A PLI letter from the Department of Pharmaceuticals and a manufacturing licence under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act are different permissions. CDSCO’s portal assigns new drugs, clinical trials, standards, and import quality to the centre, and records state FDA licences in SUGAM. Revised Schedule M, G.S.R. 922(E) dated 28 December 2023, changes the premises and quality system a licensee must run. The risk is a plant that is incentive-eligible on paper and not licensable on the day it wants to sell. Confirmation is the licence, the inspection record, and the Schedule M gap list. The incentive approval is not a substitute for those.

A fifth item belongs in the watch file even though it is not a factory risk: quoting an unrelated ministry. Renewable-energy pages do not establish API eligibility, park allotment, or a CDSCO status. If a memo’s only links are outside the Department of Pharmaceuticals, the health ministry, CDSCO, and the Department of Biotechnology, the scenario is that the memo is about the wrong sector.
