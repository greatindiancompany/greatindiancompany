# The FY26 IPO tape: more listings, a different mix

A market signal is a pattern in completed deals, not a slogan about sentiment. SEBI's Annual Report 2025-26, Chapter 3, is the pattern for India's primary market. The source note under the main tables is BSE and NSE. The year is April 2025 to March 2026.

## Equity listings rose while the public-issue rupee amount was almost flat

Public equity issues, IPOs and follow-on public offers together, raised ₹1,88,704 crore in 2025-26, against ₹1,90,478 crore the year before. That is the 0.9 per cent decline the chapter states. Inside that total, IPOs were 366 issues and ₹1,88,616 crore, against 320 issues and ₹1,72,328 crore. The follow-on public offer almost disappeared: one issue, ₹88 crore, after two issues and ₹18,150 crore in 2024-25.

Add rights issues and the equity public-and-rights total is the figure Chapter 1 and Chapter 3 both call a high: ₹2.3 lakh crore, up 11.7 per cent. The table's exact line is ₹2,34,872 crore from 506 issues, against ₹2,10,190 crore from 464 issues.

The signal is volume plus a rights rebound, not a larger IPO cheque in every deal. Main-board IPOs were 109 and raised ₹1.8 lakh crore, up 8.9 per cent, with the median issue size at ₹760 crore against ₹740 crore. SME platform listings were 257, against 241, and raised ₹11,587 crore, up 18.1 per cent. The average SME issue was ₹45.1 crore, against ₹40.7 crore. Of those 257, 197 were between ₹10 crore and ₹25 crore, and 57 were ₹25 crore or more.

## Fresh capital and exits moved in opposite directions inside the IPO

Chapter 3 splits the 366 IPOs three ways.

- Offer for sale only: 19 issues, ₹50,849 crore. A year earlier, 15 issues raised ₹58,994 crore.
- Fresh issue only: 214 issues, ₹21,866 crore, against 215 issues and ₹22,108 crore.
- Both: 133 issues, ₹1,15,901 crore, against 90 issues and ₹91,226 crore. Inside those mixed deals, the fresh-capital piece was ₹58,166 crore and the offer-for-sale piece was ₹57,735 crore.

Pure fresh issues are numerous and small in rupees. The money sits in mixed deals and in pure offers for sale. SME issues, the chapter says, stayed mostly fresh paper, with the offer-for-sale share generally in a 0 to 27 per cent range over the years it reviewed. Large deals still carry more of the exit.

Private issuers dominated the count. Of 506 public and rights issues, 504 were private sector and accounted for 98.8 per cent of the amount. Seventy-eight issues above ₹500 crore raised ₹2,01,619 crore, 85.8 per cent of public-and-rights money. By industry, financial services were 28.6 per cent of that amount, metals and mining 12.0 per cent, and consumer services 11.3 per cent.

## The listed-company pipes diverged

Qualified institutional placements fell by half: 36 issues and ₹67,853 crore, against 91 issues and ₹1,35,597 crore. The average QIP was larger, ₹1,885 crore against ₹1,490 crore. Preferential allotments went the other way, to ₹1,48,219 crore, up 76.3 per cent. Rights issues were 139 against 142, but the amount rose 134.2 per cent to ₹46,168 crore, and the single largest rights issue was about 54 per cent of that amount.

Read together, listed companies that wanted a wide institutional book used it less. Companies that could place paper with a chosen set of investors, or that could ask existing holders through a rights issue, used those routes more. That is a funding-mix signal. It is not a statement that equity capital dried up. Preferential and rights money more than offset the QIP drop inside the chapter's own tables.

Debt did cool. Funds mobilised through debt issues fell 8.4 per cent to ₹9,11,078 crore across 1,967 issues. Private placement was 98.8 per cent of that. Public debt issues were the exception: ₹11,343 crore, up 39.2 per cent, still 43 issues.

## What a reader should not infer

These figures stop at March 2026. They do not say what the book looks like in September 2026. They also do not say that a company "should" prefer a fresh issue over an offer for sale. ICDR, last amended on 21 March 2026, still sets eligibility, lock-in, and disclosure. The signal is only this: in the last full year SEBI has published, India listed more companies, raised a record public-and-rights equity total because rights issues swelled, saw fresh capital and exits sit side by side in mixed IPOs, and watched QIPs shrink while preferential deals grew.
