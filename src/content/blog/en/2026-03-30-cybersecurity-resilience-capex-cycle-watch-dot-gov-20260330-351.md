# Where the Union Budget puts cybersecurity money

Cybersecurity spending in the Union Budget is not one line. MeitY Demand No. 27, in crore of rupees, splits establishment of CERT-In from a scheme called Cyber Security Projects, and it gives the Data Protection Board its own small establishment line. The Home Ministry's assistance to state police sits in a different reply. Adding them up is a choice a reader can make. The documents do not print that sum.

## CERT-In is an establishment line with a large capital column

The demand prints revenue, capital and total for "Cyber Security (CERT-In)":

| Column | Revenue | Capital | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Actual 2024-25 | 94.98 | 159.90 | 254.88 |
| Budget 2025-26 | 109.00 | 146.00 | 255.00 |
| Revised 2025-26 | 115.00 | 162.00 | 277.00 |
| Budget 2026-27 | 117.00 | 152.00 | 269.00 |

In every one of those years the capital column is larger than the revenue column. The 2026-27 budget estimate is a little below the 2025-26 revised estimate and a little above the 2025-26 budget estimate. The note under the demand says this provision follows CERT-In's role under the Information Technology Act, 2000: incident information, guidelines and advisories, forecasts and alerts, emergency measures, coordination, and authority to monitor and collect traffic data for cybersecurity and to identify intrusion or the spread of a computer contaminant.

Capital here is the budget's capital column. The demand does not itemise servers, buildings or software inside that ₹152 crore.

## The scheme line swings more than the establishment line

Cyber Security Projects are shown entirely in the revenue column in this demand (the capital cells are blank):

| Column | Amount (₹ crore) |
| --- | ---: |
| Actual 2024-25 | 210.99 |
| Budget 2025-26 | 782.00 |
| Revised 2025-26 | 600.00 |
| Budget 2026-27 | 790.00 |

The objective printed with the line is a holistic approach to securing cyberspace through security policy, compliance and assurance, security, incident early-warning and response, security training, an enabling legal framework, and collaboration.

The pattern that matters for a capex watch is the gap between intention and revision. The 2025-26 budget estimate of ₹782 crore was revised to ₹600 crore. The following budget estimate, ₹790 crore, is close to the earlier estimate and well above both the revised estimate and the ₹210.99 crore actually spent in 2024-25. A project scheme that undershot its estimate inside the year is a delivery question. The demand, by itself, does not give the reason for the revision.

## The Data Protection Board is an establishment provision, not a scheme

The demand note says the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 was enacted on 11 August 2023 and that Chapter V provides for a Data Protection Board. The budget provision is for salary and other establishment expenses. The figures:

| Column | Total (₹ crore) |
| --- | ---: |
| Budget 2025-26 | 5.00 |
| Revised 2025-26 | 2.00 |
| Budget 2026-27 | 10.00 |

The 2026-27 estimate is higher than the year before. It is still an establishment line. It does not disclose how many inquiries the Board will take, and the rules stagger the duties that would generate many of those inquiries.

## Do not fold in the Home Ministry figure

The Lok Sabha reply of 24 March 2026 says the Ministry of Home Affairs had released ₹132.93 crore under the Cyber Crime Prevention against Women and Children scheme for state and Union territory capacity: forensic-cum-training laboratories, junior cyber consultants, and training of police, prosecutors and judicial officers. Laboratories had been commissioned in 33 states and Union territories, and more than 24,600 people in those roles had been trained.

That is public capital and training for investigation. It is not inside Demand No. 27. A capex cycle for national incident response (CERT-In and the projects scheme) and a capex cycle for state police labs are both real. They are not the same cycle.

MeitY's whole demand for 2026-27 is shown at ₹21,632.96 crore net. The cyber lines above are a slice of that demand. They are not a private-sector spending forecast.
