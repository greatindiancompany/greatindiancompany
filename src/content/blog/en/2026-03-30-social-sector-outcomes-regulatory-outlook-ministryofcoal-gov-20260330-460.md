# The Rules Now Set for Nutrition Services, Pensions, and Rural Wages

Regulation of India’s social outcomes is not a single code. It is a wage statute that replaced another on a fixed date, a set of nutrition guidelines that tell states how an Anganwadi is run, and a pension rate the Centre has said it is not revising. A compliance calendar that still cites only the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act is citing a repealed law.

## Rural wages: duties that are now statutory

From 1 July 2026 the VB-G RAM G Act is in force in all rural areas and the 2005 Act stands repealed. The operative duties in the Ministry of Rural Development’s 11 May 2026 note are specific enough to audit.

The household guarantee is 125 days in a financial year. Employment is due within the prescribed time, or an unemployment allowance is due at not less than one-fourth of the notified wage for the first thirty days of the year and not less than one-half after that. Wages go by direct benefit transfer within fifteen days of muster-roll closure, weekly if sooner. Delay compensation is 0.05 percent of unpaid wages per day after the sixteenth day.

Attendance uses the National Mobile Monitoring System and face authentication, with an exception path when the technology fails. Workers with completed e-KYC keep their existing job card until a Gramin Rozgar Guarantee Card is issued. Pending e-KYC is not, by itself, a ground to deny work. Demand may be oral, on Form-6, or digital.

States may notify up to sixty days of pause in a year for sowing and harvesting. The pause is a notified instrument. An unnotified slowdown in demand registration is a different event, and the Act’s remedy for work not offered is the unemployment allowance.

Worksites must have drinking water, shade, and a first-aid kit. Five or more children under five trigger a paid childcare arrangement. Accident treatment, and ex-gratia via the Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana in death or permanent disability, are part of the worksite rules. Janata boards must show the sanctioned work, labour, expenditure, and material. Notified wages from 1 July 2026 are nowhere below ₹300. The national average notified wage is ₹327.4, up from ₹298.8.

Administrative expenditure is 9 percent, up from 6 percent. That is the rule for field capacity, not a discretionary overhead.

## Nutrition: committees, menus, and who delivers which service

Poshan 2.0 guidelines, issued under provisions of the National Food Security Act, 2013, place policy and planning with the Centre and day-to-day implementation with the states. Of the six Anganwadi services — supplementary nutrition, pre-school education, nutrition and health education, immunisation, health check-up, and referral — the last three are delivered through the National Health Mission and the public health system, not by the Anganwadi department alone. A rule that treats the Department of Women and Child Development as the sole implementer of immunisation is wrong on the ministry’s own page.

On 23 June 2026 the ministry issued guidelines for Anganwadi Management Committees, chaired by the Anganwadi supervisor. The 8 September 2026 Poshan Maah note says those committees are to be activated, to meet at least once during the month, and to sit alongside a displayed supplementary-nutrition menu board. Hot cooked meals are to be tested with the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India during the month. Take-home ration is tied to a registered beneficiary through facial recognition on the Poshan Tracker. Those are process rules. They do not rewrite the NFHS-6 anthropometry.

The published scheme guidelines approved Anganwadi services under Poshan 2.0 for 2021-22 to 2025-26. Whether a later notification extends that approval into 2026-27 is a document to confirm on the ministry site, not a fact this note supplies.

## Pensions: a rate the Centre has frozen in public

On 21 July 2026 the Minister of State for Rural Development told the Lok Sabha that central assistance under the National Social Assistance Programme pension schemes was last revised in 2012, and that no proposal to revise it is under consideration. States may top up between ₹50 and ₹5,700 a month. The programme remains aimed at below-poverty-line old age, widow, and disability pensions, plus the family benefit and Annapurna food support described in the ministry’s scheme notes. Evaluations cited in that reply recommend more adequate assistance and more regular payment. The regulatory fact is that the recommendation has not become a revised central rate.
