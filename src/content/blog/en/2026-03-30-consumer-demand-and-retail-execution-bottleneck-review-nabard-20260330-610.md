# Where retail execution actually gets stuck

Execution problems in Indian retail are written into rules and into the enterprise survey. They are not a vague shortage of “ease of doing business.”

## The platform cannot quietly become the seller

Paragraph 5.2.15.2 of the Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2020 allows 100% automatic FDI in marketplace e-commerce and bars FDI in inventory-based e-commerce. The operational test is specific. If more than 25% of a vendor’s purchases come from the marketplace or its group companies, that vendor’s inventory is deemed controlled, and the model is treated as inventory-based. A seller in which the marketplace has equity, or whose inventory the marketplace controls, cannot sell on that platform. The marketplace cannot require exclusivity and cannot influence the sale price, directly or indirectly. Services to vendors, including logistics and advertising, have to be at arm’s length and non-discriminatory.

That cluster is the bottleneck for any foreign-funded platform that also wants to steer catalogue, price, or a house brand. The annual proof is concrete: a statutory auditor’s report by 30 September for the preceding financial year. On 24 September 2026, the report for 2025-26 is days away, not a conceptual control.

## Multi-brand FDI stops at the state and at the website

Multi-brand retail is 51% and government-route. The circular makes it an enabling policy: outlets may be set up only in states and Union territories that have agreed, in cities above the population threshold or in other cities the state allows. Retail trading by e-commerce is not permissible for companies with FDI in multi-brand retail. A plan that assumes a national website plus foreign multi-brand equity is blocked by that sentence, whatever the demand story.

## Most shops are still outside the corporate file

ASUSE 2025 (January–December 2025) found 7.92 crore unincorporated non-agricultural establishments. Other retail trade was 26.49% of them. The share of registered establishments in the whole unincorporated sector was 37.50%. More than half of trading establishments used the internet for the business in the 2025 round. The bottleneck beside the FDI rules is formalisation: a large retail enterprise population can transact online while remaining unincorporated and, in most cases, unregistered in the survey’s sense.

Consumer-facing execution adds the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020: company form, an India-resident nodal contact, seller identity, and grievance handling. A marketplace that satisfies DPIIT and misses the consumer rules has not finished the job.
