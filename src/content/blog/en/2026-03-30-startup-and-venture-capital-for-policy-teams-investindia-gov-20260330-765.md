A ministry that reports one startup number will be asked questions that number cannot answer. The Parliament replies of early 2026 already contain the split. Publishing it on purpose is the policy task.

## Keep five series in five lines

Recognition stock. The latest exact national figure here is 2,40,092 entities as on 30 June 2026. The previous milestones are a stock that crossed 2.23 lakh on 31 March 2026 and 2,12,283 on 31 January 2026. After 4 February 2026 the stock is measured under G.S.R. 108(E), with a ₹200 crore turnover ceiling and a deep-tech category. Any brief that compares 2026 with 2024 should say the eligibility rule changed. DPIIT has not published a split between newly formed firms and older firms that became eligible only because the ceiling rose. That split is the missing line.

Self-reported direct jobs. The 13 February 2026 annexure uses those words. The total it supports is over 21.9 lakh as on 31 December 2025. PIB’s later line is more than 23.36 lakh as on 31 March 2026, with a 36.1 percent rise in the financial year against a 51.6 percent rise in recognitions. Leave the self-reported label on the later number until the method changes. Do not add Atal Tinkering Lab students, or AIM’s ₹2,750 crore outlay up to March 2028, into this line. Those are a skills programme reported in the 15 January 2026 PIB note.

Closures. MCA, via the 17 March 2026 PIB release, classed 6,789 recognised startups as dissolved or struck off as on 31 January 2026, out of 2,12,283. Inside the schemes the same reply records 17 closed among 1,382 Fund of Funds selections and 26 closed among 3,311 seed-fund selections. A dashboard that shows only the gross stock will be read as a survival rate. It is not one.

Scheme coverage. At the end of 2025-26 the first Fund of Funds had reached over 1,420 startups, the seed fund more than 3,400, and the guarantee more than 410 loans. Coverage is the policy outcome. The registry is the eligibility pool. Dividing 1,420 by a stock of about 2.23 lakh is a coverage ratio a reader can compute. It is not a failure of recognition. It is evidence that recognition was never the funding.

Geography. The 28 July 2026 annex puts 1,32,499 of 2,40,092 recognised startups in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, and Gujarat. PIB’s January note says around 50 percent of startups were emerging from Tier-II and Tier-III cities and does not publish the city classification. States’ Startup Ranking Framework bands exist in that note. The note does not name the current band for each state. A policy release should either print the latest framework result or stay silent. It should not borrow a sponsor’s state ranking.

## Women need two indicators

Directors and partners are one series: 1,02,054 of 2,12,283 recognised startups on 31 January 2026, more than 1.07 lakh and about 48 percent on 31 March, more than 1.15 lakh on 30 June. Capital is another. As on 31 January 2026, total investment by Fund of Funds portfolio managers was around ₹25,859 crore. Women-led investment, a split the reply publishes only from 2020, was around ₹2,995 crore. Seed approvals to women-led startups were around ₹294 crore within around ₹592 crore approved. Guaranteed loans to women-led startups were around ₹39 crore within around ₹925 crore guaranteed. A single “women in startups” percentage will describe the board series and hide the credit series.

## Run the fund of funds on the clock already written down

The Fund of Funds 2.0 guidelines require a departmental review at least every half year, an annual utilisation report with funds, companies, and net asset value, a third-party evaluation every five years from operationalisation, and a second implementation agency beside SIDBI. They cap government money at 50 percent of a fund’s corpus and send distributions, after up to 5 percent of returns for capacity building, back to the Consolidated Fund of India. A policy team’s next public act is to name the second agency or to say it is still unfilled, and to publish the first half-yearly review on the date the guideline implies. Another recognition milestone will not answer whether the ₹10,000 crore corpus has moved.
