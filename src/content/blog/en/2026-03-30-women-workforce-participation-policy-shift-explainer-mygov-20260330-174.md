# The survey redesign and the laws that frame women’s work

Two different kinds of shift are easy to tangle. One is a change in how India measures women’s work. The other is a change in the rules and schemes that apply when a woman is employed, or when unpaid care keeps her out of measured employment. They answer different questions.

## The measurement shift is dated and explicit

From January 2025 the National Statistics Office changed the Periodic Labour Force Survey. MoSPI’s note on the changes, and the endnote to the Annual Report for January–December 2025, set out the break in plain language. The survey period moved from the July–June agricultural year to the calendar year. Rural and urban areas are now both in a rotational panel with monthly visits. The sample rose from about 12,800 first-stage units and about 1.02 lakh households to 22,692 units and about 2.72 lakh households. The concepts of labour force participation, worker population ratio and unemployment are broadly unchanged. The sample and the estimation procedure are not. MoSPI says the 2025 annual estimates may not be strictly comparable with earlier annual reports or with calendar-year results it derived for 2022–2024.

That warning is the policy fact. A line that runs from the July 2017–June 2018 female labour force participation rate of 23.3 percent to the calendar-2025 rate of 40.0 percent stitches two designs into one slope. The older July–June series, published on 23 September 2024, is still the right history for 2017–18 through 2023–24: female usual-status participation at age 15 and above moved from 23.3 percent to 41.7 percent. The 2025 report then says participation was stable compared with 2024, at 40.0 percent for women and 79.1 percent for men. Use the sentence MoSPI wrote — stable, on the new design — rather than a single rising line across the break.

The redesign also changed what a “quarter” can mean. Through December 2024 the quarterly bulletin was urban only. From April–June 2025 it covers rural and urban India on the current weekly status. A rural quarterly series before that date is not in this product.

## The legal shift employers still operate under

The Ministry of Labour and Employment has said it is implementing the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, as amended in 2017. The amendment, which MoLE has described in Parliament and which PIB has carried, raised paid maternity leave from 12 weeks to 26 weeks, of which not more than eight weeks precede the expected date of delivery. The Act text limits that 26-week period to a woman with fewer than two surviving children, and sets 12 weeks where she has two or more. A woman who legally adopts a child below three months, and a commissioning mother, are entitled to 12 weeks from the date the child is handed over. Where the work allows it, the employer may agree to work from home after the maternity benefit. Every establishment with 50 or more employees is to have a creche, and the woman is allowed four visits a day, including the rest interval.

The same MoLE reply notes that the Code on Social Security, 2020, contains provisions for 26 weeks of paid maternity leave, a mandatory creche in establishments with 50 or more employees, and permission for women workers on night shifts with adequate safety measures. That is a statement about what the Code contains. The statute MoLE says it is implementing remains the 1961 Act as amended in 2017. An operator should follow the notified law and the establishment’s coverage, and should read the Code as the consolidation Parliament has enacted, with commencement checked on the Labour Ministry’s own notifications.

## The care scheme that sits beside the statute

Creche law at 50 employees covers a formal establishment. Most women workers are not in regular wage jobs. In usual status for 2025, 64.2 percent of women workers were self-employed and 18.2 percent were in regular wage or salaried employment. The Ministry of Women and Child Development’s answer on the public side is Mission Shakti. Its Samarthya sub-scheme includes Palna, the creche component, and Sakhi Niwas, accommodation for women who are in the workforce or aspire to join it, with a day-care provision for residents’ children.

PIB’s note on Palna says the erstwhile National Creche Scheme was reorganised and renamed in 2022. It is a centrally sponsored scheme: 60:40 between Centre and States, 90:10 for the North East and special category States, and 100 percent central funding for Union Territories without a legislature. Palna is for children aged 6 months to 6 years and is available to mothers irrespective of employment status. The Ministry’s stated aim for Anganwadi-cum-creches includes raising women’s workforce participation. As of March 2025, 11,395 Anganwadi-cum-creches across 34 States and Union Territories had been approved, against a stated target of 17,000 new units in 2024–25. That approval count is a dated administrative figure, not a count of centres open on a given day in 2026.

## What a reader should carry out of the shift

The statistical shift changes which number you may trend. The legal shift changes the cost and the process at a covered workplace: 26 weeks, a creche threshold at 50 employees, and a workplace committee under the sexual harassment law once the establishment has 10 employees. The scheme shift is the public creche and hostel layer for women the establishment threshold never reaches. Each one is real. None of them is a substitute for the others.
