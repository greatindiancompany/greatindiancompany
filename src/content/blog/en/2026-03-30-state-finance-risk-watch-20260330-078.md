# SDL spreads and the risks inside state borrowing

States finance a large part of the gross fiscal deficit by issuing state government securities, usually called state development loans or SDLs. Investors hold them as rupee bonds. The risk that shows up in the auction is the spread: the yield on the state security minus the yield on a central government security of similar maturity. A wider spread means the state pays more to borrow that week. That extra interest becomes committed expenditure in later budgets. The Reserve Bank's *State Finances: A Study of Budgets of 2025-26*, released on 23 January 2026, is the official place the spread, the issuance, and the maturity pile are published together.

## What the spread is, and what this brief will not pretend to update

Chart II.16 of the study is "Movement of SGS Yield and Spread." Table II.5 and Statement 21 are market borrowings. Statement 22 is the loan-by-loan record, which is where tenor, yield, and amount sit. This brief does not quote a basis-point spread. A spread from an older year, or from a single auction, is a different fact from the chart in the latest study. The chart is the number to read, and the next auction result is the number to update it with. Inventing a "current" spread would be a false precision.

The spread moves for more than one reason. Supply matters: how much paper states bring in a month. Liquidity and the tenor matter. So does the buyer's willingness to hold a less frequently traded bond than a central gilt. Fiscal indicators can matter as well, which is why the same study publishes the deficit, the debt stock, and guarantees beside the yield chart. A state with a quiet deficit ratio can still pay up in a crowded auction week. A state with a heavy guarantee book can still clear an auction tightly if the supply is small. The spread is a market price. It is one risk indicator, read next to the fiscal tables.

## The deficit the market is financing

The consolidated gross fiscal deficit was 3.3 per cent of GDP in 2024-25 and is budgeted at 3.3 per cent of GDP for 2025-26. The press release says the move above 3 per cent mainly reflects 50-year interest-free loans from the Centre for capital investment, over and above the normal net borrowing ceiling. Those loans are from the Union. They are a financing item inside the deficit. They are a different instrument from the SDL the market prices every fortnight. Statements 5 to 10 decompose the financing of the deficit for 2023-24 accounts, 2024-25 revised estimates, and 2025-26 budget estimates, and they show the share that is market borrowing. A reader who wants the SDL risk wants that share, for the state, in the year.

## Maturity and the interest bill

Statement 23 is the maturity profile of outstanding state government securities, and Statement 24 expresses it as a share of the total. Chart II.15 is the same profile at end-March. A cluster of redemptions forces a state back into the market to refinance, whatever that year's spread happens to be. Statement 13, interest payments, is where yesterday's spread becomes this year's committed spending. Outstanding liabilities are budgeted at 29.2 per cent of GDP at end-March 2026, and the Bank calls the post-pandemic stock elevated. The stock is the base on which the spread compounds.

## Guarantees sit beside the bond

Statement 28 is outstanding guarantees of state governments. A guarantee is a promise to pay if a state enterprise or a distribution company does not. It may stay outside the gross fiscal deficit until it is invoked. It is still a claim on future receipts, and it can arrive in a year when the spread is already wide. The Sixteenth Finance Commission asked states to bring off-budget borrowing onto the budget. The government's memorandum accepted the ordinary borrowing ceiling in principle and left the off-budget recommendation for separate examination. Until that examination changes the reporting, the guarantee statement is part of the risk file.

Chart II.17, indicators of debt sustainability, is the study's summary view of whether the debt path is stabilising. It belongs with the spread chart. A tight spread in one quarter does not rewrite a rising interest-to-receipt ratio.

## A practical watch list

For each state that matters to a portfolio or a project, keep four lines from the latest study and then update only the market line from auctions: the gross fiscal deficit and its financing share from market loans; the yield spread on Chart II.16 and the latest comparable auction; the share of securities falling due inside the next few years; and outstanding guarantees. The all-state average spread, even when you have read it off the chart, is a poor description of the state whose bond you hold.
