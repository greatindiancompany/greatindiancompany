# What an investor can underwrite from public agri series

Public agri numbers are investable only when the series matches the cash flow. The figures below are all official. They do not add up to one market size.

| What was published | Figure | What it actually measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Department of Commerce, 10 June 2026 | USD 52.55 billion in FY 2025-26 | Agricultural exports in that briefing, up from USD 51.12 billion |
| APEDA annual report, DGCIS | USD 51.91 billion in FY 2024-25 | A different official print of the previous year |
| APEDA scheduled products | USD 28.59 billion, 55 percent of that agri total | The 2024-25 slice APEDA monitors, not the whole export bill |
| PLI-supported product sales | ₹58,758 crore in FY 2019-20 to ₹1,08,854 crore in FY 2025-26 | Sales of approved applicants, not the food industry |
| PLI incentives disbursed | ₹3,271.44 crore up to June 2026 | Cash paid under a ₹10,900 crore outlay through FY 2026-27 |
| Cold-chain grants | ₹3,089.08 crore sanctioned, ₹2,540.89 crore released | One PMKSY component, 409 projects, as on 30 June 2026 |
| Completed cold storage under that scheme | 9.236 lakh tonnes | Capacity on completed projects, not national cold storage |
| Bank credit to agriculture | 17.0 percent year on year as on 31 July 2026 | Outstanding credit at 41 banks, about 95 percent of non-food credit |
| Ground-level credit target | ₹32.50 lakh crore for FY 2025-26 | A target, including ₹5 lakh crore for allied activities |
| NABARD short-term refinance | ₹1,83,152.53 crore in 2023-24 | Refinance to cooperatives and regional rural banks, not the target |
| Wheat procurement | 266 lakh tonnes in RMS 2024-25 | One crop, one season, about ₹0.61 lakh crore paid |

The Commerce figure and the APEDA figure for “agri exports” differ for FY 2024-25: USD 51.12 billion in the later speech, USD 51.91 billion in the annual report. Underwrite a shipment against the commodity line and the agency that certifies it. Buffalo meat on APEDA’s animal-products page was USD 5,098.54 million in FY 2025-26. Fresh fruits and vegetables on the companion page were USD 1,921.42 million. Rice in the Commerce briefing was USD 11.5 billion, marine products USD 8.4 billion, spices USD 4.3 billion. Those lines come from different tables. A model that subtracts buffalo meat from USD 52.55 billion and calls the residual “everything else” is mixing extracts.

A processing investment has a cleaner pair. PLI sales of ₹1,08,854 crore in FY 2025-26 are the supported book, against ₹9,207 crore invested and ₹3,271.44 crore of incentive already disbursed. The incentive is linked to eligible incremental sales, and the manufacturing chain has to be in India. It is not a grant for civil works. Civil works sit in the cold-chain and PMKSY grant, where the investor’s question is release versus sanction: ₹2,540.89 crore had been released against ₹3,089.08 crore sanctioned, and 98 of 409 projects were still ongoing. Capacity of 9.236 lakh tonnes is what the completed projects created under that scheme.

A credit investment has three layers and a rate. Outstanding agriculture credit was growing at 17.0 percent, after 7.3 percent a year earlier. The policy target for disbursement in FY 2025-26 was ₹32.50 lakh crore. The refinance that NABARD actually disbursed short-term in 2023-24 was ₹1,83,152.53 crore, of which ₹65,675.67 crore was concessional seasonal refinance. From 17 April 2025 the published refinance rate on crop loans to state cooperative banks and regional rural banks was 4.5 percent. Use the rate for a cost-of-funds note. Use the 2023-24 rupees only as the last full split, not as this year’s drawdown.

A procurement-linked mill should underwrite the agency, not the MSP list. Wheat at 266 lakh tonnes is an FCI season. Tur at 2.46 lakh tonnes by 25 March 2025 is a PM-AASHA season so far. The floors themselves, ₹2,441 for common paddy this kharif and ₹2,585 for wheat next rabi, are in the Cabinet releases. They cap the downside price. They do not promise the tonne.
