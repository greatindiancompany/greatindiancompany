# What an investor can underwrite from the public record

An investor reading India's agri value chain is usually handed one large rupee figure and asked to treat it as a market. The public record supports something narrower and more useful. It separates money the government has authorised, money banks have sanctioned, money private applicants have invested, and money paid to farm families. It does not support a valuation, a return, or a share of the consumer rupee.

## Underwrite the loan book with the label on the reply

The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund is the main credit instrument. From the August 2025 ministry note: a facility of Rs 1 lakh crore, an interest cap of 9 per cent, a 3 per cent subvention for at most seven years and only on the first Rs 2 crore, and a government-paid guarantee through CGTMSE, with NABSanrakshan added for FPO borrowers. The scheme is described as operational from 2020-21 to 2032-33. New loan disbursement was described as ending with 2025-26. Subvention on loans already made can continue inside the seven-year cap. An investor pricing a 2027 sanction should not assume the disbursement window is still open just because the scheme year runs to 2032-33. Check a reply newer than the July 2026 stock.

That July stock, as on 15 July 2026, is cumulative capital investment leveraged of Rs 1,61,537.22 crore, of which Rs 50,332.66 crore is farm-gate storage and logistics. The January 2026 stock is a different label: Rs 80,224.15 crore of loans sanctioned for 1,50,431 projects, mobilising investment of Rs 1,27,508 crore. Mobilised investment is larger than the sanctioned loan because a project has a borrower contribution. Using either figure as "the market size of agri infra" drops the label. The four largest state cells in the July storage-and-logistics annexure, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Gujarat, add to Rs 25,162.72 crore of the Rs 50,332.66 crore. Concentration in that column is an underwriting fact. It is not a ranking of farm economies.

Eligibility is part of the credit. Since 28 August 2024, standalone secondary processing is outside the fund. Integrated primary and secondary processing is inside. A company whose plant starts after the farm gate, with no primary step, does not have this subvention in its model.

The June 2025 composition is the last published mix: 30,202 custom hiring centres, 22,827 processing units, 15,982 warehouses, 3,703 sorting and grading units, 2,454 cold stores sanctioned for Rs 8,258 crore. Cold storage was not the bulk of the project count. A fund sold as a cold-chain book should be asked for a later mix.

## Underwrite the grant and the incentive as public toppers, not as the capex

The food-processing cold-chain grant, as of 30 June 2026, was Rs 3,089.08 crore sanctioned and Rs 2,540.89 crore released, against 409 projects of which 311 were complete, creating 9.236 lakh metric tonnes. The rate is 35 or 50 per cent of eligible cost, capped at Rs 10 crore for most components. The investor's asset is the project cost. The grant is a fraction of it, and the national cold-storage stock cited beside it, 402.87 lakh metric tonnes on 31 January 2026, shows how much capacity already existed outside this scheme.

The production-linked incentive had, as on 31 December 2025, 168 approved applicants who had invested about Rs 9,207 crore and created around 35 lakh metric tonnes a year of processing and preservation capacity. Incentive disbursed was Rs 2,714.79 crore, against a scheme outlay of Rs 10,900 crore for 2021-22 to 2026-27. Three numbers, three meanings: outlay, private investment, public payout. Sixty-nine applicants were MSMEs. The incentive is payable when the whole manufacturing chain, including primary processing, is in India, and the window the reply states runs to 2026-27. That is a short remaining life for a new applicant, and a defined tail for one already approved.

## Do not underwrite the transfer or the support price as project equity

PM-KISAN, continued to 2030-31 with an outlay of Rs 3.15 lakh crore, had transferred more than Rs 4.47 lakh crore through 23 instalments by the Cabinet note of 31 July 2026. The 23rd instalment was over Rs 18,984 crore to more than 9.49 crore farmers. The Cabinet note reports that farmers use the Rs 6,000 a year for seed, fertiliser, and similar costs, and it cites a DMEO finding that more than 92 per cent said they used it for agricultural activity. That is household cash. It does not capitalise a warehouse, and it does not belong in an enterprise value.

The FPO equity grant is capped at Rs 15 lakh. Guarantee cover issued by 31 December 2025 was Rs 662.71 crore across 2,671 organisations, against a per-organisation ceiling of Rs 2 crore. Those are real credit enhancements. They are also small relative to a growth-equity cheque. MSP is a price for a season, strongest as a cash-flow assumption where procurement has a history. That history, in the May 2026 release, is dominated by paddy: 8,418 of 8,746 lakh metric tonnes of kharif procurement over 2014-15 to 2025-26.

Exports are a demand line with a published composition, not a multiple. Agri exports were USD 51,913 million in 2024-25. Inside APEDA's 2025-26 animal-product page, buffalo meat is over 81.75 per cent of a USD 6,236.79 million total. An investor in that basket is underwriting a concentrated meat trade. The page names the markets. It does not name a buyer who cannot switch.
