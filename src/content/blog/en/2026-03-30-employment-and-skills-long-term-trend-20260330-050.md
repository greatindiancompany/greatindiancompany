India's comparable annual labour-force series, on one design, runs for seven July–June years: 2017–18 through 2023–24. The National Sample Survey Office published that span in the Periodic Labour Force Survey press note of 23 September 2024. After that, the office changed the survey.

The rates below are for persons aged 15 and above, in usual status. Usual status is the activity that took the major part of the previous 365 days, plus subsidiary economic activity of 30 days or more.

## The seven annual rounds

All-India, persons aged 15 and above, usual status:

| July–June year | Participation | Employment | Unemployment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2017–18 | 49.8% | 46.8% | 6.0% |
| 2018–19 | 50.2% | 47.3% | 5.8% |
| 2019–20 | 53.5% | 50.9% | 4.8% |
| 2020–21 | 54.9% | 52.6% | 4.2% |
| 2021–22 | 55.2% | 52.9% | 4.1% |
| 2022–23 | 57.9% | 56.0% | 3.2% |
| 2023–24 | 60.1% | 58.2% | 3.2% |

Participation is the labour force participation rate. Employment is the worker population ratio. Unemployment is the unemployment rate, which uses the labour force as its denominator.

The last step is the one to read carefully. From 2022–23 to 2023–24, participation rose by 2.2 percentage points and employment by 2.2 percentage points. Unemployment for all persons stayed at 3.2%. Rural unemployment moved from 2.4% to 2.5%. Urban unemployment moved from 5.4% to 5.1%. The long decline and the flat last year are both in the table.

Over the full span, rural unemployment fell from 5.3% to 2.5%, and urban unemployment from 7.7% to 5.1%. Rural participation rose from 50.7% to 63.7%. Urban participation rose from 47.6% to 52.0%.

## Who produced the rise in participation

Female participation rose from 23.3% in 2017–18 to 41.7% in 2023–24. Male participation rose from 75.8% to 78.8%, with a dip to 75.5% in 2018–19 in between. Female employment rose from 22.0% to 40.3%. Male employment rose from 71.2% to 76.3%.

Female unemployment fell from 5.6% to 3.2% over the seven years, and rose from 2.9% to 3.2% in the last year. Male unemployment fell from 6.1% to 3.2%, and from 3.3% to 3.2% in the last year. The household reading of the female series, including unpaid helpers, is in the [participation brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-impact-on-households-20260330-040).

## The weekly-status path in the same notes

Current weekly status, the seven days before the interview, starts higher and falls further. All-India unemployment on that status was 8.7% in 2017–18, 8.7% in 2018–19, 8.8% in 2019–20, 7.5% in 2020–21, 6.6% in 2021–22, 5.1% in 2022–23, and 4.9% in 2023–24. Participation on the weekly status rose from 48.4% to 56.4%. Employment rose from 44.1% to 53.7%.

The usual-status unemployment rate and the weekly-status unemployment rate fell together. They never described the same week of a person's life. In 2023–24 the two all-India rates were 3.2% and 4.9%.

## Where the annual series stops being one series

The calendar-year note of 9 April 2025 rebuilds 2023 and 2024 from the four quarters of each January–December year. On usual status, all-India participation was 59.8% in calendar 2023 and 59.6% in calendar 2024. Unemployment was 3.1% and then 3.2%. Those are overlapping cousins of the July–June rates, not an eighth year glued on the end.

From January 2025 the survey methodology was modified to produce monthly and quarterly estimates for the country. The Ministry of Labour and Employment, in a Lok Sabha reply on 1 December 2025, said changes in the monthly ratios are expected because of the higher frequency and the season, and do not necessarily reflect a lasting trend. The August 2026 monthly bulletin is the seventeenth in that new series. Its 5.0% weekly-status unemployment rate is the current level of the new print. It is not the next point on the 6.0%–to–3.2% usual-status line.

The month itself is read in the [August 2026 bulletin brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-what-changed-20260330-010).
