## Ninety days from this update

This page was updated on 24 September 2026. Ninety days from that date runs to 23 December 2026. The list below is a reading order for that window. It is not a calendar of announcements the government has not made. If a document is unchanged since the dates cited here, the honest result is “no newer official figure,” which is a result.

## Cover and eligibility

Open the National Health Authority’s PM-JAY about-page and write down the design rules before any dashboard number: ₹5 lakh a year per family, cashless listed hospitalization, no family-size cap, pre-existing conditions from day one, three days before admission, 15 days after, portability, public hospitals paid at par with private ones. Then open the senior-citizen FAQ. Confirm that cover for people aged 70 and above is irrespective of income, that ₹5 lakh is shared by the 70-plus members of a family, and that an already covered family does not become a ₹10 lakh family for every member.

Note the household-count conflict and leave it unresolved until a newer Authority document picks one figure. The about-page says over 12 crore families, about 55 crore people. A November 2021 empanelment guideline says about 10.74 crore households. Do not average them in a board note.

## Primary care

Read the ministry reply of 25 March 2025: 1,76,573 Ayushman Arogya Mandirs operationalised till February 2025; 107.10 crore hypertension screenings; 94.56 crore diabetes screenings; 5.06 crore wellness sessions as on 28 February 2025. Then read the Press Information Bureau figures of 1,81,873 centres as on 30 November 2025 and 1,82,944 as on 31 December 2025. If the mandir portal shows a newer total in this ninety-day window, record the date and the label. Keep screenings in a column that does not say “patients.”

## Capital

Read the ministry’s PM-ABHIM update: ₹64,180 crore for 2021–22 to 2025–26, and approvals of ₹33,081.82 crore for 10,609 building-less mandirs, 5,456 urban mandirs, 2,151 block public health units, 744 district laboratories, and 621 critical care blocks. The scheme period in that text ended with FY 2025–26. In this window, look for a budget document that either continues those lines or replaces them. Absence of a new outlay is not permission to reuse ₹64,180 crore.

## One state and one district

For the state where you operate, record trust, insurance, or mixed mode, and who pays the hospital. Record any cover above ₹5 lakh and who funds it. Record packages reserved for public hospitals. The Authority’s about-page lists these as state flexibilities. A mode table from an older annual report is a historical exhibit.

Then walk one district on paper. Name the mandir, the primary health centre that is the referral point, the district hospital, and at least one empanelled private hospital. Ask whether a patient screened for diabetes has a public secondary-care destination, and whether a listed admission would be paid by the state agency or an insurer. That single district will expose more than another national total. The [execution note](/blog/healthcare-access-execution-bottleneck-review-education-gov-20260330-593) is the longer version of this walk.

## Identity and standards

Confirm that an Ayushman Bharat Health Account, the 14-digit identifier from the digital mission launched in September 2021, is not the same lookup as PM-JAY eligibility. Confirm what “IPHS compliant” means on the standards portal: a score of 80 percent or higher. A photograph of a new block is not that score.
