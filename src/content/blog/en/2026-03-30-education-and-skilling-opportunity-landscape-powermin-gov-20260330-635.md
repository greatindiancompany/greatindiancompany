## Openings that have a written home

The opportunities below are programme features already described by the Cabinet or by Parliament. They are not market-size estimates.

**Use a school or college building after hours.** The 3 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply says PMKVY, in line with NEP 2020, supports skilling for school and college dropouts, out-of-school children, and unemployed youth aged 15 to 45, in coordination with the Department of School Education and Literacy. School infrastructure has been used as skill hubs beyond regular hours and on non-working days. The 7 February 2025 Cabinet note lists skill hubs in premier and public institutions, including IITs, NITs, Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas, Kendriya Vidyalayas, Sainik Schools, Eklavya Model Residential Schools, and PM SHRI schools.

**Put labs where residential schools already are.** The same Lok Sabha reply describes 1,200 vocational skill labs, aligned with the CBSE curriculum, in 400 Navodaya schools and 200 Eklavya schools, two labs and two trained skill teachers in each selected school, funded through the National Skill Development Fund. Eklavya schools serve Scheduled Tribe students, including in remote areas. The opening is vocational teaching inside those schools, not a new national school network.

**Count skill courses toward a degree, up to a limit.** The University Grants Commission procedure cited on 10 August 2026 allows up to 50% of programme credits to come from skill-based courses. The National Credit Framework is meant to give equivalence among skill, general, and experiential learning. AISHE 2023-24, cited that day, says more than 700 universities and over 7,500 affiliated colleges have adopted it.

**Move from an ITI into a diploma.** Under the AICTE Approval Process Handbook 2023-27, as described in that reply, Class 10 plus a two-year ITI can make a student eligible for the second year of an engineering and technology diploma. A two-year undergraduate diploma in engineering at NCrF level 5.0 can support lateral entry to the third year.

**Short credentials and community courses.** The February 2025 note describes micro-credentials and occupational-standards courses of 7.5 to 30 hours, and Jan Shikshan Sansthan training at ages 15 to 45 for people who need a low-cost course near home, including links with PM JANMAN and ULLAS. Certifications under the Skill India Programme are mapped to the National Skills Qualifications Framework and integrated with DigiLocker and the credit framework.

**Courses in more than one language.** The Cabinet note says more than 600 trainee and trainer handbooks have been translated into eight regional languages. It does not name the eight languages in the text used here.

None of these openings comes with a published placement rate or a private revenue pool. An operator or a college still has to meet the scheme rule that creates the opening.
