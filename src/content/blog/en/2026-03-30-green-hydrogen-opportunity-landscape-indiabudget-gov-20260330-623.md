# Where a Company Can Enter India’s Green Hydrogen Stack

Opportunity, in this record, is a door with a scheme document behind it. A generic “hydrogen economy” pitch does not name a door. These do.

## Make the machine, or run it for someone else

Electrolyser manufacturing is the SIGHT Component I door. On 17 June 2026, Minister Pralhad Joshi said financial incentives had been awarded to 15 companies to establish 3,000 MW a year of indigenous manufacturing capacity. The guidelines sit on MNRE’s [schemes list](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/), including the 28 June 2023 Component I text and the 16 March 2024 Tranche II text. A firm that missed the award can still supply components, testing, or operations. On 24 July 2026 MNRE published a model agreement for electrolyser-as-a-service. That is a second door: operating the machine for a party that holds the offtake. The mission page says competitive bids are to use equipment the government has approved. A service offer that ignores that clause is aimed at the wrong tender.

## Sell ammonia to fertiliser plants

The November 2025 PIB note records an auction for 7.24 lakh tonnes a year of green ammonia at ₹55.75 per kg. The June 2026 speech records agreements for 6.7 lakh tonnes a year to 11 fertiliser plants. The quantities differ, and both are official. The customer type does not differ. The product is ammonia for fertiliser feedstock, which is the derivative the [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) names when it discusses domestic demand. A developer who cannot make ammonia is not in this contract. Mode-2A guidelines, dated 16 January 2024 on the schemes list, are the instrument to read.

## Build, own, and operate at a refinery

The 11 August 2026 reply lists 30 kilo-tonnes a year under Mode-2B: Panipat 10, Bina 5, Vizag 5, Numaligarh 10. Projects are build-own-operate, with the developer’s own investment. The opportunity suits an operator who can site a plant at one of those refineries. It suits a spot trader less well, because the offtaker and the location are named.

## Use port land and common infrastructure

The 5 August 2026 Lok Sabha annex is the land door. Kandla has allotted 3,400 acres on a 30-year lease. Paradip’s jetty, at ₹797.17 crore and 4.0 million tonnes a year of handling capacity, is common infrastructure for manufacturers in the hinterland, approved for public-private partnership. Tuticorin’s annex lists specific allotments with expected capacities of 0.1, 0.22, and 0.5 million tonnes a year. A company on that land is in the hub geography Parliament described. A company that is merely “looking at the coast” should not assume the berth.

## Join a steel or mobility pilot, or fit a smaller window

Steel is pilot-sized. The June speech sanctions ₹84 crore for projects testing 100 percent hydrogen injection. The November 2025 note says five steel pilots were started with public and private producers. The opportunity is a trial partner, a materials supplier, or a safety case.

Road transport is the 12 awarded pilots in the 29 July 2026 annex: named consortia, named routes, viability-gap funding. A component supplier can look for a seat in that list. A national fleet order is not what the answer describes.

Smaller doors are explicit. The minister said the Mission had allocated ₹100 crore for startups and that a first list of nine startups was being approved with ₹22 crore. He cited a research roadmap of ₹113 crore supporting 21 projects, beside the mission page’s ₹400 crore research component. Testing is related: ₹193.35 crore across seven national testing facilities, in the same speech. Skills are adjacent: a call for skilling, up-skilling, and re-skilling dated 23 March 2026 on the schemes list. A lab, a startup, or a training firm that fits those calls has a narrower path than a production target.

Great Indian Company lists doors that have a dated government document. A pitch that cannot name which door it is using is not yet a project in this market.
