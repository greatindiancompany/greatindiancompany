# What India actually publishes on MSME productivity

A global benchmark needs the same question asked of two countries. India's official MSME pages currently answer at least three different questions. Putting them in one cross-country cell produces a number nobody published.

## Three official objects

**Contribution shares.** The Press Information Bureau's 26 June 2026 MSME note, citing January 2026 data, says the sector contributes about 31.1 percent of GDP, 35.4 percent of manufacturing output, and 48.58 percent of exports. A 14 May 2026 PIB note uses the same three shares. These are weights in the economy. They do not say how much value one worker adds.

**Registration stock.** The 14 May 2026 note says that as of March 2026 more than 7.9 crore enterprises were registered, 4.72 crore on Udyam and 3.21 crore on the Udyam Assist Platform. The 26 June 2026 note says the combined registrations had crossed 8.7 crore by June 2026. A registration is an identity. It becomes a productivity statistic only after output and labour are measured for that same set of firms.

**Labour productivity in a defined survey.** ASUSE 2025, released 24 March 2026, estimates gross value added per worker in unincorporated non-agricultural manufacturing, trade, and other services at ₹1,56,539 in current prices, up from ₹1,49,742 in ASUSE 2023-24. MoSPI states the increase as 4.54 percent. The survey covers proprietorships, partnerships other than limited liability partnerships, co-operatives, societies, and trusts. It excludes construction. About 12.81 crore workers were estimated in the 2025 round.

Employment language in the two PIB notes does not match the survey, and the notes do not match each other. The May note speaks of livelihoods for about 32.8 crore people. The June note says more than 38.9 crore people are employed in MSMEs. Those sentences can be quoted only with their dates. They are not a revision log of ASUSE.

## What a fair comparison would require

A foreign small-firm productivity ratio can sit next to ₹1,56,539 only when the foreign figure is also value added per worker, in a stated price basis, for unincorporated non-agricultural establishments outside construction, over a stated year. If the foreign figure is incorporated manufacturing, or output per hour, or a purchasing-power parity level, it answers another question.

India does publish instruments that other countries also use, and those can be compared as design choices. CGTMSE, set up by the Ministry and SIDBI, guarantees collateral-free credit to micro and small enterprises, with a ceiling of ₹10 crore on guarantees approved on or after 1 April 2025. The MSMED Act sets a statutory outer limit of 45 days for agreed payment to micro and small suppliers. ZED certifies manufacturing quality and environmental practice for Udyam-registered manufacturing MSMEs. Comparing those designs is a policy comparison. It is not a productivity ranking.

## The useful international sentence

India's published small-enterprise labour productivity, on the only nationwide establishment survey aimed at this part of the economy, rose modestly in current prices between the 2023-24 and 2025 ASUSE rounds, while establishment counts and internet use rose faster. Contribution to GDP, exports, and manufacturing is a second sentence. The width of Udyam is a third. A brief that collapses the three into "India's MSME productivity versus the world" is not using the official record.
