# Two State Maps: 2011 Urban Share and Smart Cities Completion

## The maps measure different objects

A state can be slightly urban on the Census definition and still have finished every Smart Cities project on its selected list. A highly urban state can still have a selected city with projects open. The first map is population. The second is a closed mission’s project ledger. MoHUA does not publish a single index that folds them together, and this brief does not invent one.

## Map one: Census 2011, still the official distribution

The Ministry of Home Affairs placed Census 2011 rural-urban counts in Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 6458, answered on 7 May 2013. Urban share of population, as printed there:

- Himachal Pradesh 10.0 percent, Bihar 11.3 percent, Odisha 16.7 percent.
- Uttar Pradesh 22.3 percent, Rajasthan 24.9 percent, Madhya Pradesh 27.6 percent.
- West Bengal 31.9 percent, close to the national 31.2 percent in that reply’s rounding.
- Karnataka 38.7 percent, Gujarat 42.6 percent, Maharashtra 45.2 percent.
- Kerala 47.7 percent, Tamil Nadu 48.4 percent, Mizoram 52.1 percent, Goa 62.2 percent.
- Chandigarh 97.3 percent and the National Capital Territory of Delhi 97.5 percent.

Andhra Pradesh is printed at 33.4 percent in that table. The Census frame predates the formation of Telangana in 2014, so the figure is undivided Andhra Pradesh. Using it as today’s Andhra share is a mistake the table itself cannot correct.

MoHUA’s urban-growth page adds the town composition behind the national 31.16 percent: 2,774 more towns over the decade, of which 2,532 were census towns. States that gained census towns without a matching gain in municipal bodies will look more urban in 2011 than their statutory-town map suggests. Kerala’s jump from one million-plus urban agglomeration to seven is the clearest published example of urban population gathering outside a single metro story.

PMAY-Urban is implemented through urban local bodies, and the December 2024 PIB note tied the first scheme’s sanctions to 4,618 of them. A state with a low 2011 urban share can still have a large absolute urban population. Uttar Pradesh’s 22.3 percent sat on a state population, in that same reply, of 199,812,341. Share and headcount answer different operating questions.

## Map two: project completion inside the 100 selected cities

The annex to Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 3148, answered on 6 August 2026, reports Smart Cities projects as on 31 March 2026. Completion is city-wise, not a state urbanisation rate. Cities reported at 100 percent on that annex include Indore (231 of 231 projects), Surat (87 of 87), Pune (55 of 55), Bhopal (82 of 82), and Varanasi (117 of 117).

The lower end of the same annex is also specific. Silvassa is printed at 14 of 29 projects, 48 percent. Port Blair is printed at 9 of 18, 50 percent. Kavaratti is printed at 8 of 12, 67 percent. Amaravati is printed at 14 of 19, 74 percent. Aizawl is printed at 33 of 45, 73 percent. Shillong is printed at 10 of 13, 77 percent. Itanagar is printed at 25 of 32, 78 percent. Faridabad is printed at 35 of 44, 80 percent.

The national line in the minister’s answer is 7,790 of 8,064 projects. A state average would hide Silvassa and Port Blair, and it would also hide that several large cities are already at the top of the ledger. The mission is financially closed. These gaps are a completion problem on a fixed list, not a queue for a new selection round.

## How to use the two maps

Site a plant or a branch against Map one when the question is how urban the Census says the state was, and say the year. Site a municipal-technology or street project against Map two when the question is whether that selected city still had open Smart Cities projects on 31 March 2026. For water, use the AMRUT city list and the state water action plan, which is a third map and is not reproduced here.
