# How a founder should read a PLI scheme

Founders lose months by treating India's manufacturing policy as one form. It is a stack. The documents below tell you what the stack is. They do not tell you that your company qualifies. Eligibility is in the scheme guideline of the ministry that runs your product, and that guideline is not reproduced here.

## Start with the product, then the ministry

The sector annexure published on 21 July 2026 names the ministry next to each PLI line ([PIB PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008)). White goods is the line marked DPIIT. Electronics and IT hardware are MeitY. Drugs, bulk drugs, and medical devices are the Department of Pharmaceuticals. Automobiles and ACC batteries are Heavy Industries. Solar modules are MNRE. If your product is not on that list, a Make in India sub-sector in DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 may still be a facilitation conversation. It is not automatically a PLI claim. Toys, furniture, fisheries, and ethanol appear in that sub-sector list. They are not separate rows in the PLI annexure. The report is the [DPIIT Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/3d9c9c2daeefb97bb9ce964370938b71.pdf).

The 28 July 2026 reply says 892 applications had been approved across 14 sectors as on 31 March 2026 ([Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf)). That number is not a count of open seats, and it is not a count of factories already in commercial production. The only company counts in the papers used here are narrower: 59 companies for incremental electronics production and 72 companies for incremental auto and auto-component production, in FY 2025–26 up to December 2025 ([PIB PRID 2246089](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246089)).

## Do not put the incentive in the first year's revenue

The same 28 July reply says ₹35,354 crore of incentives had been disbursed as on 31 March 2026, against an approved outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore, while applicants had reported investment over ₹2.40 lakh crore and sales over ₹22.66 lakh crore. The incentive is small next to reported sales, and most of the outlay was still unpaid at that date. A model that books the outlay, or even a pro-rata share of it, as contracted revenue is not reading the reply. Ask the scheme text which year's incremental sales qualify, what the claim calendar is, and what happens if you miss a threshold. Those answers are not in the parliamentary summary.

If you sell to the central government, read the procurement order as well. DPIIT's annual report says the Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) Order was last amended on 19 July 2024. Class-I local content is at least 50 percent. Class-II is at least 20 percent and under 50 percent. The report says a PLI manufacturer can be treated as a deemed Class-II supplier for that item unless Class-I content is already met, with a proviso you should read in the order. A domestic factory can still be ineligible for a tender.

## Land is a separate workstream

Cabinet approval of a corridor node is not a lease. The 30 August 2024 note describes Dholera, AURIC, Greater Noida, and Vikram Udyogpuri as SPVs with state partners, and a further set of approved sites ([PIB PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136)). Check the India Industrial Land Bank and the SPV before you announce a site. Quality control orders, for products in DPIIT's domain, are a third workstream: the annual report's count of 83 orders is not a certificate that your product is covered or exempt.

If a shareholder is from a country that shares a land border with India, read the 2026 paragraphs in the Lok Sabha reply before you take the money. Specified manufacturing activities are described as having a 60-day process, with Indian majority control. That is a condition, not a slogan.
