The next Monetary Policy Committee meeting is 5 to 7 October 2026. The useful preparation is not a guess about the vote. It is a short list of documents and a short list of series the August resolution and minutes already said would decide whether 5.25 per cent is still the right repo rate.

## Read these, in this order

Start with the resolution of 5 August 2026. It is the committee’s text: repo rate 5.25 per cent, standing deposit facility 5.00 per cent, marginal standing facility and Bank Rate 5.50 per cent, stance neutral, growth 6.7 per cent for 2026-27, CPI inflation 5.0 per cent, third-quarter inflation 5.9 per cent. Anything said about the meeting in a market note has to match that page.

Read the Governor’s statement the same day for the operating detail the resolution summarises: the 125 basis point easing cycle from February 2025, the liquidity surplus, the call rate, and the external balances. Then read the minutes of 19 August. Section 45ZL requires them on the fourteenth day, and they are where a unanimous vote splits into six tests. Gupta writes that further easing does not look available and that a hike may come into view. Malhotra writes that a supply shock earns a rate response when it spreads, unanchors expectations, or persists.

Skim the August Bulletin’s “State of the Economy” for the data that arrived after the vote: July CPI, the liquidity surplus through 21 August, and the transmission table. Keep the April 2026 Monetary Policy Report for the framework and the operating procedure. The October report, when it is published, replaces it as the half-yearly stocktake. The meeting calendar on the Reserve Bank’s monetary policy page confirms the later dates: 2 to 4 December 2026, and 3 to 5 February 2027.

Separate the committee’s vote from the other announcements of 5 August. Draft urban co-operative bank licensing and a harmonised framework for interest rates on advances are Reserve Bank proposals. They are not the repo decision.

## The checks the August text already named

**Inflation against the path, not against last month’s headline.** The resolution’s year is 5.0 per cent, with the second quarter at 4.7 per cent and the third at 5.9 per cent. July, in the Bulletin, was 4.45 per cent. The question for October is whether August and September are walking toward that third-quarter peak or past it. The target remains 4 per cent, inside a 2 to 6 per cent band, through March 2031. A print inside the band can still be a problem if it is stuck near 6.

**Composition.** The committee’s reason for waiting is that the rise is food and fuel. Core inflation was 3.9 per cent in July. Core excluding precious metals was 2.7 per cent, up from 2.5 per cent in June. Restaurant inflation had already reached 6.9 per cent in June. Bhattacharyya’s June diffusion count, 69 per cent of the weighted basket at or below 4 per cent, is the kind of breadth measure the minutes say to watch. If that share falls and core excluding precious metals keeps rising, the “not broad-based” sentence in the resolution is out of date.

**Oil against the assumption used in the forecast.** Gupta’s statement puts the August projection on oil near US$90 a barrel, after June had used US$95 and April US$85. The Indian basket was US$89.7 in August up to the 20th, after US$83.22 in June and US$106.23 in May. A reader tracking one price can track the forecast.

**The monsoon, because it is the food-price risk.** As on 3 August, rainfall was about 12 per cent below the long-period average and deficient in 15 of 36 subdivisions. Reservoirs were at 44.4 per cent of capacity on 30 July, against a decadal average of 47.7 per cent and 69.3 per cent a year earlier. Rice and wheat stocks were 4.8 times and 1.9 times buffer norms on 16 July. October will know whether August and September rain closed that gap.

**The call rate, because the corridor is only real if the market rate sits near the repo.** The Bulletin’s weighted average call rate averaged 5.13 per cent in August up to the 21st, below the 5.25 per cent repo, while average daily liquidity absorption rose to ₹3.48 lakh crore. The Governor’s objective is alignment with the repo rate. A call rate stuck well below 5.25 per cent means surplus liquidity is doing work the policy rate is not.

**Lending and deposit rates, because the easing cycle has stopped transmitting smoothly.** From February 2025 to June 2026 the repo rate fell 125 basis points, the external benchmark rate fell 125, and the fresh-loan weighted average fell 80. In June, with the repo rate unchanged, fresh deposit rates rose 16 basis points and fresh lending rates rose 2. An operator repricing a loan or a deposit before October should look at that monthly row, not only at the 125 basis point cumulative cut.

## What not to do with the hold

A neutral stance is not a forecast that October is another hold. Bhattacharyya says a pause does not mean an extended pause. Gupta says a hike may emerge and that further cuts do not look available. Malhotra says he wants the inflation path to be clearer before any recalibration, and that evidence of a spreading shock may require tightening. Treat those as the committee’s stated reaction, and wait for the October resolution to see which description the vote matches.
