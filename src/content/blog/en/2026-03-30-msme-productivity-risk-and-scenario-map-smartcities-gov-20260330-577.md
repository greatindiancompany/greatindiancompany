# A risk map for MSME productivity claims

Productivity claims in this sector fail in a few repeatable ways. Each way below is tied to a rule or a published figure. None of them is a probability.

## Reclassification after a good year

From 1 April 2025 the ceilings are higher, and the test is still composite. Investment above ₹2.5 crore, ₹25 crore, or ₹125 crore, or turnover above ₹10 crore, ₹100 crore, or ₹500 crore, moves the enterprise to the next category. A downshift requires both investment and turnover to fall below the ceiling. Scenario: a micro unit buys equipment that crosses ₹2.5 crore of plant and machinery while turnover is still under ₹10 crore. It is no longer micro. Non-tax benefits of the earlier category can continue for three years, on the Press Information Bureau's 14 May 2026 description. Scheme maps that are micro-only need a fresh check on day one of the new category.

## Guarantee assumed for the wrong firm

CGTMSE, the Ministry and SIDBI guarantee, covers micro and small enterprises. The ceiling of ₹10 crore applies to guarantees approved on or after 1 April 2025. Scenario: a teaser financial model shows medium-enterprise capex "covered by CGTMSE." That cover is outside the scheme. A second scenario: the model treats the ₹3.77 lakh crore of guarantees approved from 1 January to 30 November 2025 as money already spent on machines. Approval is not disbursement, and disbursement is not output.

## Cash locked past the statutory clock

Section 15 of the MSMED Act gives micro and small suppliers 45 days as the outer written payment period, and fifteen days where nothing is written. Scenario: a productivity gain is real on the shop floor and invisible in cash because the buyer is on a 90-day habit. Samadhaan, as on June 2026, had 2,56,892 applications claiming ₹55,244.29 crore, with 58,148 cases disposed by facilitation councils. A filing is not a recovery. The risk is the gap between those two counts, plus the rule that registration must pre-date the invoice.

## Current prices read as real output

ASUSE reports gross value added up 10.87 percent and gross value added per worker up 4.54 percent, both at current prices, between the 2023-24 and 2025 rounds. Scenario: a strategy deck calls 10.87 percent "productivity growth." The per-worker figure is the productivity line, it is nominal, and the two survey calendars do not line up (October 2023–September 2024 against January–December 2025). A claim about volume needs a deflator and a note on the break.

## Registers mixed together

Scenario: "7.9 crore MSMEs produce ₹1,56,539 each." The first number is a March 2026 registration stock of Udyam plus Udyam Assist, from the 14 May 2026 note. The second is ASUSE gross value added per worker in the unincorporated survey. By June 2026 the Ministry's later note put registrations above 8.7 crore. Multiplying either stock by the survey ratio invents a gross-value-added total. Contribution shares — about 31.1 percent of GDP, 35.4 percent of manufacturing output, 48.58 percent of exports in the January 2026 data cited on 26 June 2026 — are a third object. They do not convert into the survey ratio.

## Enrolment read as a finished upgrade

Scenario: a ZED registration is reported as a certified process upgrade. As on May 2026 the Ministry counted more than 93.61 lakh ZED registrations and more than 6.68 lakh certifications. The risk is reporting the first number as the second.
