# What moved on India's digital public rails from April to August 2026

This note covers the calendar quarter that was still open on 24 September 2026, using the last complete official months. September statistics were not out when this page was updated. Nothing here is a forecast.

The useful question is narrower than "is digital India growing?" Several rails published fresh cuts between April and August. They did not all move the same way.

## Payments: more transactions, not a straight rise in value

NPCI's UPI product statistics, which count volume in millions and value in crore rupees, show this path for 2026:

| Month | Banks live | Volume (million) | Value (₹ crore) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| April | 713 | 22,346.80 | 29,02,988.05 |
| May | 720 | 23,201.93 | 29,90,424.21 |
| June | 731 | 22,716.07 | 28,92,138.67 |
| July | 741 | 23,658.35 | 29,87,880.49 |
| August | 752 | 24,508.96 | 29,82,355.95 |

Two facts sit next to each other. August is the high month for volume in this window, at 24,508.96 million transactions, and the bank count rose from 713 to 752. August is not the high month for value. May (₹29,90,424.21 crore) and July (₹29,87,880.49 crore) are both above August. June is the soft month on both volume and value inside this five-month stretch.

A reader who only hears "record volume" will miss the value dip from July to August. NPCI's own note on the series says that, from August 2018 onward, the table excludes transactions that debit and credit the same account. The series is the right one to follow month to month. It is not a measure of merchant margins or of any app's market share.

A separate government snapshot, given to Parliament and released by the Press Information Bureau on 29 July 2026, described UPI as serving more than 55.49 crore individuals and 6.5 crore merchants, and connecting 731 banks. That bank count matches NPCI's June row, not the August row. Treat 731 banks and 55.49 crore individuals as a late-July official description, and treat 752 banks as the August product-statistics row.

## Consent: July was a different month

Sahamati's public Account Aggregator dashboard, which it says covers entities dealing with live customers, shows a steadier linking trend and a sharp July jump in fulfilled consents.

| Month | Accounts linked in the month (million) | New consents fulfilled (million) | Data delivered on linked accounts (million) | Cumulative accounts linked (million) | Cumulative consents fulfilled (million) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| April 2026 | 9.86 | 19.62 | 290.73 | 294.42 | 450.69 |
| May 2026 | 10.40 | 23.50 | 302.53 | 304.82 | 474.19 |
| June 2026 | 9.68 | 19.30 | 295.20 | 314.50 | 493.50 |
| July 2026 | 11.68 | 44.84 | 343.78 | 326.30 | 538.32 |

Sahamati defines the consent column as consent requests against which data was successfully delivered at once. It says that column is not the count of all data-fetch requests. The data-delivered column is the monthly count of successful pulls. By July, cumulative linked accounts stood at 326.30 million and cumulative fulfilled consents at 538.32 million.

July's consent figure, 44.84 million, is more than double June's 19.30 million. Account linking rose too, from 9.68 million to 11.68 million, but not by the same proportion. This page does not have an official explanation for the July consent jump. The right use of the table is to notice that consent fulfilment and account linking are different series.

## Documents, health IDs, and commerce

DigiLocker figures moved even inside July, because two parliamentary answers use different cuts.

On 29 July 2026 the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology told the Lok Sabha that DigiLocker had over 71.66 crore registered users and more than 936.03 crore documents accessible on the platform. On 7 August 2026 the Minister for Electronics and Information Technology told the Rajya Sabha, in a statement the Press Information Bureau carried on 12 August, that as on 31 July 2026 DigiLocker had more than 72.43 crore registered users, about 72.86 crore document-access transactions in the last three years, and 5,437 document types or services (661 from the Centre and 4,776 from states). The 936 crore figure is a stock of documents. The 72.86 crore figure is a three-year flow of accesses. They should not be added together.

The same Rajya Sabha statement put UMANG at more than 11.66 crore registered users and about 798 crore transactions in the last three years, with 2,575 services (880 central and 1,695 state) as on 31 July 2026.

Health identity is a stock, not a monthly flow, in the latest parliamentary cut. The Minister of State for Health told the Lok Sabha, in a reply the Press Information Bureau posted on 24 July 2026, that as of 20 July 2026 the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission had created 94.87 crore ABHA IDs, registered 5.36 lakh facilities on the Health Facility Registry, and registered 10.09 lakh professionals on the Healthcare Professionals Registry.

Commerce crossed a round number. The Minister of State for Commerce and Industry told the Lok Sabha on 4 August 2026 that the Open Network for Digital Commerce had transacted a cumulative total of over 500 million orders as of July 2026, and that the network was processing over 500 orders a minute. The same answer put active retail participation at approximately 2 lakh or more merchants, about 60 percent of them small sellers, and approximately 10 lakh or more service providers in mobility and logistics. ONDC's own about page marks 500 million transactions as of 25 July 2026.

## What did not change, on the public record

The quarter did not produce a new state-wise UPI table in the releases used here. It did not produce a seller-by-state count for ONDC. The Commerce Ministry's answer says ONDC does not keep a network-wide, individually attributed count of every seller, because sellers sit behind seller-network participants and can move between them.

Aadhaar's latest full state table in this set is still the Unique Identification Authority of India's saturation report as on 30 June 2026, not a July or August update. That report is the right source for state gaps. It is not a "this quarter" change.

## How to use this quarter

If you operate on these rails, the practical update is:

- Quote UPI with the month. August volume and August value tell different stories.
- Quote DigiLocker with the metric. Users, documents stored, and document accesses are not interchangeable, and the user count in the 31 July cut is higher than the user count in the 29 July Lok Sabha answer.
- Treat the July Account Aggregator consent spike as a published fact without a published cause.
- Treat ONDC's 500 million as cumulative orders, not as gross merchandise value. The Lok Sabha answer does not give a rupee value for those orders.

The next clean comparison will be NPCI's September row, Sahamati's August row, and whatever UIDAI posts after the 30 June saturation report. Those pages had not replaced the figures above when this brief was updated.
