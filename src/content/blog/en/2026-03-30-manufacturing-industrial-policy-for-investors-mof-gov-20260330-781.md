# What an investor can take from India's PLI record

This note is a reading of official stocks. It is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security, and it is not a forecast of scheme extensions.

## What the 31 March 2026 stock supports

The 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf) supports five statements, each dated:

- The government states an approved PLI outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore for 14 sectors.
- Approved applications were 892.
- Actual investment reported under the schemes was over ₹2.40 lakh crore, larger than the incentive outlay. The annexure total is ₹2,40,138 crore ([PIB PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008)).
- Production and sales were over ₹22.66 lakh crore, and exports over ₹15.2 lakh crore.
- Incentives paid were ₹35,354 crore.

The investment-larger-than-outlay fact means applicants have reported more capital than the incentive ceiling. It does not mean the policy "returned" that capital, and it does not convert sales into earnings. Sales of ₹22.66 lakh crore are scheme sales. Margin is not in the reply.

Disbursement of ₹35,354 crore against an outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore is, on this page's division, a little under one-fifth. The government did not publish that ratio. An investor who treats unpaid outlay as a receivable of the sector is adding an assumption. An investor who treats unpaid outlay as cancelled is adding a different assumption. The reply supports neither.

## Where the capital sits

Three annexure lines — solar PV modules ₹64,873 crore, pharmaceutical drugs ₹45,158 crore, and automobiles and auto components ₹44,326 crore — add to ₹1,54,357 crore of the ₹2,40,138 crore total. Specialty steel and large-scale electronics are the next lines, at ₹23,896 crore and ₹20,580 crore. A diversified "PLI basket" that ignores this concentration is not diversified inside the official table. IT hardware at ₹908 crore and drones at ₹595 crore are in the programme. They are not the programme's reported capital.

Employment does not confirm the same concentration. The annexure column is led by food products. The jobs note explains why that column is not the 14.15 lakh headline. Using headcount as a proxy for invested capital will point at the wrong sectors.

## Series an investment memo should not merge

Manufacturing FDI of USD 19.04 billion in FY 2024–25, and gross FDI of USD 94.53 billion in FY 2025–26, are in the same Lok Sabha reply and in different units. Eleven-year FDI of USD 748.78 billion from 2014–25 is a third line. The corridor approval of an estimated ₹28,602 crore ([PIB PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136)) is public node investment, not PLI applicant capital. Adding any of these to ₹2.40 lakh crore creates a number no agency has published.

The annual report's outlay of Rs. 1.97 lakh crore should not be used as a sensitivity upside on the ₹1.91 lakh crore figure. It is the earlier document's statement of the outlay. Quote it as that, or leave it out of the model.

## What still has to be underwritten at the company

The reply gives no list of the 892 applicants, no claim-payout schedule, and no statement that application windows remain open. The December 2025 reply's count of 59 electronics companies and 72 auto companies is the closest official company count, and it is a production count for part of one year ([PIB PRID 2246089](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246089)). Land-border FDI still has government-route conditions, with a 2026 description of a 60-day path only for specified manufacturing activities and only with Indian control. Corridor land is an SPV question, not a PLI question.

A supportable investor conclusion from these pages is narrow: reported capital and sales under PLI are large, paid incentives are much smaller than the outlay, results are concentrated, and the latest full stock stops at 31 March 2026.
