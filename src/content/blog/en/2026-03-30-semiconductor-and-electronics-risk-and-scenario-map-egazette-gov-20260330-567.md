The risks worth mapping are the ones the official record already implies. This brief does not assign probabilities, and it does not invent a downside case with a rupee loss attached. Each risk below is a gap between two sentences the government has published. The page is current to 24 September 2026.

## Most of the approved book is still before commercial production

On [22 July 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) Parliament said 12 manufacturing projects had been approved and commercial production had commenced at three: Micron, Kaynes Semicon, and CG Semi. The [Cabinet note of 15 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) says the first fab is scheduled for 2028, and that one more unit, unnamed, was expected to start in 2026. Nine of the twelve were therefore not in the commercial-production sentence. The scenario this supports is a long gap between approval and output, concentrated in the silicon fab, the compound and display lines, and several packaging units. A model that books ₹1.64 lakh crore of commitment as if it were capacity already online is booking the approval, not the ramp.

The jobs caveat sits in the same place. Around 32,000 direct jobs are expected, depending on factory automation. Higher automation is the path that cuts that expectation. Indirect jobs of about 60,000 were attached, on 29 February 2024, to three of the plants only. Using both figures as if they described the same set, on the same date, overstates the employment case.

## Technology partners carry process knowledge the approval does not transfer on paper

The named partners are the process risk in plain view. PSMC of Taiwan is the technology partner for the Dholera silicon fab. Renesas of Japan and Stars Microelectronics of Thailand are the partners on the Sanand specialised-chip unit. Clas-SiC of the United Kingdom is the partner for the Bhubaneswar silicon-carbide fab. APACT of South Korea is the partner for the Andhra Pradesh system-in-package unit. Micron's Sanand plant is Micron's own. The releases describe these tie-ups as how the plants will be built. They do not describe a completed transfer of process intellectual property into an Indian entity, and they do not describe what happens to the schedule if a partner's home-country licence, tool export, or engineering deployment slows down.

Parliament's reply records memoranda with the United States, the European Union, Japan, Singapore, and the Netherlands as platforms for business-to-business supply talks. A platform is not a stockpile of specialty gases, photomasks, or spare parts. The risk is treating a memorandum as supply-chain cover for a plant whose day-to-day inputs are still contracted by the applicant.

## Machines and materials were made a pillar because they are not yet a domestic industry

Semicon 2.0's second pillar is incentives for companies that make semiconductor equipment and the materials, chemicals, and gases the process uses. The government wrote that pillar in July 2026, after a decade of electronics-assembly growth and after 12 factory approvals. The existence of the pillar is the disclosure. The plants can be approved before the domestic equipment and materials base exists. Until MeitY notifies who qualifies and at what rate, this risk also has a policy form: the constraint is named, and the claim formula is not yet published in the Cabinet release.

## Display risk is a product-mix risk

The approved display manufacturing project is Crystal Matrix at Dholera, a gallium-nitride mini and micro-LED facility, counted by the July Cabinet note as the one integrated display fab in the set of 12. The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme separately incentivises display-module sub-assembly on a turnover basis. The 15 July text still lists display fabs among the categories of fab Semicon 2.0 wants more of. The risk for a company planning phones, laptops, or televisions is specific. Module assembly and a mini-LED compound line are what the record contains. A large liquid-crystal or organic light-emitting display fab is not in the 12-project composition the Cabinet described.

## Design risk is milestone risk

Twenty-four design projects have financial support. The [15 July note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) says these companies are at different stages and will enter deployment after successful prototyping. The gazette pays product-design support against eligible expenditure, capped at ₹15 crore, and pays the deployment incentive only on net sales, capped at ₹30 crore. A startup that has tool access, including one of the 105 with design tools, has not yet earned the deployment incentive. Tape-out counts in the [7 February 2026 note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) — academic tape-outs, and a smaller set of startup tape-outs including work at 12 nm — show designs reaching silicon, often at an external foundry or at 180 nm in Mohali. Reaching silicon at a foundry abroad is a technical milestone. It is not a domestic fab, and it is not revenue.

## Disclosure risk is already visible in the public texts

Training hours, startup counts, and employment expectations differ across the February 2026 budget note, the May 2026 approval, the July Cabinet note, and the July parliamentary reply. The differences have dates, and several of them count different programmes. The risk is operational for anyone who publishes a single "India has trained X engineers" line. A reader who pulls the February SMART Lab figure and the July parliamentary figure into one press note will be wrong about at least one of them. The same is true of fiscal totals if ₹76,000 crore, the ₹1,000 crore annual provision, the ₹8,000 crore modified-programme outlay for the year, and ₹1,27,500 crore are added.
