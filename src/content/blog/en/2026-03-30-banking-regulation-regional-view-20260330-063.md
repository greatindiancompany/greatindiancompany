# Priority-sector targets differ by bank type and by district

"Priority sector" is one label and several targets. The split is by the kind of bank and, for some banks, by the district where the incremental credit is booked. The source is the [Reserve Bank of India (Priority Sector Lending – Targets and Classification) Directions, 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12799&Mode=0), in force from 1 April 2025 and updated as on 7 August 2026.

The base is adjusted net bank credit, or the credit equivalent of off-balance-sheet exposure, whichever is higher, taken as on the corresponding date of the previous year. The Directions abbreviate that credit equivalent as CEOBSE.

## The target depends on the bank

| Bank | Overall priority sector |
| --- | --- |
| Domestic commercial banks, excluding regional rural banks and small finance banks, and foreign banks with 20 or more branches | 40 percent |
| Foreign banks with fewer than 20 branches | 40 percent, of which up to 32 percent may be export credit and not less than 8 percent must be any other priority sector |
| Regional rural banks | 75 percent. Lending to medium enterprises, social infrastructure, and renewable energy counts only up to 15 percent of adjusted net bank credit |
| Small finance banks | 60 percent |
| Primary urban co-operative banks | 60 percent |

Sub-targets are not common to every column. Agriculture is 18 percent for domestic commercial banks in the first column, regional rural banks, and small finance banks, with 14 percent for non-corporate farmers and 10 percent for small and marginal farmers inside that. It is not applied to foreign banks with fewer than 20 branches. Micro enterprises are 7.5 percent for the banks that have the sub-target, including urban co-operative banks, and are not applied to those smaller foreign banks. Weaker sections are 12 percent for domestic commercial banks in the first column, small finance banks, and urban co-operative banks, and 15 percent for regional rural banks.

The [press release of 24 March 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=60048) described the urban co-operative bank overall target of 60 percent as one of the revisions taking effect on 1 April 2025. For small finance banks, a [circular of 20 June 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12875&Mode=0) reduced the flexible slice from 35 percent to 20 percent from financial year 2025-26, on top of a 40 percent allocation to the prescribed sub-sectors, which is how the overall figure became 60 percent. The updated Directions show that 60 percent.

## District weights are an incentive, not a second target

From financial year 2024-25, incremental priority-sector credit in identified districts with per capita priority-sector credit below ₹9,000 receives a weight of 125 percent. Incremental credit in identified districts above ₹42,000 per capita receives a weight of 90 percent. Other districts stay at 100 percent. The district lists in the annexes are valid through financial year 2026-27, and then are to be reviewed.

Banks still report the actual outstanding amount. The Reserve Bank applies the weight from district-level data. Regional rural banks, urban co-operative banks, local area banks, and foreign banks, including wholly owned subsidiaries, are exempt from the weight adjustment because of their limited area or niche book.

A state-wise ranking of credit is not in these Directions. The regional instrument they do contain is this district weight, plus the older fact that a regional rural bank's book is scored against a 75 percent target and reported to NABARD.

## Supervision is also split by institution

Prompt corrective action for scheduled commercial banks does not cover small finance banks, payments banks, or regional rural banks. Urban co-operative banks in tiers 2, 3, and 4 have a [separate prompt corrective action framework](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12711&Mode=0). That framework watches the capital-to-risk-weighted-assets ratio, the net non-performing advances ratio, and net profit, including losses in two consecutive years at the first threshold. Its capital indicator follows the regulatory minimum applicable to the bank, and the circular refers to a glide path toward a regulatory minimum of 12 percent by 31 March 2026. Commercial-bank capital directions are a different document and exclude small finance banks, payment banks, and local area banks.

A comparison of two districts, or of a regional rural bank and a private commercial bank, starts from these different denominators. It does not start from a single national percentage.
