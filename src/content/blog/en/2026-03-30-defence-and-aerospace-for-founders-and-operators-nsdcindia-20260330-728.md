# Which defence or space door a founder should open first

## Sort the firm before sorting the market

Indian defence and space policy is a set of doors with different keys. A founder who collects every scheme will spend the quarter on the wrong form. The question is what the firm is trying to sell, and to whom, in the next transaction.

## If you make a part an Indian buyer already imports

Open SRIJAN. Defence public sector undertakings and service headquarters offer items there. The positive indigenisation lists are the subset with a published consequence: after indigenous development succeeds, and on the item's timeline, procurement is from Indian industry. The 18 August 2026 list is the newest Department of Defence Production set, 405 items, with the line-level dates on the portal rather than in a press summary.

Bring a manufacturing plan for one item. The accepting side is a defence public sector undertaking or, for 16 items on that list, the Coast Guard. MSMEs are named as participants. A grant is not the product this door sells.

## If you are building a new licensed factory, possibly with foreign capital

Open the industrial-licence path and Press Note 4 of 2020 together. DPIIT consults the Ministry of Defence and the Ministry of External Affairs. Foreign investment up to 74 percent is automatic for a company seeking a new industrial licence. It is not automatic, on those same terms, for a company that already holds an approval. That company declares changes up to 49 percent to the Ministry within 30 days and needs government approval above 49 percent.

Budget for security clearance by the Ministry of Home Affairs, for design work inside India, and for maintenance and life-cycle support beside the factory. The government also reserves a national-security review. A foreign partner's termsheet should attach those conditions, not a slide that says only "74 percent".

## If you have a prototype and no production line

Open iDEX, and read the FAQ before the brochure. DISC and Open Challenge grants go up to Rs 1.5 crore. ADITI grants go up to Rs 25 crore. The startup puts up at least half the project cost as a matching contribution. The scheme window printed by the Department runs from 2021-22 to 2025-26. Confirm whether that window still accepts new challenges. The organisation does not promise a minimum pilot quantity or a commercial order. The user buys, if it buys, under the acquisition procedure. This is a research door. It becomes a revenue door only later, and sometimes not at all.

## If the product must leave India

Open two pages. DGFT's SCOMET list says whether the item, software, or technology is controlled. The Department of Defence Production's authorisation route is how munitions-list exports are permitted. In FY 2024-25 the Department issued 1,762 such authorisations. Your shipment still needs its own. An industrial licence and a SRIJAN listing do not travel with the cargo.

## If the product flies as a space system

Open IN-SPACe, not the Ministry of Defence licence counter, unless you are also making a controlled defence article. The Indian Space Policy 2023 makes IN-SPACe the single window for non-government entities. ISRO's published role is research and development. NewSpace India Limited commercialises platforms built with public money. Authorisation considers safety, national security, international obligations, and foreign policy. A defence-corridor plot in Tamil Nadu or Uttar Pradesh does not authorise a launch.

## What to ignore in the first month

Ignore a national market-size slide, a jobs multiplier, and any contract figure that is not on a government page. The private-industry slice of the FY 2025-26 modernisation budget, Rs 27,886.21 crore, is a real earmark and still not your contract. Great Indian Company wrote the companion pages so this choice can be checked against the notice, one door at a time.
