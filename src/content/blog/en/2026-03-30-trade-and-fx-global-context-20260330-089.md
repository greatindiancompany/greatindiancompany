# India's FTA markets and the services surplus behind them

India's global position, in the documents the government and the RBI actually publish, is a goods deficit with the world, a services surplus concentrated on the United States and Europe, and a lengthening list of preferential trade agreements whose merchandise levels are still modest next to the total. That is the external context. It does not require a global-trade forecast.

## The completed year India takes abroad

The Commerce release of 15 April 2026 puts 2025-26 goods and services exports at US$860.09 billion, up 4.22 per cent from US$825.26 billion. Merchandise exports were US$441.78 billion, up 0.93 per cent. Services exports were US$418.31 billion. The services surplus was US$213.89 billion. The merchandise deficit was US$333.19 billion. The combined trade balance was −US$119.30 billion.

A PIB note on 18 August 2026 calls combined exports a record US$863.1 billion, with merchandise exports of US$441.8 billion. The merchandise figure is the April total, rounded. The combined figure differs from US$860.09 billion. Anyone citing "the record" should cite the August note and keep the April table for the deficit and the services surplus. This brief does both, and it does not blend them into a third number.

The August note's point is narrower than the record line. It argues that FTA partners have become commercially important inside that export base, and it publishes the merchandise levels.

## What the agreements have on the board

Merchandise exports to FTA partners in 2025-26, million US dollars, Ministry of Commerce via that PIB note:

| Market | Exports |
| --- | --- |
| ASEAN | 38,416.33 |
| SAFTA | 25,774.68 |
| UAE | 37,359.11 |
| United Kingdom | 13,444.19 |
| Singapore | 11,863.66 |
| Australia | 7,284.17 |
| Japan | 6,039.50 |
| Republic of Korea | 6,012.03 |
| Oman | 4,021.48 |

The UAE figure is the largest single FTA country market in the table. The note dates the India-UAE CEPA to 1 May 2022 and says bilateral trade crossed US$100.06 billion in 2024-25, with growth of 19.6 per cent. It also records a stated aim of doubling trade to US$200 billion by 2032. That aim is a government target in the note. It is not an export outcome.

Australia's ECTA has been in force since 29 December 2022. The note says bilateral trade reached US$24.1 billion in 2024-25, and that India's exports to Australia went from US$4 billion in 2020-21 to US$7.28 billion in 2025-26. It says Australia gave immediate zero-duty access on 98.3 per cent of tariff lines, and that from 2026 Indian exports are eligible for zero duty. The India-UK CETA, in the same note, is described as making nearly 99 per cent of Indian exports eligible to enter the UK duty-free. The Oman CEPA is described as covering concessions on 98 per cent of Oman's tariff lines.

Preferential access is used when a certificate of origin is issued. The note says the EFTA TEPA, operational from October 2025, had generated 7,885 certificates, and the Oman CEPA, implemented in June 2026, had generated 783. The India-UK agreement allows self-declaration of origin, importer's knowledge, and a waiver of origin documents below £1,000. Those provisions are aimed at smaller consignments and e-commerce. They sit beside Niryat Disha, the non-financial window of the Export Promotion Mission, which is where compliance and trade-fair support for MSME exporters is named.

April–August 2026 customs data are a check on whether the FTA story and the monthly story match. UAE merchandise exports in that DGCIS quick estimate were US$13,588.28 million, down 10.61 per cent from a year earlier. The agreement can be in force, the 2025-26 level can be large, and the next five months can still fall. Singapore, also an FTA partner, was up 96.56 per cent in the same five months, to US$9,497.67 million.

## Services are the global receipt that pays for a large part of the goods bill

In April–June 2026 the RBI recorded a goods deficit of US$86.1 billion and net services receipts of US$51.6 billion. Personal transfers added US$42.9 billion. The current account deficit was US$4.2 billion. India's global goods position and India's global services position pull in opposite directions, and the quarterly table shows the net.

The software survey gives the geography of the largest services activity. In 2025-26, US$119.7 billion of software exports, 54.1 per cent, went to the United States. Europe took US$70.3 billion, 31.8 per cent. The United Kingdom took US$34.0 billion. The US dollar invoiced 72.6 per cent. Including foreign affiliates, software exports were US$239.3 billion. That is a different global footprint from the FTA merchandise list, where the largest single country line is the UAE at US$37,359.11 million for the full year.

Capital flows are the third global link. In the June quarter, net FDI was US$6.1 billion and portfolio investment was a net outflow of US$9.6 billion. The older-format reserve note shows a capital account, on that format, of −US$3.9 billion, and a valuation loss of US$14.4 billion, larger than the current-account deficit of US$4.2 billion. July's preliminary tables then showed non-resident deposits of US$33.5 billion in a single month. That inflow belongs in the global picture after the next quarterly release revises it. Until then, it is a preliminary cell with an outsized weight.

## How to hold the context

Use US$860.09 billion and the deficit table when you need 2025-26 as a whole. Use the August FTA note when you need partner levels and certificate counts. Use the RBI quarter when you need to see services and transfers covering the goods gap. Use the software survey when the question is which foreign market buys India's computer services. Use the DGCIS month when you need to know whether a partner that looked strong in the completed year is still strong.
