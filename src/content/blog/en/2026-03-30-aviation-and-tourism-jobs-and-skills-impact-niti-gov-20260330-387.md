# Tourism’s job count is a satellite-account figure

The employment number attached to Indian tourism is not a payroll census of hotels, and it is not a count of pilots, cabin crew, or air traffic controllers. The India Tourism Data Compendium 2025 states it in one place, under the Tourism Satellite Account, the United Nations framework for tourism’s economic contribution. In 2023-24 the travel and tourism sector generated approximately 84.63 million jobs, direct and indirect. In the same year its total contribution to GDP was 5.22 percent.

A Press Information Bureau backgrounder on 31 July 2026 rounds the jobs figure to 84.6 million and pairs the 5.22 percent with a rupee size: ₹15.73 lakh crore of GDP in 2023-24. Use the compendium’s 84.63 million when the need is the published estimate, and the PIB note when the need is the rupee figure the government has since repeated. They describe 2023-24. They do not update themselves when 2024 domestic visits or 2025 foreign-exchange earnings move.

## What the 84.63 million includes

Direct and indirect means the satellite account reaches past hotel and tour-operator staff into the supply chain the framework treats as tourism-supported. The compendium does not, in that paragraph, split the 84.63 million into hotels, restaurants, transport, or civil aviation. A claim that aviation employs some slice of the 84.63 million is an inference the publication does not make.

The visit totals beside it show why a headcount and a visit count diverge. Domestic tourist visits in 2024 were 2,948.19 million, up 17.51 percent. That is a visit statistic from state tourism departments. It can rise because more people travelled, because the same pilgrims were counted at more sites, or because a state’s reporting improved. None of those three is a job created in 2024-25, and the jobs estimate on the page is still the 2023-24 satellite-account year.

## The aviation manpower line that does exist

Modified UDAN, in the 16 July 2026 scheme comparison, adds an operation-and-maintenance support for manpower salary costs at aerodromes developed under the regional scheme. The cap is up to ₹3.06 crore a year per airport, and up to ₹0.90 crore a year for a heliport or water aerodrome, for three years. A cap is a ceiling on support. It is not a statement that each airport will hire staff worth that amount, and it is not a national jobs target. Two HAL Dhruv helicopters and two HAL Dornier aircraft are the only airframes the Cabinet line of the scheme names. They do not come with a published crew requirement in that reply.

The same July workshop note mentions Udan Yatri Cafe prices — tea at ₹10 and snacks at ₹20 — as a passenger amenity. That is a fare for a cup of tea. It is not a hospitality employment programme.

## What a skills reader should ask for next

The missing official piece, for anyone training crew or hotel staff, is a current occupational split: how many of the 84.63 million are direct, how many sit in civil aviation, and whether 2024-25 has been estimated yet. Until that table is published, the defensible statement is the one the compendium already printed. Travel and tourism, broadly defined, supported about 84.63 million jobs and 5.22 percent of GDP in 2023-24. Regional-airport salary support is a per-airport ceiling starting with the new scheme. Airline and airport hiring plans are operator decisions, which the ministry has said it does not set.
