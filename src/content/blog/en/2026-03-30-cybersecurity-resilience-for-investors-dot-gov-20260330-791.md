# What an investor can check in a cyber diligence file

Cyber diligence for an Indian company is a file of duties, not a national loss multiple. CERT-In told Parliament, in the reply published on 24 March 2026, that it does not maintain estimated financial loss from the incidents it tracks. NCRB does not maintain amounts recovered. The incident totals — 20,41,360 in 2024 and 29,44,248 in 2025 — describe volume reported to the national agency. They do not describe the loss in any one portfolio company.

## Which rulebook applies this year

Ask for the licences, not the slogan. A body corporate is inside the CERT-In directions of 28 April 2022 unless a specific extension says otherwise. A bank or an NBFC in the middle, upper or top layer is also inside the Reserve Bank directions effective 1 April 2024. A SEBI-regulated entity has a category — market infrastructure institution, qualified, mid-size, small-size, or self-certification — fixed for the financial year from the previous year's data. If the firm holds more than one SEBI registration, the framework says the highest category applies. An insurer or IRDAI intermediary is inside the 24 April 2023 guidelines. A telecom licensee has the 2024 rules; a firm that only uses mobile numbers to identify customers needs a written view on whether the October 2025 telecommunication-identifier amendment applies.

If the answer is "we follow ISO and a global policy," ask where that policy meets the six-hour CERT-In clock and the 180-day logs kept in India.

## Governance you can see on a page

For a Reserve Bank regulated entity, the diligence page is the CISO's reporting line. The direction requires a senior executive with a technical background, no direct report to the Head of IT, no business targets, a report to the executive who oversees risk, and a cyber review placed before the board or its committees at least quarterly. A CISO who also owns the product roadmap fails that page.

For an insurer, ask for the information-security risk committee's membership against the IRDAI list, and for the twice-a-year full meeting. For a telecom entity, ask for the Chief Telecommunication Security Officer's name, citizenship and residence, and the date those details were filed on the government portal.

For everyone else, ask who the CERT-In point of contact is, and when that person last had a number that still works.

## Monitoring, audit, and the last report

SEBI entities should show which SOC they use and the last efficacy review, half-yearly or yearly as the category requires. Small and self-certification entities should show Market SOC onboarding rather than a claim that they will build a centre later. The glide path dates have passed.

Ask for the last cyber audit and the last vulnerability assessment, the open observations, and where they were placed — the IT committee, in SEBI's framework. If the auditor is described as empanelled, the name should be on CERT-In's panel. The panel had 176 organisations in the 2023 annual report and 237 in the March 2026 reply. Membership of the panel is a qualification screen, not a finding that this company passed an audit.

Logs are a sample, not a policy sentence. Pull one system and see whether 180 days inside India is what the storage setting says.

## Personal data, timed to the investment date

On 24 September 2026, part of the DPDP Rules is in force and the safeguard and breach-notice rules are not yet commenced. Diligence should record that split. Ask whether the company has drafted the notice, the safeguard measures in Rule 6, and the seventy-two-hour Board report, and whether those drafts are labelled with the eighteen-month commencement. Ask whether anyone intends to register as a Consent Manager, because that registration date arrives one year after 13 November 2025. A company that says it is "fully DPDP compliant" in September 2026 is ahead of the commencement clause or using the phrase loosely. The file should show which.

## What not to put in the model

Do not convert CERT-In's national totals into an expected loss for the company. Do not treat the more-than-₹8,690 crore saved through the financial-fraud reporting system as the size of fraud, or as a market. Do not add MeitY's ₹269 crore CERT-In estimate and ₹790 crore projects estimate for 2026-27 into a private spending forecast. Those lines show what the Union government proposed to spend on its own institutions.

The investment question the record supports is narrower. Can this company show the clock, the log, the separated security officer, the audit, and a data-protection draft that matches the commencement date? If the file cannot show those, the gap is operational. It is visible without a loss statistic the government says it does not keep.
