# August 2026 inflation by state and by rural and urban areas

All-India retail inflation of 4.82% in August 2026 is a weighted average. MoSPI’s state table for the same month runs from **6.27% in Telangana** to **2.35% in Mizoram**. Rural India as a whole was at 5.23%. Urban India was at 4.31%. Anyone setting a price, a wage, or a state rollout from the national headline is using a number several large states have already left behind.

All figures below are provisional combined, rural, or urban CPI inflation for August 2026 on base 2024=100, from Annexure III of the [14 September press release](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1789381904344_6c792dcf-8a9f-4fca-93d3-99d833bdb358_Press_Release_of_CPI_for_August_2026.pdf). State and item indices can also be downloaded from [eSankhyiki](https://esankhyiki.mospi.gov.in/). MoSPI warns that some state item indices use thin samples of price quotes. The rates here are the general index, not those item indices.

Telangana at 6.27% is above the 6% upper tolerance of the national inflation target. That tolerance applies to the national headline the Monetary Policy Committee is charged with, not to a state print. It is still the right comparison for a reader who wants to know which states are already living with a faster price rise than the national band’s ceiling. Delhi at 2.61% is closer to the lower tolerance of 2% than to the national 4.82%.

The press release also draws a chart of states with population above 50 lakh in Census 2011. The chart labels are graphical. The table itself is what is quoted here. On that table, the highest large-state readings are Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, and Odisha. The two Union Territories in the top seven, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and Puducherry, are small populations with high prints.

Uttar Pradesh is the clearest rural–urban split among the largest states in this extract: 5.58% rural and 4.04% urban. Maharashtra is 5.67% and 4.21%. West Bengal is lower on both sides, 3.62% and 2.85%. Tamil Nadu is high on both sides, 6.10% rural and 5.78% urban. Delhi’s urban rate is 2.57%, with rural at 4.17%. Chandigarh has no rural market in the release; its urban and combined rate is 4.02%.

## How to use a state rate

A national 4.82% will mis-state Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Delhi, and West Bengal by more than a percentage point. Rural rates run above urban rates in most of the large states above, in line with the all-India gap of 5.23% versus 4.31%. Food has a larger weight in the rural basket (41.983% for food and beverages, against 30.251% urban), which is why a food-led month shows up more strongly outside cities. Household basket detail is in the note on [August prices and the household basket](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/inflation-trends-impact-on-households-20260330-034).

These state rates are provisional. They are general inflation, not food inflation by state. The August release charts food inflation for large states as a graphic and does not print those state food rates in Annexure III, so this note does not assign a food number to a state. For a city or a product line, the next check is the general rate in this table, then the item indices on eSankhyiki, read with MoSPI’s thin-sample warning.
