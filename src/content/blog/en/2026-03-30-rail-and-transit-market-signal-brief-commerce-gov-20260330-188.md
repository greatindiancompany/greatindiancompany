# What Indian Rail Freight Loading Says About Industrial Demand

Indian Railways closed FY 2025-26 with a freight number that is large and a revenue number that grew more slowly. The gap is the signal. The source is the ministry’s own release of 1 April 2026.

## Tonnes rose faster than freight revenue

Loading reached 1,670 million tonnes, up 3.25 percent on the year. Wagons handled rose 4.56 percent, from 2,79,12,271 in 2024-25 to 2,91,86,475 in 2025-26. Freight earnings were about ₹1,77,754 crore, up 1.44 percent from about ₹1,75,302 crore. Volume and wagon turns improved more than the freight bill. A shipper reading only the tonne headline will overstate the revenue outcome. The release does not give a cause. It does show the two rates side by side.

## The commodity mix is construction, steel, and farm inputs

The same release names the fastest reported commodity growth. Fertilisers rose 13.49 percent. Pig iron and steel rose 13.11 percent. Iron ore rose 6.74 percent, to 190.12 million tonnes. Cement rose 4.74 percent, to 157.17 million tonnes. That is a construction and industrial mix, not a container story. The release does not publish a coal growth rate in this note, so coal should not be inferred from the total.

Earnings by commodity, which are not the same as tonnes, put iron ore first at ₹14,600.51 crore, then cement at ₹13,599 crore, pig iron and finished steel at ₹12,181 crore, fertilisers at ₹9,039 crore, food grains at ₹8,312.5 crore, and mineral oil at ₹7,249.54 crore.

## Passengers told a different story

Passenger numbers rose to 741 crore in 2025-26, from 716 crore, an increase of 3.54 percent. Passenger revenue was about ₹80,000 crore, up 5.96 percent from about ₹75,500 crore. On the passenger side, revenue grew faster than the count. On the freight side, tonnes grew faster than earnings. They are two businesses on one network.

## What the corridors add to the signal

The 7 August 2026 Rajya Sabha reply places this loading on a network that now includes commissioned eastern and western dedicated freight corridors, with about 443 freight trains a day on those corridors. Freight handled at Gati Shakti cargo terminals in 2025-26 was 146 million tonnes, against an estimated terminal capacity of 224 million tonnes a year. Terminal capacity and origin tonnes are not interchangeable, and 146 is not a share of 1,670 that this brief calculates as a strategy claim. Both figures are simply what the ministry printed.

A zone-level reading of the same 3.25 percent is in the [state and zone brief](/blog/rail-and-transit-state-wise-patterns-commerce-gov-20260330-308). The earnings-versus-tonnes gap is one of the risks mapped in the [scenario note](/blog/rail-and-transit-risk-and-scenario-map-commerce-gov-20260330-548).

Great Indian Company reads the freight release as a demand print for operators who move cement, steel, ore, and fertiliser, and stops where the ministry stopped.
