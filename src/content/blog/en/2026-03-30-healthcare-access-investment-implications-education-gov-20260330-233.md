## An entitlement is not a volume contract

PM-JAY gives an eligible family a cover of ₹5 lakh a year for listed hospitalization. The National Health Authority’s about-page is clear that the government funds the scheme and that the patient should not pay at an empanelled hospital for covered care. Nothing on that page promises a hospital a minimum number of cases. An investment memo that treats “55 crore beneficiaries” as occupied beds has skipped the step between eligibility and admission. The about-page’s “over 12 crore” families and a 2021 Authority guideline’s “about 10.74 crore households” already disagree. Utilization is a third number, and it lives in claims, not in the eligibility sentence.

The practical question is narrower. For this hospital, in this district, which packages are open to private providers, at which state rate, and who remits.

## Who holds the payment risk

States choose an assurance model, an insurance model, or a mixed model. In assurance, also called trust mode, the State Health Agency pays providers and holds the financial risk. It may hire an implementation support agency for claims. In insurance mode, the agency tenders an insurer, pays a premium per eligible family, and the insurer pays the hospital and holds the risk. Mixed mode uses both, often where a state scheme was already larger than the SECC list.

That choice changes the counterparty. A trust-mode hospital is a vendor to a public agency. An insurance-mode hospital is a vendor to an insurer working under a public contract. The about-page also limits what an insurer may keep: administrative-cost bands that tighten or loosen with the claim ratio, surplus returned to the agency, and a sharing rule when claims run past set thresholds. Those bands differ for the page’s Category A and Category B states. A premium discovered in one state is not a valuation input for another state until the tender and the sharing ratio are read. The Centre–state split on the same page is 60:40 in the general case and 90:10 for North-Eastern states and for Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand, as that page states them. Union territories without a legislature can be funded up to 100 percent, case by case.

Extra families beyond the SECC floor, and any cover above ₹5 lakh, are paid by the state. A state that has widened eligibility is a different revenue pool from a state that has not. The widening is not free federal money.

## Public beds are a competing supply line

Public hospitals are reimbursed at par with private hospitals. Scheme revenue is therefore available inside government facilities, which the Authority describes as a way for those facilities to fund gaps. An investor underwriting a private hospital next to a district hospital should treat that public reimbursement as a competitive fact.

The Ministry’s PM-ABHIM update adds a capital fact. The mission’s published outlay is ₹64,180 crore for 2021–22 to 2025–26. Approvals of ₹33,081.82 crore over those years include 621 critical care hospital blocks and 744 district public-health laboratories, along with primary-care buildings. A critical-care block that opens in a district hospital changes where a heart attack or a trauma case can be treated without a private ICU. The approval count is not a commissioning count. It is enough to put public capacity on the diligence list. The [capital-cycle note](/blog/healthcare-access-capex-cycle-watch-education-gov-20260330-353) stays with that list.

## Figures that should not enter a model

The PM-JAY about-page still prints National Health Accounts 2015 markers: government health spending around 1.2 percent of GDP, 21 percent of health spending from government revenue, and 62 percent out of pocket. They are labelled 2015. Using them as the 2026 out-of-pocket pool double-counts a rationale as a forecast. The senior-citizen extension of 12 September 2024 is usable in a model only as a design change: people aged 70 and above can be covered without an income test, on a shared ₹5 lakh family amount. It is not a ready-made revenue line.

Failure modes that belong beside the base case are mapped in the [risk note](/blog/healthcare-access-risk-and-scenario-map-education-gov-20260330-553). Package rates below cost, reserved public-only procedures, and slow settlement are design risks a memo can name without inventing a default rate.
