# Housing Credit, Commercial Property, and Listed REIT Markets

An investment memo that says "Indian real estate credit is strong, so REITs are supported" has joined three instruments the official record keeps apart. Home loans are household credit. Commercial real estate is a services-sector line. A real estate investment trust is a SEBI-registered trust that holds property and issues units. The July 2026 credit release speaks to the first two only at the level of growth highlights. It does not publish a distribution yield, a vacancy rate, or a unit price.

## What the credit release will support

The [31 August 2026 release](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63478) covers the fortnight ended 31 July 2026 for 41 banks and about 95 percent of non-food credit. Personal loans grew 16.2 percent year on year, against 11.9 percent a year earlier, and housing was one of the segments that sustained double-digit growth. The highlights do not print the housing stock or the housing growth rate. Those sit in the statements linked from the [sectoral deployment page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/Data_Sectoral_Deployment.aspx). Quoting a housing percentage that is not in the press note is how a memo invents a number.

Commercial real estate is named, not quantified, in the same note. Services credit grew 22.9 percent, against 10.2 percent, "supported by" faster growth in NBFCs, trade, and commercial real estate. "Supported by" means the Bank is pointing at contributors. It is not a 22.9 percent growth rate for commercial property loans.

Two further limits matter for anyone marking a book to this release. Non-food credit in the headline uses the Section-42 return for all scheduled commercial banks, while the sectoral cuts use the narrower SIBC return. And from December 2025 the year-on-year comparison mixes the new end-of-month definition with the old fortnight definition for the base month. Direction is usable. A basis-point model is not, until the statements are read with that footnote.

## What a REIT is, and what a 2014 note is not

Listed property exposure of the REIT kind is governed by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Real Estate Investment Trusts) Regulations, 2014. The consolidated text on SEBI's site is the version [last amended on 18 April 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/apr-2026/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-real-estate-investment-trusts-regulations-2014-last-amended-on-april-18-2026-_101013.html). That page is the rulebook for registration, investment conditions, and disclosures. This brief does not restate the current investment mix or the current distribution rule, because those clauses have been amended more than once and the April 2026 text controls.

The [2014 board note](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/docfiles/28905_t.html) that approved the original regulations describes the design at birth: a trust registered with SEBI, with a trustee, sponsor, and manager; funds raised by an offer of units; investment in commercial real estate directly or through special purpose vehicles; and, in that note, not less than 80 percent of the value of REIT assets in completed and revenue-generating properties. Treat the 80 percent sentence as the 2014 decision, not as a substitute for the amended regulation. An investor who needs the current ceiling on under-construction assets, or the share of cash flow that must be distributed, should open the April 2026 text and the master circular SEBI issues under it.

REIT units are not PMAY interest subsidy, and they are not a home loan. The subsidy, up to ₹1.80 lakh on loans sanctioned and disbursed on or after 1 September 2024, is a household transfer inside [PMAY-U 2.0](https://pmaymis.gov.in/PMAYMIS2_2024/faq.html). It reduces a borrower's principal. It does not capitalise a listed trust.

## A usable split for a memo

Write three lines, and stop when the source stops.

- Household housing credit: still in double-digit growth inside a personal-loan book that grew 16.2 percent to 31 July 2026. Stock and the exact housing rate are in the statements, not in this paragraph.
- Commercial real estate bank credit: named as a support for faster services credit. Not a valuation of offices or malls.
- Listed REIT markets: a SEBI regime, last consolidated on 18 April 2026. Performance of any trust is in that trust's filings, which this note does not summarise.

The capex reading of the same credit release is in the [credit-cycle brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-capex-cycle-watch-mca-gov-20260330-361). Great Indian Company publishes this split so an allocator does not buy a trust, a home loan, and a subsidy with one adjective.
