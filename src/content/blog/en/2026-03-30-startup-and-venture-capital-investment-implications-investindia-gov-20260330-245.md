An investor who hears “the government has ₹10,000 crore for startups” still has to ask which rupee, in which vehicle, and on what condition. DPIIT runs three flagship money programmes. They do not substitute for one another.

## Equity through funds, not through DPIIT

The original Fund of Funds for Startups is operated by SIDBI. SIDBI commits capital to SEBI-registered alternative investment funds. Those funds invest in startups through equity and equity-linked instruments. A Rajya Sabha reply of 13 February 2026 states that design, and gives the stock as on 31 December 2025: ₹25,547.98 crore invested in 1,371 startups across 29 states and Union Territories. Supported startups under that scheme had generated over 2 lakh jobs.

PIB’s 17 April 2026 release moves the clock to the end of financial year 2025-26 and switches the verb from “committed” to “disbursed”. More than ₹7,000 crore had been disbursed to over 135 funds. Those funds had invested more than ₹26,900 crore in over 1,420 startups. Disbursement to funds in 2025-26 was 1,039+ crore rupees, after 980+ crore in 2024-25. The corpus often cited for this first programme is ₹10,000 crore. Disbursed capital and invested capital are later, larger-looking numbers because private money sits beside the government cheque and because investment is reported at the portfolio, not at the SIDBI drawdown.

Fund of Funds 2.0 is a second ₹10,000 crore corpus. DPIIT’s guidelines, issued with the 25 April 2026 PIB note, say the scheme will not invest directly in startups. It will participate in Category I and Category II funds registered with SEBI. Those funds invest in equity, equity-linked instruments, and debt instruments of DPIIT-recognised startups. Commitments are spread across the 16th and 17th Finance Commission cycles. The guidelines do not convert that sentence into a drawdown timetable.

Four segments carry hard caps. Deep-tech funds: government contribution up to 40 percent of corpus, maximum ₹500 crore, and the fund must invest at least 1.5 times the amount committed to it. Smaller funds for early-growth startups: corpus up to ₹400 crore, contribution up to 30 percent and ₹100 crore, multiplier 2 times, with at least half the corpus for seed or early stage and funding up to ₹10 crore per startup. Tech-driven manufacturing funds: contribution up to 30 percent and ₹200 crore, multiplier 1.75 times. Sector- and stage-agnostic funds: contribution up to 25 percent and ₹180 crore, multiplier 2.5 times. Across government funds of funds, aggregate contribution to one alternative investment fund stays within 50 percent of that fund’s corpus, unless the Venture Capital Investment Committee sets a lower limit.

If a company stops qualifying as a recognised startup after a fund has committed an amount, the guidelines allow the unreleased balance of that commitment to continue. A certificate that later lapses does not, under this clause, unwind a commitment already made.

## Seed cheques through incubators

The Startup India Seed Fund Scheme started on 1 April 2021. Incubators pass on grants, convertible debentures, or debt and debt-linked instruments. As on 31 December 2025, incubators had approved ₹590.93 crore to 3,271 startups across 32 states and Union Territories, and those startups had reported over 22,600 jobs. By the end of 2025-26, PIB reports 219 incubators selected, the entire ₹945 crore corpus committed, and approved funding of over ₹605 crore to more than 3,400 startups. The scheme has been extended so approved activity can be disbursed and completed. For a seed-stage founder, the counterparty is an incubator with an allocation, not SIDBI and not a bank.

## A guarantee, not a loan from DPIIT

The Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups has been operational since 1 April 2023, through the National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company. It guarantees credit; it does not write the loan. As on 31 December 2025, 334 loans amounting to about ₹808.18 crore had been guaranteed across 20 states and Union Territories. By the end of 2025-26 the stock was more than 410 loans and over ₹1,250 crore. In 2025-26 the per-borrower cover rose from ₹10 crore to ₹20 crore. PIB also says the extent of cover was enhanced and the annual guarantee fee was reduced for lenders in identified sectors. The 2025-26 flow was 135+ loans and 600+ crore rupees, after 130 loans and ₹305 crore in 2024-25.

The scheme page on Startup India is the place a lender checks the current cover rules. This brief stops at the amounts and the institutional design PIB and the Rajya Sabha reply have already printed.
