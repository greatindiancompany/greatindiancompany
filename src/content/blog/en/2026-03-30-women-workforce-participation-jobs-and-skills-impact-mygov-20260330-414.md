# Training coverage and the jobs women hold after it

Skilling schemes and the labour force survey measure different things. A scheme counts candidates trained or apprentices engaged. PLFS counts whether a person is in the workforce, and whether they report formal vocational or technical training. Putting a scheme’s enrolment next to a placement slogan skips the survey cell that actually links training to work.

## How many people report formal training

In the 2025 annual PLFS, 4.2 percent of persons aged 15–59 reported having received or receiving formal vocational or technical training. Among persons aged 15–29 the share was 5.0 percent. Inside the 15–59 group who had that training, 83.3 percent of men and 51.4 percent of women were in the workforce.

The training stock is thin for everyone. The step from training into work is much thinner for women than for men, on the same question, in the same year. That 51.4 against 83.3 is the jobs-and-skills fact in the official survey. It is not a placement rate for any one scheme. PLFS does not identify which scheme provided the training.

Years of schooling do not show a large gap at the all-India level. Among persons aged 15 and above who have formal education, women averaged 9.9 years and men 10.2 years. Rural women averaged 9.2 years and urban women 10.9 years. For persons aged 25 and above, women averaged 9.5 years. Schooling and vocational training are separate lines. The vocational line is the one where women’s presence in the workforce, conditional on training, diverges from men’s.

## What the apprenticeship scheme reports about women

The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship’s year-end review, issued through PIB on 23 January 2026, gives a sex split for apprentices that PLFS does not give for a named scheme. Under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme, women’s participation rose from 11.30 percent in 2018–19 to 22.84 percent in 2024–25. More than 10.01 lakh women apprentices have been engaged since 2016–17. In 2025–26, up to 31 October 2025, 1.76 lakh women were engaged, 24.80 percent of apprentices engaged in that part of the year.

Those are engagement figures under the Apprentices Act route, with stipend support. Engagement is not the same as the PLFS workforce rate a year later. The scheme can become more female while the survey still finds that only about half of women who report formal vocational training are in work. Both statements can be true because they count different populations: apprentices on the portal, and all persons who told the survey they had formal vocational or technical training.

PMKVY 4.0, in the same review, had trained 27.08 lakh candidates as on 7 December 2025. The review does not give a women-only headcount for that 27.08 lakh in the passage that states the total. A women share for PMKVY should be taken from a table that states it, not inferred from the apprenticeship percentage.

## Time use is the other constraint on a trained woman

The Time Use Survey for January–December 2024, also from MoSPI, finds that women aged 15–59 who participated in unpaid domestic services spent 305 minutes a day on them, down from 315 minutes in the 2019 survey. For persons aged 6 and above, women participants spent 289 minutes and men 88. Participation in unpaid domestic services was 81.5 percent for women and 27.1 percent for men. Unpaid caregiving took women participants 137 minutes and men 75, with participation of 34.0 percent and 17.9 percent.

A training certificate does not change that time account. PLFS also records that 44.4 percent of women outside the labour force named child care or personal commitments in home-making as the main reason. The skills gap and the care gap are both in official surveys. A scheme that raises women’s share of apprentices addresses the first registration problem. It does not, by itself, change the 305 minutes.

## What an employer or a training provider can check

Ask three questions that map to published cells. What share of the intake is women, using the scheme’s own table, as NAPS does with 22.84 percent in 2024–25. What share of women with formal vocational training are in the workforce, which PLFS puts at 51.4 percent against 83.3 percent for men. Whether the job on offer is regular wage, which is 18.2 percent of women workers overall and 50.9 percent of urban women workers. A short-term certificate aimed at rural self-employment is landing in the 70.7 percent rural self-employed share, not in the urban regular-wage share.
