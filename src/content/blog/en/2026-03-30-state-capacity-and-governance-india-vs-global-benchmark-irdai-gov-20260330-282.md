# NeSDA and the UN Online Service Index: A Federal Benchmark

A global league table is a weak way to read Indian state capacity. Most of the delivery a citizen actually meets is a state portal, a city portal or a scheme account, and a single national rank hides that. The benchmark the Union government itself runs is built to keep the federation visible.

## What DARPG says it adapted

PIB's note of 28 May 2026, on the launch of the NeSDA 2025 portal, states the design in one sentence. NeSDA is a biennial assessment by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances. It measures the depth and effectiveness of e-governance service delivery from the citizen's side. It is based on the Online Service Index of the UN e-Government Survey, and it is customised for the Indian federal structure. It covers states, Union Territories and focus central ministries.

This brief does not assign India a UN e-Government rank. That rank is the UN survey's product, and it is not the number DARPG asked states to submit in May 2026. The operational benchmark is NeSDA.

The 2025 round classifies portals into two families: state, UT, city and central ministry portals, and the service portals that sit under them. It covers government-to-citizen and government-to-business services in finance, labour and employment, education, local governance and utility services, social welfare (including health, agriculture and home security), environment (including fire), tourism, public grievance, and transport and travel. The 2025 framework proposes adding services of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Fifty-nine mandatory services are proposed for every state and UT. Forty-three services are proposed for central ministries and departments.

The parameters PIB lists are the actual comparison set: accessibility, content availability, ease of use, information security and privacy, end service delivery, integrated service delivery, status and request tracking, open government data, e-participation, and leveraging emerging technologies. A state can add services and still score poorly on tracking, security or integration. That is the point of a maturity benchmark as against a raw count.

## The federal scoreboard already in print

While the 2025 round collects a fresh submission, the NeSDA Way Forward dashboard is the running federal comparison. It started in April 2023 to watch monthly movement in e-services, mandatory e-services and unified portals. A Lok Sabha reply published by PIB on 10 December 2025 said that between April 2023 and October 2025 the number of e-services rose from 11,614 to 23,919, and saturation of the identified mandatory services rose from 66 percent to 80 percent. Ten states and UTs delivered over 90 percent of their services through a unified portal, and six provided 100 percent through that portal.

The later stock, in the December 2025 monthly report released on 22 January 2026, is 24,090 e-services as of 31 December 2025, with the local-governance and utility block at 8,656. The same note names seven unified portals at 100 percent of services and four more above 90 percent. Figures are whatever states and UTs uploaded. A benchmark built on self-upload is still a benchmark. It is a weak one if the upload is late or thin, which is why the May 2026 portal asks nodal officers for a structured submission and why DARPG said it would run review meetings through the cycle.

DARPG's own scheme page for NeSDA Way Forward describes the collaboration as saturation of mandatory services, faceless and suo-motu entitlement-based delivery, and the spread of practices that already work in one state. The December 2025 release points at two central examples, the National Digital Library of India and the Central Government Health Scheme, and at municipal portals in Puducherry and Guwahati, because citizen contact is largely municipal.

## Where a global frame still helps, and where it stops

The UN index is a national online-service concept. India's official adaptation splits the score across states, cities and ministries, then adds a monthly saturation series so the biennial study does not go stale. Civil-service capacity has a parallel structure and no UN twin in these documents: DoPT's Mission Karmayogi aims the iGOT platform at about two crore employees, and the 11 March 2026 PIB note recorded more than 1.51 crore users and MoUs with 29 states and UTs as on 6 March 2026. Competency is benchmarked inside the federation, by completions and by whether a state has signed, not by a cross-country training rank.

A reader who wants a single "India versus the world" figure will not find it in these pages, and should not invent one. The comparison DARPG is willing to publish is state against state, portal against portal, and this year's submission against the parameter list.
