# Where Scheme Delivery and Local Bodies Still Bottleneck

Execution fails in specific gates. For Indian state capacity in 2026, the gates that official documents actually name are the scheme bank account, the finance secretary's certificate, the municipal service, the missing link between grievance systems, and the district officer who is accountable for outcomes and invisible on the learning dashboard.

## The scheme account is a hard stop

Centrally Sponsored Schemes have been pushed through a Single Nodal Agency account so the Centre can see the float. The Department of Expenditure's page on that arrangement is the standing instruction. The SASCI guidelines of 27 March 2026 turn the same arrangement into a release condition for capital loans. The second installment of untied assistance, 34 percent after a 66 percent first slice, requires that all those SNA bank accounts be closed and that the unspent central share, with interest up to the date of closure, be deposited in the Consolidated Fund of India. A certificate goes through PFMS, signed by the senior-most finance secretary. Utilization of at least 75 percent of the first installment has to be shown in Form 12-B.

That is a bottleneck by design. It is aimed at idle scheme balances. It also means a state with one stubborn scheme account can hold up a capital installment that has nothing to do with that scheme. Contractors on a road or a water work will experience a finance-department problem as a payment problem. The guideline's five-sector priority adds a second queue: departments outside the five sectors the state nominates do not originate the untied project list. They wait, or they hope the incentive money, once earned, is spent their way. The guideline allows incentive proceeds to fund capital works in any sector. It does not let every department pose a project.

## Local bodies hold the service volume and a tied grant history

The December 2025 NeSDA Way Forward report, via PIB on 22 January 2026, puts 8,656 of 24,090 e-services in local governance and utility services. The same note says citizen interactions largely occur at the municipal level, and it illustrates city platforms with Puducherry and Guwahati. A state unified portal at 100 percent of "its" services can coexist with a thin municipal layer. The release keeps those facts in separate sentences. An execution review should refuse to collapse them.

The money side of local bodies was set for 2021-26 by the Fifteenth Finance Commission. PIB's 1 February 2021 summary: Rs 4,36,361 crore in local government grants, Rs 2,36,805 crore rural, Rs 1,21,055 crore urban, Rs 70,051 crore in health grants through local governments. Basic grants were for cities and towns under a million. Million-plus cities were 100 percent performance-linked through the Million-Plus Cities Challenge Fund. Health facilities at sub-centre, primary and community level were routed through local governments. The bottleneck this design accepted was performance: a large city that misses the challenge fund's conditions does not receive a basic grant as a substitute. The bottleneck it tried to relieve was the inability of panchayats and municipalities to fund primary health infrastructure from their own receipts.

## Two systems that do not see the same complaint

PIB's 10 December 2025 Lok Sabha reply says there is, at present, no API integration between CPGRAMS and state Right to Service systems. Right-to-service laws are how many states promise a deadline on a certificate or a connection. CPGRAMS is how a citizen escalates when the experience fails, and it connects ministries and states on one portal, with an appeal. Without an interface, a delayed service can be inside the statutory clock, inside a grievance file, or in neither, depending on which door the citizen found. DARPG says it is working with state commissions on saturation and on time-bound delivery, and that the National Centre for Good Governance is studying whether those timelines hold across districts. That study is an admission that the Centre does not yet have a single operational picture.

## The district is the job, and it is under-measured

NITI Aayog's Aspirational Districts Programme, from January 2018, covers 112 districts and says plainly that states are the drivers while NITI anchors and ministries take pieces of the indicator set. The collaboration it names includes the district collector. The ranking is a monthly delta. That is an execution model aimed at the bottleneck of a distant state capital. It covers 112 districts, not the whole map.

Mission Karmayogi, meanwhile, told Parliament through PIB on 11 March 2026 that district-level monitoring of learning performance is not undertaken on iGOT, even as 29 states and UTs have MoUs and more than 1.51 crore users were onboarded as on 6 March 2026. The people who run the district can be on the platform. The platform does not yet tell a chief secretary which district has actually learned. That gap sits underneath every scheme that assumes a trained block or municipal cadre.
