# Consumption and capital formation in the same quarter

Retail capex is not a line in India’s quarterly national accounts. What MoSPI does publish, side by side, is private final consumption and gross fixed capital formation. For a reader watching whether the cycle is a spending cycle or an investment cycle, that pair is the official watch.

In April–June 2026-27, at constant 2022-23 prices, GFCF grew 11.9% to ₹27,95,605 crore. PFCE grew 7.1% to ₹44,74,278 crore. A year earlier the same two rates were 5.8% and 6.8%. Over that one-year gap, investment growth moved from trailing consumption to leading it.

At current prices the share shifted with the growth gap. GFCF was 34.3% of nominal GDP in the quarter, against 31.4% in April–June 2025-26. Nominal GFCF growth was 20.4%. PFCE’s nominal share eased from 55.8% to 55.6%. Consumption stayed the larger block. Investment took a bigger share than it had in the year-earlier quarter.

## Indicators MoSPI actually ties to the expenditure side

The press note’s annex is the place to see which activity indicators entered the quarter. Household vehicle registrations rose 15.9% year on year, after 8.7%. Sales of commercial vehicles rose 18.3%, after a 0.3% decline. The IIP for capital goods rose 15.2%. Finished steel consumption rose 8.3%. These are inputs MoSPI lists. They are not a census of store fit-outs, dark stores, or retail working capital.

The production side offers one more related line. Real GVA in trade, hotels, transport, communication, broadcasting-related services and storage grew 8.5%. Overall real GVA grew 8.2%. Construction grew 7.7%. None of those is a series for retail capex.

Users should treat the quarter as open to revision. MoSPI says source-agency corrections flow into later rounds, and it has scheduled the July–September estimates for 30 November 2026. The annual detail of the new series is in [National Accounts Statistics 2026](https://mospi.gov.in/publications-reports/innerpage/2861), released with the quarterly note.
