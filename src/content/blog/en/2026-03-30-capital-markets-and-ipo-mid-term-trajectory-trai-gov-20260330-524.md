# The medium-term shape of India's equity issuance market

A medium-term path is a structure that will still be here after one issuance season. SEBI's Annual Report 2025-26 describes four structures that meet that test. None of them is a forecast of the next quarter's subscription rate.

## Domestic institutions are now the larger holder

Chapter 1 says that by March 2026 domestic institutional holdings in the universe it measures were 17 per cent, and foreign portfolio ownership was 15.8 per cent, a 15-year low for the foreign share. Chapter 4 puts the flow under that stock: domestic institutions bought a net ₹8.5 lakh crore of equities in the year, and mutual funds were about three quarters of it, ₹6.4 trillion. The household mechanism is the SIP book in Chapter 5. Contributing accounts rose from 10.05 crore to 10.45 crore. Average net monthly contribution rose from ₹13,052 crore to ₹16,413 crore. Assets through SIPs were ₹15.11 lakh crore.

The same chapter shows the limit of that story. Registrations were 7.2 crore and discontinuations or maturities were 6.8 crore. Net account growth was 3.9 per cent. The trajectory is a large, churning domestic bid, not a one-way accumulation. For the primary market, the anchor rules in Chapter 3 already assume this bid: 33.33 per cent of the anchor portion is reserved for domestic mutual funds, and 6.67 per cent for life insurers and pension funds, inside a 40 per cent reservation. Over a few years, the buyer across the table from a selling shareholder is more likely to be that domestic pool than a foreign portfolio investor. Chapter 2's equity outflow of USD 19.7 billion in 2025-26, concentrated in the secondary market, is the caution. Foreign money can still set the secondary price even when it is a smaller owner.

## Listing can start with a smaller public slice

The minimum public shareholding destination remains 25 per cent. Chapter 3 says the minimum public offer and the path to that 25 per cent were rebuilt as six tiers based on post-issue capital. Large issuers may sell less at the IPO and take longer to reach the threshold. Over the medium term that changes which companies can list. A very large private firm no longer has to place a quarter of itself on day one. It also changes the float. Early trading and index inclusion will reflect a thinner public piece, with later supply as the company walks toward 25 per cent. The tier boundaries belong in the regulation. The direction of travel is what the chapter states.

Demat is already deep enough that this path is operational. Unique demat accounts were 12.2 crore and total accounts 22.5 crore. The pre-filing demat rule for promoters, sellers, key managers, and directors means a large issuer cannot treat dematerialisation as a post-listing cleanup.

## Large deals remain exit-capable, small deals remain the listing engine

From April 2011 to December 2025, 63 per cent of IPO money in SEBI's review went to selling shareholders. The share has eased. In 2025-26 the fresh piece of mixed IPOs, ₹58,166 crore, slightly exceeded the offer-for-sale piece, ₹57,735 crore. Pure fresh IPOs were still only ₹21,866 crore, spread over 214 issues. Pure exits were ₹50,849 crore over 19 issues. The medium-term pattern that fits both the decade and the latest year is this: many small and SME issues raise operating capital, and a short list of large issues still transfers ownership. SME listings were 257 and raised ₹11,587 crore. Main-board IPOs were 109 and raised ₹1.8 lakh crore. Seventy-eight issues above ₹500 crore were 85.8 per cent of public-and-rights money.

Follow-on equity by listed firms is less predictable. QIPs halved to ₹67,853 crore. Preferential allotments rose 76.3 per cent to ₹1,48,219 crore. Rights issues rose 134.2 per cent to ₹46,168 crore, with one deal about 54 per cent of the total. A medium-term model that assumes QIPs fund the capex cycle would have missed 2025-26. A model that watches all three pipes would not.

## Debt and passive money set the background

Corporate debt mobilisation fell 8.4 per cent to ₹9.11 lakh crore, and private placement was 98.8 per cent. That is not an equity story, but it is the alternative a treasurer actually used. Public debt issues grew and were still small, ₹11,343 crore. Municipal bonds reached ₹1,756 crore. REITs raised ₹9,300 crore and InvITs ₹21,026 crore, with net assets of ₹2.4 lakh crore and ₹6.4 lakh crore. Those vehicles are the medium-term home for completed assets. They are not a substitute for a growth IPO.

Passive funds were about 30 per cent of foreign-portfolio equity assets and 28 per cent of domestic mutual fund equity assets at 31 March 2026. The closing auction, scheduled from August 2026 for stocks with derivatives, exists because that money needs a close. Primary issuance will increasingly list into a market whose close is an auction and whose marginal buyer is domestic and partly passive.

The path those facts support is a deeper domestic bid, a legal route for large firms to list in stages, and a continued split between fresh SME paper and exit-heavy large IPOs. It does not support a claim about the index level in 2028.
