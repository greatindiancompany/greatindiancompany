# How the Reserve Bank pulled digital credit back to the regulated lender

The shift in Indian digital credit is not that loans moved to an app. It is that the Reserve Bank refused to let the app become the lender. A platform can acquire the customer, show an offer, and service the account. The loan itself has to sit with an entity the Reserve Bank already regulates, and the money has to move between that entity and the borrower.

## The principle, stated before the consolidated directions

On 10 August 2022 the Reserve Bank published its implementation stance on the Working Group on Digital Lending. The [press release](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=54187) put the principle in one sentence: lending can be carried on only by entities regulated by the Reserve Bank or permitted under another law. The concerns named there are still the ones operators run into: third parties with too much of the customer relationship, mis-selling, data privacy, unfair conduct, very high rates, and recovery practices.

The guidelines that followed on 2 September 2022, and the default-loss-guarantee circular of 8 June 2023, have been repealed. The text that now binds is the [Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12848), issued on 8 May 2025. It applies to commercial banks, urban and state and central co-operative banks, non-banking financial companies including housing finance companies, and all-India financial institutions, when they do digital lending. Digital lending, in these directions, means a remote and automated process for acquisition, assessment, approval, disbursement, recovery, and service.

## What the lender still cannot outsource away

A lending service provider is an agent. The contract has to define roles. Outsourcing does not reduce the regulated entity's liability. Before the loan, the lender has to record enough of the borrower's economic profile to judge creditworthiness, at least age, occupation, and income. A credit limit does not rise automatically. The borrower has to ask, and the lender has to evaluate and keep that request.

Disbursement goes to the borrower's bank account. The exceptions are narrow: a statutory or regulatory mandate, co-lending between regulated entities, and a specific end-use where the money goes to the end beneficiary. It does not go to the lending service provider. Repayment goes to the regulated entity's account, not through a pass-through or pool account that a third party controls. Fees owed to the service provider are paid by the lender, not collected from the borrower on the side.

The borrower gets a key fact statement. On execution, the key fact statement, sanction letter, terms, account statements, and the privacy policies have to reach the borrower by email or SMS as digitally signed documents on the lender's letterhead. Every digital loan is reported to a credit information company, whatever the tenor.

## What 2025 changed in the operating detail

Three updates matter more than the repeal footnote.

The cooling-off floor is now not less than one day, set by the lender's board. The repealed 2022 guidelines had required at least three days for a loan of seven days or more, and one day for a shorter loan. A one-time processing fee may be retained if the borrower exits during cooling-off, but only if the key fact statement disclosed it. Prepayment after that window still follows the Reserve Bank's other rules.

From 1 November 2025, a service provider tied to more than one lender must show a digital view of every matching offer: the lender's name, amount, tenor, annual percentage rate, monthly repayment, and penal charges, plus a link to that lender's key fact statement. Unmatched lenders are named. The screen cannot push one lender with dark patterns. Ranking is allowed only on a metric disclosed in public beforehand.

From 15 June 2025, every digital lending app, whether the lender's or a service provider's, had to be reported on the Reserve Bank's Centralised Information Management System. The Reserve Bank publishes what the lender files and does not verify it. A listing is not a registration, authorisation, or endorsement. The chief compliance officer, or another board-designated official, certifies the filing.

Default loss guarantee, often called first-loss default guarantee, sits inside the same directions. Cover on a portfolio specified upfront cannot exceed five per cent of the amount disbursed from that fixed set of loans. It is accepted only as cash, a lien-marked fixed deposit with a scheduled commercial bank, or a bank guarantee. It is not a substitute for underwriting, it is not available for revolving credit facilities offered digitally or for credit cards, and invoking it does not change the borrower's liability or the lender's asset classification.

## Where this sits next to the other rails

UPI, described by MeitY's [Digital India note on UPI](https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/initiative/unified-payment-interface-upi/), moves money between accounts. It does not make an app a lender. An account aggregator, under the [NBFC-Account Aggregator Directions, 2016](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=10598), may present financial information with consent. It may not support transactions, and the customer's financial information must not reside with the aggregator. Open Credit Enablement Network specifications are a private protocol for wiring loan agents to lenders. They do not create a licence. If a protocol disburses a loan, the 2025 directions still decide who the lender is.
