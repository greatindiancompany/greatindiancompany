# Concentration, Wireless Yield, and the Public Balance Sheet Beside It

This note is a reading of published industry structure. It is not a valuation, a target price, or a view on any listed security. The figures are TRAI's. The public-capital figure beside them is the Press Information Bureau's BharatNet outlay, kept on a separate line so it is not mistaken for private free cash flow.

## Who holds the lines

At end-July 2026, five providers held 98.59 percent of broadband connections, wired and wireless together: Reliance Jio 535.12 million, Bharti Airtel 384.23 million, Vodafone Idea 129.96 million, BSNL 27.28 million, and Atria Convergence Technologies 2.47 million. Private access providers held 92.75 percent of wireless mobile subscribers. BSNL and MTNL together held 7.25 percent. On wireline telephones, BSNL, MTNL, and APSFL together held 16.28 percent. The mobile book and the fixed book do not have the same public-sector weight.

The fixed wired top five held a lower share, 71.71 percent, and included Kerala Vision Broadband. Concentration is a fact about mobile broadband. It is a softer fact about fibre-to-the-building. Fixed wired access was 48.14 million lines. Mobile wireless broadband was 1,027.50 million.

Active wireless subscribers on the peak visitor-location-register day were 1,204.01 million, about 93.48 percent of the wireless mobile base. Airtel's active proportion was 99.12 percent. TRAI warns that visitor-location counts can exceed the home register where roaming is heavy, so a single circle's ratio above 100 is not a data error by itself. The national 93.48 percent is the cleaner "how much of the book is actually on the network" check.

Users are not locked. July portability requests were 15.98 million. Machine-to-machine connections, 137.70 million, are a second book inside the same industry, and Airtel led it with 61.33 percent. An investor comparing only consumer broadband share will miss that lead.

## What the traffic yields

For January–March 2026, monthly wireless ARPU was Rs 196.04. Average use was 26.70 GB per wireless data subscriber per month. Revenue realisation was Rs 7.51 per GB. The quarter's wireless data volume was 77,953 petabytes. Gross revenue was Rs 1,05,118 crore, up 2.58 percent on the previous quarter. Adjusted gross revenue was Rs 86,716 crore, up 2.90 percent. Public-sector undertakings were 3.12 percent of access adjusted gross revenue. Minutes of use were 1,017 per wireless subscriber per month. Voice is still in the ARPU, and the volume growth the network feels is data.

5G fixed wireless, 13.21 million lines at end-July and slightly more rural than urban, is the new revenue line that is not a handset. It was 12.94 million at end-June. The March quarter had grouped 5G fixed wireless and unlicensed-band radio fixed wireless at 16.61 million. The July note's combined fixed-wireless-access summary is 18.35 million. That is a real addition of customer-premises radio. It is still small beside the mobile base.

## What sits on the public balance sheet

The Amended BharatNet Programme is an outlay of Rs 1,39,579 crore in the December 2024 official note, with ten years of maintenance inside the design. Phases I and II are described separately, at about Rs 42,000 crore, for nearly 2.14 lakh gram panchayats. That spending does not appear in the Rs 86,716 crore of industry adjusted gross revenue. Spectrum is a possible future cash outflow: TRAI recommended an auction on 24 February 2026, including 3300 MHz and 26 GHz, and recommended reserve prices. A notice inviting applications is the document that would turn the recommendation into a bid. Until then, booking the reserve-price table as a committed payment is a different decision from reading it.

How those three cash ideas stay apart is the [investment-implications brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/telecom-broadband-investment-implications-mohfw-gov-20260330-232).
