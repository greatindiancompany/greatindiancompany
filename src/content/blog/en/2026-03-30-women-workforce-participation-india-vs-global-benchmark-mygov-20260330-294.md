# The benchmark already inside India’s labour survey

A global league table is only as good as the definition on each row. India’s official row is produced by the Periodic Labour Force Survey. MoSPI prints two rates for the same women, in the same year, and they differ because the reference period differs. That gap is the first benchmark. The second is the male rate in the same table. A third number, taken from an international model and a different reference week, is not substituted here.

## Two Indian rates for one year

For persons aged 15 and above in January–December 2025, female labour force participation in usual status (principal and subsidiary) was 40.0 percent. Male participation was 79.1 percent. The all-person rate was 59.3 percent. On the current weekly status for the same year, female participation was 34.3 percent, male participation was 77.7 percent, and the all-person rate was 55.7 percent. Rural women were at 45.9 percent on usual status and 38.3 percent on the weekly status. Urban women were at 27.7 percent and 25.9 percent.

Usual status uses the 365 days before the survey. Principal status is the activity that took the major time. Subsidiary status adds economic activity of 30 days or more alongside that principal activity. The weekly status uses the seven days before the survey. A woman whose economic work is seasonal, or secondary to unpaid household work for most of the year, can be inside the usual-status labour force and outside a given week. The 40.0 and the 34.3 are both official. Quoting one of them against a foreign weekly rate, and the other against a foreign annual rate, moves India by the gap between those two definitions.

MoSPI’s 2025 sample was redesigned. The Ministry says the 2025 estimates may not be strictly comparable with earlier PLFS annual reports. A benchmark that needs a long Indian time series should use the July–June reports through 2023–24 as one series, and the 2025 calendar report as the next design, with the break marked. In that older series, female usual-status participation rose from 23.3 percent in 2017–18 to 41.7 percent in 2023–24. The 2023–24 weekly rate for women was 35.6 percent. The same definitional gap existed before the redesign.

## The within-India comparison MoSPI actually balances

In 2025 the usual-status male rate is 79.1 percent and the female rate is 40.0 percent. The weekly male rate is 77.7 percent and the female rate is 34.3 percent. Men’s rate barely moves when the window shortens. Women’s rate moves more. That pattern is what a subsidiary-and-seasonal reading predicts, and it is visible without leaving MoSPI’s tables.

Unemployment is not where the gap sits. Usual-status unemployment was 3.1 percent for women and 3.1 percent for men aged 15 and above. Urban women were at 6.4 percent. People outside the labour force are the larger group. Among women in that group, 44.4 percent named child care or personal commitments in home-making as the main reason. Among men, 69.8 percent named continuing studies. A global comparison that treats India’s gap as “women cannot find jobs” is describing a different fact from the one MoSPI records. The recorded fact is a lower rate of being in the labour force, with home-making and child care as the leading reason given by women who are outside it.

Time use, measured separately by MoSPI for January–December 2024, shows the same structure in hours. Women participants spent 289 minutes a day on unpaid domestic services for household members. Men participants spent 88 minutes. Participation in that activity was 81.5 percent for women and 27.1 percent for men, among persons aged 6 and above. Those minutes are not in the labour force rate. They are why a weekly employment question and a yearly employment question can both be accurate and still differ.

## How to hold a foreign figure next to India

If a foreign statistical office uses a one-week or four-week reference period, the Indian number beside it is the current weekly status, 34.3 percent for women in 2025, or the latest quarterly weekly rate. The April–June 2026 quarterly weekly rate was 33.2 percent. If the foreign figure is closer to a long reference year that includes secondary activity, the Indian number is the usual-status 40.0 percent. Age bands have to match. PLFS headlines above are 15 and above. A rate for all ages, or for 15–59, is a different cell in the same report.

The male-female pair inside one Indian table remains the comparison that does not depend on someone else’s model. Rural 45.9 percent against urban 27.7 percent, on the same usual-status definition, is the geographic comparison inside that table.
