A scenario is useful when it says what can be imposed, by whom, and from when. The DPDP Schedule is easy to quote and easy to mis-time.

## The Schedule, as ceilings

The Schedule, read with section 33, sets the maximum monetary penalty:

| Breach | Ceiling |
| --- | --- |
| Reasonable security safeguards, section 8(5) | May extend to ₹250 crore |
| Intimation of a breach to the Board and the affected person, section 8(6) | May extend to ₹200 crore |
| Additional obligations for children, section 9 | May extend to ₹200 crore |
| Additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary, section 10 | May extend to ₹150 crore |
| Duties of the Data Principal, section 15 | May extend to ₹10,000 |
| A voluntary undertaking accepted by the Board, section 32 | Up to the penalty applicable to the breach in the underlying inquiry |
| Any other provision of the Act or the Rules | May extend to ₹50 crore |

"May extend to" is the Act's phrase. The Schedule is not a tariff that is levied in full, and section 42 says a later amendment cannot raise a penalty to more than twice the amount originally enacted. That cap on future increases is in the Act. It is not a statement that the government has amended the Schedule.

## The date on which those ceilings can be used

Sections 28 to 34, which include the inquiry and the penalty, come into force eighteen months after 13 November 2025. That is 13 May 2027. Sections 8, 9, 10, and 15, which are the duties the Schedule refers to, are on the same date. Quoting ₹250 crore as a fine the Board can levy in September 2026 is ahead of the commencement notification.

There is a narrow earlier function. On 13 November 2026, section 27(1)(d) alone lets the Board inquire into a breach of a Consent Manager's registration conditions and impose a penalty "as provided in this Act." The rest of section 27, and sections 28 to 34, are still on the May 2027 list. A reader should not treat 13 November 2026 as the day every ceiling becomes collectable. The [ninety-day checklist](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-next-90-days-checklist-meity-gov-20260330-470) keeps that distinction.

This series has not cited a notification that the Board has been established under section 18 or that a Chairperson has been named. A penalty needs a Board that exists. The search-committee rules are not that notification.

## What can already hurt, on a different statute

CERT-In's directions of 28 April 2022 are in force. A service provider, intermediary, data centre, body corporate, or government organisation that notices a reportable incident, including a data breach, a data leak, or an incident affecting AI and machine-learning systems, has six hours to report. Logs must be kept for 180 days in India. The directions say failure to furnish information or non-compliance may invite action under section 70B(7) of the IT Act. That exposure does not wait for May 2027, and it is not the DPDP Schedule.

The Reserve Bank's payment-data rule is also live. The risk there is supervisory, under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, not a DPDP crore figure. Mixing them produces a scenario the law does not contain: "a payment incident costs ₹250 crore under DPDP today."

MeitY's advisory that replaced the 1 March 2024 note asks intermediaries to label under-tested model output. It does not create the Schedule.

## Scenarios that stay inside the text

A security incident in 2026 at a body corporate is, on the documents used here, a CERT-In reporting question first. It becomes a DPDP section 8 question, with the Schedule above it, only once those sections are in force and a Board can inquire.

A company that moves payment data offshore and leaves it there is a Reserve Bank question now. It is not, by itself, a section 16 question until section 16 is in force, and even then section 16(2) keeps the stricter payments law in place.

A startup that is not yet notified under section 10 is not a Significant Data Fiduciary. The ₹150 crore row cannot be pointed at a firm the Central Government has not notified, and the notifying power is itself on the May 2027 list.
