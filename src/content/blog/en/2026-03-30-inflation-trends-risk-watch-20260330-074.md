# Food, fuel, and the October inflation calendar

August 2026 left retail inflation at 4.82% and wholesale inflation at 9.92%, both provisional. The risk that matters next is not a new adjective for that gap. It is whether food and fuel keep carrying the retail index toward the Monetary Policy Committee’s October–December projection of **5.9%**, and whether that rise stays inside those two groups.

The projection is from the [MPC resolution of 5 August 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63287). The realised August retail print is from [MoSPI](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1789381904344_6c792dcf-8a9f-4fca-93d3-99d833bdb358_Press_Release_of_CPI_for_August_2026.pdf). The wholesale print is from the [14 September WPI press note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2309977).

## What the committee said could go wrong

The August resolution names a short list.

The south-west monsoon was described as deficient and uneven, with El Niño conditions, and as a risk to agriculture and rural demand. The same paragraph points to crop diversification, shorter-duration and climate-resilient crops, and water conservation as offsets the government was already using. A later paragraph says El Niño’s effect on the timing and spread of rain remains a risk, while proactive supply management and foodgrain stocks could cushion it.

Global oil prices were described as volatile, with sharp moves in both directions from geopolitical developments, including the resumption of conflict in West Asia in July. The resolution says the near-term oil outlook is blurred for that reason.

The composition risk is stated directly: food, fuel, and other input prices could still turn into a broader rise in inflation, even though the committee judged that this had not happened yet. Headline inflation was expected to rise further, peak in Q3 (October–December 2026), and ease afterward. The peak was attributed primarily to food and fuel. Core excluding precious metals was described as benign, and as likely to line up with core inflation toward the end of the financial year. Core itself is projected at 4.3% for 2026–27.

The year projection is 5.0%. Q3 at 5.9% sits under the 6% upper tolerance. The Reserve Bank’s Annual Report records the 25 March 2026 notification: headline CPI at 4%, with an upper tolerance of 6% and a lower tolerance of 2%, for the national index. [Annual Report chapter](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1463). That band is the national target range. A single month, or a single state, is a different reading.

## What August already showed

A few realised rates tell you where the committee’s list is already visible.

Food inflation was 5.95% in August, from 5.52% in July. Onion was 48.27%, garlic 43.60%, tomato −31.09%, and potato −13.14%. The food risk is a mix of items, not a single vegetable index moving together.

On the wholesale side, fuel and power was 22.93% and mineral oils were 38.48%. Electricity inside that group was −1.73%. Retail “operation of personal transport equipment” was 7.40%, while the housing-and-fuels division was 2.61%. Fuel is already a wholesale shock. It is only partly a household-index shock.

Precious metals are the other split. Silver jewellery was 107.11% and gold, diamond, and platinum jewellery was 35.53%. The personal-care and miscellaneous division was 15.17%. The committee has been separating that from core excluding precious metals, which it put at 2.3–2.5% in May–June. An August rise in headline that is jewellery, rather than rent, health, or a wide set of goods, is a different risk from the one the resolution calls a broad-based increase. Health in August was 1.34%. Actual rents were 2.27%.

Restaurants and accommodation were 8.38%. The resolution had already tied higher restaurant charges to the fuel spike. That line is an early place to look if fuel costs are spreading into services.

## Dates, in the order they fall

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 5–7 October 2026 | Next MPC meeting. The August resolution set this date. The committee will have August CPI and August WPI. It will not have September CPI or September WPI. |
| 12 October 2026 | September CPI, or the next working day if the 12th is a holiday. MoSPI’s August release states this date. |
| 14 October 2026 | September WPI, Output PPI, and trial Input PPI. The August WPI note states this date. |

August CPI is provisional. August WPI was compiled with an 84.4% weighted response rate. June WPI was revised from 9.87% to 9.97% at finalisation. A risk watch that treats 4.82% and 9.92% as finished will miss that revision channel.

## A practical watchlist

Before the October meeting, the useful comparisons are already published: August food at 5.95% against the Q2 projection of 4.7% for the full July–September quarter, with September still missing; WPI fuel at 22.93% against CPI transport-fuel use at 7.40%; jewellery and the miscellaneous division against health, rent, and the May–June core split. After 12 and 14 October, the same comparisons can be rerun for September.

The reading order for those releases is the [checklist for the October meeting](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/inflation-trends-action-checklist-20260330-094).
