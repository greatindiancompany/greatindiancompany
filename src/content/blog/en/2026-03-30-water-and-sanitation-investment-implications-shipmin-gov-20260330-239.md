# Where public money sits in India's water and sanitation programmes

An investment reader needs four different rupee concepts, kept apart. An indicative mission ceiling is not an approved project list. An approved project cost is not money released. Money released is not work physically completed. India's water programmes currently publish all four, for different schemes, on different dates.

Nothing on this page is a return, a tariff, or a valuation.

## Jal Jeevan Mission: ceiling, then a small cash release

The 10 March 2026 Cabinet decision sets the JJM 2.0 ceiling at a total outlay of ₹8.69 lakh crore, with central assistance of ₹3.59 lakh crore. That central figure is the 2019-20 approval of ₹2.08 lakh crore plus an additional central share of ₹1.51 lakh crore. The 13 March 2026 ministry note describes the movement in the total outlay as ₹3.60 lakh crore to ₹8.69 lakh crore. Use the central pair when the question is the Government of India's assistance. Use the total pair when the question is the size of the programme including the state share. They answer different questions.

Cash that the Centre actually reported moving in FY 2026-27, as on 6 August 2026, is much smaller than either ceiling: ₹6,154.96 crore in all. Of that, ₹5,289.09 crore reimbursed ten states for approved schemes they had continued with their own money during the transition into JJM 2.0. The other ₹865.87 crore went out through SNA SPARSH across 13 states, tied to individual schemes.

The investment implication is about timing and control. The large number is an outlay through December 2028. The observed release, five months after the Cabinet decision, is a reimbursement of work already done plus a thin stream of scheme-level payments. A model that books ₹8.69 lakh crore as near-term orders is reading the ceiling as a work order.

## AMRUT 2.0: three official totals, none of them interchangeable

The original Cabinet approval, recorded by the Prime Minister's Office on 12 October 2021, set an indicative outlay of ₹2,77,000 crore, including a central share of ₹76,760 crore, for FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26. Later guideline material published by the Press Information Bureau states a total indicative outlay of ₹2,99,000 crore and discusses a central share that includes provision for ongoing AMRUT works. This brief does not collapse those documents into one "true" outlay. They were written for different perimeters.

What the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs told the Lok Sabha on 6 August 2026 is a third object: State Water Action Plans already approved. That stock was 8,777 projects costing ₹1,97,191.85 crore, including operation and maintenance, with a central share of ₹66,638.59 crore. Inside it, water supply is ₹1,18,968.38 crore across 3,572 projects, and sewerage and septage management is ₹71,133.01 crore across 605 projects. Water-body rejuvenation and parks are the remainder.

Against that approved stock, states had reported, as on 9 July 2026, awards of 7,687 projects with capex of ₹1,37,130.13 crore, and physically completed works of ₹66,472 crore. Approved, awarded, and completed are three balances. An investor who quotes only the approved cost is quoting the largest of them.

## Namami Gange: sanctioned treatment versus completed treatment

Under the Department of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation, Namami Gange's sewerage book till June 2026 stood at 219 projects, ₹35,970 crore, and 6,610 million litres a day taken up, with 146 projects and 4,263 million litres a day completed. The department's own site is [jalshakti-dowr.gov.in](https://jalshakti-dowr.gov.in/). The figures are from the ministry's statement to Parliament, not from a ticker on that homepage.

Completed capacity is the figure that changes river loading. Sanctioned capacity is the figure that fills a pipeline chart. Both are public. They are not the same number.

## What cannot be underwritten from these pages

The Cabinet note does not publish a rural household tariff. It assigns in-village operation and maintenance to arrangements the state must establish before a Gram Panchayat certifies Har Ghar Jal. Urban projects under AMRUT include an operation-and-maintenance component inside the approved cost, which is why that cost is higher than a pure capital estimate. Neither document states a private-equity return.

The texts are the [JJM Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2237548), the [6 August 2026 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295509), the [2021 AMRUT 2.0 Cabinet note](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/cabinet-approves-the-atal-mission-for-rejuvenation-and-urban-transformation-amrut-2-0-till-2025-26/), the [August 2026 AMRUT answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3022_tT0QXq.pdf), and the [Namami Gange answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3190_Ip1l3X.pdf).
