# India’s inflation path into the new CPI base

The retail series readers are looking at in September 2026 is not a seamless continuation of the old headline. MoSPI now publishes the Consumer Price Index on **base 2024=100**. In the 14 September 2026 release, year-on-year inflation on that base begins in January 2026. Earlier months in the annexure show the index, not a year-on-year rate, because the base year itself is 2024.

That limit is the first fact in any long view. The second is the path those eight months actually print.

## Eight months on the new base

Combined inflation from Annexure IV of the [August CPI release](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1789381904344_6c792dcf-8a9f-4fca-93d3-99d833bdb358_Press_Release_of_CPI_for_August_2026.pdf):

| Month | Rural | Urban | Combined |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January 2026 | 2.73% | 2.75% | 2.74% |
| February | 3.37% | 3.02% | 3.21% |
| March | 3.63% | 3.11% | 3.40% |
| April | 3.74% | 3.16% | 3.48% |
| May | 4.25% | 3.53% | 3.93% |
| June | 4.74% | 3.93% | 4.38% |
| July (final) | 4.84% | 3.96% | 4.45% |
| August (provisional) | 5.23% | 4.31% | 4.82% |

The combined rate moved from under 3% at the start of the calendar year to just under 5% by August. It crossed 4% in June. Rural inflation has been above urban inflation in every month in this table after January, when the two were almost level.

The combined index was 101.67 in January 2025 and 108.74 in August 2026. Those levels are on the new base. They are not percentage changes.

## How to connect this series to the old one

MoSPI’s FAQ on the new series gives linking factors at the general level for back series on base 2024=100: rural **0.5222**, urban **0.5320**, combined **0.5267**. [Annexure V](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/documents/documents/1770891066052-Annexure_V.pdf).

The basket changed with the base. Food and beverages are 36.75% of the combined 2024 index under COICOP 2018. On the 2012 classification they were 45.86%. A long chart that pastes old food-inflation rates onto new ones is comparing different weights. The linking factor handles the index level. It does not make the baskets identical.

## What the Reserve Bank says about the longer stretch

The new MoSPI table does not, by itself, show 2024 or 2025 inflation. For that, the usable official account is the Reserve Bank’s.

The Annual Report dated 29 May 2026 says headline CPI trended down in 2025–26, mainly because of food, and reached an all-time low in the 2012=100 series in October 2025. It records the policy path that accompanied that decline: a 25 basis point cut in April 2025, a 50 basis point cut in June 2025 that took the repo rate to 5.50%, and a further 25 basis point cut in December 2025 that took it to **5.25%**. At the December meeting the report says average headline inflation had moderated to 1.7%, below the 2% lower tolerance, and the projection for 2025–26 was revised to 2.0%. [Monetary policy chapter](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1463).

A speech by Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta on 5 May 2026 puts a longer average on the record: headline CPI inflation averaged **8.1% in 2006–16** and **4.6% in 2016–26**. The speech gives 2025 headline inflation at **2.2%**, against 5.2% for emerging market and developing economies and 4.1% for the world in that year. The range of Indian inflation in the inflation-targeting years is given as 0.3–7.8%, compared with 3.3–13.4% in the decade before. [Speech](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1557).

These are Reserve Bank figures, including for 2025. They are not rows in the August 2026 CPI annexure, and the October 2025 low is described on the old 2012 base. Keeping the base attached to the number is the whole point of reading the trend this year.

## Where August sits against the target

The same Annual Report records the Gazette notification of 25 March 2026: headline CPI at 4%, upper tolerance 6%, lower tolerance 2%, from 1 April 2026 through 31 March 2031. August 2026 at 4.82% is above the 4% point and inside the band. The MPC’s August projection, made before this print, has the year at 5.0% and the October–December quarter at 5.9%.

The August resolution also said June, at 4.4% on the committee’s rounding, was the first month above the target after 16 months below it. That sentence is the bridge between the quiet 2025 the Annual Report describes and the climb in the 2026 table.

The policy mechanics of the band are in the companion note on [the target and the August hold](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/inflation-trends-policy-explainer-20260330-054).
