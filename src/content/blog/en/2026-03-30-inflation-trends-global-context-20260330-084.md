# The external setting around India’s fuel inflation

India’s August inflation story meets the rest of the world mainly through fuel. The Monetary Policy Committee’s resolution of 5 August 2026 is the official account of that setting. The resolution gives the external picture in words. This note stays with that text, plus the Indian price releases that show how fuel landed at home.

The text is the [MPC resolution](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63287), also carried in the [August Bulletin](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24366). The Bulletin issue was announced on 25 August 2026. [Bulletin press release](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63444).

## What the August resolution says about the world

The resolution describes 2026 so far as a year of sharp market swings, persisting inflation concerns, and shifting policy expectations. It says a temporary ceasefire in West Asia gave way to renewed conflict in July. It says persistent inflation has prompted several central banks to raise rates, while others remain watchful. It says the US dollar appreciated, supported by elevated yields, a hawkish Federal Reserve tone, and a relatively strong US economy linked to productivity gains from artificial intelligence. Global equities were volatile as investors repriced AI-related stocks.

The risks it lists for the outlook are the West Asia conflict, volatile oil prices, sticky inflation expectations, and fragile public finances in systemic economies.

On the domestic side of the same document, energy prices and supply-chain pressures are described as elevated and uncertain. The committee says the damage is being contained with supply-side measures, and that global oil prices have moved sharply in both directions because of geopolitics, which blurs the near-term outlook. It expects headline inflation in India to rise further and to peak in October–December 2026 primarily because of food and fuel.

That is the external channel in the resolution: oil and geopolitics into Indian fuel inflation, with food as the other domestic driver. It is not a claim that Indian retail inflation has converged to a foreign rate.

## How that channel showed up in Indian prices

The Indian numbers that match this paragraph are domestic official prints, not imported CPI rates.

Provisional wholesale inflation in August was **9.92%**. Fuel and power was **22.93%**. Mineral oils, the petroleum-product group, were **38.48%**, up from 32.40% in July. Crude petroleum and natural gas were 34.41%. Electricity was −1.73%. The source is the [14 September WPI press note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2309977). For April–August 2026–27, cumulative fuel-and-power inflation was 25.20%, against −3.73% in April–August 2025–26.

Retail inflation did not absorb that wholesale fuel rate one for one. Combined CPI was 4.82% in August. The housing, water, electricity, gas, and other fuels division was 2.61%. The group for operating personal transport equipment was 7.40%. The MPC, looking at data through June, said cost pass-through was still limited and that higher inflation was mostly food and fuel.

A firm that imports fuel, chemicals, or metals feels the wholesale and global side. A household index feels a smaller slice of it. Both readings are in the Indian releases. The cost side for smaller firms is set out in the note on [wholesale inflation and invoices](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/inflation-trends-impact-on-smes-20260330-024).

## A dated comparison, not an August world rate

For a longer comparison, Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta’s speech on 5 May 2026 reports average inflation in **2025** of 2.2% in India, 5.2% in emerging market and developing economies, and 4.1% in the world. Over 2016–25 the speech puts India at 4.4%. [Speech](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1557).

Those are 2025 and decadal averages in an RBI speech. They are not August 2026 global CPI. They do explain the backdrop the committee inherited: India had just come through a year of unusually low inflation relative to the emerging-market average the speech cites, and by August 2026 the domestic headline was climbing again, with fuel the external piece.

The August Bulletin’s “State of the Economy” note, summarised in the 25 August press release, says the global economy is confronting a fragile geopolitical environment and continuing trade-related uncertainties, and that the domestic economy has stayed resilient. It says headline CPI inflation rose fractionally, reflecting food, and that core remained stable. That Bulletin sentence describes conditions up to its publication on 25 August. It is earlier than the 14 September CPI release, which put August inflation at 4.82% after July’s 4.45%.

## What not to borrow from a global headline

A foreign CPI rate for August 2026 is not in these Reserve Bank documents. An oil price in dollars is not in them either. Using one from a market screen, without saying so, would invent a precision the committee itself did not put in the resolution. The operational facts the resolution and the Indian price releases do support are these: several central banks were dealing with persistent inflation and some were raising rates; oil was volatile because of West Asia; India’s wholesale fuel group was at 22.93% in August; India’s retail headline was at 4.82%, with personal transport operation at 7.40%.
