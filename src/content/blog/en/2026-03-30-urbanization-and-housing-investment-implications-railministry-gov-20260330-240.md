# Where Urban Housing Capital Is Actually Committed

## Three pools, three owners of risk

An investment memo that says “urbanisation capex” and then adds every MoHUA scheme is adding unlike things. One pool pays a household or a lender. One pool builds water and used-water systems through cities. One pool has stopped taking new central projects.

## Household assistance, already sized by Cabinet

The December 2024 PIB background note on PMAY-Urban 2.0 states that the Cabinet, on 9 August 2024, approved assistance for 1 crore additional urban poor and middle-class families. Government assistance is ₹2.30 lakh crore. The note states an investment of ₹10 lakh crore. That investment figure is the ministry’s published scale for the scheme. It is not a forecast of private equity deployment, and this brief does not convert it into a return.

For the first PMAY-Urban, the same note reports, over the preceding eight to nine years, total investment of ₹8,07,393 crore, central assistance sanctioned of about ₹2 lakh crore, and central assistance released of ₹1.66 lakh crore, against 1.18 crore houses sanctioned. Those are stock figures as printed in that note. The July 2025 PIB reply adds the closed CLSS result: ₹58,885 crore of interest subsidy to 25,04,220 beneficiaries. A new credit book has to fit the Interest Subsidy Scheme caps, which are set out in the market-signal brief.

The risk on this pool sits with beneficiary identification, state memoranda, and, for the subsidy, with the loan staying alive and more than half the principal remaining outstanding. It does not sit with a city special purpose vehicle.

## Water and sewerage, sized in the 2021 guidelines

PIB’s 27 October 2021 release on the AMRUT 2.0 operational guidelines states an indicative outlay of ₹2,99,000 crore, including ₹22,000 crore for the ongoing first AMRUT mission, for five years from 2021-22 to 2025-26. The central share is ₹86,760 crore, including ₹10,000 crore for those ongoing projects. The targets named there are household taps in 4,700 statutory towns, and sewerage or septage coverage in the 500 AMRUT cities, with 2.68 crore new tap connections and 2.64 crore sewer or septage connections proposed.

The same release says 10 percent of funds allocated to million-plus cities are to be implemented in public-private partnership mode, and that project funds move in instalments of 20:40:40. From the third year, uninterrupted funding depends on property-tax and user-charge reforms. That is the investable condition: a city that does not clear the reform gate can lose the later instalments. The published window runs through financial year 2025-26. This brief does not extend it.

## Smart Cities capital is in the utilisation phase

MoHUA’s mission page states a central outlay of ₹48,000 crore for 100 cities, financial closure on 31 March 2025, claims of ₹47,458 crore, and no further budgetary provision. No new city is to be taken up. The Lok Sabha reply of 6 August 2026 adds the private slice that was actually taken up: 197 public-private partnership projects worth ₹9,277 crore, of which 194 worth ₹9,190 crore were completed, as reported as on 31 March 2026. Against 8,064 projects worth ₹1,64,811 crore, the partnership book is a small, mostly finished subset.

Post-closure value, if any, is operating the command centres and repurposing the special purpose vehicles under Advisory No. 27 of 10 June 2025. That is a service contract on assets already built. It is not a second ₹48,000 crore.

A position that needs new central Smart Cities tenders is outside the published budget. A position that needs the interest subsidy must live inside the loan, property, and carpet caps. A position in urban water has to name the city plan, the reform gate, and the fact that the guideline horizon was financial year 2025-26.
