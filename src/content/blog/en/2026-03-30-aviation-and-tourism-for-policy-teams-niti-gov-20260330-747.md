# How a state should read UDAN before it nominates a strip

A state aviation or tourism cell preparing a Modified UDAN nomination has one public scorecard and one public filter. Mixing them up is how the last round produced routes that did not stay.

## Count the network that is still flying

On 23 July 2026 the ministry told Parliament that 679 routes had been operationalised and 348 were still operational. The annexure of routes discontinued before the viability-gap tenure ended sums to 165, led by Uttar Pradesh (34), Uttarakhand (17), Assam (15), Madhya Pradesh (14), and Karnataka (12). A state whose early-exit number is high should read that as its own execution record before it asks for a longer list.

As on 10 July 2026 the cumulative result of the routes that did fly was 95 airports, including 17 heliports and 2 water aerodromes, 168 lakh passengers, and 3.58 lakh flights. Quote those when the question is “what did the scheme deliver.” Quote 348 when the question is “what can a passenger still board.”

Tamil Nadu’s 30 July annexure is a model of the distinction. Four airports are identified for development and operation: Salem, Vellore, Neyveli, and Tanjore. The operationalised-route list is a different and shorter kind of fact, mostly Salem and Chennai spokes, with Salem–Bengaluru printed twice and no explanation of the duplicate. Identified, operationalised, and still flying are three statuses. A press note that collapses them will not survive the next parliamentary question.

## File what the challenge actually scores

The 6 August statement lists the tests for a state nomination: encumbrance-free land, infrastructure readiness, technical and operational feasibility, traffic potential, statutory clearances, and the state government’s support. Routes the state recommends can be placed on a priority list for award. That is the new sequence. Under the old one, airlines chose from a pool and development followed the choice.

The definition of “served” decides whether a strip belongs in the scheme at all. In the year before the scheme is published, an airport is served if it has more than 14 scheduled departures a week, connections to more than two city pairs, and more than 40,000 passengers. Priority areas raise the departure bar above 21 a week. A strip just under those lines is the candidate. A strip already well above them is not an unserved victory to announce.

Support for a route that is won tapers after year two, and salary support at the aerodrome is capped. Policy teams should not put the full ₹3.06 crore manpower ceiling into a state budget as if it were a grant already received.

## Keep the tourism statistic in its own column

Domestic tourist visits are state-department counts of visits, not of unique residents and not of airline passengers. In 2024 the national total was 2,948.19 million. Uttar Pradesh’s 646.81 million domestic visits did not prevent 34 early UDAN exits. Maharashtra’s lead in foreign tourist visits (3.70 million) is not a lead in the domestic table. A nomination that uses the visit total as if it were the 40,000-passenger airport test is using the wrong series. The airport test is passengers at that aerodrome. The visit total is a tourism-department census of trips to the state.

Foreign air arrivals are more concentrated than state visit tables suggest. In 2024, Delhi and Mumbai airports handled 57.2 percent of foreign arrivals who came by air. A new regional airport’s published rationale is domestic and regional access, including to circuits the tourism ministry has already built, not an immediate international gateway.
