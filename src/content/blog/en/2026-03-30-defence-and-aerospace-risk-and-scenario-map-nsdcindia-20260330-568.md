# Five defence-industry risks that are already in the public rules

## What this map refuses to do

A risk map for a factory, a fund, or a ministry desk can be built from published rules. It does not need force dispositions, weapon performance, or a guess about a classified programme. Those topics are outside this note. The five risks below are failures of timing, permission, or arithmetic that the government's own texts already allow.

## The list date moves, or development is not accepted

The 18 August 2026 positive-list notice says procurement from Indian industry follows successful indigenous development, on an indicative timeline. Two delays sit inside that sentence. The timeline can be later than a business plan assumed, because the date lives on SRIJAN item by item. Development can also fail the buyer's test. Until both are cleared, the import the list is meant to replace has not been replaced. A revenue forecast that starts on the day of the press note has skipped the gate the press note itself wrote.

## Foreign money can be reviewed after it arrives

Press Note 4 of 2020 allows automatic foreign investment up to 74 percent only for companies seeking a new industrial licence. Other companies hit a 49 percent declaration rule. Above the relevant line, government approval is required. Every case needs security clearance from the Ministry of Home Affairs and must follow Ministry of Defence guidelines. Condition (vi) then reserves a further right: foreign investment in defence is subject to scrutiny on national-security grounds, and the government may review any such investment that affects or may affect national security.

The scenario is not exotic. A shareholder change, a missed declaration, or a review under condition (vi) can reopen a deal that a teaser described as automatic.

## A prototype grant is not a production contract

The iDEX FAQ states that the Defence Innovation Organisation gives no assurance of minimum pilot quantity or of assured commercial quantities. Any order is the nodal agency's decision, under the Defence Acquisition Procedure or whatever procedure is in force. The scheme can fund a prototype up to the grant ceiling and still end without a purchase. Budget use on the services' capital line, which was reported as full for FY 2025-26, does not convert an individual grant into a share of that outlay.

## A space activity can be refused in public

The Indian Space Policy 2023 makes IN-SPACe the single window for authorising space activities by government entities and by non-government entities. Authorisation is subject to government directives and to safety, national security, international obligations, and foreign policy. A refusal on those grounds is a designed outcome of the policy, not a rumour about a particular startup. A defence industrial licence, a foreign-investment approval, and an IN-SPACe authorisation do not travel as a bundle.

## The numbers can be added incorrectly

The most common risk in a board pack is a false total. Production, exports, the Ministry budget, items on a positive list, and vendors on SRIJAN are different series. FY 2024-25 production is stated as Rs 1,50,590 crore in one release and as Rs 1.54 lakh crore in a later note. Private firms dominate export rupees and, on the November 2025 note, account for about 23 percent of production. Mixing any of these into one "sector size" will move a valuation or a policy claim without a new fact.

Great Indian Company stops the map at these five. A sixth box labelled with an adversary's inventory would be speculation, and it would not help a firm that still has to pass a timeline, a clearance, or an authorisation.
