The Union can vote a capital provision in February and still have little built by the monsoon. The bottleneck is not a missing announcement. It is the path from the Consolidated Fund to a project account, and from that account to a measured asset. The 2026-27 papers describe the path. They also leave the slow points visible.

## Three pipes, not one cheque

Capital expenditure in the Budget estimates is ₹12.22 lakh crore. Part of that is spending the Centre books on its own works. Part is a loan. The special assistance to states for capital expenditure is ₹2.0 lakh crore inside the capital line. A loan released to a state is Centre capex in the accounts on the day it is disbursed. The road or the water scheme is state execution after that day.

A third pipe is easy to misfile. Grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets are ₹4.93 lakh crore and are accounted as revenue expenditure. Effective capital expenditure of ₹17.15 lakh crore adds them back. Anyone who monitors only the capital account will miss this pipe, then double-count it if they add the effective total on top of total expenditure. The classification is the first bottleneck: the asset is real in the ministry’s story and revenue in the accounting identity.

## What “just in time” changes

The fiscal statement says the Treasury Single Account, the Single Nodal Agency, and the Central Nodal Agency rules are the Centre’s tools for seeing cash and avoiding parked balances. SNA SPARSH is the just-in-time variant: funds move from the Consolidated Fund of India and the states’ consolidated funds when the system pays, rather than sitting in a layer of agency accounts. The statement says a pilot was notified and that 50 centrally sponsored schemes across states and union territories had been onboarded.

Fifty schemes is not every scheme. A vendor or a state department still on the older route can show a budget head with no corresponding payment. Direct Benefit Transfer is described in the same section as the household version of the same idea: track the rupee until it reaches the intended account. That tracks transfers to people. It does not, by itself, commission a capital work.

The practical test is dull. Is the scheme on SPARSH? Has the state opened the single nodal account? Has the implementing agency passed the validation that releases the next tranche? The Budget speech does not answer those questions project by project. The system status does.

## The last full year showed the lag

The Reserve Bank’s state-finance study, released in January 2026, looked at the earlier size of the interest-free capital loan. For 2024-25, against an allocation of ₹1.5 lakh crore, ₹1,49,484 crore was eventually released, 99.7 percent. The study also says that by 21 October 2025, in the following year, only ₹48,903 crore of that year’s ₹1.5 lakh crore allocation had been released, about one third. The first half was slow. The study says the Centre had relaxed conditionalities and raised the untied portion so that states could move faster later.

Both sentences can be true. The year can close near the allocation, and the first half can still be a bottleneck for contractors who need site payments before March. The 2026-27 provision is larger, at ₹2.0 lakh crore. A larger provision does not remove a first-half release pattern. It raises the cost of repeating it.

Through June 2026, the Controller General’s accounts show loans disbursed at ₹1,00,579 crore, 36.1 percent of the annual loan estimate, and capital expenditure at 27.8 percent of its estimate. That is an early Centre-side release, ahead of last year’s capital pace. It is not evidence that states have booked the corresponding capital outlay. State accounts lag the Union monthly print. The Reserve Bank’s study is the place those state outlays are consolidated, and the next edition will be the one that can see 2026-27.

## Where execution usually fails in the reading, not only on the ground

Readers fail in predictable ways. They treat a Budget estimate as an authorisation already cash-backed. They treat a loan disbursed as an asset commissioned. They add effective capital expenditure to total expenditure. They ignore the accounts controller’s warning that the fiscal deficit through a given month is mistimed and will not match one quarter of 4.3 percent.

The Centre’s stated strategy for the year still “supports and strengthens the capital spending of states” and uses technology for expenditure management. Those are the government’s words for the remedy. The residual bottleneck is everything those systems have not onboarded, plus the state’s own ability to spend a loan without blowing its deficit ceiling. Sixteen states, in the Reserve Bank’s reading of 2025-26 budgets, had already set their own deficit above 3 percent of state product. A state near its ceiling can be slow to take a capital loan even when the Centre has provided it.
