# Green Hydrogen Jobs in India: An Aim of Over 6 Lakh, and a Training Count

The largest jobs number in the official hydrogen record is a projection. The smaller number is a training count. They answer different questions, and a district plan that uses only the larger one is using the wrong document.

## What the Mission says it expects

On 17 June 2026, Minister Pralhad Joshi said the Mission aims to create over 6 lakh jobs. The sentence also carries 5 million tonnes of production capacity, 125 GW of renewable capacity, investment expected above ₹8 lakh crore, and a reduction of about 50 million tonnes a year in carbon emissions. The Press Information Bureau note of 12 November 2025 uses the same job expectation. Neither document says those jobs exist on a payroll today. The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) is quieter still. It says a coordinated skill-development programme, covering requirements in various segments, will be undertaken with the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship. It does not print a headcount.

## What has been counted as training

The November 2025 note says the skill programme has already led to certification of more than 5,600 trainees in hydrogen-related qualifications. That is educational output. It does not, in that note, say which trades were certified, or whether the person is employed on a hydrogen plant. More than 5,600 certificates against an expectation of over 6 lakh jobs is a gap large enough to state plainly. It is also early relative to a 2030-style outcome. It is not evidence that the expectation has failed, and it is not evidence that the jobs have arrived.

## The scheme dates a college or a state mission can use

MNRE’s [guidelines list](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/) includes “Guidelines for Skill development under the National Green Hydrogen Mission,” dated 16 March 2024. On 23 March 2026 the same list adds a call for proposals for skilling, up-skilling, and re-skilling. A call for proposals is an invitation to submit a programme. It is not a placement report, and this brief does not invent the grant size of that call, because the figure lives in the document itself rather than in the index line.

## Where work sits, without a vacancy census

The projects imply skills. They do not publish a vacancy count, so this brief will not assign one.

Electrolyser factories under the award to 15 companies, for 3,000 MW a year of manufacturing capacity, need production technicians if those lines are built. Port hubs need people who can handle ammonia and methanol. Kandla, Tuticorin, and Paradip are the ports Parliament treated as hubs on 5 August 2026, and the annex is full of methanol, ammonia, and bunkering items. Certification needs verifiers: the Bureau of Energy Efficiency accredits the agencies that check the 2 kg standard, and the certification portal was launched on 17 June 2026. Refuelling stations on the 21 routes in the 29 July 2026 pilot annex need operators who can support the efficiency and safety reports that answer requires.

Each of those is a skill demand implied by a scheme that already exists. A training firm, a polytechnic, or a state skill mission can answer the March 2026 call and map a course to one of those tasks. It cannot turn “over 6 lakh jobs” into a guaranteed placement scheme. Great Indian Company states the larger figure as the Mission’s expectation and the 5,600 figure as the training count given in November 2025.
