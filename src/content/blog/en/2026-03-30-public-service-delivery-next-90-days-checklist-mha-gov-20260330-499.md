# A 90-day watchlist after the NeSDA 2025 portal launch

From 24 September 2026, the next ninety days run into late December. The official process already open in that window is the National e-Governance Service Delivery Assessment cycle. The [28 May 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266244) says DARPG launched the NeSDA 2025 portal on 25 May 2026, walked nodal officers through submission workflows and timelines, and will hold consultations, capacity-building sessions, and review meetings during the cycle. The release does not print the calendar dates from that walkthrough. A checklist that invents a filing deadline would be stricter than the note. This one sticks to checks the notes support.

## For a department nodal officer

Open the assessment guidelines the 28 May release told nodal officers to review. Confirm whether the service you own is inside the proposed 59 mandatory services for a state or Union Territory, or inside the 43 proposed for central ministries. The sectors named in the release include finance, labour and employment, education, local governance and utilities, social welfare, environment, tourism, public grievance, and transport.

Score yourself against the parameters the release lists, before DARPG does. The ones that usually surprise a team that already has a website are end service delivery, integrated service delivery, and status and request tracking. An information page can pass a content check and fail the service. The release also splits portals into government portals and service portals. Know which of the two you are submitting.

Coordinate with the state or ministry nodal officer. The release says data are collected on the portal from states, Union Territories, and central ministries. A vendor dashboard is not that submission.

## For an operator shipping a citizen flow

Check the document class. DigiLocker's [note](https://blog.digilocker.gov.in/digilocker-an-initiative-towards-paperless-governance/) ties the "at par with original" claim to issued documents fetched from the issuer under Rule 9A. If your flow accepts an uploaded scan, say so in the interface. Do not label it as an issued document.

If the flow pays a person, confirm the account. UIDAI's [DBT FAQ](https://uidai.gov.in/en/308-faqs/direct-benefit-transfer-dbt.html) says benefits land in the one account the person mandated for seeding, which the bank then maps at NPCI. Ask the scheme owner whether Aadhaar is required for that scheme. The DBT Mission says Aadhaar is not mandatory for DBT schemes as a class. Section 7 of the Aadhaar Act still allows a government to require it for a Consolidated Fund benefit.

If the flow is a state certificate, test the state portal, not only UMANG. The [6 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635) says state portals and apps deliver certificates and pensions, and it records 17 Tamil Nadu services on UMANG against 2,575 services nationally. A pass on the national app can hide a failure on the state status page.

## For a citizen team handling complaints

File and track on [CPGRAMS](https://pgportal.gov.in/) with the registration number. The portal is free to the government; a CSC counter may still collect a charge that the portal says goes to the CSC. After closure, a "Poor" rating is what enables the appeal. Keep the number. A social-media post is not the appeal.

Re-read the 6 August counts only if you need a baseline: DigiLocker issuers at 2,822, UMANG at 2,575 individual services, DBT at 318 schemes of 56 ministries. Movement after those dates belongs in a later parliamentary note, not in a guessed December figure.

The rules behind each check are in the [regulatory outlook](/blog/public-service-delivery-regulatory-outlook-mha-gov-20260330-459).
