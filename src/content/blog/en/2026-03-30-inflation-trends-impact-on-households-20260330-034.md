# What August 2026 prices do to a household basket

The household index is the Consumer Price Index, base 2024=100. In August 2026 it rose **4.82%** from a year earlier on a combined basis. That provisional figure, published by MoSPI on 14 September 2026, is an average across India and across the basket. A rural household, a city household, and a household that spends heavily on vegetables or on jewellery did not experience the same 4.82%.

The release is here: [CPI for August 2026](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1789381904344_6c792dcf-8a9f-4fca-93d3-99d833bdb358_Press_Release_of_CPI_for_August_2026.pdf). Weights come from MoSPI’s FAQ on the new series: [Annexure V](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/documents/documents/1770891066052-Annexure_V.pdf).

## The basket is less food-heavy than the old one, and still food-heavy in villages

Weights follow the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey 2023–24 and the COICOP 2018 classification. Under that classification, food and beverages are **36.753%** of the combined index, **41.983%** of the rural index, and **30.251%** of the urban index. MoSPI’s own summary rounds the combined food-and-beverages share to 36.75%.

The same FAQ explains why this is lower than figures many readers remember. On the old CPI 2012 classification, food and beverages were 45.86% of the combined basket. If that old classification were applied to the new expenditure data, the share would be 40.10%. The further drop to 36.75% is the change of classification, including the way restaurants are separated from food bought for the home.

Food inflation and restaurant inflation are therefore different lines. In August the Consumer Food Price Index was **5.95%** combined, 6.13% rural, and 5.64% urban. The “food and beverages” division was 5.66%. Restaurants and accommodation services were **8.38%**, and food-and-beverage serving services inside that division were 8.41%.

## Rural and urban prints

| | Rural | Urban | Combined |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CPI inflation, August (provisional) | 5.23% | 4.31% | 4.82% |
| CPI inflation, July (final) | 4.84% | 3.96% | 4.45% |
| Food inflation, August | 6.13% | 5.64% | 5.95% |

Rural inflation is higher, and food has a larger weight in the rural basket. Both facts are in the official tables. Together they mean a food acceleration lands harder outside cities. State differences are larger still; those are in the [state table note](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/inflation-trends-regional-view-20260330-064).

## What was expensive, and what was not

MoSPI’s list of high item rates in August, combined:

- Silver jewellery, weight 0.3127: **107.11%** (109.89% in July).
- Ginger, weight 0.2556: **73.82%**.
- Onion, weight 0.7006: **48.27%**, up from 22.54% in July.
- Garlic, weight 0.3738: **43.60%**.
- Gold, diamond, and platinum jewellery, weight 0.6230: **35.53%**.

Low item rates:

- Tomato, weight 0.4961: **−31.09%**.
- Potato, weight 0.7549: **−13.14%**.
- Motor car and jeep, weight 0.4665: **−6.72%**.
- Lady’s finger: **−5.41%**.

Vegetables are not one price. Onion and garlic accelerated while tomato and potato were down on a year ago. A household that buys the first pair felt the month. A household that buys the second pair did not, at least on these year-on-year rates.

## The quiet parts of the basket

Several large, regular bills were well below the headline:

- Health: **1.34%**. Medicines and health products were 1.19%.
- Actual rental payments: **2.27%**.
- Housing, water, electricity, gas, and other fuels, the whole division: **2.61%**.
- Electricity, gas, and other fuels as a group: **3.09%**.
- Information and communication: **2.01%**.
- Clothing and footwear: **3.56%**.
- Education services: **3.73%**. The FAQ puts the weight of education services at 3.3% of the 2024 basket; books and stationery sit elsewhere.

The fast non-food lines were elsewhere. Operation of personal transport equipment was **7.40%**. Transport services of goods were 14.64%. The personal-care and miscellaneous division was **15.17%**, pulled by “other personal effects” at 44.56%. That is the jewellery group showing through a division that is only 5.038% of the combined basket.

The Reserve Bank’s August resolution said core inflation excluding food and fuel stayed at 3.9% in May and June, and that the same measure excluding precious metals was 2.3–2.5% in that period. August’s item table is consistent with that distinction: a small jewellery weight can lift a division without describing the price of rent, medicine, or school fees. MoSPI did not publish an August core rate in this release.

## What this does and does not say

The index says the average combined basket cost 4.82% more than in August 2025, on the 2024 base, and that the figure is provisional. It does not say every household’s spending rose by 4.82%. Rural food, onion, eating out, and jewellery ran hotter. Health, rent, and several vegetables ran cooler. Anyone pricing a product to households, or reading wage pressure off “inflation,” needs that split.
