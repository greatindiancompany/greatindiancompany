# What LODR still requires after the July 2026 text

ICDR gets a company listed. The Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015, decide whether it stays listable. The consolidated text on SEBI's website was last amended on 14 July 2026. The outlook that can be stated from official paper is the shape of that text and of the circulars already issued under it. It is not a guess about the next board memorandum.

## The July book is the one to cite

Any internal policy that still quotes a 2015 original, or a consolidation from January 2026, is behind the legal page. The 14 July 2026 text is the citation. Two neighbouring instruments belong in the same folder. Gazette notification SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2026/312 of 10 July 2026 amended regulation 40(7). Circular HO/38/13/11(14)2026-MIRSD-POD/I/17111/2026 of 23 July 2026 then specified transmission: a harmonised process, a low-value Quick Transmission Processing path, no mandatory probate, a combined affidavit and no-objection, and acceptance of a QR-coded death certificate. It applies to listed securities and to mutual fund units after the death of the sole holder or of every joint holder. It starts 30 days after the circular date. Contested claims stay outside it.

Chapter 4 of the Annual Report 2025-26 records further LODR amendments that are already part of how the regulation works, whether or not a reader has matched each one to a sub-clause. Regulation 39 no longer routes duplicate or corporate-action paper through a letter of confirmation. The registrar credits the demat account after due diligence. SEBI describes that credit moving from about 150 days to about 30 days. A new regulation 39(2A) requires securities issued in a consolidation, a split, or a scheme of arrangement to be issued in demat, so a corporate action cannot mint a fresh physical holding. A one-year window, 5 February 2026 to 4 February 2027, lets a holder of original certificates and a pre-1 April 2019 transfer deed lodge them for transfer, excluding fraud and dispute cases.

## Governance thresholds the annual report says were rewritten

Chapter 3 is the plain-language account of LODR changes made through 2025-26. They are the substance an outlook has to include, because they are already in force and they change who approves what.

Related-party transactions moved off a flat ₹1,000 crore materiality line to a scale tied to consolidated turnover, with a cap of up to ₹5,000 crore. For SMEs the line is ₹50 crore or 10 per cent, effective 1 April 2025. A subsidiary transaction needs audit-committee approval when it crosses both ₹1 crore and 10 per cent of the subsidiary's turnover. Transactions under ₹1 crore skip the detailed disclosure. Omnibus shareholder approvals have a validity period written into the regulations. "Holding company," for this framework, means a listed holding company.

High-value debt listed entities are identified at ₹5,000 crore of outstanding listed debt, up from ₹1,000 crore. The chapter says governance for that group was aligned closer to equity-listed practice: a special resolution for a director over 75, three months to fill a board or key-managerial vacancy, and an exemption from shareholder approval for nominee directors. Debenture-trustee and debenture-holder no-objection requirements stay.

Unclaimed interest and principal on listed non-convertible securities transfer to the Investor Education and Protection Fund seven years after maturity, matching section 125 of the Companies Act. The old mismatch, seven years after escrow rather than seven years after maturity, is what the amendment closed.

Minimum public shareholding is still the 25 per cent destination. What changed, on the chapter's account, is the path. Minimum public offer and minimum public shareholding were recast as a six-tier structure based on post-issue capital, so a large issuer can sell a smaller slice at listing and take longer to reach 25 per cent. The tier boundaries and the timelines are in the regulation and in the Ministry of Finance instruments that set the contractual rule. They should be read there, not reconstructed from a summary.

## Enforcement is part of the outlook

LODR without the exchange procedure is an unread rule. Chapter 3 says exchanges follow a standard operating procedure of penalties, promoter-demat freezes, and suspension. For 2025-26 the exchange-wise freeze counts are 486 companies at BSE, 99 at NSE, and 41 at MSEI. The fine figures are marked provisional, and the note says the quarter ended 31 March 2026 had not been fined on the date of the table. Suspensions under the procedure were 22 at BSE, 8 at NSE, and 1 at MSEI. A regulatory outlook that stops at the amendment date and ignores this ladder will surprise a promoter.

The filing standard for a new issuer remains ICDR, last consolidated on 21 March 2026, including the April 2026 mechanism that marks pledged lock-in shares non-transferable at the depository. LODR begins when the listing approval is used. From this quarter forward, the July consolidation, the transmission circular, the demat-only corporate-action rule, the scaled related-party test, and the exchange procedure are the operating set.
