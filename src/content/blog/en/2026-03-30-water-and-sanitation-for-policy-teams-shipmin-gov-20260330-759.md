# What policy teams should separate in the drinking-water record

A policy note on Indian water that uses one percentage, one ministry, and one end date will be wrong in a way the source documents make easy to avoid. The separations below are the ones those documents already draw.

## Separate the household count from the village declaration

The reported tap-connection rate is a household statistic. Around 15.80 crore of 19.36 crore rural households, 81.61 percent, on 10 March 2026. More than 15.90 crore, 82.19 percent, on 4 August 2026. The [dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx) then has a village path: entry on the information system, a report by the state department that supply reaches households and schools and anganwadi centres, and a Gram Sabha certificate.

JJM 2.0 adds a policy condition to the certificate. The Gram Panchayat declares Har Ghar Jal only once the state has established in-village operation and maintenance. A brief that recommends "declaring the remaining villages" without that condition is recommending a step the Cabinet note of 10 March 2026 has closed.

Also separate December 2028 from 2047. The first is the horizon for connections to the 19.36 crore households and for Har Ghar Jal certification of Gram Panchayats. The second is the vision, in the same note, of assured 24×7 supply. Writing a 2028 target of continuous supply borrows a year from the wrong sentence.

## Separate the Centre's share from the programme's size, and the release from both

Central assistance under JJM 2.0 is ₹3.59 lakh crore, up from ₹2.08 lakh crore approved in 2019-20. The total outlay in the Cabinet note is ₹8.69 lakh crore. A 13 March 2026 note describes the total as having moved from ₹3.60 lakh crore to ₹8.69 lakh crore. A talking point that says "the Centre is spending ₹8.69 lakh crore" has merged the state share into the central share.

Cash observed in FY 2026-27, as on 6 August 2026, was ₹6,154.96 crore: ₹5,289.09 crore reimbursed to ten states for transition spending, and ₹865.87 crore through SNA SPARSH across 13 states. Policy language about "front-loading" should be checked against that print. The new normal the ministry describes is scheme-level release, which will look small until schemes are individually payable.

Implementation sits with states and Union Territories under separate memoranda. The Cabinet note also says other central departments will be lined up for maintenance and source sustainability. That whole-of-government line is a direction. It is not, in the note, a published list of budget heads. A convergence paragraph that invents the list will not survive contact with the budget documents.

## Separate the four programmes

Rural drinking water is Jal Jeevan Mission, under the Ministry of Jal Shakti, with the public record on the JJM dashboard.

Rural sanitation is Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen). A 12 February 2026 PIB note says sanitation is a state subject, and that Phase II runs from 2020-21 to 2026-27, aimed at ODF Plus Model villages. The Centre does not become the operator because it funds a mission mode.

Urban tap and sewerage projects are AMRUT 2.0, under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs. States appraise and implement inside approved State Water Action Plans. The ministry's independent review agencies look at grounded projects and return observations to the state.

River sewage in the Ganga basin is Namami Gange, through the National Mission for Clean Ganga, in the Department of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation ([jalshakti-dowr.gov.in](https://jalshakti-dowr.gov.in/)). Its unit is million litres a day commissioned, not rural households.

A Cabinet paper that adds these four into one outcome indicator will mix a household, a village class, a city project, and a treatment plant.

## What a usable note contains

The indicator, the stage, the as-on date, the government that implements, and the document. The [10 March 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2237548), the [12 February 2026 sanitation note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2226999), and the [6 August 2026 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295509) are enough to test a draft against.
