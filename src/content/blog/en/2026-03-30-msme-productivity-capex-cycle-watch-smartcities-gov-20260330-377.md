# Where MSME capital expenditure is supposed to come from

Capital expenditure in a small Indian firm is a machine, a tool room, or a share in a common facility. The Ministry of MSME does not run one capex cycle. It runs several instruments that touch equipment at different points. Watching the cycle means watching which instrument can actually pay for the asset.

## Room on the firm's own balance sheet

From 1 April 2025 the classification ceilings on investment in plant and machinery or equipment are ₹2.5 crore, ₹25 crore, and ₹125 crore for micro, small, and medium. The Press Information Bureau's Budget 2025-26 note named technological upgradation as one reason for the higher limits. A firm can install more equipment and remain inside its category, until it crosses the ceiling. Crossing on investment alone is enough to move it up. A capex plan is therefore also a classification plan.

## Credit that can sit under a guarantee

For micro and small enterprises, CGTMSE — the guarantee trust of the Ministry and SIDBI — can cover collateral-free credit from member lending institutions. From 1 April 2025 the Development Commissioner states the ceiling as credit support up to ₹10 crore. The 18 March 2025 circular applies that ceiling to guarantees approved on or after 1 April 2025, including working-capital enhancement on accounts already covered. Annual guarantee fees on the new upper bands are 1.10 percent and 1.20 percent.

That is term credit and working capital for MSEs, if a bank sanctions it. The guarantee follows the sanction. It does not originate the machine. Medium enterprises are outside CGTMSE even when their investment is inside the medium ceiling of ₹125 crore.

Between 1 January and 30 November 2025, the Ministry's 26 June 2026 note records 29.03 lakh guarantees amounting to ₹3.77 lakh crore approved. Use that as evidence that the guarantee window was active, not as evidence that plant and machinery rose by the same amount.

## Shared and certified equipment

Micro and Small Enterprises Cluster Development Programme spends on common facility centres and industrial-estate infrastructure. The 26 June 2026 Ministry note says 612 projects had been approved as on June 2026, of which 364 were completed. In 2025-26, up to 20 November 2025, 11 projects with a total cost of ₹253.23 crore were approved and two were completed. A firm inside an approved cluster can use a facility it does not own. A firm outside one cannot book that capex.

ZED, the Zero Defect Zero Effect certification, is for manufacturing MSMEs with Udyam registration. Certification fees published by the Development Commissioner are ₹10,000 for Bronze, ₹40,000 for Silver, and ₹90,000 for Gold, with a subsidy of 80 percent, 60 percent, and 50 percent for micro, small, and medium, plus an extra 10 percent in specified cases and 5 percent for units also in SFURTI or MSE-CDP. The guidelines also provide subsidy on technology upgradation for zero-effect solutions. As on May 2026, the Ministry's June note says more than 93.61 lakh MSMEs were registered under ZED and more than 6.68 lakh were certified. Registration is the top of the funnel. Certification, and any equipment subsidy that follows it, is the narrower gate.

Technology Centres are the public shop-floor layer: 18 centres in operation, with 20 new centres and 100 extension centres under the Technology Centres and Extension Centres scheme. As of November 2025, 25 extension centres were operational. They train and support. They are not a grant for a firm's own press line.
