# Pharma Jobs the Schemes Mention, Without a Headcount

Employment claims in Indian pharma policy are easy to inflate because the founding documents promise jobs and withhold a number. The honest reading is the technology the plants will use, the public institutions that must staff up around them, and the training routes the science ministry already runs. A headcount that does not appear in those documents should not appear in a brief.

## What the approvals actually say

The 21 March 2020 cabinet note on critical bulk drugs says the incentive “will lead to expected incremental sales of ₹46,400 crore and significant additional employment generation over 8 years.” Significant, additional, expected. There is no lakh of jobs in that sentence. The park half of the same decision is about common facilities — solvent recovery, distillation, power and steam, effluent treatment — which require operating crews once built. The note does not staff those crews on paper.

The 6 February 2026 Lok Sabha reply describes park works still in tender or in progress at Una, Bharuch, and Anakapalli, and 48 approved greenfield bulk-drug projects. Construction employment and steady-state plant employment are different periods. The reply does not split them. Until a Department or state implementing-agency disclosure does, a workforce model is an assumption, and it should be labelled as one.

On 24 August 2024 the Cabinet approved the Department of Biotechnology’s BioE3 policy, Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment. The Prime Minister’s Office note says the policy will facilitate expansion of India’s skilled workforce and provide a surge in job creation, alongside biomanufacturing and bio-AI hubs and biofoundries. It names sectors: high-value bio-based chemicals, biopolymers and enzymes; smart proteins and functional foods; precision biotherapeutics; climate-resilient agriculture; carbon capture and utilisation; marine and space research. It does not attach a jobs target to those sectors. “Employment” in the policy title is a purpose. It is not a measured outcome.

## Skills that follow from the product split

The 2020 cabinet list was 26 fermentation-based bulk drugs and 27 chemical-synthesis bulk drugs, with different incentive rates. Those are different shop floors. Fermentation at pharmaceutical scale needs microbiology, sterile utilities, and downstream purification. Chemical synthesis needs process chemistry, solvent handling, and the recovery and distillation plants the park scheme lists as common facilities. A hiring plan copied from a formulation packing line misses both.

Around the factories, the public skill demand is regulatory and environmental. CDSCO’s own description of its mandate covers new drugs, clinical trials, standards, import quality, and coordination of state drug control organisations. State licensing authorities issue manufacturing permissions for a large part of the ordinary drug catalogue under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act. Revised Schedule M, notified on 28 December 2023, raises the documented quality system a licensee has to show. That is a demand for quality-assurance and validation staff inside firms, and for inspectors who can read those systems. None of the notes above publish a vacancy count for those inspectors.

The park designs add a third cluster of roles that are industrial utilities: effluent treatment and zero-liquid-discharge operations, solvent recovery, steam and power distribution, waste facilities, and stability and microbiology laboratories. The February 2026 reply lists an advanced laboratory testing centre, suitable for complex API work, among the common facilities the central grant can fund. Laboratory technicians in a park testing centre are not the same hire as a medicinal chemist in a BIRAC-supported startup.

## Where training routes are public

The Department of Biotechnology supports postgraduate programmes in biotechnology and allied fields, with the Graduate Aptitude Test — Biotechnology used for admission to DBT-supported seats. That is a national entry route into the skilled workforce BioE3 talks about. It is not a placement guarantee into a bulk-drug park. BIRAC, set up by the Department as its industry-facing organisation, runs bio-incubators. Incubator headcount and factory headcount should stay in separate lines.

A skills strategy that wants a number has to collect it from a later official source: a park detailed project report that states operating manpower, a PLI review that states employment committed by approved applicants, or a labour statistical release. The cabinet sentence is permission to expect employment. It is not the statistic.

The longer industrial timeline those skills sit on is the [mid-term trajectory](/blog/pharma-and-biotech-mid-term-trajectory-mnre-gov-20260330-514). Great Indian Company would rather publish the missing headcount as missing than borrow one: [greatindiancompany.com](https://greatindiancompany.com).
