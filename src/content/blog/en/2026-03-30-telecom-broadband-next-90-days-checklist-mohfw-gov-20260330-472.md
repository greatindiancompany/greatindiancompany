# The Official Telecom Pages to Reopen After September 2026

This checklist is dated 24 September 2026. It names documents to reopen. It does not assign probabilities to what those documents will say. The last subscriber month in hand is July 2026. The last full performance-indicator quarter is January–March 2026. The last spectrum recommendation is 24 February 2026. The last ministerial scoreboard for National Broadband Mission 2.0 is 12 February 2026.

## Subscriber series

Reopen TRAI's monthly subscription note and check four lines against the July 2026 base:

- Total broadband subscribers, last printed at 1,094.48 million.
- The three segment rates: fixed wired, fixed wireless, and mobile wireless. In July those rates were 0.90 percent, 2.60 percent, and 0.59 percent.
- 5G fixed wireless subscribers, last printed at 13.21 million, with the urban and rural split.
- Mobile number portability requests, last printed at 15.98 million in the month.

Also check whether a new quarterly indicator has replaced the 22 June 2026 book for January–March. Until it has, leave adjusted gross revenue at Rs 86,716 crore and wireless data usage at 26.70 GB as the last closed financial quarter. Do not splice a new month onto that quarter and call the result a run-rate.

TRAI's subscription index is the reliable door: the July PDF is Press Release No. 116/2026.

## Spectrum file

Reopen the Department of Telecommunications side of TRAI's 24 February 2026 recommendations. The questions that have a yes-or-no answer in a later government document are:

- Has a notice inviting applications been issued for the bands TRAI listed, including 600 MHz, 3300 MHz, and 26 GHz?
- Has DoT accepted, changed, or parked the 35 percent caps, the lower net-worth test for new entrants, and the four-year 600 MHz moratorium?
- Has the upper 6 GHz range stayed out of the auction, as TRAI recommended pending the next World Radiocommunication Conference?
- Has the coverage-hole scheme, trading up to 10 percent of the auction price for new 4G or 5G sites, been adopted?
- Have the draft network authorisations dated 9 October 2025, for a digital connectivity infrastructure provider and a cloud-hosted network, been notified?

If none of those documents exist yet, the status is unchanged. Absence of a notice is itself the update.

## Mission and permission file

Reopen the next PIB or parliamentary note on National Broadband Mission 2.0 and BharatNet, and compare it with the 12 February 2026 baselines:

- Villages with optical fibre at 95 percent uptime: 42,000 in December 2025, target 2.7 lakh by 2030.
- Anchor institutions: 68.8 percent, target 90 percent.
- Fixed broadband download speed: 61.55 Mbps, target 100 Mbps.
- Right-of-way disposal: 30.4 days nationally. Check whether the states named as pending, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal, have notified the 2024 rules, and whether Tamil Nadu's disposal time is still near 85 days.
- States and union territories implementing the rules: 33 of 36.
- Fibre mapped on the PM GatiShakti platform: 94 percent, with the reply's target set at 100 percent "by March."
- Gram panchayats online under Phases I and II: nearly 2.14 lakh of 2.56 lakh. Keep that number separate from the uptime-village number.

The 2030 gaps those baselines leave open are set out in the [trajectory brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/telecom-broadband-mid-term-trajectory-mohfw-gov-20260330-512).
