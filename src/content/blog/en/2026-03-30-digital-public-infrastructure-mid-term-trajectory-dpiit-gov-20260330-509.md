# What India's digital rails are already scheduled to do next

A trajectory is a path someone has committed to, not a guess about adoption. On India's digital public infrastructure, the committed path for the next year and a half is short and specific. Most of the operative Digital Personal Data Protection Act is appointed for eighteen months after 13 November 2025. A small-enterprise onboarding scheme for the Open Network for Digital Commerce runs through financial year 2026-27. Everything else in the public record is a system already in production, whose next step is continued publishing rather than a new law with a date on it.

## The legal path has two remaining dates

Notification G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025 split the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 into three commencements. The first, from publication, covered the institutional provisions, including those under which the Data Protection Board was established by G.S.R. 844(E) the same day. The Board's head office is in the National Capital Region. That part is not in the future.

The second commencement is one year from publication: section 6(9) and section 27(1)(d). That date falls in November 2026. The third is eighteen months from publication: sections 3 to 5, subsections (1) to (8) and (10) of section 6, sections 7 to 17, the rest of section 27 except the clause already covered, sections 28 to 34, sections 36 and 37, and section 44(2). Eighteen months from 13 November 2025 falls in May 2027.

The Rules, 2025 were published on 14 November 2025, with a corrigendum on 16 December 2025. Between September 2026 and May 2027, the scheduled legal work is therefore to absorb two clauses in November 2026 and the main operative block in May 2027. This page does not restate the clauses. The trajectory is the calendar itself. Builders who treat the Act as already fully in force, and builders who treat it as still a draft, are both misreading the gazette.

The Account Aggregator rulebook is not on this calendar. The Reserve Bank's directions of 28 November 2025 are already in force. Over the next year and a half they do not "switch on." They continue to forbid aggregators from executing transactions, from retaining financial information, and from outsourcing the aggregation business. Any mid-term story in which Account Aggregators become lenders or payment companies would require a change to those directions. No such change is in the documents used here.

## The commerce scheme has an end year

The Lok Sabha reply of 4 August 2026 approves ₹277.35 crore under the MSME Trade Enablement and Marketing initiative for financial years 2024-25 through 2026-27. The money supports micro and small enterprises joining ONDC: awareness, training, cataloguing, onboarding, account management, logistics, and packaging. Half of the beneficiary enterprises are targeted to be women-owned. Financial year 2026-27 ends on 31 March 2027, which sits inside the same window as the data-protection commencement in May 2027.

That is a scheduled public effort at the edge of the network, not a scheduled expansion of the section 8 company itself. The reply describes ONDC as already operating across retail, mobility, logistics, financial services, and agriculture. As of July 2026 it reported over 500 million cumulative orders, over 500 orders a minute, about 2 lakh or more active retail merchants (about 60 percent small sellers), and about 10 lakh or more mobility and logistics service providers. DigiDukaan, the kirana onboarding effort named in the reply, was operating in Hyderabad and Jaipur. A mid-term reading is that seller support has a budget through March 2027 and a field model demonstrated in two cities. It is not a published list of the next fifty cities.

ONDC's about page, which is the network's own account rather than Parliament's, describes the same spread of domains and points readers to an annual report and a quarterly update. Those documents are the right next read for anyone who wants management's view. They are not a substitute for the parliamentary totals above.

## Systems that are in production, and therefore have no "launch" left

Several rails are past the point where a trajectory can mean a launch date. Their public path is deeper use of a design that is already stated.

Identity. UIDAI's saturation report as on 30 June 2026 puts live Aadhaar penetration at 94.59 percent nationally and at 62.41 percent in Nagaland, 77.77 percent in Arunachal Pradesh, and 88.29 percent in Bihar. The trajectory implied by the report, though not promised as a target, is closure of those gaps. The report does not set a date by which Nagaland reaches the national rate. A mid-term plan that assumes universal authentication in every district is ahead of the publisher.

Payments. NPCI's monthly table already shows 752 banks and 24,508.96 million transactions in August 2026. The government has separately said, in the 29 July 2026 PIB account, that UPI serves more than 55.49 crore individuals and 6.5 crore merchants. The scheduled behaviour is the next month's row. There is no second "UPI launch" in these papers.

Documents and APIs. DigiLocker, as on 31 July 2026, had more than 72.43 crore users and 5,437 document types, most of them from state governments (4,776, against 661 central). API Setu had 8,904 published APIs in the 29 July account. The work ahead, on the face of those numbers, is more issuers and more APIs, which is incremental onboarding. MeitY's disclosed DigiLocker allocation since inception, ₹168.58 crore in the 29 July Lok Sabha answer, does not describe a new capital programme.

Health. The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission was launched in September 2021, according to its own site. By 20 July 2026 the ministry reported 94.87 crore ABHA IDs, 5.36 lakh facilities, and 10.09 lakh professionals, with no central store of health records and with a sandbox plus a security audit before integration. Named public systems, including PM-JAY and Nikshay, are already connected. The mid-term path in the reply is more integration of the same design, including the Unified Health Interface as the discovery layer. The reply does not set a target for facility registrations.

Education. DIKSHA's about page describes a platform states already use, in 36 languages, with NISHTHA as the teacher-education programme and the National Digital Education Architecture, launched 29 July 2021, as the building-block frame. The trajectory is federated: each state keeps the freedom to run its own programme on the common platform. A single national course count is not the design.

Language. The 29 July PIB account describes BHASHINI, the National Language Translation Mission, as a public digital platform for Indian language technologies. It does not publish a user total in the passage used here. The commitment is the existence of the platform as the inclusion layer for other services, including features MeitY mentioned for e-governance interfaces.

## The physical constraint on every digital path

As of June 2026, the same PIB account says 2.21 lakh gram panchayats were service-ready under BharatNet, while points of presence were operational at 80,472 gram panchayats during that month. Over the next year and a half, rural use of UPI, DigiLocker, DIKSHA, or a telehealth flow still depends on which of those two numbers moves. The account does not publish a completion date for operational points of presence. It does make the gap official. A trajectory for digital rails that ignores it is a trajectory for cities and for people who already have another connection.

Common Service Centres are the human workaround where the connection or the skill is thin. The June 2026 Rajya Sabha cut reported 4,07,122 centres functional at gram panchayat level. They are part of the mid-term delivery system, not a legacy to be assumed away.

## What not to add

There is no official 2030 target in the documents this brief relies on. There is no published path for ONDC order value, because the Lok Sabha answer published order counts and not rupees. There is no scheduled rewrite of UPI into a different product. The G20 framework of August and September 2023 remains a voluntary external description, not an Indian implementation timetable.

The scheduled items are the November 2026 and May 2027 data-protection dates, and the TEAM outlay through March 2027. The rest of the mid-term is operation: publish the next official counts, and close gaps the current counts already admit.
