# India’s Two Pharma PLI Schemes Cover Different Products

Indian pharma manufacturing policy shifted when the Cabinet decided to pay for domestic production of inputs the country was importing, and then, a year later, to pay separately for a wider set of pharmaceutical goods. Those decisions became two schemes inside the Department of Pharmaceuticals. Reading them as one “PLI for pharma” is how pitches mix a fermentation intermediate with a complex generic and call both eligible.

## The first scheme is a named list of bulk drugs

On 21 March 2020 the Cabinet approved a production-linked incentive for critical key starting materials, drug intermediates, and active pharmaceutical ingredients. The stated purpose was a sustainable domestic supply of those inputs, in a country the same note called the third largest pharmaceutical producer by volume and still dependent on imports for some specific bulk drugs in the range of 80 to 100 percent. The design was narrow on purpose.

The financial implication was ₹6,940 crore over eight years. Eligible manufacturers of 53 identified bulk drugs would receive an incentive on incremental sales over 2019–20 for six years: 20 percent for fermentation-based products and 10 percent for chemical-synthesis products. Twenty-six of the 53 were fermentation-based and 27 were chemical synthesis. A project management agency nominated by the Department would implement it. The cabinet text limits the scheme to manufacturing of those identified bulk drugs.

A written Lok Sabha reply on 6 February 2026 describes the operating scheme in later language: financial incentives on the sale of 41 identified products from greenfield projects, with 48 projects approved for 33 bulk drugs, and the same ₹6,940 crore outlay. The purpose stated there is to reduce the risk of supply disruption where critical ingredients have no alternative and dependence sits on a single source. Between the cabinet list and that reply, the Department has issued guidelines, corrigenda, and fresh invitations, all indexed on its schemes page. Eligibility is whatever the current notified list and the firm’s approval letter say.

## The second scheme is a different basket, with revenue groups

On 3 March 2021 the Department notified the Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Pharmaceuticals, gazette notification 31026/60/2020-Policy-DoP. The Press Information Bureau note that followed put the approved outlay at ₹15,000 crore. The aim in that note is higher investment and production, and a shift toward high-value goods, including the creation of firms that can enter global value chains. Applications were grouped by global manufacturing revenue of pharmaceutical goods in FY 2019–20, with a carve-out for MSMEs. SIDBI was named the project management agency. The application window in that announcement ran from 2 June 2021 to 31 July 2021. That window is history. It is not an open call.

The gazette groups the incentive pool, which is about ₹15,000 crore including administrative expenditure, as ₹11,000 crore for Group A, ₹2,250 crore for Group B, and ₹1,750 crore for Group C. Group A is applicants with global manufacturing revenue of pharmaceutical goods of ₹5,000 crore or more in FY 2019–20. Group B is revenue from ₹500 crore up to ₹5,000 crore. Group C is revenue below ₹500 crore. The rate table lives in the operational guidelines issued on 1 June 2021 and in later corrigenda on the Department’s page. Quote the guidelines for the rate. Quote the gazette for the group pools.

The 6 February 2026 reply draws the product boundary in one sentence that belongs in every term sheet. The pharmaceuticals scheme incentivises biopharmaceuticals, complex generics, patented drugs or drugs nearing patent expiry, autoimmune drugs, anti-cancer drugs, and production of key starting materials, intermediates, and APIs other than those notified under the bulk-drug PLI. It reports 55 approved applicants, covering pharmaceuticals and in-vitro diagnostic medical devices, in both greenfield and brownfield projects.

## Parks are a third instrument

The same March 2020 cabinet meeting approved the Scheme for Promotion of Bulk Drug Parks: three parks, ₹3,000 crore, and a maximum of ₹1,000 crore of grant-in-aid per park for common infrastructure, implemented by state agencies. A park plot is shared steam, effluent treatment, solvent recovery, and testing. A PLI award is a sales-linked incentive for a specific product. A firm can care about both. They are approved on different paper.

## What a policy reader should write down

Name the scheme, the notification date, and whether the product sits on the bulk-drug list or in the residual category the pharmaceuticals scheme is allowed to cover. If the pitch says only “PLI,” ask which of the two gazette streams it means. The Department’s schemes index also lists a separate medical-devices PLI, medical-device parks, a research scheme called PRIP with a gazette notification dated 1 October 2025, and the Strengthening of Pharmaceuticals Industry scheme. Each of those has its own guidelines. None of them renames the two pharmaceutical PLIs into one programme.

For the underwriting questions that follow from this split, see [how to read a PLI award](/blog/pharma-and-biotech-investment-implications-mnre-gov-20260330-234).
