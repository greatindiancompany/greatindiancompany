# What the April–June 2026 labour survey shows for women

The quarter that matters for a current reading is April–June 2026. On 10 August 2026 the National Statistics Office in the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation released the Periodic Labour Force Survey quarterly bulletin for that window. It uses the current weekly status: activity in the seven days before the survey, for persons aged 15 and above, in rural and urban areas.

## The weekly rate moved, and the move was rural

Overall female labour force participation stood at 33.2 percent in April–June 2026, compared with 34.7 percent in January–March 2026. Figure 3 of the press note places the rural female rate at 37.2 percent and the urban female rate at 25.0 percent. In the previous quarter those cuts were 39.2 percent rural and 25.4 percent urban. The quarter’s decline is concentrated in rural areas. Urban female participation was already the lower of the two and barely changed.

The same press note puts the all-person labour force participation rate at 54.6 percent, from 55.5 percent in the previous quarter. Rural participation for all persons was 56.9 percent, from 58.2 percent. Urban participation for all persons was unchanged at 50.2 percent. Male labour force participation in urban areas rose from 75.0 percent to 75.3 percent. Women’s weekly participation and men’s urban participation did not move together.

## Employment and unemployment in the same release

The female worker population ratio was 31.3 percent in April–June 2026, against 31.6 percent in April–June 2025. In urban areas the female worker population ratio was 22.8 percent, against 23.1 percent in January–March 2026. Urban unemployment for all persons aged 15 and above stayed broadly stable at 6.7 percent. Female urban unemployment decreased to 8.7 percent from 9.1 percent in the previous quarter, and from 8.9 percent in April–June 2025.

Using population projections from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, MoSPI estimates that 56.6 crore persons aged 15 and above were employed on average during the quarter, of whom 16.4 crore were women and 40.2 crore were men. That count is a current-weekly-status quarter. It is a different object from the 20.0 crore women MoSPI counts as employed on usual status across calendar 2025.

Usual status looks back 365 days and includes subsidiary economic activity of 30 days or more. A person can be in the annual usual-status workforce and outside the labour force in a particular week. The 3.6 crore gap between those two official women-worker counts is a change of definition and reference period. Treating it as jobs lost between the annual report and this quarter misreads both releases.

## June sits inside the quarter

The June 2026 monthly bulletin, also on the weekly status, puts female labour force participation at 32.7 percent, against 32.8 percent in May 2026 and 32.0 percent in June 2025. Rural female participation was 36.6 percent in June, against 35.2 percent a year earlier. Urban female participation was 24.8 percent, against 25.2 percent in June 2025. June is one month inside April–June. It should be read beside the quarterly 33.2 percent, not substituted for it, and not averaged with the calendar-2025 usual-status rate of 40.0 percent.

## What did not change in this bulletin

The quarterly press note reports a rise in the share of regular wage or salaried employees among all workers, from 15.5 percent to 16.1 percent in rural areas and from 48.9 percent to 49.3 percent in urban areas. It also reports rural agriculture’s share of workers at 52.9 percent, from 55.8 percent in the previous quarter. Those shares are for all workers. The press note does not reprint them for women alone. A claim that women’s industrial employment rose this quarter needs the sex-wise industry table in the full bulletin, which this press note does not give in prose.

The bulletin is the fifth since quarterly results began covering rural as well as urban areas in April–June 2025. Bulletins through December 2024 were urban only. A rural comparison that starts before April 2025 is not available in this quarterly series.

## How to use the quarter

An operator hiring in a city should start with the urban female weekly rate, 25.0 percent, and the urban female worker population ratio, 22.8 percent. A rural programme should start with 37.2 percent participation and with the fact that the quarter’s decline was rural. A full-year planning number remains the usual-status annual figure, with MoSPI’s warning that the 2025 sample design is not strictly comparable with earlier annual reports.
