# State patterns in India's manufacturing trade corridors

A national logistics ratio hides the decision a plant manager actually makes: which state, on which corridor, with which last mile. India's official pattern is a corridor programme under DPIIT, a state logistics-policy push, and a state scorecard called LEADS. It is not a league table this brief will invent.

## Corridors are the geography

DPIIT's industrial corridor programme is meant to put manufacturing nodes on multi-modal spines, planned under PM Gati Shakti. The Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor was the first. In December 2016 the government expanded the old DMIC trust into the National Industrial Corridor Development and Implementation Trust so later corridors could be developed in one frame. National Industrial Corridor Development Corporation, the project agency, describes the wider set as including the Amritsar-Kolkata, Chennai-Bengaluru, Vizag-Chennai, and Bengaluru-Mumbai corridors, with freight corridors or national highways treated as the transport backbone depending on the corridor.

A plant's state pattern is the spine it sits on. A node on the western spine inherits the Delhi-Mumbai freight logic. A node on the eastern spine inherits a different one. PIB's 27 November 2025 logistics note gives a concrete eastern example: the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor cut wagon turnaround from 15-16 days to 2-3 days and transit from over 60 hours to about 35-38 hours, with a control centre in Prayagraj, and a link from the Ganga waterway at Varanasi toward Haldia. The same note says NICDC parks are being offered as plug-and-play industrial infrastructure. Those are location facts. They do not rank states.

## States are scored on logistics performance

DPIIT's Logistics Ease Across Different States (LEADS) is the comparison tool. PIB's 27 November 2025 note says LEADS 2025 mixes perception with objective data, and that objective indicators were 32.5 percent of the framework, with room to rise. The assessment covers institutions, enablers, infrastructure, services, the operating environment, and sustainability. On 13 May 2026 the Commerce and Industry Minister released the LEADS 2025 report. Use the report for a state's relative position. This page does not reprint a ranking it has not copied line by line from that release.

A PIB progress note on the National Logistics Policy says 26 states and Union Territories had notified their own logistics policies in line with the national policy. A state without a notified policy is a different execution surface from a state that has one, even before you read LEADS. The same national-policy note is also where the 2030 aim of a top-25 Logistics Performance Index ranking is stated. That aim is national. The daily variance is local.

## Cities are the missing layer

The 27 November 2025 note says smaller firms pay more for logistics than the aggregate 7.97 percent of GDP estimated for 2023-24, and that on trips of about 600 kilometres the first and last 50 kilometres move the cost. That is a state and city problem. DPIIT's SMILE programme, with the Asian Development Bank, launched logistics plans in eight pilot cities across eight states. The note does not name the eight cities in the summary used here, so this brief will not guess them. The design is two-level: connect growth hubs to trunk corridors at state level, and fit urban freight to city master plans at city level.

PM Gati Shakti district master plans for all 112 Aspirational Districts, also recorded in that note, are the inland version of the same idea. A supplier park in an aspirational district is not automatically connected because a national corridor exists on a map.

## How an operator should pick

Pick the corridor spine first, then the state's logistics policy and LEADS position, then the city last mile. Do not average them into "India logistics."
