# What SECI's Solar Auctions Actually Signal

A market signal in Indian solar is a contract someone can win, sign, and get paid on. Solar Energy Corporation of India publishes that machinery on its own site. A ministry target is a different object.

## What SECI says it is

SECI's "What we do" page describes a Navratna central public sector undertaking under MNRE, set up in 2011 to implement the National Solar Mission. It is a designated Renewable Energy Implementing Agency for MNRE schemes and a Category-I power trading licensee under the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission.

The commercial core is tariff-based reverse bidding for grid-connected solar, wind, hybrid, round-the-clock, and firm and dispatchable renewable energy. SECI signs long-term power purchase agreements with developers and back-to-back power sale agreements with state distribution companies or other final buyers. The page calls that intermediary role a way to keep tariff discovery transparent and to provide payment security.

That structure is the signal. The number that clears an auction is a price a buyer has accepted through SECI, not a commentary price. This brief does not reprint tariff headlines from newspapers. SECI's pages used here do not set out a single national solar tariff.

## Awarded capacity is not commissioned capacity

As of 1 May 2025, the same page says SECI has awarded over 73.8 GW of renewable capacity: 45.9 GW solar, 16.4 GW wind, and 11.5 GW hybrid. "Awarded" means a developer has been selected. It does not mean the plant is generating.

SECI's solar page, in a separate sentence, says that as of March 2025 the organisation had commissioned 21.67 GW of solar. The two dates are a month apart, and the two words are not interchangeable. A 45.9 GW award book and a 21.67 GW commissioned book can both be true. The gap between them is the part of the market still under construction, delayed, or not yet built. SECI's scheme table dated 31 March 2025 shows that pattern line by line: several later ISTS tranches have awarded capacity and little or no commissioned capacity.

One line on that table is easy to misread as ordinary generation. "ISTS Solar PV Plant linked with Solar Manufacturing Plant" shows 12,000 MW awarded, 4,300 MW commissioned, and 4,234 MW under implementation, as on 31 March 2025. That is a manufacturing-linked generation scheme, not the module PLI factory programme.

## Storage is already inside the product, not beside it

SECI does not describe storage as a footnote. The what-we-do page lists round-the-clock supply and firm and dispatchable renewable energy as tender types, and names SECI as the implementing agency for utility-scale battery projects as well as green hydrogen and offshore wind leasing. It also lists integration of battery storage with solar in transmission-constrained regions.

The market signal for a storage developer is therefore whether the tender asks for energy at a defined hour, not whether a separate "battery market" press cycle is loud. A plain solar auction and an FDRE auction clear different products. Comparing their tariffs without saying which product was bought produces a false decline or a false premium.

## Manufacturing is a second SECI signal

MNRE's PLI page says SECI is the implementing agency for Tranche-II of the high-efficiency solar module PLI scheme, and that letters of award went to 11 bidders in April 2023 for 39,600 MW of fully or partially integrated module manufacturing. SECI's solar page repeats that award and says a PLI amount of around Rs 13,937.535 crore has been allocated to those manufacturers, to be paid over five years after the plants are commissioned.

That is a factory signal: who received an award, and when cash can start. It is not a signal of modules sold this month. MNRE's page dates the tranche-II outlay at Rs 19,500 crore, which is the scheme envelope, not the same sentence as SECI's allocated PLI amount. Keep the two rupee figures on the pages that state them.

## How to read the next SECI release

Ask four questions of any new SECI page. Which product was tendered: solar, hybrid, RTC, FDRE, or storage. Was the figure awarded or commissioned, and on what date. Is the buyer a distribution company under a power sale agreement. Is the megawatt a generating plant or a manufacturing line.
