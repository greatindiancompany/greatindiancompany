# Where India's Climate Adaptation Capital Spending Sits

The adaptation fund is not the capital cycle. It is a thin project list beside a much wider development budget. Models that use only one of those numbers will mis-state the spend.

## The wide definition

On 29 January 2026, the Press Information Bureau reported the Economic Survey 2025-26. The release says adaptation and resilience-related domestic spending was 3.7 percent of GDP in FY16 and 5.6 percent in FY22. It describes the strategy as development-led: public investment in core sectors, with national missions and state plans as the organising frame. The note does not extend the GDP share past FY22. A chart that does has left the release.

That wide spend is where the missions point. The ministry's FAQ of 1 December 2021 describes water-use efficiency and basin management, climate-resilient agriculture, habitat standards and city plans, and the Green India Mission's ecosystem and livelihood aims. Those are programme intents inside ordinary budgets. They are not a project-wise release schedule.

## The narrow definition

The National Adaptation Fund for Climate Change is the narrow stock. On 5 August 2024, a Lok Sabha reply reported 30 sanctioned projects in 27 States and Union Territories, at a total project cost of ₹847.48 crore. Releases go to NABARD in installments, based on performance and the guidelines. Sanctioned cost is therefore not spent capital.

The Andhra Pradesh dairy project in that reply makes the gap concrete. It was sanctioned on 16 August 2016 for ₹12,71,36,316. NABARD received ₹6,35,68,108. It released ₹5,12,78,000 to the executing entity. Utilisation at the entity is printed as 228,49,000. One file is enough to stop a model that treats sanction as capex.

## What bounds an "upcycle" story

Three dates are the constraint. The fund was set up in 2015-16 as a Central Sector Scheme, as PIB described on 5 February 2018. It was made a non-scheme in November 2022, as the August 2024 reply states. The project stock this brief can cite is still those 30 projects. A rising fund line after November 2022 is not in these documents. Neither is a claim that ₹847.48 crore is India's adaptation effort. The GDP share and the project stock answer different questions.
