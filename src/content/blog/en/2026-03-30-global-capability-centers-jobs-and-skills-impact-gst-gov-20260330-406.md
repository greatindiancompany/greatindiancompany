# Jobs and Skills Around India's Captive Technology Hubs

Employment is the claim most often attached to captive hubs, and the claim with the weakest official frame. Three government-adjacent numbers are in circulation. They count different groups.

## Three counts that do not add

On 25 February 2025, PIB Delhi reported that the Secretary, Ministry of Labour and Employment, highlighted over **1,700** GCCs employing **1.9 million** professionals, with a projection of over **2.8 million** people by 2030. The occasion was an International Labour Organization visit to a centre in Gurugram. The note does not name a household survey, an administrative register, or a filing from which 1.9 million was drawn. Treat it as a ministerial statement. The 2030 figure in that note is a projection stated there, not a workforce forecast produced for this brief.

STPI's annual report for 2024-25 says the overall direct employment base reached **5.8 million**, in the industry chapter that tags **NASSCOM** as the source of those industry totals. That figure is an industry-association count for IT-BPM employment, reproduced by a MeitY society. It is wider than captive centres: it includes third-party IT and business-process firms. It should not be added to 1.9 million, and it should not be relabeled as an STPI census.

The Reserve Bank's software-export survey published on 18 September 2026 has no employment table. It has a delivery table. On-site services were **US$ 18.4 billion**, **8.3 percent** of software services exports, down from **US$ 19.0 billion** and **9.3 percent**. On the wider total that includes commercial presence, presence of natural persons was also **US$ 18.4 billion**, **7.7 percent**, down from **8.7 percent**. Off-site delivery was **91.7 percent**. The jobs implication that is actually in the data is compositional: the export machine is people working from India, and the cross-border movement of people is the smaller mode.

MoSPI's provisional GDP note for 2025-26 does not publish a GCC occupation count or a Periodic Labour Force Survey break for this workforce. The national-accounts line that contains IT also contains finance, real estate, professional services, and ownership of dwellings. This brief does not invent a PLFS number to fill that gap.

## Units are not jobs either

STPI registered **920** units in 2024-25: **90** new units under the STP scheme and **830** for Softex attestation only. A registration is a compliance relationship. It does not disclose headcount, and a captive may or may not be among those units.

## The skill programmes STPI does count

The annual report is on firmer ground when it counts its own schemes, as on 31 March 2025:

- **24** Centres of Entrepreneurship had onboarded **805** startups.
- The Next Generation Incubation Scheme, aimed at Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, offers funding support of up to **₹25 lakh**. **686** startups had taken that assistance.
- Incubation services covered **411** incubatees.

These are startup and product programmes under a MeitY society. They are a feeder a captive might hire from. They are not a training guarantee for the 1.9 million figure, and the report does not show a placement rate into captives.

The PIB note of February 2025 also says, without a separate table, that upskilling in areas such as artificial intelligence was discussed, and that the ILO Director-General pointed to India's talent pool. Qualitative remarks are not a skills account. A skills plan that needs numbers should start from STPI's scheme counts and from the RBI mode split, and should label NASSCOM if it uses 5.8 million.

Visa grants for foreign specialists rotating into a centre are administered outside these four documents. This brief does not quote a visa volume. The RBI mode for presence of natural persons is the export consequence of people moving, not the queue at a consulate.
