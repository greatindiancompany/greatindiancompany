# Unified Portals as a Market Signal for State Capacity

Firms that sell to state governments, or that depend on a state to deliver a licence, a utility connection or a benefit, need a signal that is narrower than "governance is improving." The public record now offers three that can be checked: whether services sit on one portal, whether the finance and works systems talk to each other, and how fast a grievance is closed.

## Portal coverage is a different signal from a service count

PIB's note of 22 January 2026, on DARPG's December 2025 NeSDA Way Forward report, separates these. One line is the stock of e-services: 24,090 across states and UTs as of 31 December 2025, with 8,656 in local governance and utility services. Another line is mandatory-service saturation against a list of 59. A third line is the share of services actually offered through one unified portal.

On that third line, the release names seven portals as providing 100 percent of services: Assam's Sewa Setu, Jammu and Kashmir's e-UNNAT, Karnataka's Seva Sindhu, Odisha's Odisha One, Madhya Pradesh's MP e-Service, Uttarakhand's Apuni Sarkar and Kerala's e-Sevanam. It names four more above 90 percent: e-District Chandigarh, e-District Delhi, Saral Haryana and Rajasthan's e-Mitra. A company integrating once, rather than department by department, is looking at this list. A company counting how many services exist on paper is looking at the 24,090. They will not rank states in the same order. Assam and Odisha appear on the 100 percent portal list. The same release's list of states above 90 percent mandatory saturation does not open with those two names. Read the column that matches the contract.

The 28 May 2026 PIB note on the NeSDA 2025 portal adds the next signal: city portals are in scope, beside state and central portals, and the assessment asks about integrated service delivery, status tracking, open government data and information security. A portal that lists a service and a portal that can track a request to completion are scored apart.

## The finance stack is becoming a buying signal

The Department of Expenditure's SASCI guidelines of 27 March 2026 pay states for a specific stack, under Part IV, Rs 4,000 crore in all. The modules named are e-sanction, e-budget, e-payment through integration with banking systems including the Reserve Bank's e-Kuber, e-receipt, e-voucher, e-HRMS, and full Aadhaar-based direct benefit transfer for beneficiary schemes including state schemes. A second component is a works-management system across the life of a work, plus an e-procurement system that carries the bid through to award. A third is live integration of the financial system with works and with e-procurement. A fourth is 100 percent digitisation of drawing and disbursing officers, treasuries and works divisions.

Each completed component is an incentive of Rs 50 crore to the state, usable for capital projects in any sector. For a vendor, the market signal is the reform condition, because that is what the finance department has been told to finish in 2026-27. The Rs 50 crore is not a procurement budget ring-fenced for software. It is a state incentive released after the Comptroller and Auditor General examines the reform report.

## Grievance time is a service-quality signal

DARPG's June 2026 CPGRAMS report, published on 29 July 2026, puts the average disposal time in central ministries and departments for 2026 at 14 days. That month, 1,96,133 cases were received and 1,99,968 were redressed, with 78,793 pending. The Grievance Redressal Assessment Index, which DARPG has published since May 2023, ranked Department of Posts, Department of Telecommunications and the Insurance Division of the Department of Financial Services at the top of the group with 500 or more grievances. Group size is part of the signal: a small ministry and a large department are not ranked on one list.

A firm whose customers meet the state at a municipal counter should weigh the local-governance service count, and the city-portal examples PIB flagged for Puducherry and Guwahati, more heavily than a central ministry's GRAI rank. The central clock is the cleanest published number. The municipal counter is where the December 2025 report says most citizen interaction happens.
