# Payment, identity, and credit rules around MSME productivity

A more productive shift still fails if the buyer pays late or the bank cannot see the firm. The regulatory outlook for MSME productivity in India is mostly an identity rule, a payment clock, and a credit-classification rule.

## Identity comes first

Udyam Registration is the Ministry's online register for micro, small, and medium enterprises. The category on that certificate follows the investment and turnover ceilings in force since 1 April 2025. The Udyam Assist Platform, launched in January 2023, covers informal micro enterprises. The Reserve Bank's priority-sector FAQ says banks, for priority-sector classification, follow the category recorded on the Udyam Registration Certificate or the Udyam Assist Certificate.

The Press Information Bureau note of 14 May 2026 says retail and wholesale traders have been inside the MSME ambit since July 2021 for Udyam registration and priority-sector lending. The same note says that when an enterprise moves up a category, non-tax benefits of the earlier category continue for three years.

Without the certificate, the later instruments have nothing to attach to. The Samadhaan FAQ states that registration must pre-date the disputed invoice. Benefits are not retrospective.

## The payment clock

Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006 requires a buyer to pay a micro or small supplier by the date agreed in writing, and that agreed period cannot exceed 45 days from the day of acceptance or deemed acceptance. Where there is no written agreement, payment is due before the appointed day, which is the day after fifteen days from acceptance or deemed acceptance. Section 16 sets compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank when the buyer misses that timeline.

Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, inserted by the Finance Act, 2023 and applicable from assessment year 2024-25, defers the buyer's tax deduction for a sum payable to a micro or small enterprise when that sum is paid beyond the section 15 limit. This brief is not tax advice. The design link is enough: the payment statute and the tax statute both point at the same clock, and both apply to micro and small suppliers, not to every medium enterprise.

The Ministry's Samadhaan portal, launched in October 2017, lets a registered micro or small enterprise file a delayed-payment application online. The application goes to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council of the state or Union Territory. The 14 May 2026 PIB note says 161 such councils have been set up. The 26 June 2026 note says that as on June 2026 the portal had received 2,56,892 applications with claims of ₹55,244.29 crore, and that councils had disposed of 58,148 cases. An Online Dispute Resolution portal was launched in June 2025.

Filing is not a judgment. The council decides. The Ministry's own Samadhaan FAQ says the Ministry does not intervene in that judicial function.

## Credit classification

All bank loans to MSMEs qualify as priority sector under the Reserve Bank's directions of 24 March 2025, as restated in the MSME lending Master Direction updated on 9 February 2026. Factoring through the Trade Receivables Discounting System is eligible for priority sector. The 14 May 2026 note says Budget 2026-27 mandates TReDS for central public sector enterprises.

CGTMSE, with SIDBI, guarantees collateral-free credit to micro and small enterprises up to ₹10 crore for guarantees approved on or after 1 April 2025. The guarantee is a credit rule. The 45-day clock is a cash rule. Productivity shows up in the accounts when both are in force.
