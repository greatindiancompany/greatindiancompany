# City tiers, demat totals, and the states SEBI named

A search for "IPO by state" usually wants a ranking of Maharashtra against Gujarat or Tamil Nadu. SEBI's Annual Report 2025-26 does not publish that ranking. The geography it does publish is city-tier data for mutual fund investors, a national demat total, and a short list of states where the regulator ran bond and trust workshops. Using those three as if they were one map will mislead a state government and a broker alike.

## The investor map is Tier I, Tier II, and Tier III

Chapter 5, Table 5.9, counts unique mutual fund investors.

| Tier | 31 March 2025 | 31 March 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier I | 1,13,23,349 | 1,33,72,677 |
| Tier II | 1,02,91,153 | 1,41,55,550 |
| Tier III | 3,26,34,133 | 3,38,77,743 |
| Total | 5,42,48,635 | 6,14,05,970 |

Tier III is 55 per cent of the 2025-26 base, which is the share the chapter quotes. The fast growth is Tier II: from about 1.0 crore to 1.4 crore, the 37.6 per cent rise the chapter states. Tier I grew as well, from 1.13 crore to 1.34 crore. The base was already deepest outside the biggest cities.

This is not a state table. A Tier III city in one state and a Tier III city in another are in the same row. It is also not an IPO-subscription table. It is the mutual fund investor, the person most likely to meet an equity issue through a systematic plan or through a fund's anchor bid. Chapter 5's other household facts belong next to it. Individuals held 97.7 per cent of folios and 58.3 per cent of the ₹73.7 lakh crore of assets. SIP accounts were 10.45 crore. The beyond-top-30-cities distributor incentive now pays only for a new PAN, capped at ₹2,000, and is funded from the investor-education fund. That is the official tool aimed at places outside the largest 30 cities. It is not a state subsidy.

## Demat growth is published as a national stock

Chapter 4 says demat accounts at NSDL and CDSL together were 22.5 crore at the end of 2025-26, of which 18.8 crore had been opened since April 2020. Unique demat accounts were 12.2 crore, against 3.3 crore at the start of that span. The table underneath is by depository, not by state: 444 lakh investor accounts at NSDL and 1,801 lakh at CDSL on 31 March 2026, and 1,218 lakh unique accounts across both. Listed companies signed up for demat numbered 6,668 at NSDL and 7,006 at CDSL.

A state cabinet that wants "our share of new demat accounts" will not find it in this chapter. The regulatory fact that does travel to every state is the demat precondition in the primary market. Chapter 3 says promoters' group entities, selling shareholders, key managerial personnel, senior management, qualified institutional buyers, and directors had to dematerialise holdings before the draft red herring prospectus was filed. That rule is national. A company in a smaller city does not get a paper exception.

## Where states are named

Chapter 3's outreach box names states for two different products, both debt or yield vehicles, not equity IPOs.

For REITs and InvITs, SEBI says it held programmes with the governments of Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, and Gujarat on using those trusts to monetise state assets. A separate pan-India session brought in 49 insurance companies, 12 pension funds, IRDAI, and PFRDA.

For municipal bonds, the chapter names programmes in Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Tamil Nadu, aimed at urban local bodies and state departments. The market result next to that outreach is small beside equity IPOs and specific: 14 municipal issues raised ₹1,756 crore in 2025-26, after a single ₹100 crore issue the year before. Public corporate-debt issues, a cousin product, raised ₹11,343 crore across 43 issues. Private placement was still 98.8 per cent of the ₹9,11,078 crore debt total.

Tamil Nadu appears in both lists. That is a fact about workshops. It is not a ranking of capital raised.

## How a state team should read this

If the question is household participation, start with Table 5.9 and accept that the cut is city tier. If the question is whether local companies can list, the binding geography is the SME platform and the main board, which Chapter 3 reports nationally: 257 SME listings raising ₹11,587 crore, and 109 main-board IPOs raising ₹1.8 lakh crore. If the question is a city's own balance sheet, the relevant instrument is the municipal bond, and the relevant precedent is the ₹1,756 crore raised in 2025-26, plus the outreach record for the states named above.
