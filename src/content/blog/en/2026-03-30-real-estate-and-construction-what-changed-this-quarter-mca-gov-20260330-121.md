# What the August 2026 RBI Housing Prints Actually Change

A founder reading "what changed this quarter" in Indian real estate should separate a new statistical print from a regime that has been in force for years. As of 24 September 2026, the dated official housing prints are two Reserve Bank of India releases from August. The sale statute and the urban housing mission did not start in this window.

## Two prints, two clocks

On 24 August 2026 the RBI released the all-India House Price Index for Q1 2026-27. The index uses transaction-level data from registration authorities, base year 2022-23, and eighteen cities. It stood at 117.5, up 1.1 percent from the previous quarter and 3.6 percent from a year earlier. The year-on-year pace matched Q1 of the previous year. The release names Chandigarh, Lucknow, and Thiruvananthapuram as the main quarter-on-quarter drivers, and adds Jaipur and Kanpur among the cities contributing to the annual rise.

On 31 August 2026 the RBI released sectoral deployment of bank credit for the fortnight ended 31 July 2026, drawn from 41 scheduled commercial banks that account for about 95 percent of non-food credit. Non-food bank credit grew 19.1 percent year on year, against 9.9 percent in the corresponding fortnight of 2025. Personal loans grew 16.2 percent, against 11.9 percent. Inside that, housing and vehicle loans sustained double-digit growth, while credit-card outstanding and loans against gold jewellery decelerated. Services credit grew 22.9 percent, against 10.2 percent, and the release says that pace was supported by NBFCs, trade, and commercial real estate. Industry credit grew 20.0 percent, against 6.5 percent, and the release names infrastructure among the industries with buoyant growth.

These are not the same quarter. The price index covers April to June 2026 and was published in August. The credit print is July, published on the last day of August. Adding them into one "Q2 story" mixes a registration-price quarter with a later credit fortnight.

## What did not launch

[PMAY-U 2.0](https://www.mohua.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/pradhan-mantri-awas-yojana-urban-MjNzYjMtQWa) has been the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs mission with effect from 1 September 2024. The ministry page states an investment of ₹10 lakh crore, central assistance of ₹1.6 lakh crore, and one crore additional households, through beneficiary-led construction, affordable housing in partnership, affordable rental housing, and an interest subsidy. That is the standing mission. It is not an August 2026 announcement.

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, is older still. Sections on registration and sale have been in force since 1 May 2017. Nothing in the August statistical releases amends the separate project account, the bar on advertising before registration, or state Real Estate Regulatory Authorities.

## How not to misuse the growth rates

The credit release carries a comparison warning. From 31 December 2025, the last reporting fortnight is the last day of the month under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025. Year-on-year rates from December 2025 compare end-of-month data with the older fortnight definition for the same month a year earlier. A 19.1 percent non-food figure is the release's figure. It is not a clean like-for-like series back through 2024.

The highlights also refuse a single "construction credit" number. Housing sits inside personal loans. Commercial real estate sits inside services. Infrastructure is named inside industry. The press note does not publish a separate growth rate for the construction-industry row. That row is in Statements I and II on the [sectoral deployment page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/Data_Sectoral_Deployment.aspx). Copying the 20 percent industry rate onto "construction" is a reading error.
