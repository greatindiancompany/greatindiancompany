India's approved semiconductor factories are in six states. The [Lok Sabha reply of 22 July 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) names them: Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Gujarat, Punjab, Odisha, and Uttar Pradesh. It also says the programme leaves the choice of location to the investor, and that the government encourages distributed manufacturing. The map below is that investor choice, as recorded in Cabinet releases and the [15 August 2025 PIB factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=149242&reg=48&lang=2). Project details in full are what the reply points to on [ism.gov.in](https://www.ism.gov.in/). This page is current to 24 September 2026.

## Gujarat holds the silicon fab, the display fab, and most of the early packaging

Gujarat is the dense state in the set.

Sanand has three approved units. Micron's assembly and test plant was approved in June 2023. CG Power's specialised-chip unit, with Renesas and Stars Microelectronics, was approved on 29 February 2024 at ₹7,600 crore and 15 million chips a day; later official texts call the producing entity CG Semi. Kaynes Semicon is listed in the August 2025 factsheet as a September 2024 approval at Sanand, ₹3,307 crore, 6.33 million chips a day. On 22 July 2026 Parliament said commercial production had commenced at Micron, Kaynes Semicon, and CG Semi. All three of those names are in Gujarat.

Dholera has the silicon fab and the gallium-nitride display plant. Tata Electronics with PSMC was approved on 29 February 2024 at ₹91,000 crore and 50,000 wafer starts a month, including 28 nm high-performance compute. Crystal Matrix was approved on [5 May 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2258116) for an integrated compound fab and assembly line making mini and micro-LED modules, with gallium-nitride epitaxy on 6-inch wafers.

Surat has Suchi Semicon, approved the same day, an OSAT for discrete semiconductors with a stated capacity of 1,033.20 million chips a year, aimed at power electronics, analog chips, automotive, industrial automation, and consumer electronics.

The May 2026 pair, both in Gujarat, was approved at a combined investment of around ₹3,936 crore. Gujarat's weight in the national total is the Dholera silicon fab. ₹91,000 crore of the national commitment of about ₹1.64 lakh crore sits in that one project.

## One project each in Assam, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, and Andhra Pradesh

Assam's approved factory is Tata Semiconductor Assembly and Test at Morigaon, approved on 29 February 2024 at ₹27,000 crore, with a stated capacity of 48 million chips a day and indigenous flip-chip and integrated system-in-package technology. The Lok Sabha reply also records a separate training decision: a semiconductor assembly and ESD laboratory at NIELIT Guwahati, aimed at the North-Eastern manufacturing pipeline. The laboratory is a skills project. It is not a thirteenth manufacturing approval.

Uttar Pradesh's approved project in the factsheet is the HCL-Foxconn joint venture at Jewar, dated May 2025, at ₹3,700 crore, with capacity stated as 20,000 wafers a month. The factsheet's unit-output figure for this plant is not used here, because official wording of that output has not been consistent enough to quote. Jewar is the state's entry in the six-state list.

Punjab's entry is a brownfield expansion, not a new logic campus. On [12 August 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2155459) the Cabinet approved Continental Device India at Mohali to expand high-power discrete devices: MOSFETs, IGBTs, Schottky bypass diodes, and transistors, in silicon and silicon carbide, at 158.38 million units a year. Mohali also has the Semiconductor Laboratory, where the February 2026 budget note places academic chip fabrication at 180 nm. CDIL's discrete line and the Semiconductor Laboratory are different facilities. A state note that merges them will mis-state both the product and the node.

Andhra Pradesh's entry is Advanced System in Package Technologies, approved on 12 August 2025, with a technology tie-up with APACT of South Korea and an annual capacity of 96 million units for mobile phones, set-top boxes, automobiles, and other electronics. The approval release names the state and does not name the city. The factsheet lists the investment at ₹468 crore.

## Odisha has the compound fab and the glass packaging line

Both of Odisha's units are in Info Valley, Bhubaneswar, and both were approved on 12 August 2025.

SiCSem, with Clas-SiC Wafer Fab of the United Kingdom, is an integrated silicon-carbide compound facility. The release calls it the first commercial compound fab in the country, with 60,000 wafers a year and packaging capacity of 96 million units. The applications named are missiles, defence equipment, electric vehicles, railways, fast chargers, data-centre racks, appliances, and solar inverters.

3D Glass Solutions is a vertically integrated advanced-packaging and embedded-glass-substrate unit. Planned capacity is about 69,600 glass panel substrates, 50 million assembled units, and 13,200 3D heterogeneous-integration modules a year, for defence, high-performance computing, artificial intelligence, radio frequency, automotive, photonics, and co-packaged optics.

The four projects approved that day, Odisha's two plus Punjab and Andhra Pradesh, were grouped at around ₹4,600 crore and 2,034 skilled professionals. That group total is what took the national count from six projects to ten, at cumulative commitments the release put at around ₹1.60 lakh crore.

## States that asked, and states outside the six

Question 483 asked specifically whether any approved semiconductor project was in Rajasthan. The reply answers by naming the six states above and saying plants are located in those six. Rajasthan is not among them on that date.

A state government reading this map for a new bid has a narrow official lesson. Location is the investor's choice. The distribution Parliament describes is six states, with Gujarat holding the largest single commitment and the only approved commercial silicon fab. Components under the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme can land elsewhere. That scheme is national, and its target segments are sub-assemblies, bare components, and capital goods, not the 12 mission projects.
