# When Solar Module PLI Cash Follows the Factory

The investment question in India's solar module PLI is timing. The factory spends capital first. The incentive is defined as a payment on manufacture and sale after the plant is commissioned, and only for five years.

## The scheme as MNRE states it

MNRE's PLI page, last updated 8 August 2023, says the ministry is implementing the Production Linked Incentive scheme under the National Programme on High Efficiency Solar PV Modules, with an outlay of Rs 24,000 crore. Manufacturers are selected through a transparent process. The incentive goes to selected manufacturers for five years after commissioning, on the manufacture and sale of high-efficiency modules.

The stated aims are a gigawatt-scale high-efficiency industry, less import dependence, integrated plants, local materials, jobs, and technology. The page says the scheme is technology agnostic and will allow all technologies, while giving a higher incentive to technologies that yield better module performance. Integration is an objective because the ministry ties it to quality control and competitiveness. An investor who models every awarded megawatt as the same incentive rate is ignoring that sentence.

## Two tranches, two agencies, two rupee envelopes

Tranche I began with Union Cabinet approval on 7 April 2021. MNRE issued guidelines on 28 April 2021 with an outlay of Rs 4,500 crore. Indian Renewable Energy Development Agency was the implementing agency. Letters of award went out in November and December 2021 to three bidders for 8,737 MW of fully integrated module manufacturing, inside that Rs 4,500 crore outlay.

Tranche II followed Cabinet approval on 21 September 2022. Guidelines were issued on 30 September 2022 with an outlay of Rs 19,500 crore. SECI is the implementing agency. Letters of award went to 11 bidders in April 2023 for 39,600 MW of fully or partially integrated manufacturing.

The two outlays add up to the Rs 24,000 crore headline. They are not one cheque. A tranche-I fully integrated plant and a tranche-II partially integrated plant sit under different guidelines, different agencies, and different award years.

SECI's solar page states a further figure that the MNRE overview does not: a PLI amount of around Rs 13,937.535 crore allocated to the 11 tranche-II manufacturers, to be disbursed over five years after commissioning. That allocated amount is lower than the Rs 19,500 crore tranche-II outlay. This brief does not invent a reason for the gap. It only insists that a model label the page it came from. Outlay, allocation, and cash paid are three balances.

## What the pages do not give an investor

The MNRE page updated in August 2023 does not report modules sold, incentive disbursed, or a commissioning date for each bidder. SECI's page says disbursement follows commissioning and then runs for five years. Until a later official note reports a payment, an investor should treat disbursement as unstated, not as zero and not as paid.

Eligibility to sell into many Indian projects is a separate regime. MNRE's ALMM page says List-I modules are required for government projects, schemes, open access, and net-metering, and that the first cell list was issued on 31 July 2025. A PLI award does not, on these pages, substitute for enlistment. Revenue from a government-linked buyer can depend on the list even when the incentive depends on production and sale.

## How to underwrite the lag

A serious note on this scheme has a simple cash calendar. Capital goes in to build the awarded line. Commissioning starts the five-year window. Incentive is then tied to modules manufactured and sold, with better performance and deeper integration treated more favourably under the scheme's own objectives. Partial integration, which tranche II explicitly allows, is not the same asset as the fully integrated capacity awarded in tranche I.
