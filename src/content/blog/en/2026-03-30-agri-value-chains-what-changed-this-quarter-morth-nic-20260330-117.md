# What the kharif papers show in September 2026

A reader opening this cluster in late September 2026 needs the papers that actually moved in the quarter, and a hard line between last year's crop and the crop now in the ground. Three official prints do that work. A Cabinet decision on 31 July continued PM-KISAN. A sowing report dated 8 September gave area as on 4 September. A Lok Sabha reply dated 15 July restated the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund stock. The May MSP and the May production estimate sit underneath those prints. They are not this quarter's news, and they are not this year's harvest.

## The income transfer was given another five years

On 31 July 2026 the Union Cabinet continued the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi from 2026-27 to 2030-31, with a financial outlay of Rs 3.15 lakh crore. The design is unchanged: eligible landholding farmer families receive Rs 6,000 a year in three equal instalments, paid into bank accounts.

The same note is the latest count of money already paid. It says more than Rs 4.47 lakh crore has been transferred through 23 instalments. Under the 23rd instalment, more than 9.49 crore farmers benefited and over Rs 18,984 crore was released. Women farmers have received more than Rs 1.06 lakh crore, and the note says nearly one in four beneficiaries is a woman farmer.

An earlier Lok Sabha reply, on 10 March 2026, had put the cumulative at over Rs 4.09 lakh crore through 21 instalments. The two sentences can both be true. They are different dates. Use the July figure as the later stock, and do not subtract them to invent a quarterly flow.

The July note also reports a DMEO evaluation at NITI Aayog: more than 92 per cent of beneficiaries said they used the assistance for agricultural activities, and about 85 per cent reported an improvement in agricultural income and less dependence on informal credit. That is the evaluation's finding as the Cabinet note prints it. It is an income-support result. It is not a warehouse, a mandi, or an export.

## The new crop is an area number, not a tonne number

On 8 September 2026 the Ministry of Agriculture published kharif area as on 4 September, sourced to the Crop Weather Watch Group. All-India coverage stood at 1,086.31 lakh hectares, against 1,104.00 lakh hectares at the same point in 2025 and a normal of 1,104.46.

Rice is the line that moves the chain. Coverage was 421.82 lakh hectares, down 16.37 lakh hectares from 438.19. The ministry names the largest declines: Karnataka 4.28, Telangana 3.61, Andhra Pradesh 1.91, Uttar Pradesh 1.84, Madhya Pradesh 1.75, Jharkhand 1.38, Tamil Nadu 1.32, and Maharashtra 1.15 lakh hectares. Assam and Odisha were higher. Rice was still above its normal of 412.00 lakh hectares. A year-on-year drop and a position above normal are both in the same row.

Pulses were 117.13 lakh hectares, up 1.84 from last year and still short of the normal 123.63. Urad rose 2.68 lakh hectares to 25.15, against a normal of 29.60. Tur was 45.66, above both last year and its normal of 44.31. Moong fell 1.04 lakh hectares to 33.19. Cotton was 109.17 lakh hectares, barely down on the year and far below its normal of 125.51. Oilseeds were 191.99, against a normal of 200.08. Maize was 91.09, down 2.99 on the year and still above its normal of 80.77.

None of these hectares is a production estimate for 2026-27. The latest tonne figure in hand is the Third Advance Estimate for 2025-26, released on 27 May 2026: foodgrain at 376.563 million tonnes, rice at 154.024 million tonnes, wheat at 120.657 million tonnes. That estimate describes the crop already grown. Putting it next to the September sowing report as if it were the same harvest is the mistake this quarter invites.

## The price list the marketing season will use

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs set kharif minimum support prices for marketing season 2026-27 on 13 May 2026. Common paddy is Rs 2,441 a quintal, up Rs 72 from Rs 2,369, against a cost of Rs 1,627 and a margin of 50 per cent. The largest absolute increases were sunflower seed (Rs 622 a quintal), cotton (Rs 557), nigerseed (Rs 515), and sesamum (Rs 500). The pricing rule, restated from the Union Budget for 2018-19, is at least one and a half times the all-India weighted average cost of production.

That price list is what kharif procurement walks into. The May release also reports history, not a promise for this season: paddy procurement of 8,418 lakh metric tonnes during 2014-15 to 2025-26, and MSP payments to paddy growers of Rs 16.08 lakh crore over the same period. Whether this season's arrivals are bought at the new MSP is a later procurement print.

## The infrastructure stock, dated July

A Lok Sabha reply placed the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund, as on 15 July 2026, at a cumulative capital investment of Rs 1,61,537.22 crore for post-harvest infrastructure and viable farming assets, including Rs 50,332.66 crore for farm-gate storage and logistics. An earlier reply, as on 26 January 2026, had said loans of Rs 80,224.15 crore were sanctioned for 1,50,431 projects, mobilising investment of Rs 1,27,508 crore. The labels differ: capital investment leveraged, and loan amount sanctioned. They should stay in separate sentences.

NABSanrakshan is the credit-guarantee name the ministry uses, alongside CGTMSE, for AIF loans up to Rs 2 crore. The operating totals above are the ministry's, not a figure taken from a NABARD annual report.
