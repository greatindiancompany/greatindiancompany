# What changed in India’s kharif support prices

The price that matters for paddy arriving this autumn is the Kharif Marketing Season 2026-27 minimum support price. It was fixed on 13 May 2026, before this quarter, and it is the floor state agencies use when they open purchase centres.

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs raised MSP for 14 kharif crops. Common paddy is ₹2,441 a quintal, against ₹2,369 in KMS 2025-26. Grade A paddy is ₹2,461, against ₹2,389. Both are up ₹72. The cost figure used for common paddy is ₹1,627 a quintal, and the announced margin over that cost is 50 percent.

Paddy is not where the largest rupee increases sit. In the same decision sunflower seed rose ₹622, to ₹8,343. Medium-staple cotton rose ₹557, to ₹8,267, and long-staple cotton rose ₹557, to ₹8,667. Nigerseed rose ₹515, to ₹10,052. Sesamum rose ₹500, to ₹10,346. The Cabinet release says the margin over cost is highest for moong at 61 percent, then bajra at 56 percent, maize at 56 percent, and tur at 54 percent. For the other kharif crops the margin is 50 percent.

## Wheat is a different season

Wheat is a rabi crop. On 1 October 2025 the Cabinet set wheat MSP for Rabi Marketing Season 2026-27 at ₹2,585 a quintal, ₹160 above the ₹2,425 used in RMS 2025-26. The cost figure for that wheat price is ₹1,239, and the margin is 109 percent. That wheat floor applies to the 2027 rabi harvest. It is not the price for paddy moving in October, November, and December 2026.

Last kharif season’s common-paddy MSP, for comparison, was ₹2,369 against a cost of ₹1,579, also a 50 percent margin, and only ₹69 above KMS 2024-25. The step from 2024-25 to 2025-26 and the step from 2025-26 to 2026-27 are both small in paddy and larger in oilseeds and cotton. Reading only the paddy line understates what the Cabinet changed.

## What the price decision does not say

MSP remains an administered floor for mandated crops, announced ahead of the season, at least one and a half times the all-India weighted average cost of production. The Union Budget for 2018-19 set that principle. The May 2026 kharif release and the October 2025 rabi release both repeat it. The cost includes paid-out expenses and the imputed value of family labour. It is one national number per crop, not a state quote and not a mandi price.

A higher MSP also does not tell you how many tonnes will be bought. The Food Corporation of India and state agencies procure cereals. Pulses, oilseeds, and copra move under PM-AASHA when a state asks and the market price is below the floor. The May 2026 papers change the floor. They do not close the books on KMS 2026-27 procurement, because that buying had not happened yet.

A miller, a commission agent, or a founder buying paddy this quarter should treat ₹2,441 and ₹2,461 as the policy floor, then check the state’s own centre list and moisture rules. Those operating details are not in the Cabinet table. An investor who blends the May 2026 paddy MSP with the October 2025 wheat MSP and calls the blend a quarterly index is mixing two seasons.
