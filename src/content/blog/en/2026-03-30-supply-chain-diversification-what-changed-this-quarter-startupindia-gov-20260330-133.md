# What changed this quarter in India's manufacturing trade stack

A founder scanning "supply chain diversification" this quarter will not find one circular that rewrites the map. The useful change is that India now has a dated stack of manufacturing and trade instruments, and a few numbers with a year attached. Treat the quarter as a check against that stack, not as a new slogan.

## The standing instruments

The Foreign Trade Policy 2023, notified by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade on 31 March 2023, has been in force since 1 April 2023 and continues until it is amended. Importers and exporters still need an Importer-Exporter Code from DGFT. Preferential sourcing still depends on whether a free trade agreement is actually in force, and on a certificate of origin, not on a headline that a deal was "announced."

Production-linked incentives remain the manufacturing localization tool. On 2 August 2023, the Minister of State for Commerce and Industry told the Lok Sabha that schemes for 14 sectors had been notified, with an incentive outlay of Rs 1.97 lakh crore, and that the Union Cabinet had not approved any new PLI sector as of that reply. Later notes can update participation. They do not turn the outlay into money already spent.

The National Logistics Policy, launched on 17 September 2022, still sits beside PM Gati Shakti: one side is integrated infrastructure planning, the other is logistics services, process, and skills. On the input side, the Union Cabinet approved the National Critical Mineral Mission on 29 January 2025, covering the chain from exploration through processing and recovery from end-of-life products.

## What a reader can actually date

Three official statements are recent enough to use as quarter markers. Each one answers a different question.

The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade and NCAER published an assessment of logistics cost for 2023-24. A Press Information Bureau note on 27 November 2025 reports that estimate as 7.97 percent of GDP, 9.09 percent of non-services output, and Rs 24.01 lakh crore in absolute terms. The same note says commonly cited figures of 13 to 14 percent of GDP were overestimates drawn from partial data. That is a change in the official yardstick. It is not, by itself, a measured quarter-on-quarter fall in freight rates.

On 13 May 2026, the Commerce and Industry Minister said India's exports reached USD 863 billion in the year ending 31 March 2026, and set an aspirational export target of USD 1 trillion for the then-current year. He also said nine trade agreements over the previous three and a half years covered 38 economies, with four already operational and five expected to come into force within 12 months. Use that as a status claim from the minister. Confirm which four are operational on the Department of Commerce and DGFT sites before a contract assumes a duty preference.

The India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement is one agreement whose start date is already on the Commerce record: signed on 18 February 2022, in force from 1 May 2022. A first-anniversary Commerce note reported bilateral goods trade rising from USD 72.9 billion in 2021-22 to USD 84.5 billion in 2022-23. That is a historical trade print, not a 2026 quarterly print.

## What did not change just because the quarter turned

No official series in this set publishes a single "diversification index" for the quarter. PLI selection counts from August 2023 (733 applications selected, 176 of them MSMEs in named sectors) are a stock of approvals at that date. They are not this quarter's output. A buyer's "China-plus-one" slide is not an Indian notification.

The practical quarterly habit is short. Open new DGFT notifications. Check whether any FTA moved from signature to entry into force. Read the latest PIB note on PLI before repeating the 2023 outlay. Pull merchandise lines from the Commerce trade databank at tradestat.commerce.gov.in rather than from a recycled chart.
