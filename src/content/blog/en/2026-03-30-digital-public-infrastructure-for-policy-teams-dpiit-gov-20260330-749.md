# What policy teams should measure on India's digital rails

Policy teams do not need another slogan for digital public infrastructure. They need a short list of series they can defend in a file note, and a refusal list of claims the current publications cannot carry. The good news is that several publishers already meet a high standard. The work is to copy that standard, and to stop borrowing numbers from a neighbouring rail.

## Copy the Aadhaar note, including the caveats

UIDAI's saturation report as on 30 June 2026 is the model. It publishes a projected population, a raw assignment count, a live estimate, and a penetration rate, for every state and Union Territory. It then says two things that change the interpretation. Holders are counted by the address on the Aadhaar, so they may not be domiciled in the state. The live estimate adjusts for deaths using Registrar General death rates. National live penetration is 94.59 percent. Nagaland is 62.41 percent. Kerala is 105.02 percent. All three figures can be true.

A state government writing a scheme note should paste the row for its own state and the two caveats, not the national 94.59 percent alone. Bihar's row is 88.29 percent and 11,67,82,847 estimated live assignments against a projected population of 13,22,65,000. Gujarat is 89.74 percent. A note that says "Aadhaar is universal" will be wrong in those states on UIDAI's own page. A note that treats a figure above 100 percent as surplus population will also be wrong. It is an address-based statistic.

If the scheme authenticates, the policy metric is the live column, not the raw column. Raw assignments nationally are 1,44,80,05,767. Live estimates are 1,34,76,52,046. Using the raw stock overstates the number of living holders.

## Do not ask ONDC for a table it says it does not have

On 4 August 2026 the Commerce Ministry answered a Lok Sabha question that asked for sellers, traders, and MSMEs on the Open Network for Digital Commerce, state by state. The reply says the network does not maintain an individually attributed count of every seller, because sellers join through network participants and can move. The figures it will stand behind, as of July 2026, are national: over 500 million cumulative orders, over 500 orders a minute, approximately 2 lakh or more active retail merchants, about 60 percent of them small sellers, and approximately 10 lakh or more mobility and logistics service providers.

A state claim such as "ONDC has onboarded this many MSMEs in our state" is not supported by that answer. Policy teams that need the state figure have to build it with the seller applications, or require a reporting clause in the state's own onboarding scheme. The TEAM initiative, with ₹277.35 crore approved through 2026-27 and a target that half of beneficiary enterprises be women-owned, is a place where a state can attach reporting, because it is a government assistance programme rather than a property of the section 8 company. DigiDukaan, as described, was operating in Hyderabad and Jaipur. Other states should not cite it as if it were already local.

## Health has a dashboard. Date it.

The health ministry's reply of 24 July 2026 gives a frozen national picture as of 20 July 2026: 94.87 crore ABHA IDs, 5.36 lakh facilities, 10.09 lakh professionals. It then points to dashboard.abdm.gov.in for state and district distribution, and it gives Tamil Nadu as 2.57 crore ABHAs in that cut. The right policy practice is to quote the parliamentary national total when you need a stable anchor, and to cite the dashboard with the date you exported it when you need a district. Do not call a live screen "as of 20 July" three months later.

The same reply states the design constraints a health department should not reverse in a circular: no centralised repository of health records, exchange after consent, sandbox validation, and a web application security audit before integration. A state project that asks for a consolidated copy of clinical records "for analytics" is asking for something the national reply says the mission does not do. Named integrations, including PM-JAY, Nikshay, and specific state platforms, show the permitted pattern.

The third-party evaluation from June to August 2025 is summarised as reduced waiting times, cost savings, and better access, especially for women and underserved groups. The reply does not print the study's numbers. A policy note should not promote those sentences into percentages. If the study has a table, cite the study. If it does not, keep the finding qualitative.

## Spending notes should use the account that matches the claim

MeitY's Lok Sabha reply of 29 July 2026 is clear that Digital India is an umbrella and that MeitY's own eight central-sector sub-schemes are not a state-wise budget. The published envelope for those schemes in 2025-26 was ₹5,144.45 crore allocated and ₹4,638.28 crore spent. DigiLocker's own disclosed allocation since inception was ₹168.58 crore, with ₹105.14 crore spent, beside more than 936.03 crore documents accessible. UMANG's was ₹149.91 crore allocated and ₹129.15 crore utilised.

A state file that says "digital public infrastructure capex" and then quotes only the MeitY umbrella has mixed a ministry envelope with fibre, health, and payments. BharatNet is the figure to add when the subject is rural reach: as of June 2026, 2.21 lakh gram panchayats service-ready, and points of presence operational at 80,472 gram panchayats during that month. Both numbers belong in the note. Quoting only the service-ready count will overstate what a panchayat can use.

UPI should be discussed as a payment system with a published monthly table, not as a MeitY expenditure line. August 2026 on NPCI's product statistics was 752 banks, 24,508.96 million transactions, and ₹29,82,355.95 crore. The PIB account of 29 July, which says 731 banks and more than 55.49 crore individuals, is an earlier snapshot. Label the month.

## Service delivery needs a channel metric, not only an app metric

The Rajya Sabha statement as on 31 July 2026 says UMANG and DigiLocker are integrated with Aadhaar, eSign, and API Setu, and that 4,07,122 Common Service Centres were functional at gram panchayat level on 30 June 2026, including 70,069 operated by women. The May 2026 PIB cut said 5.01 lakh CSCs were functional in all, including 3.91 lakh at gram panchayat level, with over 800 services.

For a rural scheme, the policy metric is whether the resident can finish at a centre, not only whether the app exists. PMGDISHA, which trained 6.39 crore people, concluded on 31 March 2024. Do not list it as a live accompanying measure. If digital literacy is still part of the scheme, name the instrument that replaced it. The 2022-23 survey cited in the July 2026 account, in which 78.4 percent of people aged 15 to 24 could send an attachment, is a baseline from that survey year. It is not a 2026 administrative statistic.

Language is a stated inclusion tool. The 29 July account describes BHASHINI as the public platform for Indian language technology. DIKSHA supports 36 languages and lets each state run its own programme. A scheme note that ships a Hindi-only or English-only flow should say so, against those two facts.

## The legal calendar is now part of scheme design

G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025 brought some provisions of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 into force immediately, including the basis for the Data Protection Board established the same day. It appointed section 6(9) and section 27(1)(d) for one year later, and the main operative sections for eighteen months later. Scheme architects writing in late 2026 should put the May 2027 date in the file and should read the two November 2026 clauses in the Act. Sector rules do not wait for that date. Account Aggregator consent duties are already in the Reserve Bank's November 2025 directions. A scheme that shares financial information should follow those directions now, and should not tell participants that "DPDP has not started" as a blanket sentence.

## Evaluations: ask for the table

MeitY told the Lok Sabha that IIPA evaluated the electronic-governance scheme in November 2025 and found better access, less time, and paperless delivery. The printed findings are qualitative. The same is true of the ABDM evaluation summary. Policy teams should commission or retrieve the underlying tables before a minister's brief converts "reduced time" into a number. Until then, the honest line is that an independent evaluation was done and that the parliamentary summary does not quantify it.

## A file note that will survive a question

State the rail, the metric, the date, and the publisher. State the caveat UIDAI-style if there is one. If the publisher has said the state cut does not exist, say that, and do not estimate it. If two answers give two DigiLocker user counts, show both dates: over 71.66 crore in the 29 July Lok Sabha answer, and more than 72.43 crore as on 31 July in the Rajya Sabha statement. The discipline is dull. It is also what makes a digital-infrastructure claim believable when the next question is asked.
