# Domestic visits and foreign visits rank the states differently

Domestic tourist visits and foreign tourist visits are both state-reported counts in the Ministry of Tourism’s India Tourism Data Compendium 2025. They do not rank the states in the same order, and a visit is not a unique person. One pilgrim counted at several sites, or one circuit counted by more than one state, can raise the visit total without raising the number of travellers.

## Domestic visits in 2024

India recorded 2,948.19 million domestic tourist visits in 2024, 17.51 percent above 2,508.82 million in 2023. Table 4.1.2 of the compendium gives the top five and their shares:

- Uttar Pradesh, 646.81 million, 21.94 percent.
- Tamil Nadu, 306.84 million, 10.41 percent.
- Karnataka, 304.56 million, 10.33 percent.
- Andhra Pradesh, 290.27 million, 9.85 percent.
- Rajasthan, 230.08 million, 7.80 percent.

Those five were 60.38 percent of domestic visits. Uttar Pradesh alone was more than a fifth. A national “domestic tourism is booming” line that does not say this is a description of a few large states’ visitor statistics, which include pilgrimage and day visits.

Foreign tourist visits were 20.94 million in 2024, 8.84 percent above 19.24 million in 2023. The leading states in the compendium’s summary were Maharashtra (3.70 million, 17.69 percent), West Bengal (3.12 million, 14.92 percent), Uttar Pradesh (2.27 million, 10.83 percent), Gujarat (2.27 million, 10.86 percent), and Rajasthan (2.07 million, 9.90 percent). Together they were 64.34 percent of foreign tourist visits. Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, second and third for domestic visits, are not in that foreign top five. Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan are in both lists. Maharashtra leads foreign visits and is not in the domestic top five printed in that paragraph.

Air arrivals add a third map. In 2024, 83.41 percent of foreign tourist arrivals entered by air, and Delhi and Mumbai airports handled 57.2 percent of those air arrivals. The state a visitor is later counted in, and the airport they landed at, are different geographies.

## Where UDAN’s early exits cluster

The aviation map is not the visit map. On 23 July 2026 the civil aviation ministry gave the Lok Sabha a state list of RCS routes discontinued before they completed their viability-gap tenure. The annexure sums to 165 routes. Uttar Pradesh has 34, Uttarakhand 17, Assam 15, Madhya Pradesh 14, Karnataka 12, Gujarat 11, and Rajasthan 11. Tamil Nadu has 2. Andhra Pradesh has 3.

Uttar Pradesh is therefore the largest domestic-visit state and the largest early-exit state on that UDAN list. The two facts do not cancel. Domestic visits are mostly road, rail, and pilgrimage counts. An RCS route is a subsidised flight that stopped before the support period ended. A state can have hundreds of millions of visits and still have lost regional routes.

Cumulative viability-gap funding disbursed as on 30 June 2026, in a separate annexure, is also uneven: Karnataka ₹823.73 crore, Maharashtra ₹632.29 crore, Uttar Pradesh ₹523.87 crore, Rajasthan ₹282.10 crore. Karnataka’s funding stock is the largest on that list, while its early-exit count is 12, not 34. Funding paid and routes abandoned are both official, and they rank states differently.

Monument counts are a fourth cut, for 2024-25. Among centrally protected ticketed monuments, the Taj Mahal led domestic visitors at 6.26 million and foreign visitors at 0.645 million. The Sun Temple at Konark had 3.57 million domestic visitors and Qutub Minar 3.20 million. Foreign visitors at Agra Fort and at Qutub Minar were 0.22 million each.

A state tourism cell reading only its domestic rank will mis-locate the foreign visitor and the regional flight.
