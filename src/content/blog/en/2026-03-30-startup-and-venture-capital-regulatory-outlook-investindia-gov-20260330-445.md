Startup capital in India crosses at least four official gates: DPIIT recognition, an income-tax application, a set of Companies Act relaxations, and SEBI registration of the fund. Each gate has its own statute and its own date. A term sheet that treats “startup” as one status will miss the gate that actually binds.

## Gate one: the gazette identity

G.S.R. 108(E) of 4 February 2026 is the recognition test, restated on the Startup India page. The entity is a private limited company, partnership, LLP, or cooperative society. Age is up to 10 years, or 20 for a deep-tech startup. Turnover is below ₹200 crore in any previous financial year, or below ₹300 crore for deep tech. The work is innovation or improvement with a potential to create employment or wealth. A split or reconstruction of an existing business is excluded. The page routes the application through the National Single Window System. A Lok Sabha reply describes the process as self-certification. DPIIT states on the page that it charges no fee and appoints no franchise to issue the certificate.

Recognition can expire with age or turnover. Fund of Funds 2.0 guidelines still allow a fund to release the unpaid balance of a commitment made while the entity was recognised. The certificate and the commitment have different clocks.

## Gate two: tax applications the portal still offers

The recognition page tells a recognised startup it may then apply for a deduction under section 80-IAC and for an exemption under section 56, which the page calls angel tax. The 80-IAC instructions on that page are specific. The deduction is a tax holiday for three consecutive financial years out of the first ten since incorporation. Only a private limited company or an LLP may apply. Incorporation should be after 1 April 2016. Partnerships and cooperatives, though recognisable, are outside this limb. The page does not lengthen the ten-year tax window for deep-tech firms.

The section 56 link is an application path printed on the portal. It is not a commentary on whether that charging provision still applies to a given allotment. Anyone documenting a round should read the current Income-tax Act beside the portal, not instead of it.

A Rajya Sabha reply of 13 February 2026 lists related tax measures in force as government policy: profit-linked deductions under section 80-IAC, deferral of TDS on ESOP income, relaxation on carry-forward and set-off of losses, and specified GST relaxations for entrepreneurs in eligible incubators. The reply does not restate rates or the ESOP deferral period. Those details belong in the Finance Act and the Board circulars, not in a paraphrase.

## Gate three: Companies Act relaxations, as MCA listed them

The same reply’s Annexure IV records Ministry of Corporate Affairs relaxations for startups under the Companies Act, 2013. Among them: the cash-flow statement is optional; startups may hold two board meetings in a calendar year rather than four; sweat equity may go up to 50 percent of paid-up share capital, against a general ceiling of 25 percent; ESOPs may be issued to promoters and to directors who would otherwise be excluded; a convertible note of ₹25 lakh or more, convertible into equity or repayable within ten years, is not treated as a deposit; and startups may accept deposits from members without the ordinary 35 percent ceiling. These are statutory accommodations for companies that qualify. They are not a substitute for SEBI rules once the money comes from a pooled fund.

## Gate four: the fund’s SEBI category

SEBI’s Alternative Investment Funds Regulations, 2012, were last amended on 18 April 2026. The consolidated text on SEBI’s site is the document a manager should use, ahead of older FAQs. Category I funds invest in startups or early-stage ventures, social ventures, SMEs, infrastructure, or other areas the government or regulators treat as socially or economically desirable. Venture capital funds sit in that category. Category II covers funds that are not Category I or III and that do not leverage except for limited operational needs. Private equity and debt funds are the usual occupants. Category III is the third registration and is outside Fund of Funds 2.0.

DPIIT’s guidelines limit Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0 to Category I and Category II. Selection is two-stage: the implementation agency screens and a Venture Capital Investment Committee recommends, then a sub-committee of the agency’s board sanctions. The named external members on the 25 April 2026 PIB list are Vallabh Bhansali, Dr. Ashok Jhunjhunwala, Dr. Renu Swarup, Dr. Chintan Vaishnav, and Rajesh Gopinathan, plus agency representatives. An Empowered Committee chaired by the Secretary, DPIIT, monitors the scheme. Government participation in a single fund, counting other official funds of funds, stays within 50 percent of corpus.
