# The 2030 Broadband Gaps National Broadband Mission 2.0 Still Lists

National Broadband Mission 2.0 was unveiled on 17 January 2025 and, in the Department's later description, starts on 1 April 2025. The mission runs to 2030. The useful mid-term document is not a slogan. It is the paired baseline and target the minister placed on the record on 12 February 2026. This brief repeats those pairs and stops. It does not convert a gap into a required annual build rate, because the releases do not publish one.

## Two markers the reply treats as nearly done

Right-of-way application disposal had fallen from 455 days to 30.4 days. The minister described the 2030 ease marker as met ahead of schedule. That national average coexists with states that had not implemented the 2024 rules. Meeting a national average is a real administrative result. It is a weak description of every district.

Fibre mapping of government public-sector networks had reached 94 percent on the PM GatiShakti platform, with a target of 100 percent "by March." The January 2025 vision note had spoken of 2026 for that mapping. The February 2026 reply says March. Use the later parliamentary wording, and treat the month as the government's, without adding a year the sentence does not contain.

## Five markers with a visible distance

The same reply states:

- Optical fibre connectivity with 95 percent uptime was in 42,000 villages as of December 2025. The 2030 target is 2.7 lakh villages.
- Broadband at anchor institutions such as schools, anganwadis, and panchayat offices was 68.8 percent. The 2030 target is 90 percent.
- The national average fixed broadband download speed was 61.55 Mbps, which the written statement attributes to Ookla's index as of December 2025. The 2030 target is 100 Mbps.
- Rural internet subscribers per 100 population stood at 47.16. The 2030 target is 60.
- Sustainable energy at mobile towers was 12.38 percent. The 2030 target is 30 percent.

The village-uptime gap is the widest in plain language: 42,000 against a target of 2.7 lakh. It also sits beside a different BharatNet count in the same reply, nearly 2.14 lakh gram panchayats already "online" under Phases I and II. A trajectory that uses only the larger count will report the mission as almost finished. The mission's own uptime line says otherwise.

## A second rural-density series, dated differently

TRAI's January–March 2026 indicators put rural internet subscribers per 100 population at 48.31 percent, urban at 126.80 percent, and the national figure at 76.59 percent. The mission baseline of 47.16 is the number tied to the target of 60. TRAI's 48.31 percent is the number tied to a defined quarter and a population projection. Both can be cited. They should not be averaged. Urban internet density above 100 in the TRAI series means stacked connections, including more than one link per person, so the rural climb from the high 40s toward 60 is a coverage question, while the urban figure is already a multi-connection question.

Fixed wired broadband, 48.14 million lines at end-July 2026, is the physical network most relevant to the 100 Mbps fixed-speed target. Mobile broadband, 1,027.50 million lines, dominates the subscriber total and is a weak stand-in for that speed target. PIB's older October 2024 snapshot, published in December 2024, quoted a median mobile broadband speed of 95.67 Mbps. That series is not the fixed-speed series the mission is scoring.
