# Plant investment meets the jobs women already hold

A capital-expenditure cycle shows up in the labour statistics only if the new activity hires people into a status the survey can see as regular work. For women, that status is still the minority of employment. The useful watch is the regular-wage share, the establishment thresholds that attach to it, and the sector mix MoSPI publishes for all workers. This brief does not invent a rupee capex series. MoSPI’s labour releases are not a capex account.

## Where women workers sit when investment is discussed

In usual status for 2025, 18.2 percent of women workers were in regular wage or salaried employment, compared with 16.6 percent in the 2024 reading the press note cites. Self-employment was 64.2 percent, compared with 66.5 percent. MoSPI attaches a comparability warning to 2025 because the sample design changed in January 2025. The direction the press note itself describes is a modest shift toward regular wages, with self-employment still the majority status for women.

Geography decides whether a plant’s hiring market looks like that average. Rural women workers were 9.3 percent regular wage and 70.7 percent self-employed. Urban women workers were 50.9 percent regular wage and 40.4 percent self-employed. A factory, a large construction site, or a services centre in a city is hiring into the urban regular-wage pool. A rural expansion that stays inside household enterprise work does not pass through that pool at all.

Men’s regular-wage share in the same table was 26.5 percent. The all-person regular-wage share rose from 22.4 percent to 23.6 percent in the press note’s 2024-to-2025 comparison. Women’s share moved as well, and it started lower, especially outside cities.

## Sector shares are published for all workers

The 2025 press note says agriculture’s share of workers fell from 44.8 percent in 2024 to 43.0 percent in 2025, manufacturing rose from 11.6 percent to 12.1 percent, and other services rose from 12.2 percent to 13.1 percent. Those are all-worker shares. The note does not print the same industry split for women in the summary. Using the manufacturing gain as a women’s employment gain adds a sex split the paragraph does not contain.

The April–June 2026 quarterly bulletin, on the weekly status, says the share of regular wage or salaried employees among all persons aged 15 and above rose from 15.5 percent to 16.1 percent in rural areas and from 48.9 percent to 49.3 percent in urban areas. Again, that is all workers. It is consistent with a labour market in which salaried work is an urban phenomenon. It is not yet a sex-specific capex result.

## The thresholds that sit on an establishment’s books

When an establishment does hire, two labour rules scale with headcount rather than with the capex line. The Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, as amended in 2017, is the law the Ministry of Labour and Employment says it is implementing. Paid maternity leave is 26 weeks where the woman has fewer than two surviving children, with not more than eight weeks before the expected delivery, and 12 weeks where she has two or more surviving children. Every establishment with 50 or more employees is to provide a creche, with four visits a day allowed to the woman. The Code on Social Security, 2020, as MoLE has described it, contains corresponding provisions on 26 weeks, the creche at 50 employees, and night shifts with adequate safety measures.

Those obligations attach to covered establishments. They do not attach to the 70.7 percent of rural women workers who are self-employed. A capex plan that counts “women’s participation” as a social outcome, and then models cost only for the creche threshold, is modelling the formal establishment. The participation rate of 40.0 percent includes the self-employed majority.

EPFO net addition of women subscribers is the payroll trace of that formal slice. It is discussed in the market-signal brief. It moves when covered establishments enrol women. It does not move when a household enterprise adds an unpaid helper, even though usual status counts that helper as employed.

## What to watch on the next print

The next useful labour print is the regular-wage share for women, rural and urban separately, in the following annual or quarterly table, set beside the all-worker manufacturing and services shares. If the all-worker secondary share rises and the female regular-wage share does not, the investment cycle and women’s job type are diverging. If both rise, the cycle is reaching the status in which maternity and creche rules actually apply. Either reading requires the sex-wise cell. The all-worker cell is context.
