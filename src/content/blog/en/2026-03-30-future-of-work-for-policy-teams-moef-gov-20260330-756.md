# What policy teams can defend from the current work statistics

Policy teams write sentences that other people repeat. The test for a sentence about work in India is whether the source, the concept, and the date can travel with it. The sentences below can. The sentences after them cannot, even when every word in them is familiar.

## Sentences that survive a citation check

Participation has risen, and women account for most of the rise. In the Periodic Labour Force Survey, usual status, persons aged 15 and above, labour-force participation was 60.1 percent in July 2023–June 2024, against 57.9 percent a year earlier and, on the longer comparison in the same press note, against a women's rate of 23.3 percent in 2017–18. Women's participation in 2023–24 was 41.7 percent. Men's was 78.8 percent, up from 75.8 percent in 2017–18. Rural participation rose from 50.7 percent to 63.7 percent over those six years. Urban participation rose from 47.6 percent to 52.0 percent. A note that says "the labour force expanded, especially for rural women, on the usual-status measure" is inside the evidence.

Usual-status unemployment in that year was 3.2 percent. It was 6.0 percent in 2017–18 on the Directorate General of Employment's summary of the same survey. Rural usual-status unemployment in 2023–24 was 2.5 percent. Urban was 5.1 percent. Women's usual-status unemployment rose from 2.9 percent to 3.2 percent between 2022–23 and 2023–24 while their participation jumped. A note can say unemployment on this measure is lower than at the start of the PLFS, and that the latest year's small rise for women came alongside a large rise in participation. It should not say "unemployment is solved."

Current weekly status tells a harder urban story, and a note can say that too if it names the concept. In calendar 2024, all-India weekly-status unemployment was 4.9 percent and urban weekly-status unemployment was 6.7 percent. Urban women's weekly participation was 25.8 percent. In July–September 2024, urban weekly participation was 50.4 percent and women's was 25.5 percent. A city employment programme justified with the 41.7 percent women's figure is using a national usual-status rate for a problem the weekly urban rate still describes differently.

The unorganised register is large, female, and young. As on 20 August 2026, e-Shram had 31.89 crore registrations, 54.28 percent women, with 55.21 percent of registrants aged 18 to 40. As on 14 July 2026 the count was 31,78,31,749. The portal was launched on 26 August 2021. Registration is self-declaration and produces a Universal Account Number. A note can say the state has a way to see unorganised workers, including gig, platform, and migrant workers, at that scale. It cannot say each registration is a verified job.

Scheme linkage is real and must be described as linkage. The Lok Sabha reply of 20 July 2026 said fifteen central schemes had been integrated or mapped, and it reported enrolments among registrants, including 24.46 crore under One Nation One Ration Card and 15.10 crore under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY. A note can say a large share of the register is already inside those schemes. It must say the counts overlap and that the schemes are older than the portal. The "One-Stop-Solution" of 21 October 2024 and the state microsites of 29 January 2025 are the operational tools. They are the right things to fund and to audit. They are not new entitlements created by the registration count.

The codes are in force and the rulebook was still the open item in early 2026. The Ministry made the four codes effective on 21 November 2025, rationalising 29 central laws. The Economic Survey 2025–26 said rules were expected over the following months, and its paragraph 1.40 treats recognition of gig and platform workers as a step toward including them in schemes. Section 114(5) of the Code on Social Security still requires a notified date before aggregator contributions commence. A policy note can promise a scheme. It should not report the 1–2 percent contribution as money already flowing.

## Sentences that do not survive

"Unemployment is 3 percent, so the weekly urban rate is a myth." The 3.2 percent figure is usual status. The urban weekly rate in calendar 2024 was 6.7 percent. Both are published by the statistics ministry.

"Social security now covers 64 percent, so gig workers are covered." On 21 November 2025 the Ministry said coverage had risen from about 19 percent in 2015 to more than 64 percent in 2025. That is the Ministry's coverage statement. Gig schemes under section 114 still need to be framed and, for the aggregator share, started on a notified date. Using the 64 percent line as proof of gig benefits spends a broad claim on a narrow programme that the statute has not switched on.

"There are 23.5 million gig workers." NITI Aayog estimated 7.7 million in 2020–21 and extended the model to 23.5 million by 2029–30. The second number is a scenario. The first is an estimate from worker characteristics, not a register.

"e-Shram added 11 lakh jobs between July and August 2026." The register moved from 31.78 crore on 14 July to a rounded 31.89 crore on 20 August. That difference is registrations, and the August figure is rounded. The household survey is the jobs instrument.

"Every state now has the same gig-worker law." Rajasthan's 2023 Act commences on a date the state notifies. Karnataka's 2025 Act is deemed in force from 30 May 2025 and sets a fee on payout that counts toward the central contribution. Bihar's Act 8 of 2025 also starts by notification. Telangana's Act 21 of 2026 was brought into force on 2 June 2026. States without their own Act sit under the central codes and e-Shram. A model rule that ignores that split will mis-instruct the states that ask for one.

## What a policy team should publish alongside any new claim

Publish the concept (usual or weekly), the geography (all-India, rural, urban, or a named state), the age, and the date of the extract. Publish whether a gig figure is NITI's 2022 estimate or an administrative count. Publish whether a social-security figure is the Ministry's coverage statement, an e-Shram mapping, or a contribution that has a commencement notification. The Economic Survey's judgment that labour-input growth can settle above the pre-pandemic KLEMS pace of about 2.3 percent a year is usable as the government's medium-term view. It is not a monitoring indicator. The monitoring indicators are the next PLFS note, the revised EPFO payroll, the e-Shram total, and the Gazette entries under the codes. A team that reports those four, with their labels, is doing the job the current statistics support.
