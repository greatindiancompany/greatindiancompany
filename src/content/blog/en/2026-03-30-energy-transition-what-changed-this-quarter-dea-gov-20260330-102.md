# What Changed in India's Energy Transition

The useful question for this season is narrower than a slogan. What did the Government of India actually publish between the close of FY 2025-26 and the latest monthly sheet, and which of those numbers are stocks, which are additions, and which are new schemes?

This note uses the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) physical-progress sheet dated 31 July 2026, the Press Information Bureau (PIB) note on crossing 300 GW of non-fossil capacity, and the PIB energy factsheet of 13 August 2026. MNRE's advance-release calendar says the physical-achievement sheet is due by the 15th of the following month. When this page was written, the public cumulative sheet still stopped at 31 July 2026.

## The addition, not the stock

From 1 April 2026 to 31 July 2026, MNRE reports 17,037.54 MW of renewable capacity added, including large hydro. Almost all of it is solar and wind:

- Solar: 14,334.05 MW
- Wind: 2,042.05 MW
- Large hydro: 650 MW
- Small hydro: 10.40 MW
- Off-grid waste-to-energy: 1.04 MW

July alone added 2,442.75 MW of solar and 693.50 MW of wind. Nuclear capacity did not move in that window. It stayed at 8,780 MW, with a separate 100 MW kept out of the total since 31 May 2025 because of a long outage.

Read that beside last year, not beside the 2030 target. PIB's 8 April 2026 briefing said FY 2025-26 added 55.29 GW of non-fossil capacity, the highest annual addition the Ministry had recorded, after 29.5 GW in FY 2024-25. Four months of FY 2026-27 have delivered about 17 GW of renewable addition. That is a pace, not a verdict on the year.

## The milestone is a capacity share

As on 31 July 2026, MNRE's cumulative non-fossil total is 300,505.64 MW, which PIB rounds to 300.50 GW:

- Solar 164.59 GW
- Wind 58.14 GW
- Hydro, large and small, 57.24 GW
- Bio-power 11.75 GW on the PIB breakdown
- Nuclear 8.78 GW

PIB puts this at over 54 percent of total installed electricity capacity, which it describes as around 552 GW, and at over 60 percent of the 500 GW non-fossil planning mark for 2030. Large hydro in the MNRE sheet is 52,064.67 MW and includes 7,425.6 MW of pumped storage.

Solar inside that 164.59 GW is not one product. MNRE splits it into 122.57 GW ground-mounted, 30.74 GW grid-connected rooftop, 4.77 GW of solar inside hybrid projects, and 6.51 GW off-grid.

## What was decided, as distinct from what was built

Three published decisions sit on top of that capacity sheet. They are approvals, not commissioned megawatts.

On 31 July 2026 the Government approved the Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana, with an outlay of ₹5,070 crore, for 5,000 MW of floating solar with co-located storage of at least two hours, or 10,000 MWh. Projects are to be sanctioned from FY 2026-27 through FY 2030-31. The PIB factsheet of 13 August 2026 is the public summary.

On 6 August 2026 the Government approved the GOBARdhan national circular bioenergy scheme, with an outlay of ₹23,731 crore running from FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36. The same factsheet says 1,929 compressed biogas or bio-CNG plants were registered as of 13 August 2026, of which 217 were commissioned and 357 were under construction.

In July 2026 the National Green Hydrogen Mission launched 12 pilot projects covering 70 hydrogen-powered vehicles on 21 routes, and India's first indigenous hydrogen fuel-cell train began running on the Jind–Sonipat section. Commissioned green hydrogen production was still around 8,000 tonnes a year as of February 2026, against the Mission's 2030 aim of at least 5 million tonnes a year. The pilots are real. They are not the production target.

## What did not flip

FY 2025-26 generation, reported in the 8 April 2026 PIB briefing, was 1,845.921 billion units, of which non-fossil sources were 538.97 billion units, or 29.2 percent. A capacity share above 54 percent and a generation share of 29.2 percent can both be true. Solar and wind add a lot of nameplate capacity per unit of annual energy. Coal remains the bulk of electrical energy. The August 2026 PIB factsheet says coal supports over 70 percent of electricity generation and nearly 55 percent of primary energy.

PM Surya Ghar moved, and it is worth separating from the utility-solar boom. The same factsheet says that as of 12 August 2026, 51.58 lakh households were benefiting, about ₹28,024 crore of subsidy had been transferred, nearly 19 lakh households were receiving zero electricity bills in the Ministry's account, and 14.8 GW had been commissioned under the scheme. That is household rooftop progress. It is a different series from the 30.74 GW of all grid-connected rooftop solar on the MNRE sheet.

## How to use the next sheet

When MNRE posts the August and September tables, compare three lines with the July base: solar added, wind added, and the non-fossil total. Then check whether any new scheme in the factsheet has a commissioned-megawatt line of its own. Floating solar, biogas plants, and hydrogen pilots will show up in those technology rows only after they are built.
