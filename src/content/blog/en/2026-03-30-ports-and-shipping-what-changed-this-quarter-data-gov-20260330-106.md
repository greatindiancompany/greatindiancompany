# Ports and shipping: what is already in force by September 2026

September 2026 is an execution quarter for Indian ports and shipyards, not a quarter that introduced a new ports statute. The decisions a founder, lender, or policy desk needs are already on the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways site and in two Press Information Bureau notes. Cargo tonnes for the latest months are in Indian Ports Association files. This brief does not copy those tonnes.

## What is already law at the major ports

The Major Port Authorities Act, 2021 has been in force since 3 November 2021. It repealed the Major Port Trusts Act, 1963. On 13 August 2025 the Centre constituted the Adjudicatory Board for Major Ports. From that date the Tariff Authority for Major Ports ceased to exist. Major ports now set tariffs for their own services and for access to port assets, including land. Disputes with public-private concessionaires, stressed concession reviews, and port-user complaints go to the board, whose office is in Mumbai.

The ministry's Ports Wing says India has 14 major ports, of which 12 are operational, plus about 200 non-major ports under state maritime boards or state governments. About 65 of those non-major ports handle cargo. The 12 operating names on that page match the member list at the Indian Ports Association: Deendayal, Mumbai, Jawaharlal Nehru, Mormugao, New Mangalore, Cochin, V.O. Chidambaranar, Chennai, Kamarajar, Visakhapatnam, Paradip, and Syama Prasad Mookerjee. The Ports Wing page does not name the two major ports that are not yet operating.

## What the shipyard file shows

A Press Information Bureau note of 27 December 2025 records that the ministry notified guidelines for the Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Scheme and the Shipbuilding Development Scheme. A later note, a written Lok Sabha reply posted on 24 July 2026, gives the dates that matter now. Guidelines for the financial assistance scheme were issued on 26 December 2025. Guidelines for the Maritime Development Fund were issued on 20 February 2026. SBI Ventures Limited was appointed fund manager for the Maritime Investment Fund on 26 May 2026.

That July reply also says the government has given in-principle approval for three greenfield shipbuilding clusters, in Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, and Tamil Nadu, and in-principle approval for brownfield shipbuilding and ship-repair proposals at four shipyards. It states a contract value of ₹15,234 crore for in-principle assistance approvals over the previous two years. Those are approval figures in a ministerial reply, not a census of ships delivered.

## What actually updates month to month

The Indian Ports Association, set up in 1966 as the apex body of the major ports, posts operational reports from New Delhi. Its public list includes a June 2026 major-port traffic note dated 6 July 2026 and an April-to-July 2026 traffic note dated 6 August 2026, alongside daily vessel profiles. Those files are the quarterly cargo record. Restating them here would freeze a number that the association revises on its own site.

A reader comparing "this quarter" with the previous one should open those IPA notes and separate containers, coal, petroleum, and coastal cargo. The ministry's performance language for the same ports is different: average turnaround time, output per ship berth day, pre-berthing detention, and dwell time, under the Sagar Aankalan ranking guidelines. A tonne total and a turnaround time answer different questions.

## What did not change

The shipping division's fleet paragraph is still dated 30 April 2019. It is not a September 2026 fleet. The Sagarmala page still describes capacity markers against a 2025 horizon. Treat both as pages that have not been refreshed to this quarter, and use the Ports Wing, the July 2026 reply, and the IPA traffic list for anything time-stamped.
