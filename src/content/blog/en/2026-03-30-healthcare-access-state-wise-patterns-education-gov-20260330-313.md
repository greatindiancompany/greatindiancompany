## One scheme, a state operating system

Public health and hospitals are a state subject. The health ministry says so when it describes the Pradhan Mantri Ayushman Bharat Health Infrastructure Mission: the Centre can approve and fund, and the state implements. PM-JAY is built the same way. The National Health Authority sets a national design. The State Health Agency runs it. A national average of “healthcare access” that ignores the agency will mis-state what a patient in a given district can actually use.

The Authority’s about-page gives states a published list of flexibilities. They choose trust, insurance, or mixed implementation. They may use their own beneficiary database if they cover at least the SECC 2011 families. They may co-brand an existing state scheme. They may add families, with the state paying the full cost of the addition. They may raise the cover above ₹5 lakh, again at state cost. They may add packages and revise prices within a limit. They may change which packages are reserved for public hospitals. They may keep their own information system if it shares data with the Authority in the specified format. They may deduct a percentage of claims paid to public hospitals.

Each of those choices produces a different state pattern. Two states can both be “in PM-JAY” and offer different package lists, different hospitals, and a different payer.

## Trust, insurance, and mixed mode

In trust mode the state agency pays hospitals and holds the risk. An implementation support agency may process claims. The Authority’s page calls this the most common model. In insurance mode the agency selects an insurer by tender, pays a premium per eligible family, and the insurer pays providers and holds the risk. Administrative-cost ceilings and surplus-refund rules are written into that description so the insurer’s margin is capped. Mixed mode uses both, typically where a state scheme already covered more people than the SECC list.

A 2022–23 Authority annual report listed modes state by state at that time. This page does not repeat that list as the map for September 2026. Mode is a contract. It can be re-tendered. The pattern to carry is the mechanism: find the current mode for the state you care about, then read who remits the hospital and who bears a claim spike.

Sharing of the central contribution follows the finance-ministry pattern cited on the about-page: 60:40 for states other than the North-East and the three Himalayan states named there, and for union territories with a legislature; 90:10 for North-Eastern states and for Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand as the page lists them; up to 100 percent for union territories without a legislature, case by case. In insurance mode the Centre’s share is tied to a ceiling premium. In trust mode it is tied to actual claim cost or the ceiling, whichever is lower. A poor state and a richer state are not waiting on the same fiscal math.

## What still has to be common

The floor is national. Every SECC-eligible family the scheme recognizes is supposed to be covered, including where a state uses a larger local list. The ₹5 lakh family floater, cashless care, day-one cover for pre-existing conditions, and portability are national design rules. Portability is the cross-border pattern: a person from one state can be treated at an empanelled hospital in another. The about-page treats the information system as what makes that possible. The payment still has to clear between agencies. A portable entitlement with a slow interstate settlement is a state-pattern problem, not a failure of the slogan.

Primary care varies for a second reason. Ayushman Arogya Mandirs are reported by states onto a ministry portal. The ministry reply of 25 March 2025 gave a national total. It did not, in that reply, make every district equivalent. Staffing can be modified by the state, the mandir about-page says, against Indian Public Health Standards at the primary health centre. A national count of centres can sit on top of very different teams.

## How to read a state without a stale ranking

Pick the state. Record the implementation mode, the beneficiary database, any top-up above ₹5 lakh, the reserved public-only packages, and whether public-hospital claims are haircut. Then look at one district’s mandirs, community health centre, district hospital, and empanelled private hospitals. That district sheet will teach more than a league table of states from a year the Authority has since superseded.
