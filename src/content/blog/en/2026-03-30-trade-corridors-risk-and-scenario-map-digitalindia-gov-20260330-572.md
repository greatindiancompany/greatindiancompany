## Name the risk the document already names

A scenario map for India's trade corridors should use risks that ministries have already stated. Invented probabilities add nothing. Four clusters are on the record.

## Sanction and capital intensity

On 19 March 2025 the Ministry of Railways said three further dedicated freight corridors were at detailed-project-report stage and that none had been sanctioned. It said the projects are highly capital intensive, and that a decision depends on technical feasibility, financial and economic viability, traffic demand, and funds. That is an explicit go/no-go risk. The alignments were Kharagpur–Vijayawada, Palghar–Bhusawal–Nagpur–Kharagpur–Dankuni plus Rajkharsawan–Kalipahari–Andal, and Vijayawada–Nagpur–Itarsi.

The Union Budget 2026 then proposed Dankuni–Surat. By 7 August 2026 the report for that corridor was being prepared and updated. A scenario in which Dankuni–Surat is sanctioned, and a scenario in which it stays in report work, are both still open on that text. A scenario that treats the March 2025 trio as approved because a different east-west line was announced is not supported.

The operating corridors carry a different risk. The 1 February 2026 briefing said the Eastern and Western corridors were near saturation at around 400 freight trains a day, which is why a further corridor was being proposed. On 7 August 2026 the count given to the Rajya Sabha was about 443 trains a day, with both corridors commissioned. Saturation risk on the lines that exist is the minister's reason for the next line. It is also the reason a shipper should ask about paths and terminals now, rather than assume spare dedicated capacity is unlimited.

## Cost, measurement, and small operators

DPIIT's September 2025 note says challenges remain in infrastructure gaps, regulatory harmonisation, and digital literacy among smaller logistics operators, and that coordination across states and ministries is still required for multimodal integration. That is the government's own risk list for the services layer. The logistics-cost assessment with NCAER was in its final stages. A plan that needs a single official cost percentage is exposed to the study's publication date. The 3 January 2025 release unveiled a measurement framework. It did not close the number.

E-Logs had resolved 100 of 140 issues by that September note, so 40 submitted issues were still open on the published count. That is a small, concrete backlog, not a system-wide failure rate.

## Port hinterland and park delivery

Bharatmala's port and coastal road category, as on 31 October 2024, had 189 km constructed out of 424 km awarded across 18 projects. The hinterland risk is the unbuilt remainder of a sanctioned category, plus whatever last-mile link a given port still lacks. Sagarmala had identified about 839 projects and completed 272 as of 19 March 2025. Delivery risk on the port side is the gap between the identified book and the completed book.

Multimodal parks add a governance risk DPIIT stated in September 2025: implementation still has to be harmonised across agencies. The only site-level official snapshot in this set is from 15 February 2022, when Jogighopa was under construction and three other parks were at bid stage. A 2026 investment case for a named park needs a later project release. Using the 2022 bid status as current status is its own risk.

## Cross-border routes

The India-Middle East-Europe memorandum of September 2023 commits eight participants to work toward a ship-to-rail corridor with energy and digital links. Completion, timing, and border arrangements are not in that text. The commerce minister's April 2025 figures — up to 30 percent lower logistics cost and 40 percent less transportation time — are expected effects. A scenario that books those savings into a 2026 freight contract is ahead of an operating service.

The International North-South Transport Corridor has an older, specific risk list. The 20 March 2015 Lok Sabha reply on the 2014 dry run named irregular sailings from India to Iran, insurance gaps on bills of lading, and the need for non-vessel-owning common carriers, and said remedies required other signatories because the agreement is multilateral. Treat that list as the class of risk a multilateral corridor carries, and as a 2015 document. It is not a 2026 audit.
