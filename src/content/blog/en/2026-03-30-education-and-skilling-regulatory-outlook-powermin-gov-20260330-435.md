## The bodies named in the August 2026 reply

A written Lok Sabha reply on 10 August 2026, from the Minister of State for Education, is the clearest current map of who has actually issued rules on credit and vocational equivalence.

The National Education Policy 2020 remains the policy frame. It encourages skill education and treats the National Skills Qualifications Framework as the basis for recognition of prior learning. It also describes a future Higher Education Commission of India, with a General Education Council that would write a National Higher Education Qualification Framework in sync with the skills framework. The 10 August reply does not say that commission has replaced the University Grants Commission or the All India Council for Technical Education. It attributes live instruments to those two bodies, and to the National Council for Vocational Education and Training.

## Three instruments, three jobs

The University Grants Commission has notified a standard operating procedure for operationalising the National Credit Framework in higher education. That procedure allows up to 50% of a programme’s credits to be earned from skill-based courses. The credit framework itself, which the government has approved, covers elementary, school, higher, and skill education. It provides for credits to be assigned, stored, transferred, and redeemed, subject to assessment, and for equivalence among skill, general, and experiential learning.

The All India Council for Technical Education’s Approval Process Handbook for 2023-27 sets entry norms in line with the credit framework. Students who have passed Class 10 and completed a two-year Industrial Training Institute programme can be eligible for second-year admission to diploma courses in engineering and technology. Candidates with a two-year undergraduate diploma in engineering at NCrF level 5.0 can enter the third year laterally.

NCVET has issued guidelines for recognition of prior learning. Industrial bodies can assess their workforces. Credits follow assessment of skills, competencies, and knowledge. Separately, the 12 August 2026 Rajya Sabha reply says NCVET requires industry validation of job roles and qualifications, and their periodic review. Assessment in PMKVY 4.0 waits until any prescribed on-the-job training is complete.

## Adoption is wide and still partial

AISHE 2023-24, as cited on 10 August 2026, reports that more than 700 universities and over 7,500 affiliated colleges have adopted the credit framework. That is a large count. It is not a statement that every institution has adopted it. Regulatory outlook for a campus still starts with whether that institution is inside the count, and with the UGC, AICTE, or NCVET instrument that applies to the programme.
