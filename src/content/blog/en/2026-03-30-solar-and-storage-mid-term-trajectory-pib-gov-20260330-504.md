# The Storage Path Written Into India's Electricity Plan

India's mid-term solar story is incomplete without the storage figures the government itself uses. Those figures are a plan. They are dated. They split batteries from pumped storage. Treating them as capacity already built will mislead a board.

## What MNRE quotes from CEA

MNRE's energy storage overview was last updated on 21 November 2023. It states two national goals in the same opening: 50 percent of cumulative installed electricity capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030, and a 45 percent cut in the emission intensity of GDP by 2030 from 2005 levels. It then says variable renewable power needs storage if the grid is to stay stable, shift energy into peak hours, and defer some network investment.

The page attributes the quantities to the Central Electricity Authority's National Electricity Plan of 2023.

- In 2026-27, storage requirement of 82.37 GWh, of which 47.65 GWh is pumped storage and 34.72 GWh is battery storage.
- In 2031-32, 411.4 GWh, of which 175.18 GWh is pumped storage and 236.22 GWh is batteries.
- By 2047, 2,380 GWh, of which 540 GWh is pumped storage and 1,840 GWh is batteries, linked on the page to larger renewable additions and the net-zero aim for 2070.

The mix changes along that path. In the 2026-27 figure, pumped storage is the larger share. In the 2031-32 figure, batteries are the larger share. By 2047, on this page, batteries dominate. A strategy that assumes batteries are already the bulk of near-term storage is not what the 2026-27 line says.

PIB release 2118325, dated 3 April 2025, repeats one of those plan numbers in rounder form: the National Electricity Plan 2023 estimates that 236 GWh of battery storage would be required by 2031-32. That matches the 236.22 GWh on the MNRE page, rounded. The April 2025 reply did not replace the November 2023 page with a new plan.

## A funded scheme is not the plan

The same April 2025 reply describes viability-gap funding enlarged from 4,000 MWh to 13,200 MWh, still inside Rs 3,760 crore. Put next to 236.22 GWh, that scheme is a slice of the 2031-32 battery figure, not the figure itself. And 13,200 MWh is the scheme capacity for support, not a commissioning report. The reply says no project under the scheme achieved financial closure in 2024-25.

The obligation trajectory on the MNRE page is the demand-side rule that is supposed to pull storage into utility portfolios. The Ministry of Power's energy storage obligation rises from 1 percent in FY 2023-24 to 4 percent in FY 2029-30, by half a point a year. It is met only when at least 85 percent of the energy stored in the year is renewable. That condition ties storage to solar and wind rather than to charging from any source.

## Where solar manufacturing sits on the same timeline

Module supply is a parallel track, not a storage number. MNRE's PLI page sets Rs 24,000 crore for high-efficiency module manufacturing, with awards of 8,737 MW in tranche I and 39,600 MW in tranche II. Those are factory awards. They do not add gigawatt-hours of storage. They matter to the trajectory because projects built toward the 2030 non-fossil capacity goal will buy modules, and ALMM decides which modules government-linked projects may use.

## How to brief a board

Say three things in this order. The storage quantities are CEA's 2023 plan as MNRE restated them on a page last updated in November 2023. Pumped storage is the larger slice in the 2026-27 requirement, and batteries are the larger slice in 2031-32. The viability-gap scheme of 13,200 MWh is support inside a fixed rupee allocation, and as of April 2025 it had not passed financial closure.
