India did not pass one logistics law and call it a system. It split the job. PM Gati Shakti, launched in October 2021, is the map and the planning table for infrastructure. The National Logistics Policy, launched on 17 September 2022, is the attempt to make services, rules, skills, and data move at the speed of that map.

The [Prime Minister’s Office note of 21 September 2022](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/policy-introduces-unified-logistics-interface-platform-standardization-monitoring-framework-and-skill-development-for-greater-efficiency-in-logistics-services/) says this in one paragraph. Gati Shakti is for integrated infrastructure. The logistics policy is for efficiency in logistics services and in people: processes, the regulatory frame, skill development, logistics in higher education, and technology. If a founder treats them as synonyms, the org chart will be wrong. A highway concession and a warehouse standard do not come out of the same file.

## What the 2022 policy said it would hit

The same cabinet note sets three targets. Bring India’s logistics cost to a level comparable with global benchmarks by 2030. Place India among the top 25 countries in the World Bank Logistics Performance Index by 2030. Build a data-driven way to decide. The vision line is a technologically enabled, integrated, cost-efficient, resilient, sustainable, and trusted logistics ecosystem.

The launch speech on [17 September 2022](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pm-launches-national-logistics-policy/) is blunter about the cost aim: move from a 13–14 percent framing to single digits. That sentence is a political target from the day of the launch. It is not a statistical release. The tools named in the speech are the ones operators already touch: e-way bills, FASTag, paperless export-import filing through e-Sanchit, faceless customs assessment, and GST as the tax system that removed the old border posts. Sagarmala, Bharatmala, and dedicated freight corridors are named as the infrastructure already under way, not as children of the 2022 policy.

Two platforms were switched on with the policy. The Unified Logistics Interface Platform was meant to put transport-sector digital services on one portal. E-Logs was meant to let industry associations take operational problems to government agencies, with a system for resolving them. An e-handbook on warehousing and training courses on Gati Shakti and logistics on the iGOT platform were launched the same day. The institutional monitor is the Empowered Group of Secretaries already created for Gati Shakti. The cabinet note said that group would set up a Services Improvement Group patterned on the Network Planning Group. The planning group watches the network. The services group was meant to watch process, regulation, and digital gaps the planners do not cover.

## What four years of replies added

By 11 August 2026 the government was willing to say, in a [written Lok Sabha reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297818), that Gati Shakti still has no quantified targets and no expenditure requirement of its own. Ministries and states approve and fund projects under their own schemes. The master plan had onboarded 58 central ministries and departments and all 36 states and Union Territories. A public query platform, named in that reply as ugi.pmgatishakti.gov.in, had been opened in September 2025. About 1,800 data layers were integrated.

That is the policy shift in practice. The 2021 plan did not become a logistics budget. The 2022 policy did not replace the Motor Vehicles framework, the Major Port Authorities, or the GST movement rules. It tried to sit across them: one planning table, one service-improvement table, a data pipe, and a skills list.

Road corridors remain a roads-ministry programme. [Bharatmala](https://morth.nic.in/en/bharatmala) is the place that programme lives, not a chapter of the logistics policy PDF.
