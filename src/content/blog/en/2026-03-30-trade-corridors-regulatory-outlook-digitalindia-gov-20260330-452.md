## Regulation is a set of gates, not one corridor act

No single statute called a trade-corridor law decides whether cargo moves. A shipper meets railway terminal rules, road and park permissions, port and coastal-shipping orders, and a commerce-ministry process for cross-border corridors. The outlook is which gate has a published test.

## The sanction test for a new dedicated freight corridor

On 19 March 2025 the Ministry of Railways told the Lok Sabha that detailed project reports for an East Coast corridor (Kharagpur–Vijayawada), an East-West corridor (Palghar–Bhusawal–Nagpur–Kharagpur–Dankuni, and Rajkharsawan–Kalipahari–Andal), and a North-South sub-corridor (Vijayawada–Nagpur–Itarsi) were under examination, and that none of the three had been sanctioned. The stated tests were technical feasibility, financial and economic viability, traffic demand, and availability of funds and financing options. That sentence is the regulatory standard for a new dedicated freight corridor until a later reply replaces it.

The Union Budget 2026 announced a Dankuni–Surat corridor. On 7 August 2026 Railways said preparation and updation of its detailed project report had been taken up. An announcement plus report work is a stage. Sanction is a later stage, judged on the tests above. The Eastern and Western corridors are past that gate: the same August reply says both have been commissioned.

## Logistics rules under DPIIT

The National Logistics Policy's regulatory work sits with DPIIT and the states. A Service Improvement Group handles policy and regulatory issues. The E-Logs portal, by 16 September 2025, had more than 35 logistics and industry associations on it and had resolved 100 of 140 issues submitted. That is a grievance channel with a published closure count, not a promise that every permit is now simple.

State industry status is the other lever. Nineteen states had granted industry status to logistics by that date, which the note links to tax benefits and incentives. Nine state policies were still in draft. City logistics plan guidelines have been issued and are being written into state action plans. An operator's regulatory map is therefore state-specific: industry status, a notified state policy, and whether the city plan is in force.

Commodity plans are also rules of a kind. The coal logistics policy has been notified. The cement sectoral plan has been finalised. Drafts for steel, fertiliser, and food processing were still underway in September 2025. A corridor that carries those goods will meet the sector plan as well as the railway timetable.

## Coastal and port incentives already described

A Press Information Bureau note on coastal shipping records measures in force around the port system: eased licensing under the Merchant Shipping Act, a 40 percent discount on vessel and cargo charges at major ports, priority berthing, a cut in GST on bunker fuels from 18 percent to 5 percent, and green channel clearance for faster cargo evacuation. Fifteen Sagarmala projects worth ₹849 crore had been completed for coastal berths and Ro-Ro and Ro-Pax jetties. The Budget speech summary of 1 February 2026 adds a proposed Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme to shift cargo toward water, with a stated aim of moving the inland-waterway and coastal share from 6 percent to 12 percent by 2047. The scheme is a proposal in that summary. The charge discount, the bunker GST cut, and priority berthing are described as steps already taken.

## Cross-border corridors stay diplomatic as well as commercial

The India-Middle East-Europe memorandum of September 2023 is an intergovernmental commitment to arrange a future ship-to-rail corridor, including electricity, digital links, and a clean-hydrogen pipe. It is not an Indian licence. The International North-South Transport Corridor is older and multilateral. A 20 March 2015 Lok Sabha reply said a 2014 dry run had found irregular sailings from India to Iran, gaps in insurance for bills of lading, and a need for non-vessel-owning common carriers, and that fixes had to be discussed with other parties because the agreement is multilateral. That list is a decade old. It shows the class of regulatory friction — shipping services, insurance, documentation — that an international corridor still has to clear with counterpart states.
