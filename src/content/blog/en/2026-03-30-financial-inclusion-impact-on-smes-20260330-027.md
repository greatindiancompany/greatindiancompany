A founder pricing a market of “included” small firms will mis-size it by starting from Jan Dhan. Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana, on the [scheme page](https://www.pmjdy.gov.in/scheme) maintained by the Department of Financial Services, opens one basic savings bank deposit account for an unbanked person, at a branch or a Bank Mitra outlet. The account has no minimum-balance requirement. The person earns interest, can receive a RuPay debit card, and may be offered an overdraft of up to ₹10,000 if eligible. The same page says the account is eligible for MUDRA, along with direct benefit transfer and the social-security schemes. Eligibility for a scheme is a door. It is not a disbursement.

## What the strategy now asks of micro enterprises

RBI’s [National Strategy for Financial Inclusion 2025–30](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=61712), released on 1 December 2025, puts households and micro enterprises in the same first objective: an equitable, responsible, suitable, and affordable bouquet of services aimed at financial safety and security. The text then separates the work. It says new-to-credit micro enterprises, especially those onboarded through the Udyam Assist Platform, should receive focused support from financial service providers.

That is the operating distinction. The Jan Dhan stock — 57.93 crore beneficiaries as on 25 March 2026 in the country progress report — counts people holding a basic account. The Udyam Assist point in NSFI counts a different problem: a micro enterprise that has been identified and still needs credit. The FI-Index press notes do not split the Usage score into “household payments” and “firm borrowing,” so a rise in the index, including the move to 70.0 in March 2026, cannot be read as a rise in small-firm credit.

## What a household survey can and cannot say

The Global Findex 2025 is a survey of adults. Table B.1 reports that 15 percent of adults in India borrowed formally in 2024, against 89 percent who had an account. Across low- and middle-income economies the same report puts formal borrowing at 24 percent of adults. Those percentages describe people, not firms. An adult who borrows formally may be a shopkeeper, a wage earner, or neither. An enterprise loan booked to a current account, a MUDRA product, or a priority-sector facility will not appear as a Jan Dhan beneficiary.

For an operator, the useful map runs in the other direction. AePS and the business-correspondent outlet, described in the [policy brief](/blog/financial-inclusion-policy-explainer-20260330-057), are how a micro merchant in a village takes or cashes a small payment. They do not underwrite working capital. A pitch that treats account ownership as the addressable credit market is mixing the [household product](/blog/financial-inclusion-impact-on-households-20260330-037) with the firm’s balance sheet.

Great Indian Company keeps the small-firm page separate so an investor can see the account rail and the credit rail without pretending they are one stock.
