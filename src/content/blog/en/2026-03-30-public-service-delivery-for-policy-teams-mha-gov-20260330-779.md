# A department map for charters, portals, and cash transfers

A policy team designing one citizen service in India is choosing a set of owners, not a single platform. The useful map is which institution sets the standard, which one runs the case, which one holds the document, and which one moves the money.

## DARPG sets the standard and the score

The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances owns the quality frame. [Sevottam](https://www.darpg.gov.in/relatedlinks/sevottam) has three modules: a citizen charter, a grievance redress mechanism, and capability for delivery. The [charter page](https://darpg.gov.in/citizen-charter) still points back to the 24 May 1997 decision that charters include service standards, time limits, and a redress avenue. A new service that launches on an app without a published time limit is outside that decision, however modern the screen is.

DARPG also runs CPGRAMS as the common pipe. The portal [FAQ](https://pgportal.gov.in/Home/Faq) is explicit about the split: DARPG makes policy, monitors, and coordinates grievances that arise from central ministries; it forwards what it receives; the ministry or department redresses. A policy note that says "DARPG will resolve the certificate complaint" assigns the work to the wrong office. The director of public grievances in the line ministry is the officer the FAQ tells the citizen to approach.

NeSDA is DARPG's external score for the online channel. The [28 May 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266244) says the 2025 cycle, with a portal launched on 25 May 2026, proposes 59 mandatory services for every state and Union Territory and 43 for central ministries. Parameters include end service delivery and status tracking. Nodal officers submit data. A policy team should know whether its service is on that proposed list, and whether it is submitting a government portal or a service portal. The score does not replace the charter.

## MeitY and the state run the channel

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, through the National e-Governance Division, runs DigiLocker and UMANG. DigiLocker is the issuer wallet. UMANG is the mobile window, and the [6 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635) describes 2,575 services for individuals on it. The same reply says certificates, pensions, and welfare schemes are delivered through state portals and apps. e-District, funded by MeitY, is implemented by the state. A central policy team that wants a uniform certificate still has to name the state authority that signs it and the district body that operates the counter.

Common Service Centres are the assisted channel in that reply: over 800 services, 5.01 lakh centres functional as of May 2026. A service standard that assumes every resident has a smartphone and a quiet room ignores the channel MeitY's own reply still describes. BHASHINI is named in the same reply as the language platform. A service in one language is a coverage choice, and the reply treats linguistic inclusion as part of delivery.

## The Cabinet Secretariat moves the cash

If the service includes a cash component to a person, the [DBT Mission](https://dbtbharat.gov.in/static-page-content/spagecont?id=1) is the nodal point, in the Cabinet Secretariat since 14 September 2015. The electronic payment framework of February 2015 applies. The scheme ministry still owns eligibility. Banks and the Aadhaar Payments Bridge own the credit, and a wrong seed is the bank's liability to the paying department. The Mission page says Aadhaar is preferred and is not mandatory for DBT schemes as a class. A policy team that wants to require it for a Consolidated Fund benefit should read section 7 of the Aadhaar Act and the scheme guideline, and should publish that requirement in the charter. Leaving it as an unstated app error produces the grievances CPGRAMS then has to route.

## A one-page assignment

For a single service, write six names: the charter owner, the grievance office, the state or central workflow owner, the DigiLocker issuer if a document is the output, the UMANG integrator if mobile is a channel, and the DBT scheme owner if cash moves. DARPG can be the standard-setter and the assessor on that page. It should not be all six names. The August 2026 reply's reminder that Digital India is an umbrella, with MeitY running eight central sub-schemes and states running their own portals, is the budget version of the same assignment.
