# How India's manufacturing trade tools compare with a global diversification benchmark

A global buyer does not benchmark India with a single rank. The practical benchmark is whether a part can be made at scale, moved at a known cost, and landed in the buyer's market on a rule the buyer can document. India publishes tools for each of those tests. It does not publish one official "diversification score" that can be lined up against Vietnam or Mexico.

## Make: a named incentive map, not a general rank

The manufacturing test is the Production Linked Incentive list. The 2 August 2023 PIB note, from a Commerce and Industry reply in the Lok Sabha, names 14 sectors and an outlay of Rs 1.97 lakh crore. The design goal, in that note's words, is investment, technology, scale, and export competitiveness. Sectors run from mobiles and specified electronic components through auto components, specialty steel, solar modules, advanced chemistry cells, and drones.

Against a global benchmark, that list is narrow on purpose. A buyer of a part outside the 14 does not get to claim the incentive architecture. A buyer inside the list still has to ask which ministry is implementing the scheme and whether the anchor-and-vendor cascade described in the note — 176 MSMEs among 733 selected applications at that date — has reached their component. The outlay is the ceiling of the incentive design, not a cross-country capex ranking.

## Move: a cost level, and a target that is not a rank

The move test now has an official level. PIB's note of 27 November 2025 reports the DPIIT-NCAER study for 2023-24: 7.97 percent of GDP, 9.09 percent of non-services output, Rs 24.01 lakh crore. The note says figures of 13 to 14 percent of GDP, often used in global conversations, came from partial data and overstated the cost. For a benchmark conversation, cite 7.97 percent for that year and stop. Do not convert it into a league table this site has not calculated.

The National Logistics Policy sets a different kind of benchmark. A PIB note on the policy's progress says the aims include a lower logistics cost, a Logistics Performance Index ranking among the top 25 countries by 2030, and a data-driven logistics system. "Top 25 by 2030" is a target. These pages do not state India's current LPI rank, because that rank is not in the notes used here.

Two operational comparisons are in the 27 November 2025 note and are more useful than a rank. Smaller firms carry higher logistics costs than the national ratio. On hauls around 600 kilometres, the first and last 50 kilometres matter to the total. A coastal competitor can win on the line-haul and still lose if the Indian supplier's last mile is unstructured. The Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor example in that note — wagon turnaround cut from 15-16 days to 2-3 days, and transit from over 60 hours to about 35-38 hours — is a corridor fact, not a national average.

## Land: preferences are agreement-specific

The trade test is whether a preference exists. Commerce records the India-UAE CEPA as in force from 1 May 2022, with duty elimination on 97.4 percent of UAE tariff lines corresponding to 99 percent of imports from India. On 13 May 2026 the Commerce and Industry Minister said four of nine recent agreements were already operational and five were expected within 12 months, and that with Japan, Korea, and ASEAN the set covered more than two-thirds of global trade and economies. That coverage claim is the minister's. A benchmark deck should still name the agreement that applies to the shipment.

DGFT's Appendix 2A is the list of FTAs and PTAs. A deal that is signed and not in force does not beat a competitor's agreement that is already granting certificates of origin.

## How to brief a board

Put three lines on the page: product inside or outside the PLI list, logistics cost cited as the 2023-24 DPIIT-NCAER level, and the named agreement with its in-force date. Leave the composite country rank off the page until you have the source that publishes it. Great Indian Company uses that discipline across its India briefs.
