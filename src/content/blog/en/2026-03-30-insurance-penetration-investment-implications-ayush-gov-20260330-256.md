# Insurance FDI Is Now Capped at 100 Percent

Foreign capital in Indian insurance has moved in published steps. The Department of Financial Services records them on its [insurance overview](https://financialservices.gov.in/beta/en/page/insurance-overview). The Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015 raised the cap in an Indian insurance company from 26 percent to 49 percent, with Indian ownership and control. The Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2021, enacted on 25 March 2021, raised that cap from 49 percent to 74 percent. IRDAI followed with the IRDAI (Indian Insurance Companies) (Amendment) Regulations, 2021, notified on 7 July 2021.

Intermediaries took a different path. DFS says the Indian Insurance Companies (Foreign Investment) Amendment Rules, 2019 removed the foreign-equity cap for intermediaries, which opened that channel to 100 percent FDI.

The latest step is legislative. The [PIB note of 23 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) says the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025 raises the FDI limit in Indian insurance companies from 74 percent to 100 percent. PIB expects that change to attract long-term capital, support technology transfer, and support penetration. The same note says the Indian Insurance Companies (Foreign Investment) Amendment Rules, 2025, notified on 30 December 2025, rationalised conditions for insurers and intermediaries. Residual conditions live in those rules. This brief does not invent a repeal of any ownership test the rules still contain.

## Capital-structure changes that sit beside the cap

PIB lists four other changes in the 2025 Act that matter to anyone underwriting an insurance investment:

- One-time registration for insurance intermediaries, in place of repeated registration.
- The share-transfer threshold that needs IRDAI approval rises from 1 percent to 5 percent.
- The net owned fund requirement for foreign reinsurers falls from ₹5,000 crore to ₹1,000 crore.
- Insurance law is aligned with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

DFS separately records an earlier, narrower change: the Finance Act, 2019 reduced the net owned fund requirement from ₹5,000 crore to ₹1,000 crore for foreign insurers doing reinsurance through a branch in an International Financial Services Centre. That 2019 IFSC rule and the 2025 Act's reinsurer provision should be read as the instruments each page describes. They are not interchangeable citations.

Governance costs of capital also moved. PIB says IRDAI can order disgorgement of wrongful gains by insurers or intermediaries, and that the maximum penalty for non-compliance rises from ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore.

## What the balance sheet already is

FDI is a funding door. The stock of assets is already large. PIB puts insurance assets under management at ₹74.44 lakh crore on 31 March 2025, with life insurance accounting for 91 percent of that stock. Public-market capital is already in use at the largest life insurer: DFS says the government disinvested 3.5 percent of LIC on 17 May 2022.

Penetration remains the depth constraint on how much new capital can be deployed into premium. The [IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620) keeps the ratio at 3.7 percent of GDP. A higher statutory FDI cap does not, by itself, raise that ratio. It changes who may own the capital that tries to.
