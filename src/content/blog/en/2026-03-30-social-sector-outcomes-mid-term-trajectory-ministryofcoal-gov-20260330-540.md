# The Uneven Medium-Term Path of India’s Social Outcomes

India’s social outcomes over the last survey interval are not one curve. Height-for-age, insurance, and institutional delivery moved. Weight-for-height, underweight, and fertility did not, in any way a planner should call a transformation. School participation improved across four UDISE+ years and still leaves about half of students outside the secondary retention rate. The medium term is that split.

## Child nutrition: one clear gain, two flat lines, one reversal

NFHS-5 refers to 2019-21. NFHS-6 fieldwork is 2023-24, released on 29 May 2026. For children under five, stunting fell from 35.5% to 29.3%. The ministry’s headline calls this a 17% reduction, which is the relative change on the old base (about six percentage points), not a seventeen-point fall. Severe wasting fell from 7.7% to 5.2%. Wasting went from 19.3% to 19.0%. Underweight went from 32.1% to 31.8%.

Infant feeding over the same interval is also split. Initiation within an hour of birth rose from 41.8% to 50.1%. Solid or semi-solid food with breastmilk at 6-8 months rose from 45.9% to 59.5%. An adequate diet at 6-23 months rose from 11.0% to 15.3%. Exclusive breastfeeding under six months fell from 63.7% to 55.8%. Current breastfeeding under six months stayed high, 95.6% against 95.1%. A trajectory slide that shows only stunting will miss the feeding reversal and the weight indicators that did not follow height.

The programme wrapped around this period is Mission Poshan 2.0, which the scheme guidelines say was approved for the 15th Finance Commission years 2021-22 through 2025-26, folding Anganwadi services, Poshan Abhiyaan, and support for adolescent girls in aspirational districts and the North East. The survey window and the scheme window overlap. They are not the same thing: a household survey measures children, and a scheme approval measures permission to spend. The guidelines do not claim that the approval itself produced the 6.2-point stunting decline.

## Maternal care and financial protection moved further than fertility

Institutional births rose from 88.6% to 90.6%. Skilled attendance rose from 89.4% to 91.3%. Postnatal care for the newborn within two days rose from 79.1% to 85.3%. Four or more antenatal visits rose from 58.5% to 65.2%. Iron and folic acid for 180 days or more rose from 26.0% to 37.8%. Full vaccination on cards at 12-23 months rose from 83.8% to 87.1%. Household coverage by a health insurance or financing scheme rose from 41.0% to 60.2%.

The total fertility rate stayed at 2.0. Contraceptive prevalence rose from 66.7% to 69.1%. The path is more care around each birth, and a fertility level that had already reached replacement in the previous survey and then held. Caesarean births rose from 21.5% to 27.2%. That is a path in service mix, and it needs a quality reading the fact sheet does not supply.

Anaemia is absent from this path. The 29 May 2026 note does not give a new prevalence, and the fact-sheet nutrition lines used for the comparisons above do not either. Carrying NFHS-5 anaemia forward as if it were an NFHS-6 result breaks the series.

## Schooling: four years of access, a secondary rate that is still low

UDISE+ as released on 7 July 2026 gives a four-year facility and participation path, 2022-23 to 2025-26. Teachers rose from 94,83,294 to 1,02,73,020. Preparatory dropout went 8.7%, 3.7%, 2.3%, 1.8%. Secondary dropout went 13.8%, 10.9%, 8.2%, 7.0%. The middle series in the same table went 8.1%, 5.2%, 3.5%, 3.6%. Secondary retention went from 44.1% in 2022-23 to 47.2% in 2024-25 and 51.9% in 2025-26. Secondary gross enrolment in the latest year is 71.7%, from 68.5%.

The direction on dropout is real. The level on secondary retention means the medium-term problem has shifted from “children leave in Class 3” toward “children do not complete the secondary stage.” Computers in schools rose from 47.7% in 2022-23 to 69.9% in 2025-26. That is an infrastructure path. It is not a learning path. This release does not contain achievement scores.

## What the next official point has to beat

The next nutrition point has to be another survey or a clearly labelled administrative series, not a restatement of 29.3%. The next school point has to say whether secondary retention moves from 51.9% and whether the middle dropout tick from 3.5% to 3.6% reverses. The rural wage path has restarted on a new statute from 1 July 2026, so FY 2025-26 MGNREGS totals are the end of one series, not the base of the next, unless a ministry publication explicitly splices them.
