## Two instruments, one corridor problem

A trade corridor fails in two different places. Track, road, and port links can be missing. Or the track can exist while permits, data, warehouses, and trained staff keep the cargo slow. India split the response. PM GatiShakti is the infrastructure master plan. The National Logistics Policy is the services and institutional plan. The Prime Minister's Office stated that split when the Cabinet approved the logistics policy: GatiShakti is for integrated infrastructure, and the policy is for processes, the regulatory framework, skills, and technology.

GatiShakti was launched on 13 October 2021. The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved the National Master Plan on 21 October 2021. A Ministry of Road Transport and Highways note on 15 February 2022 described the digital platform as a way to bring 16 ministries, including railways and roads, into one planning view for industrial clusters and economic nodes. The logistics policy was launched on 17 September 2022 and is held by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade in the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.

## What the logistics policy actually added

The 16 September 2025 anniversary note from DPIIT lists the policy's objectives as reducing logistics costs toward global benchmarks, improving India's Logistics Performance Index rank to the top 25 by 2030, and building a data system for decisions. By that note, the Unified Logistics Interface Platform had connected more than 30 digital systems and passed 160 crore transactions as of August 2025. The Logistics Data Bank had tracked more than 75 million export-import containers across 101 inland container depots.

At launch, the Prime Minister's Office said 14 states had logistics policies and 13 more were in draft. By 16 September 2025, 27 states and Union Territories had formulated policies, 14 had logistics action plans, 19 had granted industry status to logistics, and nine policies were still in draft. That is the policy shift a state team can audit: from a central text to a state text with a legal status for the sector.

The same note records a Service Improvement Group for policy and regulatory issues, and an E-Logs portal with more than 35 logistics and industry associations, with 100 of 140 submitted issues resolved. Sector plans are uneven by commodity. The coal logistics policy and integrated coal logistics plan have been notified, the cement plan has been finalised, and steel, fertiliser, and food-processing plans were still in draft.

## Where the physical corridors sit in this split

Dedicated freight corridors, multimodal logistics parks, and port-connectivity roads are infrastructure. They are planned and built by Railways, the road ministry, port authorities, and state agencies, with GatiShakti as the coordination map. The logistics policy does not sanction a freight corridor. It tries to make the cargo that uses those corridors easier to book, see, and regulate.

DPIIT's September 2025 note says large multimodal logistics parks are being developed so that customs, cold storage, and packaging can sit in one place, and that planning across agencies is still being harmonised. The road ministry's February 2022 account is more concrete and older: 35 multimodal logistics parks identified under Bharatmala Pariyojana Phase I, with Jogighopa in Assam then under construction and bids invited for Nagpur, Chennai, and Bengaluru. Read the 2022 list as a site snapshot, and the 2025 sentence as a statement that the park programme is still being aligned. Neither document is a 2026 completion certificate for all 35 parks.

## What a policy reader should do with the split

Ask which layer a proposal sits in. A Dankuni–Surat freight corridor, announced in the Union Budget 2026, is a Railways project whose detailed project report is being prepared and updated. A state logistics policy, an industry-status notification, or an E-Logs grievance is a DPIIT and state instrument. Funding, clearance, and the minister who answers in Parliament are different.
