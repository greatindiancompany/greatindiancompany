# Who Is Buying Green Hydrogen and Ammonia in India

A market signal is a buyer with a quantity. India’s green hydrogen pages contain three such buyers, at very different stages, plus one commissioned plant that shows the difference between an award and a running unit.

## Fertiliser plants are taking ammonia

The mission page treats green ammonia and green methanol as the derivative products that designated consumers may be required to use. The concrete fertiliser record is later. The Press Information Bureau’s note of 12 November 2025, [Unlocking India’s Green Hydrogen Production Potential](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?lang=2&ModuleId=3&NoteId=155990), describes an auction for long-term green ammonia supply to fertiliser units, with aggregate procurement of 7.24 lakh metric tonnes a year, at ₹55.75 per kg.

On 17 June 2026, Union Minister Pralhad Joshi said agreements had been signed for the supply of 6.7 lakh metric tonnes a year of green ammonia to 11 fertiliser plants. The [workshop release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2274212) is the public text of that statement. The two figures are both official. They are not the same quantity, and this brief does not invent a reconciliation. What they share is the customer: fertiliser units replacing fossil feedstock with green ammonia. A developer who can produce hydrogen and cannot produce ammonia is not yet in this contract.

## Four refineries, build-own-operate

Refineries are the second named buyer. On 11 August 2026, PIB reported a Rajya Sabha reply under SIGHT Mode-2B. Awarded capacity is 30 kilo-tonnes a year: Indian Oil at Panipat, 10; Bharat Petroleum at Bina, 5; Hindustan Petroleum at Vizag, 5; Numaligarh, 10. The [release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297571) says the projects are build-own-operate arrangements through private developers, with project-specific investment by those developers.

That contract shape differs from a fertiliser auction. The refinery is the offtaker at a named site. The developer owns the plant. On 17 June 2026 the minister had already said contracts covered 30,000 tonnes a year for IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, and NRL. August adds the locations. Thirty kilo-tonnes is an industrial slice. It is not the Mission’s 5 million tonne aim.

## Steel is still a pilot budget

The November 2025 note says five pilot projects were started with public and private steelmakers to test green hydrogen for iron reduction and other process uses, and to judge technical feasibility, cost, and safety in Indian conditions. On 17 June 2026 the minister said ₹84 crore had been sanctioned for pilot projects testing 100 percent hydrogen injection in steel. A sanctioned pilot is a learning budget. It does not read as a multi-year offtake for a merchant plant.

## Road fuel is a trial, and one plant is actually cited as commissioned

The 29 July 2026 Lok Sabha answer on hydrogen vehicles funds 12 pilots so that agencies report efficiency, fuel use, safety, and utilisation. The answer says those reports support an assessment of techno-economic viability. A viability trial is the right description of the road market in that text. It is a fleet experiment across 21 routes, with ₹41.582 crore released against about ₹410.77 crore allocated.

In the same 17 June speech, the minister cited JSW’s commissioning of a 3,600 tonne-a-year green hydrogen plant in November 2025. One commissioned plant does not describe the market. It does show that “awarded” and “running” can appear in one speech, and only the second is a sale a lender can visit.

The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) still points domestic demand at an Empowered Group trajectory that this set of later releases does not reprint as a notified quota. Until that trajectory is the document on the table, the buyers a founder can name are the fertiliser agreements, the four refinery sites, and the steel and mobility pilots.
