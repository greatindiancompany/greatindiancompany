# What SIAM Reported for Indian Auto Sales in FY 2025-26

The market signal in the official series is a record year for the whole vehicle industry, with electric vehicles still a measured slice inside it. SIAM and the Ministry of Heavy Industries publish different cuts. SIAM reports wholesale domestic sales and exports by segment. The ministry reports electric-vehicle penetration and scheme reimbursements. Putting those numbers in one sentence without the label will overstate how electric the record year was.

## The year SIAM closed on 14 April 2026

In its [14 April 2026 release](https://www.siam.in/pressrelease-details.aspx?pid=605), SIAM reported domestic sales for April 2025–March 2026 of 46,43,439 passenger vehicles (up 7.9 percent), 10,79,871 commercial vehicles (up 12.6 percent), 8,36,231 three-wheelers (up 12.8 percent), and 2,17,05,974 two-wheelers (up 10.7 percent). SIAM's president described every one of those categories as the highest financial-year sales after seven years. Two-wheelers crossed the previous peak, which SIAM places in FY 2018-19.

Production of passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, three-wheelers, two-wheelers, and quadricycles was 3,47,08,984 units in the year. The footnotes matter. Passenger-vehicle figures in that annual table omit BMW, Mercedes, JLR, and Volvo. Commercial-vehicle figures omit Daimler. A headline that treats 3.47 crore as every vehicle built in India is reading past SIAM's own note.

The shape of the year was uneven. SIAM says passenger-vehicle sales fell 1.4 percent in the first half and rose 16.7 percent in the second. Two-wheeler growth was under 1 percent in the first half and about 21.5 percent in the second. SIAM attributes the second-half lift to GST 2.0, income-tax relief, and successive RBI repo-rate cuts. That is SIAM's account of demand, not a tax notification reproduced here.

## What SIAM said about electric vehicles

Inside the passenger-vehicle section, SIAM says registration of electric passenger vehicles rose by more than 80 percent in FY 2025-26 "compared to previous years," and that this rise also propelled the segment. The release does not give the electric passenger-vehicle unit count behind that percentage. For three-wheelers, SIAM says expansion of electric autorickshaws aided demand, and that some states issued new permits for ICE three-wheelers as well. For two-wheelers, it notes higher EV offtake in March, linked by SIAM to anticipation of rising fuel prices.

The prior year's SIAM release, [15 April 2025](https://www.siam.in/pressrelease-details.aspx?mpgid=48&pgidtrail=50&pid=579), is the place for a level rather than a growth rate. SIAM said total EV registrations reached 1.97 million in FY 2024-25, against 1.68 million in FY 2023-24, a rise of 16.9 percent. It named EMPS, PM E-DRIVE, and PM e-Sewa as the policy sequence behind that year.

The ministry's later penetration figure is a third series. The [28 July 2026 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696) says EV penetration went from 0.7 percent in FY 2019-20 to 8.2 percent in FY 2025-26. An 8.2 percent share and a "more than 80 percent" growth in electric passenger vehicles answer different questions. One is the electric share of vehicles. The other is how fast one electric sub-segment grew.

## Exports and the year ahead

SIAM's export lines in the same FY 2025-26 release are the external signal it chooses to publish: passenger-vehicle exports of 9.05 lakh units, up 17.5 percent; two-wheeler exports of 51.8 lakh units, up 23.4 percent; three-wheeler exports of about 4.61 lakh units, up 50.1 percent; commercial-vehicle exports of 0.95 lakh units, up 17.4 percent. Markets named include the Middle East, Africa, Latin America, Sri Lanka, and neighbouring countries.

For FY 2026-27, SIAM says it expects growth to continue in every category and flags the West Asia conflict, crude and commodity prices, the exchange rate, and shipping routes as the risks it is watching. Those are industry judgements attached to a statistical release. They are not a government forecast.
