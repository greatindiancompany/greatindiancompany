# Which agri schemes are still open after 2026

A mid-term view of India's agri chain is a set of expiry dates, not a growth curve. Some instruments were just given another five years. One incentive window closes inside the next financial year. One loan window was described as finished for new disbursement at the end of 2025-26, while its interest subsidy runs on. Production estimates are not a trajectory to extend.

## Locked through the early 2030s

PM-KISAN is the clearest horizon. On 31 July 2026 the Cabinet continued it from 2026-27 to 2030-31 and approved Rs 3.15 lakh crore for that period. The payment stays Rs 6,000 a year in three instalments. The note's stock at the time of the decision was more than Rs 4.47 lakh crore already transferred through 23 instalments. The mid-term fact is the authorisation, not a guess about how many families will still qualify in 2030. The March 2026 reply already showed that qualification is tightening at the edge: new registrations need a Farmer ID in the 19 states where the farmer registry has started.

The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund is described, in the ministry note of 9 August 2025, as operational for 13 years, from 2020-21 to 2032-33. Interest subvention of 3 per cent runs for a maximum of seven years. A loan sanctioned near the end of the disbursement period can therefore still be drawing subvention in the early 2030s. The same note says loan disbursement itself was to finish by the end of 2025-26. That is the sentence a 2026 reader has to test against later replies. The 15 July 2026 stock, Rs 1,61,537.22 crore of capital investment leveraged, tells you the size of what has been mobilised. It does not extend the disbursement calendar by itself.

APEDA's export-support instrument for the 16th Finance Commission cycle is Bharat Krishi Niryat Yojana, 2026-27 to 2030-31, as described in the authority's tender of 1 September 2026. The previous scheme in the 2024-25 annual report covered exporters from 2021-22 to 2025-26. The new description has three heads: export infrastructure, quality development, and market development, open to exporters, FPOs, manufacturers, traders, state governments, and food-testing laboratories. The tender does not state the rupee outlay. The horizon is the cycle. The budget is a separate document.

## Closing sooner

The production-linked incentive for food processing is operational from 2021-22 to 2026-27, with an outlay of Rs 10,900 crore. As on 31 December 2025 the book was 168 approved applicants, about Rs 9,207 crore of investment, around 35 lakh metric tonnes a year of capacity, and Rs 2,714.79 crore of incentive disbursed. The remaining life of the incentive, on the dates in that reply, is the rest of 2026-27. Investment already made can still be earning incentive inside the scheme rules. A new applicant reading this in September 2026 is late in the window the reply describes.

Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana was given an outlay of Rs 6,520 crore for the period up to 2025-26. By 31 December 2025, 1,607 projects had been approved. The cold-chain component was still a live execution book after that: as of 30 June 2026, 98 of 409 approved projects were ongoing, and Rs 2,540.89 crore of Rs 3,089.08 crore sanctioned had been released. "Up to 2025-26" describes the finance-cycle outlay in the ministry's statement. It does not mean every ongoing project stopped on 31 March 2026. The mid-term question for this ledger is completion and release, not a fresh umbrella.

## A target that has already been met

The 10,000-FPO scheme's registration target is done. As on 1 March 2026, 10,000 FPOs were registered, including 1,175 with entirely women members. The outlay stated on 3 February 2026 is Rs 6,865 crore. What remains is not another 10,000. It is the three-year management assistance of up to Rs 18 lakh, the equity grant, and the guarantee. As on 31 December 2025 those had reached 6,557 FPOs and 2,671 FPOs respectively. The trajectory from here is whether the balance-sheet support catches up with the register.

e-NAM does not have an end-year in the March 2026 reply. It has a stock: 1,656 mandis, and cumulative trade of Rs 4,82,350 crore through February 2026. Its mid-term path is more mandis, more inter-state licences, and the warehouse-receipt link described in the April 2026 note. Those are extensions of a platform, not a sunset.

## What is not a trajectory

Foodgrain production of 376.563 million tonnes in the 2025-26 Third Advance Estimates is 18.8 million tonnes, or 5.3 per cent, above 357.732 million tonnes the year before. The Cabinet's PM-KISAN note says that from 2020-21 to 2025-26, area rose about 9.65 per cent, productivity about 10.53 per cent, and foodgrain production about 21.18 per cent. Those are period statements. Extending 5.3 per cent, or 21 per cent, into 2030 is a forecast. None of these documents makes it.
