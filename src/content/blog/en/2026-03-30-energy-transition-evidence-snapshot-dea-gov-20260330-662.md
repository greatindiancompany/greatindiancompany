# An Evidence Snapshot of India's Energy Transition

This is a working card of figures that can be traced to a government document, with the date and the limit of each figure. It is a snapshot as of the sheets available in September 2026. It will go stale when MNRE posts the next physical-progress table. That is the point of dating it.

## Capacity on 31 July 2026

Source: MNRE physical progress, updated 11 August 2026, and the PIB note on the 300 GW mark.

- Solar: 164,594.75 MW (164.59 GW). Inside it, ground-mounted is 122.57 GW, grid rooftop is 30.74 GW, the solar part of hybrids is 4.77 GW, and off-grid is 6.51 GW.
- Wind: 58,136.89 MW (58.14 GW).
- Small hydro: 5,181.76 MW.
- Large hydro: 52,064.67 MW, including 7,425.6 MW of pumped storage.
- Renewable total, including large hydro: 291,725.64 MW.
- Nuclear: 8,780 MW.
- Non-fossil total: 300,505.64 MW (300.50 GW).

PIB describes the non-fossil share as over 54 percent of total installed capacity of around 552 GW. The "around 552 GW" is PIB's rounding of the whole system, not a line on the MNRE renewable table. A 100 MW nuclear unit remains excluded since 31 May 2025 because of a long outage.

Added from 1 April 2026 to 31 July 2026: solar 14,334.05 MW, wind 2,042.05 MW, large hydro 650 MW, all-renewable including large hydro 17,037.54 MW.

What this table can answer: how much nameplate capacity was recorded, by technology. What it cannot answer: how many units those plants generated in July, what they earned, or what share of evening peak they served.

## Generation in FY 2025-26

Source: PIB briefing of 8 April 2026.

- Total generation: 1,845.921 billion units.
- Non-fossil generation: 538.97 billion units, 29.2 percent.
- Non-fossil capacity addition in the year: 55.29 GW, after 29.5 GW in FY 2024-25.
- Solar addition: 44.61 GW. Wind addition: 6.05 GW, after 4.15 GW the year before.
- In July 2025, renewables met 51.5 percent of electricity demand of 203 GW, described as the highest renewable generation share reached.

The August 2026 PIB factsheet says coal supports over 70 percent of electricity generation and nearly 55 percent of primary energy, and that coal production in FY 2025-26 was 1,040.08 million tonnes. Use the 29.2 percent figure for the non-fossil share of annual electrical energy. Use the coal statement as the Ministry's description of coal's role. They come from different notes. Both reject the idea that a 54 percent capacity share is a 54 percent energy share.

## A global capacity rank, with its date

Source: the same 8 April 2026 briefing, quoting IRENA Renewable Energy Statistics 2026, data as of December 2025. Renewable installed capacity, in gigawatts: China 2,258.02, United States 467.92, India 250.52, Brazil 228.20, world 5,149.28. India is third on that list. MNRE's own renewable total for 31 March 2026, 274.68 GW including large hydro, is a later domestic cut. Do not subtract one from the other and call the difference a quarterly build.

## Targets beside the stock

- Updated NDC, August 2022: emissions intensity of GDP down 45 percent by 2030 from 2005; about 50 percent of installed electric capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030, with technology and low-cost finance. The 50 percent capacity mark was reached in June 2025, according to the April 2026 briefing.
- Planning target used by MNRE and CEA: about 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030. Stock on 31 July 2026: 300.50 GW.
- National Electricity Plan, May 2023, end of 2026-27: 609,591 MW likely, solar 185,566 MW, wind 72,895 MW, non-fossil share 57.4 percent. End of 2031-32: 900,422 MW, solar 364,566 MW, wind 121,895 MW, coal 259,643 MW, non-fossil share 68.4 percent.
- National Green Hydrogen Mission: at least 5 million tonnes a year by 2030. Commissioned production cited for February 2026: about 8,000 tonnes a year.

## Money and factories, so the card is not only megawatts

- Union Budget 2026-27, MNRE net of recoveries: ₹32,914.67 crore, of which PM Surya Ghar is ₹22,000 crore and KUSUM is ₹5,000 crore.
- PM Surya Ghar as of 12 August 2026: 51.58 lakh households, ₹28,024 crore subsidy transferred, 14.8 GW commissioned under the scheme. Scheme target: one crore households by FY 2026-27. Scheme outlay: ₹75,021 crore.
- Solar module manufacturing capacity as on 31 March 2026: about 172 GW. Solar PLI outlay: ₹24,000 crore.
- Advanced chemistry cells: ₹18,100 crore for 50 GWh; 40 GWh awarded; 10 GWh earmarked for grid-scale storage.

## A rule for the next time someone quotes a single number

Ask for the date, the unit, and the document. "300 GW" is non-fossil capacity on 31 July 2026. "54 percent" is a share of capacity, described by PIB as over 54 percent of around 552 GW. "29.2 percent" is a share of generation for a completed year. "172 GW" is factory nameplate, not plants. "₹19,744 crore" is a mission envelope, not this year's Budget line. Once those five are kept apart, the rest of the public record is much harder to misuse.
