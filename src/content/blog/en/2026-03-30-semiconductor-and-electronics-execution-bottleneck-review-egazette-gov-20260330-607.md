India's semiconductor bottleneck is no longer the absence of a policy or the absence of approved projects. Twelve manufacturing projects are approved. The bottleneck is the distance between that approval book and a plant that ships, a material that is made in India, and a chip design that earns deployment revenue. The texts name those distances. This brief stays with what they name. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## Finished goods came first, and the Minister said so

On 26 April 2025, launching the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme, the Minister described the sequence in order: finished products, then modules, then components, then the materials that build components. [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620) records him saying finished goods account for 80 to 85 percent of the value chain. The production table that sits under that remark is the scale: electronics output of ₹9.52 lakh crore by 2023-24 in that note, and about ₹13.11 lakh crore by 2025-26 in the later [Lok Sabha reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf). The bottleneck he was pointing at is domestic value inside that output. Mobile phones can be manufactured in India — the reply says around 99.2 percent of phones sold in the country are — while the chip, the display, and the passive are still the open problem. The component scheme exists because that gap survived the assembly boom.

## Packaging has started; the silicon fab has a year that is still ahead

Commercial production, as of 22 July 2026, is three plants: Micron, Kaynes Semicon, and CG Semi. All three are in the assembly, test, or specialised-chip category approved for Sanand. The [15 July 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) schedules the first fab for 2028 and describes the node range already in view as 28 nm to 110 nm. The execution pattern is packaging first. Wafer fabrication at the approved silicon line is still a commissioning schedule, four years after the 29 February 2024 approval and about two years after this page's update.

That pattern repeats inside the Cabinet's own composition of the 12 units: one silicon fab, one silicon-carbide fab, one gallium-nitride micro-LED display fab, and nine packaging units. The volume of approvals is in packaging. The long pole is the silicon line, and behind it the compound and display lines that do not yet appear in the commercial-production sentence.

One more production start was expected in 2026, and the July note did not name the plant. An execution review cannot close that item. It can only keep it open.

## Design tools are in use; deployment is the unpaid step

The July parliamentary reply says 105 startups and MSMEs have design-tool access, with use above 87 lakh hours, and 24 projects have financial support. Chips to Startup is larger still: 320 organisations and more than 240 lakh hours. The Cabinet note's design paragraph says these companies will enter deployment after prototyping. The [Design Linked Incentive guidelines](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Guidelines-for-Design-Linked-Incentive-DLI-Scheme-30.12.2022.pdf) pay the sales-linked incentive only once a threshold of net sales is met. Tool hours are evidence that the infrastructure bottleneck for design has eased. They are not evidence that a product has a buyer. The remaining bottleneck is foundry access for the prototype, then a customer, then the milestone claim. The February 2026 note's tape-outs, including startup silicon at nodes down to 12 nm, show some teams have cleared the first of those. The note does not say those wafers were run in an Indian commercial fab.

## Materials, equipment, and the supply contract sit outside the factory gate

The 22 July reply says approved applicants tie up with global and local partners for chemicals, gases, and other raw materials, and that memoranda with the United States, the European Union, Japan, Singapore, and the Netherlands are a platform for those business-to-business talks. A tie-up is an execution task the applicant still has to finish. It is not a stocked warehouse.

Semicon 2.0 then makes the missing layer a policy pillar. Companies that manufacture equipment, or that make materials, chemicals, and gases, "will be incentivised." The future tense is the bottleneck statement. The pillar was approved on 15 July 2026. The release does not list a single approved equipment or gas project under it. FY 2026-27 projections in the [7 February budget note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) still speak of fabs and packaging units "to be supported" during the year. Support during the year and a plant that has started are different events. The February projections and the July pillar can both be true, and the plant can still be waiting on a tool, a gas contract, or a notified scheme.

## What would count as the bottleneck moving

Three public facts would show movement, and each has a place to appear. A fourth plant named in commercial production, against the July expectation. A MeitY notification that turns the machines-and-materials pillar into a rate and a list. A commissioning update that either holds the silicon fab to 2028 or changes that year in an official text. Training counts and approval counts do not, by themselves, show that movement. India already has those.
