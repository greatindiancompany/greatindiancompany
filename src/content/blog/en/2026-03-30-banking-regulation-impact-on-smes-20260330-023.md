# How priority-sector rules shape bank credit to small firms

A small firm does not meet a priority-sector target. The bank does. The [Reserve Bank of India (Priority Sector Lending – Targets and Classification) Directions, 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12799&Mode=0), effective 1 April 2025 and updated as on 7 August 2026, decide which loans count and what a shortfall costs the bank. That cost is why a compliant micro or small enterprise can be easier to place than a loan the bank cannot classify.

## What counts as a small-firm priority loan

The Directions say all bank loans to micro, small, and medium enterprises qualify for priority-sector classification. The enterprise definition is the one in the Reserve Bank's instructions on lending to that sector, as updated. This brief does not restate turnover or investment thresholds, because those sit in the definition the Directions point to, not in a number an article should invent.

Three classifications matter in ordinary credit work:

- Loans up to ₹50 crore to start-ups that themselves meet the enterprise definition are eligible in this category.
- With-recourse factoring, where the bank factors departmentally and the assignor is a micro, small, or medium enterprise, can be classified as such on reporting dates.
- Factoring transactions for those enterprises through the Trade Receivables Discounting System are eligible for priority sector.

Export credit to these enterprises can also be classified in the enterprise category, subject to the aggregate limits the Directions set.

## The sub-target that bites

For domestic commercial banks other than regional rural banks and small finance banks, and for foreign banks with 20 branches or more, the micro-enterprise sub-target is 7.5 percent of adjusted net bank credit or credit equivalent of off-balance-sheet exposure, whichever is higher. The same 7.5 percent applies to regional rural banks and small finance banks. It is not applied to foreign banks with fewer than 20 branches.

There is no separate percentage in this table for all small and medium enterprises together. The binding sub-target inside the enterprise book is the micro slice. A loan to a larger small or medium firm can still count toward the overall priority-sector target. It does not, by itself, fill the micro sub-target.

Targets are computed on adjusted net bank credit, or the credit equivalent of off-balance-sheet exposure, as on the corresponding date of the preceding year.

## The price of a miss

A bank that reports a shortfall against a target or sub-target, other than an urban co-operative bank under all-inclusive directions, is allocated amounts to place with the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund and other funds at NABARD, the National Housing Bank, SIDBI, and MUDRA Ltd., as the Reserve Bank decides. Achievement is averaged across quarters.

The interest the bank earns on that placement falls as the overall shortfall grows: Bank Rate minus 2 percentage points if the overall gap is under 5 percentage points, minus 3 if it is 5 and under 10, and minus 4 if it is 10 or more. A shortfall only in a sub-target, with the overall target met, is priced at Bank Rate minus 2 percentage points. Missing targets is also taken into account when the Reserve Bank considers regulatory approvals.

A bank can buy priority-sector lending certificates instead of originating every loan. A micro-enterprise certificate counts toward the micro sub-target and the overall target. A general certificate does not fill the micro gap. The Directions allow issuance, up to 50 percent of the previous year's priority-sector achievement, without the underlying loans on the books, but on the reporting date the target still has to be met by the outstanding priority portfolio plus net certificates. A remaining gap can still be allocated to the funds above.

## Small finance banks are on a lower overall target

A [circular of 20 June 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12875&Mode=0) cut the extra priority-sector component for small finance banks from 35 percent to 20 percent from financial year 2025-26, so the overall target became 60 percent of adjusted net bank credit or the credit equivalent of off-balance-sheet exposure, whichever is higher. Forty percent still follows the prescribed sub-sectors. The current Directions, as updated, show that 60 percent figure for small finance banks. The micro-enterprise sub-target of 7.5 percent remains.

None of this is an entitlement to credit. It is the rule that makes a classifiable small-firm loan useful to a bank that is short of its micro or overall target.
