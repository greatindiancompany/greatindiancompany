# Where India’s holdings and mandis actually sit

The map of Indian agriculture is not one market. The count of farms, the land they operate, the mandis wired into e-NAM, and the cold stores approved by the food-processing ministry peak in different states.

Agriculture Census 2015-16, the tenth census, counted 146.45 million operational holdings on 157.82 million hectares. The largest number of holdings was in Uttar Pradesh, at 23.82 million, then Bihar at 16.41 million, Maharashtra at 15.29 million, Madhya Pradesh at 10.00 million, Karnataka at 8.68 million, Andhra Pradesh at 8.52 million, Tamil Nadu at 7.94 million, Rajasthan at 7.66 million, and Kerala at 7.58 million. Operated area ranks differently. Rajasthan had 20.87 million hectares, Maharashtra 20.51 million, Uttar Pradesh 17.45 million, Madhya Pradesh 15.67 million, and Karnataka 11.81 million. Uttar Pradesh is the holding state. Rajasthan and Maharashtra are the area states. A national average of 1.08 hectares hides that split. The census counts an operational holding, not a household and not a mandi.

## e-NAM follows a third map

The National Agriculture Market is a digital layer on physical mandis. A Lok Sabha reply answered on 17 March 2026 said 1,656 mandis in 23 states and 4 Union Territories were on the portal. Cumulative trade from inception to February 2026 was 13.22 crore tonnes and ₹4,82,350 crore. Registrations as on 28 February 2026 were 1.80 crore farmers, 2.72 lakh traders, and 4,724 FPOs.

The annexure ties these mandi counts to named states: Rajasthan 173, Uttar Pradesh 162, Gujarat 144, Madhya Pradesh 139, Haryana 114, Odisha 66, Telangana 57, Himachal Pradesh 38, Andhra Pradesh 33, Uttarakhand 27, Chhattisgarh 21, Bihar 20, Jharkhand 19, Tripura 19, Jammu and Kashmir 17, Kerala 15, Goa 7, and Assam 3. Other states and Union Territories make up the rest of the 1,656. Bihar’s 16.41 million holdings sit beside 20 integrated mandis. Kerala’s 7.58 million holdings sit beside 15. Integration is not a map of where farmers live.

Trade value is tighter still. In that annexure, cumulative value is about ₹1,30,773 crore in Rajasthan and ₹1,23,474 crore in Haryana, together about ₹2.54 lakh crore of the ₹4.82 lakh crore national total. Andhra Pradesh is about ₹63,416 crore from 33 mandis. Madhya Pradesh is about ₹37,160 crore from 139 mandis. Telangana is about ₹30,164 crore. Uttar Pradesh, with 162 mandis and the most holdings in the census, is about ₹16,559 crore. Odisha is about ₹7,175 crore. A large mandi count is not a large traded value. Uttar Pradesh’s holding numbers and Haryana’s e-NAM value are answers to different questions.

## Cold chain is a fourth map

As on 30 June 2026 the Ministry of Food Processing Industries had approved 409 integrated cold-chain projects, 311 of them completed. The state rows in that Lok Sabha annexure include Uttarakhand 30 approved (27 completed), Gujarat 29 (25), Tamil Nadu 26 (15), Uttar Pradesh 26 (21), Punjab 25 (21), and Haryana 23 (18). Uttarakhand’s project count is high relative to its 27 e-NAM mandis. Punjab’s cold-chain count is not visible in the e-NAM rows quoted above. A site-selection memo that uses one of these maps as a proxy for the other three will pick the wrong state.
