# The same agri chain, read state by state

A national agri total is a true number and a poor map. The sowing report, the infrastructure-fund annexure, and the e-NAM mandi list each show a different geography. They should not be collapsed into a ranking of "leading farm states."

## Where the 2026 kharif hectare moved

The ministry's area report of 8 September 2026, as on 4 September, puts all-India kharif coverage at 1,086.31 lakh hectares, down 17.69 lakh hectares from the same week in 2025. Almost all of that national decline is rice. Rice fell 16.37 lakh hectares, to 421.82.

The rice decline is named, and it is wide. Karnataka was down 4.28 lakh hectares, Telangana 3.61, Andhra Pradesh 1.91, Uttar Pradesh 1.84, Madhya Pradesh 1.75, Jharkhand 1.38, Tamil Nadu 1.32, and Maharashtra 1.15. Assam was up 0.52 and Odisha 0.29. Rice nationally was still above its normal of 412.00 lakh hectares. A state that lost area can still be inside a crop that is above normal. The report gives the state change. It does not give the state's level against its own normal.

Other crops reverse the cast. Pulses rose 1.84 lakh hectares nationally, with Uttar Pradesh up 3.97, Jharkhand 0.98, Telangana 0.90, Rajasthan 0.57, and Madhya Pradesh 0.38, while Maharashtra fell 2.60, Karnataka 2.07, and Odisha 0.61. Telangana lost rice area and gained pulse area in the same week. That is a cropping shift inside one state, visible only because the report splits the crops.

Cotton's national dip of 0.69 lakh hectares hides a larger swing. Rajasthan was down 1.05, Haryana 0.90, and Punjab 0.43, while Telangana was up 1.14 and Gujarat 0.66. Oilseeds fell most in Rajasthan (2.23) and Gujarat (1.93) and rose in Uttar Pradesh (2.91) and Madhya Pradesh (1.14). Coarse cereals and millets fell hardest in Karnataka (5.64) and Maharashtra (2.41) and rose in Rajasthan (2.90).

These are week-on-week-of-last-year differences in a progress report. They are not state domestic product, and they are not procurement.

## Where the storage-and-logistics rupee has landed

The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund reply dated for 15 July 2026 separates farm-gate storage and logistics from the wider fund. Cumulative capital investment leveraged for all post-harvest and viable farming assets was Rs 1,61,537.22 crore. The storage-and-logistics slice was Rs 50,332.66 crore.

The annexure of that reply is the state map of the slice. The four largest cells are Madhya Pradesh Rs 7,559.02 crore, Uttar Pradesh Rs 7,405.78 crore, Maharashtra Rs 5,484.29 crore, and Gujarat Rs 4,713.63 crore. Added as printed, those four are Rs 25,162.72 crore, half of the Rs 50,332.66 crore column. Karnataka follows at Rs 3,313.52 crore and Uttarakhand at Rs 2,833.01 crore. Uttarakhand's place on this list is a fact of the project-cost column. The reply does not explain it.

The same annexure is thin at the other end. Ladakh is Rs 0.46 crore, Manipur Rs 0.51 crore, Mizoram Rs 0.76 crore, Nagaland Rs 5.68 crore, Meghalaya Rs 10.47 crore, and Tripura Rs 10.29 crore. The fund has a state-wise allocation logic based on the value of agricultural output, described in earlier ministry notes. The July annexure is the outcome column for one asset class, not a score of administrative effort.

Andhra Pradesh's row in the reply's narrative is a useful worked example of how to read a state: Rs 2,394.26 crore of farm-gate storage and logistics investment, and, inside Palnadu district, 111 projects with a project cost of Rs 209.90 crore. A district figure exists when the reply prints one. It should not be inferred for districts the reply skips.

## Where the mandi is actually on the screen

e-NAM's Lok Sabha reply of 17 March 2026 says 1,656 mandis in 23 states and 4 Union territories are integrated. Annexure-I is the full count. Rows that the reply prints clearly enough to cite include Uttar Pradesh 162, Rajasthan 173, Gujarat 144, Madhya Pradesh 139, Haryana 114, Odisha 66, Telangana 57, Andhra Pradesh 33, Bihar 20, Jharkhand 19, Jammu and Kashmir 17, and Assam 3. The annexure total is 1,656, so these rows are a sample of the range, not the whole list.

Rajasthan has a second, more specific fact in the prose: 134 of its e-NAM mandis were using AI and machine-learning assaying machines, with central assistance under the scheme. The integration grant is up to Rs 75 lakh per mandi, for assaying labs, weighing, grading, packaging, and related kit. A state with three integrated mandis and a state with more than a hundred are both "on e-NAM." An operator's market access follows the mandi, not the slogan.

Trade value since inception, Rs 4,82,350 crore through February 2026, is national. The reply's state trade table is the place to split it. This brief stops at the mandi counts above rather than forcing a state ranking out of a table that has to be read in the original annexure.
