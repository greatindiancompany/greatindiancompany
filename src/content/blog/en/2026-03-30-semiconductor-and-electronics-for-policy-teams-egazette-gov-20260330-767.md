A policy brief on India's semiconductor programme fails in a predictable way. It adds fiscal outlays to private commitments, it quotes a February training count next to a July one, and it treats a budget aspiration as a Cabinet schedule. The public record is good enough to avoid all three, if the date stays attached to the sentence. This note is for the person writing that brief. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## Start from the parliamentary reply, then go to the Cabinet note

The cleanest status paragraph is the Minister of State's answer to Lok Sabha unstarred question 483 on [22 July 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf). It is later than the budget backgrounder and it is a ministerial text. It says the Semicon India Programme was launched in January 2022 with an outlay of ₹76,000 crore. Twelve manufacturing projects are approved, in Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Gujarat, Punjab, Odisha, and Uttar Pradesh, with investment commitments of about ₹1.64 lakh crore. They are expected to generate around 32,000 direct jobs, depending on factory automation. Commercial production had commenced at Micron Semiconductor Technology India, Kaynes Semicon, and CG Semi. Twenty-four design projects have financial support. One hundred and five startups and MSMEs have design tools, with use above 87 lakh hours. Semicon 2.0 was approved on 15 July 2026 with an outlay of ₹1.275 lakh crore, on six pillars: design, machines and materials, more fabs, ATMP and OSAT, research, and talent.

The [Cabinet release of 15 July](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) is the decision behind that last sentence, and it adds three facts the reply compresses. The outlay is written as ₹1,27,500 crore. The 12 units are described as one silicon fab, one silicon-carbide fab, one integrated gallium-nitride micro-LED display fab, and nine packaging units. The first fab is scheduled to be commissioned in 2028, and the node range of the journey so far is given as 28 nm to 110 nm. One further production start was expected in 2026 and was not named. A brief that needs the composition of the 12, or the 2028 date, has to use the Cabinet note. A brief that needs the jobs caveat and the state list has to use the reply.

The reply points project details to [ism.gov.in](https://www.ism.gov.in/). If that page and a Cabinet release differ on a capacity, cite the approval release and say the mission site is the page Parliament named for the living file. Do not average them.

## Reconcile the February budget note before it enters a speech

The [PIB note of 7 February 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) is a budget backgrounder. It is useful, and it is easy to misuse, because it was written before the July decision and it mixes official targets with industry estimates and with programme aspirations.

Safe to cite, with the date: a ₹1,000 crore provision for ISM 2.0 in FY 2026-27; an ₹8,000 crore outlay for the modified programme that year; year-ahead support targets of one fab, nine compound or packaging units, and 30 design companies; fiscal support of up to 50 percent described for the 2021 framework. Those annual figures were not withdrawn in the July text. They are also not the same object as the ₹1,27,500 crore multi-year outlay. A footnote should say so in one sentence.

Cite only with the note's own label: the Indian semiconductor market "as per industry estimates," about $38 billion in 2023 and $100 billion to $110 billion by 2030. That is not a MeitY production census. The census-style series is the Lok Sabha table for electronics goods, about ₹13.11 lakh crore of production and about ₹4.24 lakh crore of exports in 2025-26.

Treat as the backgrounder's language, not as the July decision: a 2029 aim of designing and manufacturing chips for 70 to 75 percent of domestic applications, a roadmap toward 3 nm and 2 nm, and an aim to be among the top semiconductor nations by 2035. The July Cabinet note does not restate those dates. The schedule it does set is 2028 for the first fab, and a research pillar for nodes beyond 28 nm to 110 nm.

Training counts need the same discipline. The February note's SMART Lab figure, above 62,000, is earlier than Parliament's July figure of more than one lakh people trained at that lab. The February tool-hour figure and the July figures of 87 lakh hours and 240 lakh hours are different counters. Pick the July reply for a current status line. Keep February when the sentence is about what the budget session was told.

## What the record does not yet give a policy team

The reply does not publish fiscal support disbursed against the ₹76,000 crore outlay, or capital expenditure incurred against the ₹1.64 lakh crore of commitments. Commercial production at three plants is the public evidence that some plants have crossed into output. It is not a utilisation rate.

The reply does not split the 32,000 jobs by state. The February 2024 approval did publish 20,000 direct and about 60,000 indirect jobs for three plants only. Those sentences cannot be added. A state government asking "how many of the jobs are here" does not have an official answer in the July reply. The honest reply is the plant's own approval text, where it has a jobs sentence, plus the national caveat on automation.

The reply answers the Rajasthan question by naming six states. Rajasthan is not among them. Location, the reply says, is the investor's choice, and distributed manufacturing is encouraged. A state pitch that promises a semiconductor approval as a matter of state policy is ahead of the instrument. The instrument leaves the site to the applicant. Components are different: the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme is national and is not limited to the six states.

Semicon 2.0's machines-and-materials pillar does not yet, in the Cabinet release, have a notified rate, an eligible list, or an approved project. A speech can say the Cabinet decided to incentivise equipment, chemicals, and gases. It cannot yet say at what percentage, or who has been selected.

Electronics employment of nearly 25 lakh is, in the reply's words, an industry estimate. It should not be briefed as a MeitY administrative count, and it should not be briefed as semiconductor-plant employment.

## A minimum citation set

For a two-page note, four links carry the load. The [22 July 2026 reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) for status. The [15 July 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) for the new outlay, the mix of the 12 units, and the 2028 date. The [29 February 2024 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2010132) for the 2021 programme date, the Dholera node, and the three large approvals. The [Design Linked Incentive gazette](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Notification-Design-Linked-Incentive-Scheme.pdf) for any sentence about what a design startup can actually claim. The dated ledger that keeps the rest is the [evidence snapshot](/blog/semiconductor-and-electronics-evidence-snapshot-egazette-gov-20260330-687), on the [Great Indian Company research desk](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog).
