# The Geography India Does Publish for Electric Buses and Chargers

Anyone looking for a state ranking of electric two-wheeler subsidies will not find it in the ministry's record, and the ministry has said why. The geography that is published sits in buses, in a charging sanction that names categories of recipients, and in a list of states that have filed a payment-security mandate.

## Demand incentive is national on purpose

The [28 July 2026 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696) says no state-wise allocation has been done for the demand-incentive category, and therefore state-wise data is not maintained for the vehicles incentivised. As on 22 July 2026 the reimbursed book was 23,22,878 vehicles and ₹2,281.94 crore, almost all of it two-wheelers and L5 three-wheelers. That book cannot be split by state from this reply.

The reply also answers a question about special incentives for Tier-II and Tier-III cities. The scheme, it says, is implemented across the country, including those cities and aspirational regions. That is a coverage statement. It is not a table of extra rupees per city.

The [scheme portal](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/) adds a design fact that explains the national book: private and corporate registered electric two-wheelers are eligible along with commercial ones, while electric three-wheelers must be registered for commercial use. A two-wheeler bought in any state that meets the battery and price rules can enter the same national cap. After the [10 August 2026 amendment](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/docs/policy_document/Gazette%20Notification%2010%2008%202026%20on%20enhancement%20of%20Outlay%2011900%20cr.pdf), that cap is 45,79,120 two-wheelers.

## Where city and state names do appear

Buses are procured for cities, so the geography shows up. The July 2026 reply says 14,000 electric buses have been allocated, letters of confirmation of demand have gone to cities and state transport undertakings, and letters of confirmation of quantity have gone to successful bidders for 13,800 buses. Surat has signed a concession agreement for 600 buses and Hyderabad for 915. Selected bidders still have to get prototypes approved and deploy the buses. The portal describes the bus component as support for more than 14,000 buses across nine major cities with populations above 4 million.

An earlier snapshot, in the [13 January 2026 year-end review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2214039), said CESL had concluded an e-bus tender of 10,900 buses in phase 1 for Delhi, Ahmedabad, Surat, Hyderabad, and Bangalore, and that discovered rates had been sent to those cities for letters of award and concession agreements. Read the two texts in date order. January names five cities and a 10,900-bus tender. July names an allocation of 14,000, quantity letters for 13,800, and signed concessions in Surat and Hyderabad. The January review does not say the other three cities had signed.

Charging sanctions are partly geographic and still incomplete in public. As on 1 July 2026, ₹689 crore of the ₹2,000 crore public-charging allocation had been approved for 6,562 chargers, given to three oil-marketing companies and nine states. The reply does not name the nine states or the three companies. A state pattern cannot be drawn from a count of "nine."

## The payment-security map is a list of mandates

The January 2026 review is specific about the PM e-Bus Sewa Payment Security Mechanism, notified on 28 October 2024 with an outlay of ₹3,435.33 crore. It covers 38,000 or more e-buses for up to 12 years. If a public transport authority defaults on a gross-cost contract, the mechanism can pay OEMs or operators and then recover the money. Authorities must repay within 90 days. CESL is the implementing agency.

As of 22 December 2025, the Direct Debit Mandate required under the scheme had been submitted to the Reserve Bank of India by 15 states and Union territories that were in either PM E-DRIVE or the housing ministry's PM-eBus Sewa: Gujarat, Karnataka, Rajasthan, Punjab, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Meghalaya, Maharashtra, Uttarakhand, Odisha, Jammu and Kashmir, Puducherry, Assam, and Manipur. That list is participation in a payment backstop. It is not a ranking of buses on the road.
