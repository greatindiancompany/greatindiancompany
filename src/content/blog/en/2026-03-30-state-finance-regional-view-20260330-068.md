# Why one state's fiscal position is not the national ratio

The number people quote, 3.3 per cent of GDP, is a consolidation. The Reserve Bank published it on 23 January 2026 for the states together: the gross fiscal deficit in 2024-25, and the 2025-26 budget estimate. A plant, a hospital chain, or a bond desk that operates in one state is exposed to that state's budget, that state's own-tax base, and that state's debt service. The consolidation is the context. It is a poor substitute for the state file.

## The study's own regional frame

The theme of *State Finances: A Study of Budgets of 2025-26* is demographic transition. The press release sorts states into three fiscal situations.

Youthful states have a wider window, because the working-age population is expanding and revenue mobilisation is stronger. The Bank says that window can be used for investment in human capital. Ageing states face a narrowing window: a shrinking tax base and rising committed expenditure, which calls for more revenue capacity and for reforms in healthcare, pensions, and workforce policy. Intermediate states have to hold growth priorities and start preparing for ageing at the same time.

Chapter III of the study is that argument in tables: fertility and life expectancy, the share of population above 60, the working-age share through 2026-36, revenue indicators by demographic group, and social-sector spending by group. Charts III.2 through III.8 carry the same split. The chapter is a classification, with the Bank's labels. It will not match every political map of "north and south" or "high income and low income," and it should be read as the classification the study actually uses.

## Where the average breaks

Chart II.2 is states' gross fiscal deficit as a ratio of GSDP in the 2025-26 budget estimates. The denominator is the state's own product. A state can sit near the all-state GDP ratio and still be far from its neighbours on a GSDP basis. Statement 1 and Statement 3 are the state-wise deficit tables. Statement 20 is outstanding liabilities as a per cent of GSDP, which is the stock version of the same point. The consolidated liability ratio in the press release, 29.2 per cent of GDP at end-March 2026, is the national stock. It does not say which state's bonds are the heavy part of it.

Own tax is the other fracture line. Chart II.4 is states' own tax revenue. Statement 14 is the tax-revenue table. A state that raises a large share of its receipts itself has a different shock when the Union's tax collection slows from a state that lives on devolution and grants. Statement 17 is devolution and transfers from the Centre. Box II.1, on stamp duty and registration fees, is a reminder that one own-tax head can dominate in a property-market state and barely matter in another.

Horizontal devolution is the formula that redistributes the states' 41 per cent of the divisible pool. The Sixteenth Finance Commission report, Volume I, published with the Budget documents, sets out that distribution for 2026-27 to 2030-31. The government's memorandum accepted the 41 per cent vertical share. The state-wise shares are in the Commission's report. Using the all-state average as if every state receives the average share will mis-state both a poor state's transfer and a rich state's own-tax dependence.

## What to pull for one state

For a single state, the useful stack is short.

- That state's rows in Statements 1, 2, 3, 14, 17, and 20 of the Reserve Bank study, with the column labelled as accounts, revised estimates, or budget estimates.
- Chart II.2 and Chart II.4, to see the state against the others on deficit and own tax.
- The state's own budget documents for the year, which carry the new schemes and the tax changes the consolidated study summarises.
- The Comptroller and Auditor General's finance accounts and audit report on state finances for that state, which is the audited close of an earlier year.

Capital expenditure in the consolidation was 2.7 per cent of GDP in 2023-24 and 2024-25, and is budgeted at 3.2 per cent for 2025-26. Whether a given state is inside that thrust shows up in its capital-outlay line and in whether it drew the interest-free capital loan. The all-state 3.2 does not award the project to any one capital city.

Demographic group, own-tax share, and the devolution formula are three different maps. A decision that needs one state needs all three, taken from the tables, with the state's name on the row.
