# Risks That Official Series Can Actually Trigger

A scenario is useful when a published rule or a published series can confirm it. The four below are built that way. None of them carries a probability. None of them predicts a loss in rupees, because no official document used here prices that loss.

## A transfer restriction arrives with a name attached

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, notified on 13 November 2025, leave cross-border transfer standing. Rule 15 allows transfer outside India subject to requirements the Central Government may later set by order, where data are made available to a foreign State or someone under its control. Rule 13(4), which commences on **13 May 2027**, can require a notified Significant Data Fiduciary to keep specified personal data, and the traffic data of its flow, inside India. The categories are not listed in the rule. The Central Government specifies them on a committee's recommendation, and it notifies who is a Significant Data Fiduciary under section 10 of the Act.

The risk case is an order or a specification that names a dataset the hub sends to its parent. The calm case is that no such order is issued, which is the rule text as it stands. The watch item is the Gazette, not a summary of "India's data localisation stance". Penalties in the Act's Schedule, up to **₹250 crore** for a security-safeguard failure and up to **₹150 crore** for a Significant Data Fiduciary breach, matter only after an obligation is in force and is missed. They are not a current running cost.

## The next export print does not repeat the mix

The latest survey, released 18 September 2026, shows software services exports up **8.2 percent** to **US$ 221.4 billion**. IT services and engineering services carried the growth. BPO's dollar level was almost flat at **US$ 56.0 billion**, and software product development fell to **US$ 6.4 billion**. Off-site delivery rose to **91.7 percent**. The United States rose to **54.1 percent**.

A risk case for a captive, or for a vendor that sells only into captives, is that the next annual round shows the engineering and IT-services pace cooling, or the US share reversing. The trigger is that next survey. There is no official monthly series in these documents that can confirm the turn earlier. Treating a single 8.2 percent print as a locked growth rate is the fragile planning case. The path into this print is in the [mid-term brief](/blog/global-capability-centers-mid-term-trajectory-gst-gov-20260330-526).

## Gross inflow looks abundant and net inflow stays thin

The Ministry of Finance told the Lok Sabha on 20 July 2026 that gross FDI was **USD 94.84 billion** in 2025-26 and net FDI **USD 6.95 billion**, up from a net **USD 0.96 billion**. It pointed to repatriation and to Indian outward investment. Inside that year, computer-software equity was **USD 13,946 million**, after two years at **USD 7,973 million** and **USD 7,814 million**.

The risk for a multi-year campus is underwriting it on one strong equity year, or on the gross FDI headline, without the net figure and without the sector's own prior dip. A captive funded by retained earnings is only partly visible in fresh equity. A weak net-FDI year is not, on the Ministry's own explanation, the same event as a closure of the automatic route. Make in India's listing of IT and BPM at 100 percent automatic remains the entry description until a press note changes it.

## The export stock is geographically tight

On STPI's tentative 2024-25 table, Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Telangana together are about **76 percent** of **₹10,69,270.59 crore** in registered IT/ITeS exports. Six states account for about **94 percent**. STPI's **59** Tier II and Tier III centres show a dispersed public network and do not show a dispersed export stock. A state-level shock to power, municipal permission, or a local incentive would land on a large share of registered software exports if it landed in Karnataka or Maharashtra. The rupee table is the exposure measure. A claim that the industry has already diversified because new STPI centres exist uses the centre count to answer an export question. The [state brief](/blog/global-capability-centers-state-wise-patterns-gst-gov-20260330-326) keeps the two maps apart.

## A count without a frame

Decisions that treat "over 1,700 centres" and **$64.6 billion**, as highlighted in the PIB note of 25 February 2025, as a statistical base will move whenever the unnamed underlying method moves. The Reserve Bank, DPIIT, and MoSPI have not published that frame. The operational risk is a target, a valuation, or a policy goal that cannot be re-measured from a primary table.
