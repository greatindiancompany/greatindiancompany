# Indicators a policy team can publish on women’s work

A policy note on women’s work fails in a predictable way: one rate, no status, no age, and a comparison across the 2025 survey redesign. The fix is a small set of indicators MoSPI, MoLE and MoWCD already produce, each with the comparison it can bear.

## Publish both participation rates, with the break marked

For calendar 2025, usual status, women aged 15 and above, labour force participation is 40.0 percent. The current weekly status for the same population is 34.3 percent. Rural and urban usual-status rates are 45.9 and 27.7 percent. The weekly April–June 2026 quarter is 33.2 percent, with Figure 3 at 37.2 percent rural and 25.0 percent urban.

A dashboard that shows only 40.0 will be contradicted by the next monthly bulletin, which is a weekly rate and has recently printed near 33 percent. A dashboard that shows only the monthly rate will be read as a collapse from the annual figure. Publish the pair, name the reference period, and footnote MoSPI’s statement that 2025 may not be strictly comparable with PLFS reports through December 2024.

The comparable historical series is the July–June design. Female usual-status participation moved from 23.3 percent in 2017–18 to 41.7 percent in 2023–24, with rural women at 47.6 percent and urban women at 28.0 percent in the final year. That table can be published as history. It should end in 2023–24, then start again at the 2025 design. The 2025 press note’s own word for the level against 2024 is stable.

## Publish job quality next to the rate

Participation rose, on the old design, mostly in rural areas. Job type shows what that employment is. In 2025, 64.2 percent of women workers were self-employed and 18.2 percent were in regular wage or salaried work. Rural women workers were 70.7 percent self-employed and 9.3 percent regular wage. Urban women workers were 50.9 percent regular wage. If the policy goal is regular, covered employment, the indicator is the regular-wage share and the EPFO female net addition, 26.9 lakh in 2024–25. If the goal is any economic activity, including subsidiary work, the indicator is usual-status participation. Publishing one and claiming the other is the mix to avoid.

Unemployment belongs on the page with its geography. All-India female usual-status unemployment was 3.1 percent. Urban women were at 6.4 percent. Urban women aged 15–29 were at 18.9 percent. The all-India 3.1 percent is a poor target if the programme is about young women in cities.

The reason for being outside the labour force is an indicator, not a preface. For women, 44.4 percent named child care or personal commitments in home-making. For men, 69.8 percent named continuing studies. Time use gives the hours: 289 minutes a day for women participants on unpaid domestic services, 88 for men, in the 2024 survey. A scheme review that cannot show movement in that reason code, or in a later time-use round, has not shown movement in the constraint the surveys name.

## Publish the creche and training instruments on their own dates

Mission Shakti’s Samarthya sub-scheme includes Palna and Sakhi Niwas. Palna is a centrally sponsored creche for children aged 6 months to 6 years, open to mothers irrespective of employment status, funded 60:40 in the general case, 90:10 in the North East and special category States, and fully by the Centre in Union Territories without a legislature. The target stated for new Anganwadi-cum-creches in 2024–25 was 17,000. Approvals as of March 2025 were 11,395 across 34 States and Union Territories. A policy note in September 2026 should either cite a later approval or operational count, or keep the March 2025 date in the sentence.

The establishment creche duty, under the maternity amendment MoLE implements, begins at 50 employees. It is not a substitute statistic for Palna. One is a private-establishment obligation. The other is a public scheme with a funding ratio and an approval count.

On skills, publish the PLFS cell and the scheme cell separately. PLFS: 4.2 percent of persons aged 15–59 reported formal vocational or technical training, and 51.4 percent of the women in that trained group were in the workforce, against 83.3 percent of the men. NAPS: women’s share of apprentices was 22.84 percent in 2024–25, and 24.80 percent of those engaged in 2025–26 up to 31 October 2025. The first is a population rate. The second is a scheme intake. A review that averages them has left both systems.

## A minimum table

One page can hold: usual-status and weekly female participation, rural and urban; the regular-wage share of women workers, rural and urban; urban youth female unemployment; the out-of-labour-force reason; Palna approvals with a date; NAPS women’s share with a year. Every row names the ministry document. The 2025 rows carry the comparability footnote. That page is enough for a budget brief, a state review, or a question in the House, and it does not require a number the surveys have not printed.
