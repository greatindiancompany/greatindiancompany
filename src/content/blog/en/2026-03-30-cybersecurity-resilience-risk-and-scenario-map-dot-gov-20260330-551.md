A scenario for cybersecurity resilience in India is a fork in a record you can name. It is not a story about attackers, and it is not a probability. This page does not assign likelihoods, loss amounts, or a national risk score.

## Forks an operator can define

Each fork needs a trigger, the first control that breaks, and the record that would show it. If you cannot name the record, it is not a scenario yet.

- **The test fails.** A product does not clear the security test required before connection, including a test under Indian Telecom Security Assurance Requirements. The element stays unconnected. The record is the failed report or the missing certificate. The Department of Telecommunications shelf for that gate is [dot.gov.in](https://www.dot.gov.in/).
- **The vendor is not accepted.** A trusted-source determination blocks the vendor in the build plan. The record is the determination, not a rumour. This page does not say any named vendor is blocked.
- **The report is late.** An incident misses the clock in the CERT-In direction or in the telecom process that applies. The record is your own timeline next to the direction. This page does not restate the clock.
- **Two duties disagree.** A sector regulator and a telecom rule ask for different logs or different clocks. The record is both texts, side by side. The break is the system that cannot satisfy both.
- **The log cannot be retrieved.** The platform was bought. During the incident the log is not in the form the direction asks for. The record is the failed retrieval.
- **The protected system is undesignated in your file.** You operate something you believe is critical information infrastructure, and you have not opened the designation. The record is the designation or its absence in your file.

McKinsey's India insights index was on an earlier draft. It is not a trigger. The link is omitted.

Use the map to decide which control to staff. A fork with no owner is the [bottleneck](/blog/cybersecurity-resilience-execution-bottleneck-review-dot-gov-20260330-591).
