# Bad loans are low. The stress test shows the path back up

Fast credit growth is landing on the cleanest bank loan book India has reported in decades. The June 2026 Financial Stability Report also writes down the paths on which that book gets worse, and the capital that would remain if it did.

## The stock of bad loans

The gross non-performing asset ratio of scheduled commercial banks fell to **1.8%** in March 2026. The report calls that a multi-decade low and says the improvement ran across bank groups. A year of official checkpoints sits behind it: **2.2%** at end-March 2025 and **2.1%** at end-September 2025, in the Trend and Progress release, then 1.8% at the next March.

The annual slippage ratio, the flow of new impaired assets, eased to **1.2%** in 2025-26. It had been declining for four financial years, led by public-sector and private-sector banks. The provisioning coverage ratio was broadly stable at **75.6%**. Write-offs relative to gross non-performing assets stayed high, especially at private-sector and foreign banks. A low gross ratio with heavy write-offs is a cleaner stock, not proof that nothing went bad.

Sector quality improved on every broad economic sector. Agriculture remained the weak book: a gross non-performing ratio of **5.1%**, and **37.2%** of the system’s gross non-performing assets, in March 2026. Industry sub-sectors and personal loans broadly improved. Large borrowers were **44.5%** of total credit, a slightly higher share, and their share of gross bad loans kept falling. Their own gross non-performing ratio was **1.2%** at end-March 2026, down from **2.4%** at end-September 2024. Early-stress accounts did not all move the same way. The report’s large-borrower chart shows SMA-1 volumes higher in March 2026, while other special-mention volumes contracted.

## Capital, funding, and the risk weight of new loans

Capital to risk-weighted assets was **17.7%** at end-March 2026, and common equity tier 1 was **15.3%**. Both are described as multi-decade highs. Common equity was **86%** of total capital. Credit risk weights are about **86%** of all risk-weighted assets, and in 2025-26 those credit risk weights grew more slowly than credit itself, for the first time in three years. The report reads that as a shift toward lower-risk new lending. The tier-1 leverage ratio was **8.2%**.

Liquidity buffers thinned and stayed comfortable. The liquidity coverage ratio was **124.2%** and the net stable funding ratio **122.1%**, both above the regulatory floors. The tighter point is funding mix. Deposits grew **11.5%** in 2025-26 while credit grew **14.5%**, and term deposits kept outgrowing current and savings accounts. The report calls funding an emerging challenge even with the balance sheet described as sound. Net interest income grew **4.0%** year on year in March 2026, supported by volumes, while margins were broadly unchanged as the cost of funds and asset yields fell together. Profit after tax was **₹4,05,268 crore** in 2025-26, against **₹3,78,163 crore** the year before.

## What the stress test actually says

The macro test covers 46 major banks over two years. In the baseline, their aggregate gross non-performing ratio edges from **1.8%** in March 2026 to **1.9%** by March 2028. In two adverse geopolitical cases it rises to **3.8%** and **4.1%**.

Aggregate capital to risk-weighted assets for those 46 banks starts at **17.5%** in the test (a hair under the 17.7% system figure) and falls to **15.6%** by March 2028 in the baseline, **13.3%** in the first adverse case, and **13.0%** in the second. No bank in the sample breaks the 9% minimum in the baseline. One bank does in the first adverse case, and two in the second. Common equity tier 1 falls from **15.2%** to **13.9%**, **11.6%**, and **11.4%**. Every bank in the sample stays above the 5.5% common-equity floor in all three cases.

A harsher single-factor shock, two standard deviations on the bad-loan ratio, would take the gross non-performing ratio of the 46 banks to **8.1%** and cut the capital ratio by **400 basis points**. It would still leave the system above the regulatory minimum. Four banks, with **12%** of scheduled commercial bank assets, would breach the minimum in that shock. The reverse test says it would take a 4.4-standard-deviation rise in the bad-loan ratio to push system capital through 9%.

Concentration is the sharper credit risk. If each bank’s top three individual borrowers defaulted, the system bad-loan ratio would rise by **340 basis points**, and the capital ratio would fall by **80 basis points**. A top-three group default is the companion case in the same section. These are extreme assumed defaults, not forecasts.

## The non-bank channel attached to this growth

Services credit, the fastest sector in the July 2026 deployment release, is being pulled along by loans to non-bank finance companies. In the stability report’s sources-of-funds table, borrowings from banks were **30.4%** of funds for upper- and middle-layer non-banks at March 2026, against **28.6%** a year earlier. For the sample of non-banks in the stress test, the gross non-performing ratio may rise from **2.4%** in March 2026 to **2.8%** in March 2027 even in the baseline, and their capital ratio may dip from **22.3%** to **20.8%**. Bank asset quality and non-bank asset quality are linked by that funding line.

The press note on the report says the domestic system is resilient, capital stays above the floors under the adverse macro cases, and non-banks remain sound with improving asset quality. The numbers above are the version of that sentence a risk reader can audit. Great Indian Company puts them next to the growth notes so a 19% credit rate is not read as a quality rate.
