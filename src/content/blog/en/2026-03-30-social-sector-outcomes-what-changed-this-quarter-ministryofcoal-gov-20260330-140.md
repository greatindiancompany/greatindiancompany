# What Changed in India’s Social Sector Outcomes This Quarter

The quarter that runs through 24 September 2026 did not publish one new national score for “the social sector.” It published four different kinds of change. A law for rural wages took effect. A school census for 2025-26 was released. District health fact sheets became available. And the ninth Rashtriya Poshan Maah opened with Anganwadi centres, not a new malnutrition percentage, as the organising idea.

## A new rural wage statute, not a new MGNREGS printout

On 1 July 2026 the Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 came into force in all rural areas. The Press Information Bureau recorded that the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 stands repealed from that date, and that the statutory entitlement is now 125 days of wage employment in a financial year. Notified daily wages were revised so that none sits below ₹300. The national average notified wage moved from ₹298.8 to ₹327.4. The Centre’s provision for the central share in 2026-27 is ₹95,692.31 crore.

Anyone still reading FY 2025-26 MGNREGS person-day totals as the live programme is reading a closed statute. The outcome that matters this quarter is whether demand, wage payment, and works continue under the new Act. The design of that shift is set out in the companion note on the [1 July change](/blog/social-sector-outcomes-policy-shift-explainer-ministryofcoal-gov-20260330-180).

## School participation moved; learning scores did not arrive

On 7 July 2026 the Ministry of Education released UDISE+ 2025-26. The release is a census of schools, teachers, and participation. It is not a learning assessment.

What it does show, against 2024-25, is concrete. Preparatory dropout fell from 2.3% to 1.8%. Secondary dropout fell from 8.2% to 7.0%. The middle-stage series in the same table moves from 3.5% to 3.6%, and the release text does not claim a decline there. Secondary retention rose from 47.2% to 51.9%. Secondary gross enrolment rose from 68.5% to 71.7%. Pupil-teacher ratios are reported at 10, 12, 17, and 21 for foundational, preparatory, middle, and secondary stages. Teachers number 1,02,73,020. Schools with a computer rose from 64.7% to 69.9%, and schools with internet from 63.5% to 67.4%.

A founder selling “learning outcomes improved this quarter” cannot lean on this release. A founder selling access, retention, or school infrastructure can.

## District NFHS-6 sheets, four months after the national release

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare released the NFHS-6 India and state fact sheets on 29 May 2026, for fieldwork done in 2023-24. The new event inside this quarter is the district layer. The International Institute for Population Sciences states that district fact-sheet compendiums for Andhra Pradesh, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Telangana, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, and Mizoram were released on 20 August 2026, and the remaining states and Union territories on 21 August 2026. Manipur is outside that compendium.

National levels were already public in May: stunting among children under five at 29.3%, down from 35.5% in NFHS-5; institutional births at 90.6%, up from 88.6%; household coverage by a health insurance or financing scheme at 60.2%, up from 41.0%. This quarter’s change is that a district review is now possible from the official compendiums, which IIPS says are downloaded from its publication section with guest login. This note does not reprint district cells.

## Poshan Maah is a behaviour month, already under way

On 8 September 2026 the Press Information Bureau described the ninth Rashtriya Poshan Maah, launched on 9 September 2026 in Varanasi under the theme “Hamari Anganwadi, Hamari Jimmedari.” The published priorities are dietary diversity and protein, fresh hot cooked meals, Anganwadi Management Committees under guidelines issued on 23 June 2026, the link between nutrition and early childhood care, and ten years of the Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana. Poshan Tracker counts as of 31 July 2026 — nearly 13.99 lakh Anganwadi centres, 13.30 lakh workers, and almost 8.94 crore beneficiaries — are the operating base for that month. They are not a new stunting estimate.

## Labour-market context, released mid-month

The National Statistics Office put out the August 2026 Periodic Labour Force Survey monthly bulletin on 15 September 2026. For persons aged 15 and above, on the current weekly status, the labour force participation rate was 55.6%, the worker population ratio 52.8%, and the unemployment rate 5.0%. Rural unemployment was 4.1%. That bulletin describes the wider labour market. It does not measure VB-G RAM G person-days.

## What not to mix

Coverage, participation, and body measurements answer different questions. An Anganwadi beneficiary count, a school dropout rate, and a stunting rate can all move, or fail to move, in the same quarter. The useful reading is dated and named: which ministry, which reference period, which indicator.
