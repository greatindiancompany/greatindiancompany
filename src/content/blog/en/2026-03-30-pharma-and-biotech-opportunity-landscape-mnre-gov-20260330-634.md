# Six Different Doors into Indian Pharma and Biotech

An opportunity landscape that repeats one phrase — India pharma upside — hides the fact that the government has published several doors with different keys. A company qualifies for one of them by matching a product, a site, or a stage of science. Walking through the wrong door wastes a year.

## Door 1: greenfield critical inputs

The production-linked incentive for key starting materials, drug intermediates, and APIs pays on sales of identified products from greenfield projects. The 6 February 2026 Lok Sabha reply puts the outlay at ₹6,940 crore, the identified set at 41 products, and approvals at 48 projects covering 33 bulk drugs. The March 2020 cabinet decision is the origin: incremental sales over 2019–20, six years, 20 percent for fermentation products and 10 percent for chemical synthesis, on what was then a list of 53. The current key is the latest guideline and the approval letter. Invitations listed on the Department of Pharmaceuticals schemes page show that the Department has reopened eligible products at dated intervals. An open door is a live invitation. A past invitation is an archive.

## Door 2: high-value pharmaceuticals, including a residual API category

The pharmaceuticals PLI, gazette 3 March 2021, outlay ₹15,000 crore, had 55 approved applicants in the February 2026 reply. It covers biopharmaceuticals, complex generics, patented or near-expiry drugs, autoimmune and anti-cancer drugs, in-vitro diagnostics, and key starting materials, intermediates, and APIs other than those notified under Door 1. Projects may be greenfield or brownfield. Groups in the gazette follow FY 2019–20 global manufacturing revenue, with an MSME sub-group in the smallest band. This is the door for a formulation or biopharma expansion that is not on the critical bulk-drug list.

## Door 3: a plot in one of three parks

The bulk-drug park scheme offers common infrastructure, not a sales incentive. Approved locations in the February 2026 reply are Jambusar (Bharuch, Gujarat, GIDC), Haroli (Una, Himachal Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh Bulk Drug Park Infrastructure Ltd.), and Nakkapalli (Anakapalli, Andhra Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh Bulk Drug Infrastructure Corporation). The central grant is capped at ₹1,000 crore per park. The opportunity is a lower private bill for effluent treatment, solvent recovery, steam, water, and testing, once those facilities exist. The reply’s construction language is the availability check. Thirteen states applied. These three were approved.

## Door 4: a biotechnology park for pilot scale

The Department of Biotechnology’s National Biotechnology Parks Scheme note describes parks as places for scientists and small firms to incubate technology, run demonstration and pilot plants, and move leads toward commercial development. The note lists seven parks or centres the Department had supported: Lucknow; Hyderabad; TICEL in Chennai; the Golden Jubilee Biotech Park for Women in Chennai; Guwahati; Cochin; and Bengaluru. The note’s count is “so far,” without a date on the face of the text. Treat the seven names as the scheme note’s list, and check the latest DBT annual report before calling the list complete. This door is pilot scale and state partnership. It is not a 2,000-acre bulk-drug estate.

## Door 5: a BIRAC incubator, or a BioE3 theme

BIRAC is the Department of Biotechnology’s industry organisation. Its public map of bio-incubators is the address list for bench-stage companies. BioE3, approved by the Cabinet on 24 August 2024, adds a policy frame for biomanufacturing and bio-AI hubs and biofoundries, across bio-based chemicals and enzymes, smart proteins and functional foods, precision biotherapeutics, climate-resilient agriculture, carbon capture, and marine and space research. A precision-biotherapeutic startup is in this door. It is not, by that fact, an applicant for the bulk-drug PLI.

## Door 6: research and industry upgrading

The schemes index lists the Scheme for Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma MedTech Sector, with a gazette notification dated 1 October 2025 and separate guidelines. It also lists the Strengthening of Pharmaceuticals Industry scheme, including technology-upgradation assistance and assistance for common facilities. Those are doors for research programmes and for existing plants that need to modernise, including toward the manufacturing-practice standard in revised Schedule M. They are not renamings of PLI.

Pick one door, then open its guideline. The policy difference between the first two doors is written out in [the two-scheme explainer](/blog/pharma-and-biotech-policy-shift-explainer-mnre-gov-20260330-154).
