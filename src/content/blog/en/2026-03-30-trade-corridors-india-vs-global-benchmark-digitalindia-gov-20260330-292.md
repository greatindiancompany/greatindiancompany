## The benchmark India has named

India's official logistics benchmark is the World Bank Logistics Performance Index, and the target attached to it is domestic policy. On 16 September 2025, DPIIT said the National Logistics Policy aims to lift India's rank into the top 25 by 2030, and that the Logistics Ease Across Different States exercise had supported India's rise to 38th on that index. The release states the rank and the target. It does not reprint the World Bank's pillar scores on this page. A comparison that invents customs, infrastructure, or timeliness scores is going past the document.

The same policy note says the cost goal is to bring logistics costs to global benchmarks. DPIIT's work with NCAER, started in 2024, was described in September 2025 as being in its final stages. On 3 January 2025 the government had already unveiled an NCAER logistics-cost framework, a hybrid method using export-import and domestic cargo data, guided by a task force that included NITI Aayog, the transport ministries, and MoSPI. The framework is a method. These two releases do not publish a finished cost-to-GDP figure. Until that study is out, a brief cannot honestly quote one.

## Ports, in the government's words

The Sagarmala note of 27 March 2025 says nine Indian ports rank among the world's top 100, with Visakhapatnam in the top 20 container ports. It also says Indian ports outperform many advanced maritime nations on key metrics. The note does not name the ranking publisher in that paragraph, so the usable fact is the government's claim as printed, dated 27 March 2025, alongside the programme facts in the same note: a 7,500 km coastline and 14,500 km of potentially navigable waterways.

Coastal shipping and inland waterways are the domestic leg of an export route. The note says coastal shipping grew 118 percent in a decade and inland-waterway cargo rose 700 percent. The Union Budget for 2026-27, as summarised on 1 February 2026, proposes to raise the share of inland waterways and coastal shipping from 6 percent to 12 percent by 2047. That pair is a benchmark of modal share over time, with a base and a target year, both from official statements.

## IMEC is a route design, not a rank

The India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor is India's named westward supplement to existing sea routes. The memorandum of understanding published by the Press Information Bureau in September 2023 commits Saudi Arabia, the European Union, India, the United Arab Emirates, France, Germany, Italy, and the United States to work together on two corridors: an eastern corridor from India to the Arabian Gulf, and a northern corridor from the Arabian Gulf to Europe. The text describes a railway that, on completion, would provide a ship-to-rail network beside existing maritime and road routes, among India, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Israel, and Europe, with electricity and digital cable and a pipe for clean hydrogen along the rail route.

Participants say they intend the corridor to increase efficiencies, reduce costs, and lower greenhouse gas emissions. On 16 April 2025 the Commerce and Industry Minister put numbers on that intention: logistics costs down by up to 30 percent and transportation time down by 40 percent. Those percentages are the minister's statement at a New Delhi roundtable. They are not an operating timetable, and the memorandum itself is a commitment to arrange the elements, including initial consultations.

## How to compare without a fake league table

Use three official yardsticks and label each one. Rank 38, with a policy target of the top 25 by 2030, is the index comparison DPIIT cites. Nine ports in a stated global top 100 is the maritime sentence Sagarmala uses. IMEC is a proposed ship-to-rail geography with an expected time and cost effect stated by the commerce minister. Dedicated freight corridors inside India are the operating rail asset against which any future IMEC landside handoff in India would have to connect. They are not themselves a World Bank score.
