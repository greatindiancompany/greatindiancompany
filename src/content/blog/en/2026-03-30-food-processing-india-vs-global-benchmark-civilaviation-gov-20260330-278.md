# Food Processing In India: Benchmark The Institution, Not A Fake Gap

## What this comparison refuses

A common slide says India processes a smaller share of farm output than peer countries. This brief does not use that slide. No processing rate, no tonnage gap, and no rupee gap is stated here. A benchmark that cannot point to a current official table is a slogan. The comparison that can be made from public Indian pages is institutional: what a plant must be, what a cold chain is supposed to connect, and what an export consignment must clear.

## The operating benchmark is a licence plus a standard

FSSAI, under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, is the body that sets science-based standards and regulates manufacture, storage, distribution, sale, and import. Section 31 requires a licence to carry on a food business, with registration for petty businesses where regulations specify that route. FoSTaC, FSSAI's training programme, defines a food safety supervisor as a person the food business appoints who holds a valid certificate, and it ties that role to Schedule 4 of the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011. That supervisor is supposed to train handlers on site at least quarterly and keep the record.

A global buyer asking whether the plant is "export ready" is often asking this question in different words: is there a standard, a licence, and a person accountable for hygiene on the floor. India has written that question into statute and into Schedule 4. The benchmark is whether the plant meets it, not whether a blog quotes a national processing ratio.

## The infrastructure benchmark is an unbroken chain

MoFPI's cold-chain component is written as infrastructure from the farm gate onward, so produce is not lost between harvest and sale. Cluster infrastructure and processing-capacity schemes sit beside it. Operation Greens names a perishable set, beginning with tomato, onion, and potato and described on the PMKSY page as widened to 22 products. Peer countries also struggle with the break between harvest and the truck. The Indian benchmark available here is whether the project is an integrated chain or a standalone room. It is not a claim that India is ahead or behind on loss.

## The export benchmark is a schedule and a pack house

APEDA's product list is the export benchmark for the goods it covers: fruits and vegetables and their products, meat, poultry, dairy, bakery and confectionery, honey and jaggery, cocoa and chocolate, beverages, cereals, specified nuts, pickles, papads and chutneys, guar gum, floriculture, de-oiled rice bran, green pepper in brine, and cashew, with basmati rice in the Second Schedule. Recognized pack houses are how horticulture consignments are supposed to meet quality and quarantine practice. Organic exports are tied to the National Programme for Organic Production, for which APEDA says it functions as the secretariat.

Products outside that schedule, including marine products that PLISFPI names as a manufacturing segment, need their own export authority. Comparing "Indian food exports" to a single global index mixes those regimes.
