# India's farm ranks and its processed-food exports

India's place in world food is often stated as a production rank. The ministry's own annual report uses a 2021 snapshot from Agriculture Statistics at a Glance, 2023. Those ranks describe the raw pile. They do not describe how much of that pile is processed, and the ministry does not publish, in that chapter, a single "percent of output processed" that can be lined up against another country.

## Where the 2021 production table puts India

Table 2.5 of the Annual Report for 2024-25, sourced to Agriculture Statistics at a Glance, 2023, gives India's share of world output in 2021 and the countries ahead of it:

- Milk, 210.19 million tonnes, 22.99 percent of the world total, first.
- Pulses, 23.32 million tonnes, 25.88 percent, first.
- Dry onion, 26.09 million tonnes, 24.95 percent, first.
- Vegetables and melons, 135.29 million tonnes, 11.88 percent, second after China.
- Fruit excluding melons, 106.97 million tonnes, 11.89 percent, second after China.
- Rice as paddy, 186.50 million tonnes, 24.25 percent, second after China. Wheat, 107.86 million tonnes, 14.25 percent, also second after China.
- Total cereals, 342.11 million tonnes, 11.38 percent, third after China and the United States.
- Sugarcane, second after Brazil. Tea, second after China. Meat, 4.52 million tonnes, 3.30 percent, fifth.

A processor reading this table can see a deep domestic raw-material base in milk, pulses, onion, fruit, and vegetables. The same table shows meat as a much smaller world share. A plant strategy copied from a meat-exporting country does not follow from India's milk rank.

The year on the table is 2021. Later production years exist in other government releases. This brief does not swap in a newer unofficial rank.

## Processed exports are a second, smaller story

Being first or second in the field does not fix the export basket. The Year End Review of 9 January 2026 says processed food's share of India's agri-food exports rose from 13.7 percent in 2014-15 to 20.4 percent in 2024-25. Four-fifths of agri-food exports, on that measure, are still outside the processed share.

APEDA's 2024-25 administrative report puts processed value-added products at US$8,222 million, up from US$6,014 million in 2021-22, a rise the report states as 36.72 percent over that span. Their share of APEDA's own product exports was 28.67 percent in 2024-25, after 30.06 percent in 2023-24 and 24.27 percent in 2021-22. The share dipped in the latest year even as the dollar value rose, because the wider APEDA basket grew as well (US$28,682 million in 2024-25).

Inside the processed table, gains are uneven. From 2021-22 to 2024-25 the report shows cocoa products rising from US$154 million to US$296 million, pulses from US$380 million to US$855 million, and processed vegetables from US$535 million to US$897 million. Cashew falls from US$454 million to US$339 million, and milled products from US$306 million to US$192 million. A "processed food is up" sentence hides a cashew decline and a cocoa rise.

The report names the United States, the UAE, the United Kingdom, Indonesia, and Malaysia among destinations that drove 2024-25 growth relative to 2023-24. That is a buyer list for this basket, not a ranking of India's processing industry against the European Union or China.

## What a fair comparison would still need

A global benchmark of processing would need a shared definition: farm-gate tonnes entering a factory, value added per tonne, or processed exports per unit of production. The documents above do not supply that ratio for India against a peer. The NCCD cold-chain gap study of August 2015, which the annual report still summarises, estimated large shortfalls in pack-houses, reefer trucks, and ripening chambers, and the ministry notes that the study left out milk, meat, marine products, and processed foods. A decade-old gap study is a poor stand-in for a 2026 country ranking.

The comparison that can be made today is narrower and more useful. India is a top producer of the crops and of milk that feed a processing industry. Its processed share of agri-food exports has risen over a decade. APEDA's processed value-added exports are larger in dollars than in 2021-22, with product lines moving in opposite directions. Claims that India "processes only a small global share" need a denominator the ministry has actually published. Until that denominator is in the open, the production table and the export table should be kept apart.
