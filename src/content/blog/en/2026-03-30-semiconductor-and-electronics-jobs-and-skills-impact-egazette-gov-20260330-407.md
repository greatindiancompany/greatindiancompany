India's semiconductor jobs numbers come from different releases, count different things, and move between dates. Adding them produces a headcount no official document has published. This brief lines the figures up by date and by what they count. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## Factory jobs are expectations, and the latest one carries a caveat

The number to cite for the whole manufacturing book is in the [Lok Sabha reply of 22 July 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf). The 12 approved projects are expected to generate around 32,000 direct jobs, depending on the level of factory automation. The caveat is the point. A more automated plant produces fewer direct jobs than the approval-day estimate. The reply does not publish a split by state or by plant, and it does not add indirect jobs.

Earlier releases published plant-level expectations that should stay attached to their own date:

- On [29 February 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2010132), the three units approved that day — Dholera, Morigaon, and the CG Power plant at Sanand — were expected to generate 20,000 direct advanced-technology jobs and about 60,000 indirect jobs. That sentence covers three plants, not twelve, and it includes an indirect figure the later parliamentary reply does not repeat for the full set.
- On [12 August 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2155459), four projects were expected to employ 2,034 skilled professionals, plus indirect jobs the release does not number.
- On [5 May 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2258116), Crystal Matrix and Suchi Semicon together were expected to employ 2,230 skilled professionals.

Twenty thousand plus 2,034 plus 2,230 is not the parliamentary 32,000, and it leaves out Micron, Kaynes, and the HCL-Foxconn unit, which were approved on other days and did not receive a jobs figure in the texts used here. The clean method is to quote the 22 July figure for the full book, and to quote a plant-level figure only when speaking about that approval.

The [7 February 2026 budget note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) adds year-ahead projections for 2026-27: 1,500 persons under the semiconductor-fab scheme, 3,000 under the compound and packaging scheme, and 200 design jobs under a target of 30 design companies. Those are projections for one financial year of support. They are not a revision of the 32,000.

## Electronics employment is a wider industry

The same Lok Sabha reply says industry estimates that electronics manufacturing provides employment to nearly 25 lakh people. The sentence is an industry estimate, and it covers electronics manufacturing, which on the reply's own production table is dominated by mobile phones. It is not a semiconductor-plant census. Scheme expectations sit between the two scales. PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware, at its [17 May 2023 approval](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1924766), expected 75,000 incremental direct jobs. The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme, in the [26 April 2025 note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620), envisaged 91,600 additional direct jobs over its tenure, with part of the incentive tied to employment thresholds. Both are design features of those schemes. Neither has been restated here as a count of people already hired.

## Training counts moved during 2026, so the date is part of the figure

Design-tool training and factory training are different programmes. Inside design training, the counters still change.

The [15 July 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) says 315 universities are training students on chip design with current tools, and that around 68,000 students have been trained. The 5 May 2026 release had counted design-infrastructure support at 315 academic institutions and 104 startups. The July note's design paragraph counts 105 startups and MSMEs with tool access. Use the July pair when the sentence is about Semicon 2.0's talent pillar, and keep the one-startup difference visible if both dates are in the same note.

Parliament on 22 July 2026 separates two tool programmes. Under the design scheme, tool use by the 105 startups and MSMEs had exceeded 87 lakh hours. Under Chips to Startup, tools had been provided to 320 organisations and use had exceeded 240 lakh hours. The February budget note, speaking as of January 2026, had reported about 2.25 crore tool hours, about 67,000 students, Chips to Startup access across 397 universities and startups, and a SMART Lab count above 62,000 engineers trained against a target of one lakh. The July parliamentary reply says the SMART Lab at NIELIT Calicut has already trained more than one lakh candidates. The later reply is the one to use for a July 2026 status line. The February figures explain why a speech drafted in the budget session will not match a speech drafted after Parliament's July answer. The notes do not define the counters in the same sentence, so the hours should not be forced into a single total.

Two narrower training records are stable enough to quote as they stand. The July reply says a Lam Research and Indian Institute of Science programme on SEMulator3D had trained more than 3,950 students and more than 110 faculty. The February note describes a separate Lam Research nanofabrication programme aimed at 60,000 people over ten years. Those are a completed-to-date count and a ten-year aim. The reply also says MeitY has approved a semiconductor assembly and ESD training laboratory at NIELIT Guwahati for the packaging plants coming up in the North-East.

The February note's academic fabrication line is a skills outcome with a place attached: 56 chips fabricated at 180 nm at the Semiconductor Laboratory in Mohali, from designs at more than 46 universities. That is an R&D and teaching fab result. It is not employment at the Mohali discrete-device expansion approved for CDIL, and it is not a job at Dholera.

## What an operator can take from the jobs record

A plant announcement can carry the jobs sentence from its own approval, with the date. A national claim should use the 22 July wording, including the automation caveat. A training claim should name the programme — design-linked tools, Chips to Startup, SMART Lab, the Lam and IISc course, or the Guwahati assembly lab — because the headcounts are not interchangeable. Design employment inside the incentive scheme is small relative to factory expectations: the budget note's 2026-27 projection is 200 design jobs against a target of 30 companies, and the per-application design support is capped in the [scheme guidelines](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Guidelines-for-Design-Linked-Incentive-DLI-Scheme-30.12.2022.pdf).
