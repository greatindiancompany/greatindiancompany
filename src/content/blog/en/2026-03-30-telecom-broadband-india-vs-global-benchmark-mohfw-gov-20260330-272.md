# What India Officially Claims, and What a Broadband Ranking Hides

A cross-country broadband ranking is only as good as the definition underneath it. Indian official text does make global claims. TRAI's own footnotes show why those claims and a "broadband subscribers per capita" league table answer different questions. This brief stays inside Press Information Bureau and TRAI wording. It does not add an ITU rank, an OECD average, or a comparison country that those pages do not calculate.

## Claims the government has published

PIB's BharatNet note of 21 December 2024 says India had seen the fastest rollout of 5G services in the world, with 4,62,084 5G base transceiver stations deployed across 779 districts, as of October 2024. The same paragraph gives 24,96,644 4G base stations across 783 districts, a fall in data cost from Rs 269 per GB in March 2014 to Rs 9.08 per GB, a rise in median mobile broadband speed from 1.30 Mbps in March 2014 to 95.67 Mbps, and 4G mobile coverage in 6,15,836 of 6,44,131 villages. The note calls BharatNet the world's largest rural broadband connectivity programme. It does not print the other countries' base-station counts beside the "fastest" sentence. A reader can quote the claim and still owe the comparator.

PIB's 12 February 2026 parliamentary note repeats a long-run penetration story in the minister's words: mobile subscribers from 930 million in 2014 to 1.2 billion, mobile penetration from 75 percent to 92 percent, internet subscribers from 250 million to over 1 billion, internet penetration from 20 percent to nearly 71.8 percent, and broadband subscribers from 61 million to over 1 billion. Average fixed broadband speed is given as about 61.55 Mbps. The written statement in that release attributes the 61.55 Mbps national average to Ookla's Speedtest index as of December 2025. The fixed-speed target under National Broadband Mission 2.0 is 100 Mbps by 2030. Mobile median speed and fixed average speed are different series. Putting 95.67 Mbps and 61.55 Mbps in one "India is faster" sentence mixes them.

The same 2026 reply calls the Amended BharatNet Programme the largest government-led connectivity programme globally and attaches a $16.9 billion label. The December 2024 note states the programme cost as Rs 1,39,579 crore. Use the rupee figure for the outlay and the PIB sentence for the global characterisation.

## What TRAI's definition does to a ranking

At end-July 2026 TRAI counted 1,094.48 million broadband subscribers. Of those, 1,027.50 million were mobile wireless. Fixed wired access was 48.14 million. A country that counts only fixed lines will look "behind" on a subscriber chart and may be ahead on a fibre-to-the-home chart. TRAI also computes tele-density with population projections from the 2020 technical-group report, and it includes machine-to-machine connections in the headline tele-density. Overall tele-density was 94.68 percent. Urban tele-density was 154.12 percent. Rural tele-density was 61.05 percent. Wireline tele-density was 3.36 percent, and rural wireline tele-density was 0.57 percent.

Delhi licensed service area tele-density was 368.33 percent at end-July, and Bihar's was 64.19 percent. TRAI's note warns that Delhi's telephone data includes wireless subscribers served from Ghaziabad, Noida, Gurugram, and Faridabad. A global chart that treats Delhi as a city-state comparable to a country is using the Indian series against its own footnote.

TRAI's March 2026 quarter put internet subscribers per 100 population at 76.59, urban at 126.80, and rural at 48.31. The February 2026 mission statement uses 47.16 rural internet subscribers per 100 as the baseline toward a 2030 target of 60. Those are the official domestic benchmarks. They are the right comparison until a primary Indian source publishes a like-for-like foreign series.
