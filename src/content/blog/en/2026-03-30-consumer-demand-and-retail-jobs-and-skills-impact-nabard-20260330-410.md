# Who works in India’s trade, and on what footing

Retail employment is not a single official headcount. Two National Statistics Office surveys bracket it, and they bracket it differently.

The Periodic Labour Force Survey for January–December 2025 classifies usual-status workers by NIC 2008. The broad group “trade, hotel and restaurant” is sections G and I together: wholesale and retail trade, plus accommodation and food service. In Statement 8 of the PLFS 2025 annual report, that group was 12.9% of all workers. It was 8.6% of rural workers and 23.8% of urban workers. Among men, 15.9% worked in the group. Among women, 7.3% did. Those are shares of each group of workers, not the gender split inside trade.

Calling 12.9% “the retail workforce” adds hotels and restaurants and drops the distinction between a kirana, a wholesaler, and a commercial kitchen. The report’s Appendix tables go to NIC section and division for readers who need a tighter cut. The survey design also changed in 2025, with the year moving to a January–December cycle, so a comparison with an older July–June round needs the report’s own note on that break.

## The unincorporated shop, which is where most of the enterprises are

The Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises 2025, reference period January–December 2025, covers proprietary and partnership enterprises and similar non-corporate units in manufacturing, trade, and other services. It leaves out limited liability partnerships and companies. Within that universe, other retail trade accounted for 26.49% of establishments and 27.55% of workers. The previous round, October 2023–September 2024, had put the same activity at 27.07% of establishments and 27.46% of workers. The two reference periods are not a clean twelve-month gap.

The wider unincorporated sector employed about 12.81 crore workers in the 2025 round. Trade’s gross value added, at current prices, grew 16.77% between the two rounds, faster than the sector’s 10.87% GVA growth. More than half of trading establishments used the internet for the business. Only 37.50% of unincorporated establishments were registered, up from 37.20%.

The skills implication is in that mix. Urban labour-force data show trade, hotels, and restaurants as a large urban employer. The enterprise survey shows retail as the single biggest activity inside the unincorporated sector, with a registration rate still under two-fifths for the sector as a whole and a clear turn toward internet use in trade. A hiring plan built only on organised-chain headcount is a plan for the slice ASUSE does not cover.
