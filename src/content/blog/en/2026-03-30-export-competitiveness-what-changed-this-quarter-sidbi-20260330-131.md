# What changed in India’s merchandise and services trade through August 2026

The quarter a reader is standing in on 24 September 2026 is July–September, the second quarter of 2026–27. It is not closed. Three official prints already cover part of it, and they do not measure the same thing.

## The July narrative, published on 13 August

The Ministry of Commerce and Industry’s [13 August 2026 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298878) is the last full monthly narrative used here. For July 2026 it estimated combined merchandise and services exports at US$ 80.14 billion, up 13.31 percent from July 2025, and combined imports at US$ 95.16 billion, up 15.83 percent. The combined trade balance was minus US$ 15.03 billion, against minus US$ 11.43 billion a year earlier.

Merchandise exports were US$ 44.24 billion, against US$ 36.98 billion. Merchandise imports were US$ 76.22 billion, against US$ 64.86 billion. For April–July 2026–27, merchandise exports were US$ 173.78 billion, up 17.04 percent from US$ 148.48 billion, and the merchandise trade deficit was US$ 118.60 billion, against US$ 96.66 billion.

The same release said the latest Reserve Bank services data then available ran only through June 2026, so the July services line was an estimation: exports US$ 35.89 billion and imports US$ 18.94 billion. That sentence is the change that matters. A later RBI print replaced the estimate.

## What RBI later printed for July services

On 31 August 2026 the Reserve Bank published provisional services trade for July. Exports were US$ 38,253 million, up 13.4 percent from a year earlier, and imports were US$ 20,606 million, up 19.1 percent. April, May, and June were also marked provisional: exports of US$ 37,021 million, US$ 33,355 million, and US$ 36,371 million.

Adding those four provisional months gives about US$ 145.0 billion of services exports for April–July. The Commerce estimate for the same span was US$ 142.64 billion, because it had used US$ 35.89 billion for July. The gap is the estimation, not a new export boom between the two releases.

## The August merchandise quick estimate

The Department of Commerce statistics division, citing DGCIS quick estimates, posted country and commodity annexes dated 15 September 2026. In the [country export table](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/sep/doc_9879_20260915_19571501.pdf), values are in US$ million. India’s merchandise exports were 43,811 in August 2026, against 34,737 in August 2025, a rise of 26.1 percent. July in that same table is 44,243, which matches the US$ 44.24 billion in the 13 August narrative. August was 1.0 percent below July.

April–August 2026–27 merchandise exports were 215,912, against 183,212 a year earlier, printed as 17.8 percent growth. Do not add the 13 August April–July total to this August month and expect the annex cumulative. The vintages are not the same document.

The companion [import table](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/sep/doc_9878_20260915_19565401.pdf) puts India’s total merchandise imports at 70,670 in August 2026, against 61,956 in August 2025, up 14.1 percent. July in that table is 76,223, in line with the US$ 76.22 billion narrative figure. The gap between the two August totals is 26,859 million dollars. That difference is arithmetic from the two annexes. It is not a separate headline copied from a commentary.

Inside those imports, petroleum, crude and products were 16,688 in August 2026, up 25.8 percent, and gold was 2,298, down 57.7 percent. Export competitiveness and the merchandise gap are different questions. A lower gold import bill narrows the gap without making a factory more competitive.

## What a founder should take from this quarter so far

Merchandise exports in the August annex are higher than a year earlier and slightly lower than July. The services line that still deserves to be quoted for July is the RBI provisional print, not the Commerce estimate that the ministry itself flagged. April–June remains a balance-of-payments quarter, published later, and should not be forced into this monthly quick estimate.

The credible next read is the September quick estimate when the Department of Commerce releases it, set beside the RBI services note for the same month.
