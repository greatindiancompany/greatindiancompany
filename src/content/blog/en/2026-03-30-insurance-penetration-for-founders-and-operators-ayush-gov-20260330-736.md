# What an Insurance Operator Can Build Under the Current Indian Rules

An operator entering Indian insurance is not entering an empty market, and is not entering a market whose depth ratio is rising. The [IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620) puts penetration at 3.7 percent of GDP, with life at 2.7 percent and non-life at 1 percent. The [PIB note of 23 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) puts the same year's premium at ₹11.93 lakh crore across 74 insurers, and says life is still about 74 percent of that premium. The practical question is which official door is actually open.

## Distribution is a registration problem and a churn problem

PIB says the 2025 amendment introduces one-time registration for insurance intermediaries, so a channel does not have to re-register to keep serving customers. The same note says the share-transfer approval threshold rises from 1 percent to 5 percent, which matters if the operating company itself changes owners.

The workforce an operator plugs into is already large and, in life, restless. Life insurers had 31.23 lakh individual agents on 31 March 2025, after appointing 11.15 lakh and terminating 8.87 lakh in the year. Stand-alone health agency grew faster, up 18.66 percent to 15.49 lakh. A new distribution layer has to assume replacement, not a stable roster.

Bima Sugam is not a private marketplace to copy as the market itself. IRDAI's [March 2024 press note](https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/366347/Press+Note+on+Regulatory+Revamp.pdf/9ad7286a-b0a7-ea19-d51c-4c942bad43e1?t=1711121865505&version=3.0) defines it as a digital public infrastructure and a one-stop place where insurers may make products available and provide policy servicing, including claims. The build that fits the rule is a service that works on that marketplace, or a product that can be sold and serviced there. It is not a second national exchange.

## Product freedom comes with contract duties

The product regulations are where IRDAI says it wants higher penetration: faster product response, and stronger rules on surrender values. On health contracts, PIB records the duties a retail health product has to carry. The moratorium is 60 months, cut from 8 years in 2024. The free-look period is 30 days for a term of one year or more. Renewal is guaranteed except for fraud or misrepresentation. Customers can move insurer and keep waiting-period credit. The offer has to span ages, regions, occupations, and systems of medicine. From 22 September 2025, individual life and individual health policies, including family floaters, are exempt from GST. PIB says the removal of that 18 percent GST lowers the premium citizens pay.

Rural business is not optional colour for a general or life insurer. The annual report sets Gram Panchayat cover obligations, and records that FY 2024-25 compliance relied in part on chief executive self-certification because Gram Sachiv certificates were hard to obtain. An operating plan that has no panchayat method is incomplete, even if the first customers are in Tier I, where most offices already are.

## Capital is more open than the ratio

The FDI cap in Indian insurance companies is 100 percent under the 2025 Act, up from 74 percent. That changes the cap table. It does not change the 3.7 percent. Foreign reinsurers face a net owned fund requirement PIB states at ₹1,000 crore, reduced from ₹5,000 crore. Penalty exposure for non-compliance is higher: the ceiling moves from ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore, and IRDAI can order disgorgement.
