## Enter where the government has already written a role for a firm

The opportunity on India's trade corridors is not a blank map of future lines. It is the set of instruments that already invite private capital, state equity, or a startup, next to track and ports that are operating. Each instrument has an owner.

## On the railway

The Gati Shakti Multi-Modal Cargo Terminal policy is the direct private entry to rail freight handling. Location follows industry demand, cargo potential, railway infrastructure, and the logistics potential of the area. By 7 August 2026, 142 terminals had been commissioned, about ₹10,000 crore of private investment had been mobilised, estimated capacity was 224 million tonnes a year, and 2025-26 throughput was 146 million tonnes. The March 2025 reply had put commissioned terminals at 97 and in-principle approvals at 277, so the approval pipe was larger than the commissioned stock. An operator reads both: a large approval list then, a higher commissioned count now.

Wagon schemes are the second rail entry. The August 2026 reply says about 275 rakes of special-purpose wagons and 397 rakes of general-purpose wagons were operational, including wagons for cement, oil, steel, and fly ash, plus about 54 industry-owned rakes for automobiles. That is a way to own rolling stock on someone else's track, including the dedicated corridors once a path is available.

The dedicated corridors themselves are public infrastructure, now commissioned on both the Eastern (Ludhiana–Sonnagar) and Western (Dadri–JNPT) alignments. The commercial opening they create, in the ministry's words, is double-stack container movement, higher axle loads, faster northern-hinterland access from western ports, and terminals linked to industry along the corridor. A firm does not bid to own that railway. It bids to feed it.

## Parks, ports, and the coast

Multimodal logistics parks are a partnership entry. The 15 February 2022 road-ministry note named the pattern: Assam as an equity partner at Jogighopa, JNPT as a partner at Nagpur's Sindi site, Chennai Port and Tamil Nadu's SIPCOT at Mappedu, and Karnataka's KIADB at Bengaluru, inside a list of 35 parks under Bharatmala Phase I. DPIIT's logistics policy asked for framework guidelines so parks could take private investment, and for the parks to be mapped on PM GatiShakti. The September 2025 note says customs clearance, cold storage, and packaging are the services parks are meant to combine. A park opportunity is a site plus a state or port partner, not a generic "35 parks" slide.

Sagarmala prefers public-private partnerships where a port project can carry them, uses port bodies' own resources, and uses Sagarmala Development Company Limited — set up in August 2016 — to take equity in project companies of ministries, states, ports, and state maritime boards. Grant-in-aid is reserved for high-social, lower-return work such as fishing harbours, coastal skills, and Ro-Ro jetties. The Sagarmala Startup Innovation Initiative, launched 19 March 2025, is the startup door: green shipping, smart ports, maritime logistics, shipbuilding technology, and coastal work, with funding, mentorship, and industry partnerships.

Coastal incentives already on the books — a 40 percent discount on vessel and cargo charges at major ports, priority berthing, bunker GST cut from 18 percent to 5 percent, and green channel clearance — change the economics of a coastal leg. The Budget 2026-27 summary adds a proposed Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme aimed at a shift from rail and road. The scheme is still a proposal in that text. The charge discount is described as a step taken.

## Data and state status

The Unified Logistics Interface Platform is the integration entry: more than 30 systems and over 160 crore transactions as of August 2025. The Logistics Data Bank is the visibility entry: more than 75 million export-import containers across 101 inland container depots. A software firm that can sit on those interfaces is inside the official stack. DPIIT also runs LEAPS awards for startups and MSMEs; the 2024 list included a logistics-technology startup and an MSME logistics provider, which shows the categories the ministry is willing to recognise.

Nineteen states had granted industry status to logistics by 16 September 2025. That status is the local entry condition for tax treatment and incentives. Nine policies were still draft. The first diligence item for a park or warehouse is whether the state has notified the policy and the industry status, not the national vision statement.
