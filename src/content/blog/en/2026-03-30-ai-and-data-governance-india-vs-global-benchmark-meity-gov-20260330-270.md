## A benchmark needs three names

A comparison of AI and data governance in India with the rest of the world is usable only when it names the dimension, the comparator, and the source line. "India is ahead" or "India is behind" without those three names is a slogan. This brief sets the test. The India side of the test starts at the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology site, [meity.gov.in](https://www.meity.gov.in/).

## Dimensions you can try to fill from MeitY

Use dimensions a MeitY page can actually speak to:

- Whether a public instrument exists on the topic you care about.
- Whether that instrument is final, in consultation, or descriptive.
- Which office the page names.
- Whether the page mentions implementation support, standards, or timelines.

Fill a cell only with a short note and the page title. An empty cell means MeitY's public site, as linked here, does not show that dimension. Empty is a result.

## The comparator column

This file does not attach a foreign statute, a regulator's page from another country, or a scored index. You cannot complete a global benchmark from the links on this page alone. Add a comparator only by opening that other country's primary page yourself and citing it in your own note. Until you do, the comparator column stays blank on purpose.

## Alvarez & Marsal insights

The second link is the [Alvarez & Marsal insights](https://www.alvarezandmarsal.com/insights) index. It can point you to commentary if a specific article compares jurisdictions and you open that article. The index is not a benchmark table, and this brief does not assign India a rank.

## How to publish a comparison later

When you do have both columns, show:

1. The dimension.
2. The MeitY page.
3. The comparator's primary page.
4. The difference in plain language, limited to what those two pages say.

That is a benchmark. A single Indian ministry homepage plus a consulting index is the start of the India column only.
