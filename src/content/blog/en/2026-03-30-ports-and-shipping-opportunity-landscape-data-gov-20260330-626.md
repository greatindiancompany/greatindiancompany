# Opportunity landscape across ports, coastal cargo, and shipyards

"Maritime opportunity" is five businesses with five buyers. A coastal operator, a repair yard, a recycling plot, a factory that wants waterfront, and a software firm on the port community system do not bid for the same contract. Sagarmala's pillars are a map of those businesses. They are not a single market.

## Coastal cargo, including the river

Coastal shipping is the business the shipping division describes as able to move large parcels and take load off road and rail. The commodities the Sagarmala page names are coal, cement, iron and steel, foodgrain, fertiliser, and petroleum. The buyer is a cargo owner with a regular parcel and two Indian ports, or a port and a river terminal, that can take a ship. The infrastructure the programme says is still being built is the dedicated coastal berth, bunkering, and the hinterland link at the far end.

Inland waterways are the neighbouring business, not the same one. Inland and Coastal Shipping Limited, a subsidiary of the Shipping Corporation incorporated on 29 September 2016, runs cargo craft and ro-ro vessels on national waterways under arrangements with the Inland Waterways Authority. The Jal Vahak scheme, announced on 15 December 2024, pays cargo owners a 35 percent incentive to use those waterways and is implemented by that subsidiary. A coastal exim feeder and a Ganga sailing are different licences, different ships, and different incentives.

## Repair, build, and recycle

Ship repair is the service the shipping division wants located in clusters and inside ports, so a ship does not leave India for a dry dock. The buyer is a ship owner with a survey date. The asset is a dock or a shiplift, which is exactly what the Shipbuilding Development Scheme's brownfield assistance, 25 percent of critical infrastructure in the 27 December 2025 note, is written to co-fund.

Newbuilding is a different sale. The assistance scheme pays 15 to 25 percent of an eligible vessel, against milestones, through 31 March 2036. Public demand is supposed to be bunched by the ministry's Shipping Technical Advisory Committee. The private buyer still has to prefer an Indian yard on price after that aid. The shipping division counts 51 private yards among 61 nationwide. Most of the opportunity, if orders come, is in that private majority, not only in the eight central public yards.

Recycling is concentrated. Nearly 98 percent of Indian ship recycling is at Alang-Sosiya, with 153 plots and 115 described as Hong Kong Convention compliant. The scheme note adds a credit equal to 40 percent of scrap value for an owner who scraps in India and builds again. The buyer is a ship owner at end of life. The operator is a plot holder who can meet the convention. Those are not shipyard customers.

## Industry that sits on waterfront, and the digital layer

The Ports Wing has a policy for awarding waterfront and associated land to port-dependent industry. Sagarmala names Kandla, Paradip, Ennore, and Visakhapatnam as places where land and deep water already meet. The opportunity is a plant whose logistics bill falls because the raw material arrives by sea. It is not a bet on port throughput in the abstract. The landlord is a major port authority, or a state maritime board if the site is a non-major port. The contract form follows that landlord.

The digital opportunity is narrower and real. The Indian Ports Association runs the Port Community System and the National Logistics Portal Marine, called Sagar Setu, plus an enterprise business system for the major ports. A vendor here sells integration, not steel. The user is a port, a line, a transporter, or a customs-facing process. Cruise Bharat and lighthouse tourism, also on the association and Sagarmala pages, are a visitor business. They should be sold to tourism boards and terminal operators, not presented as cargo growth.
