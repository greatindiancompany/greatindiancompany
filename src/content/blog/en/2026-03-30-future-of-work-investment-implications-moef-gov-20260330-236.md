# Future of Work in India: Investment Implications of the Labour Codes

## A cost input, and a market-size input

An investment memo on India's future of work can use the labour codes as a cost and compliance input. Workforce size has to come from a cited survey table or from the company's own rolls. This page supplies the cost design and leaves the size out.

## Aggregator contribution

For a business that is an aggregator under the Code on Social Security, 2020, the Ministry of Labour and Employment's note of 21 November 2025 states a contribution of 1 to 2 percent of annual turnover, capped at 5 percent of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers. Whether a firm is an aggregator, and how the cap is computed, are legal questions. They change contribution cost. They do not state how many workers the platform engages.

A Lok Sabha reply on 20 July 2026 says the gig and platform definitions exist so that schemes can be framed for life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity benefits, and old-age protection. Diligence asks which scheme is notified and which line is still a code provision.

## Gratuity timing under fixed-term hiring

The ministry's note says fixed-term employees receive the same benefits as permanent workers, and gratuity after one year rather than five. A firm that moves from layered contracting to fixed-term direct hire changes when gratuity accrues. The note presents that design as support for direct hiring. Conversion volumes stay with the company.

## Filing pattern and ESIC scope

The same note describes a move toward a single registration, a single pan-India licence, and a single return for the safety and conditions requirements it covers. It also describes ESIC as voluntary for establishments with fewer than 10 employees, and mandatory where even one employee works in a hazardous process. Those are coverage and process rules for a multi-state employer.

Central Rules were notified on 8 May 2026, the 20 July 2026 reply says, while states and Union Territories frame their own rules because labour is concurrent. A model that uses one national compliance cost is early if the state rulebook is still open for that establishment.

## Cash timing in IT and ITES

For IT and ITES, the ministry's note says salary is released by the 7th. That is a working-capital timing item for services firms and captive centres. Deal flow, billing rates, and headcount sit in commercial disclosures.

## Leave this out of the memo

Leave out a gig-economy projection and any bridge from the codes to a national jobs-created figure. When Parliament asked on 20 July 2026 for formal jobs created versus jobs lost in manufacturing and MSMEs, the tabled answer described legal provisions and withheld the count.
