# What labour law and payroll data change for allocating capital

Capital allocation in staffing firms, platforms, contract manufacturing, and IT services now has a few labour items that can be written as formulas. The formulas come from the Code on Social Security, 2020, from the state Acts worked through below, and from the way the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation counts members. Other states have legislated too. Bihar's 2025 Act starts on a notified date, and Telangana's Act 21 of 2026 was brought into force on 2 June 2026. A model that stops at two states is already incomplete. They do not come from a market-size slide.

## The central contribution, once a date is notified

Section 114(4) of the Code on Social Security applies to aggregators in the Seventh Schedule. The schedule names ride sharing, food and grocery delivery, logistics, e-marketplaces for wholesale or retail goods and services, professional services, healthcare, travel and hospitality, content and media, and any other goods and services platform.

The contribution is a rate the Centre notifies, not less than 1 percent and not more than 2 percent of annual turnover. Annual turnover in this subsection excludes tax, levy, and cess paid or payable to the Centre. A proviso caps the contribution at 5 percent of the amount paid or payable by the aggregator to gig and platform workers.

The shape of the formula matters more than picking the midpoint. A platform whose payouts to workers are a large share of turnover will often meet the 5 percent-of-payout cap before a 2 percent-of-turnover charge would. A platform with a high take rate and a thinner payout can hit the turnover rate first. Either way, the cash does not start on the day the code took effect. Section 114(5) says the commencement date of the aggregator contribution is notified separately. Until that notification exists, putting the full 1–2 percent into a current-year cash-flow model is a choice, not a reading of a demand that has already been raised.

Schemes under section 114 can also be funded by the Centre, by states, by beneficiaries, and from corporate social responsibility funds. The aggregator line is one source, not the whole design.

## State fees sit on top, and one of them is designed to count toward the Centre

Karnataka's Platform Based Gig Workers (Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2025 (Act 72 of 2025) received the Governor's assent on 11 September 2025 and is deemed to have come into force on 30 May 2025. It charges a welfare fee of not less than 1 percent and not more than 5 percent of the payout to the gig worker in each transaction. The state notifies the percentage, including different rates or caps by category, and the Act required that notification within six months of commencement. By September 2026 the right document to underwrite is the notification, not the statutory band.

The Karnataka Act then says the welfare fee collected counts as the contribution payable under section 114(4) of the central code, with a yearly reconciliation if the two amounts differ. For a platform whose workers are in Karnataka, the investment question is the reconciliation, not a reflexive assumption that the state fee and the central levy are both paid in full on the same rupee.

Rajasthan's Platform Based Gig Workers (Registration and Welfare) Act, 2023 (Act 29 of 2023) received assent on 12 September 2023. Its fee is a percentage of the value of each transaction related to a platform-based gig worker, excluding tax, at a rate the state notifies. The Act comes into force on a date the state notifies in the Official Gazette. Delay interest, once payment is due, is written into the Act at 12 percent a year. The sections of the Rajasthan Act that set the fee do not contain Karnataka's sentence treating the state fee as the section 114(4) contribution. A book that uses one India-wide "gig tax" line for both states is not reading the statutes.

## Fixed-term hiring changes the cost of a short contract

The Ministry's 21 November 2025 note says fixed-term employees are to receive benefits equal to permanent workers, including leave, medical care, and social security, at equal wages, and that gratuity eligibility arrives after one year of continuous service rather than five. For a project-staffed business — a construction package, an export order, a plant shutdown — the old arithmetic that treated a one-year or two-year contract as free of gratuity no longer matches the Ministry's description of the code. Price the contract on that basis, and then confirm the specific provision against the commencement notification and the transition rule. The same note says that during the transition, relevant provisions of the existing Acts and rules continue until they are replaced.

Appointment letters become mandatory for all workers in the Ministry's account, and workers above 40 are to receive a free annual health check. Those are operating costs, small per person and material across a large contractor base.

## What the payroll print is worth in a model

EPFO's provisional June 2025 payroll showed a net addition of 21.89 lakh members, the highest since the series began in April 2018, with about 10.62 lakh new subscribers. Net addition equals new members minus exits plus rejoins, inside covered establishments, and the file is revised in later months. It is a fair coincident read on formal covered employment. It is a poor total-addressable-market input for a business whose workers are outside the EPF net. NITI Aayog's estimate of 77 lakh gig workers in 2020–21, and its extension to 2.35 crore by 2029–30, is a 2022 modelled scenario. It is not a revenue line.

The allocation implication is narrow and usable. Underwrite the statutory formula, the state overlay, and the one-year gratuity. Treat EPFO as a formal-employment tape with revisions. Leave the 2029–30 gig headcount out of the valuation until a later official estimate replaces it.
