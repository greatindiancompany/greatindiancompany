# An Operator's Map Of India's Energy Transition

Founders in India's energy transition are not choosing a slogan. They are choosing a buyer, a point of connection, and a cash cycle. The physical system underneath those choices is a coal-heavy energy mix with fast renewable additions, a grid that is still catching up in places, and state distribution companies whose finances differ sharply.

## Pick the buyer before the technology

Utility auctions, commercial open access, rooftop and institutional solar, and agricultural feeder programmes are different businesses. Utility bids are a volume game with a long construction tail and a single offtaker. Open access is a relationship with industrial load, state surcharges, and scheduling discipline under Ministry of Power rules. Rooftop and scheme-linked solar follow Ministry of New and Renewable Energy programme design, including approved module lists and installer obligations. Agricultural solarisation lives or dies on feeder work with the local utility.

A product that fits one of those buyers often fails in the next. Operators should write the buyer on the first page of the plan.

## The grid interface is part of the product

In a renewable-heavy midday, energy that cannot be forecast, scheduled, or stored is worth less to the system. Hybrid plants, storage add-ons, and better forecasting are operating features, not marketing. So is the unglamorous work of protection settings, reactive power, and a bay that exists on the ground. Coal plants still cover much of the evening. A startup selling "firm green power" has to say which hours are contracted and which hours are an aspiration.

## Cash follows the utility, not the ribbon-cutting

Construction capital comes back only when the plant is commissioned and bills are paid. Distribution payment cycles have been a standing risk in Indian power. Metering, subsidy timing, and state regulator orders change that cycle by buyer. A collection period copied from a pitch deck is not a national fact. Read it from the utility's recent bills.

Equipment rules move as well. The approved list of solar modules has narrowed and widened over time. A procurement plan copied from an older bid can be non-compliant. Check the list that applies to the tender in hand.

## What to staff

Development, commissioning, and operations are different crews. Utility-scale needs land and transmission people. Distributed solar needs installers and utility coordination in many districts. Grid and storage products need protection and market-operations skills that module installation does not teach. Hire for the buyer and the hour of day you serve. Leave national capacity totals to the published series. They are context, not a revenue forecast.
