India publishes three different counts that people often quote as if they were one. They answer three questions: how far the formal system has moved on RBI’s composite index, how large the Jan Dhan stock is on a stated date, and what share of adults say they own, use, save in, or borrow from an account.

## The index

RBI’s Financial Inclusion Index is a single value from 0 to 100. The 17 August 2021 release set the weights: Access 35 percent, Usage 45 percent, Quality 20 percent. There is no base year. The end-March prints in RBI’s own releases are:

| End-March | FI-Index |
| --- | --- |
| 2017 | 43.4 |
| 2021 | 53.9 |
| 2022 | 56.4 |
| 2023 | 60.1 |
| 2024 | 64.2 |
| 2025 | 67.0 |
| 2026 | 70.0 |

The 2017 and 2021 figures were published together when the index was introduced. Each later row is the annual July (or, for 2023, September) press note, which also gives the previous year. The [March 2026 note](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63164), issued on 16 July 2026, puts the index at 70.0 against 67.0 a year earlier. Those short notes do not reprint the three sub-index levels. A separate window is in the [long-term reading](/blog/financial-inclusion-long-term-trend-20260330-047).

## The Jan Dhan stock

The [PMJDY country progress report](https://www.pmjdy.gov.in/account-statistics-country.aspx) is a bank-submitted stock, and the date is part of the number. As on 25 March 2026 the table shows 57.93 crore beneficiaries, of whom 45.27 crore were at rural or semi-urban centre bank branches and 12.66 crore at urban or metro centre branches. Women beneficiaries were 32.29 crore. Deposits were ₹3,05,244.71 crore. RuPay cards issued were 40.27 crore.

Public sector banks held 44.80 crore of those accounts, regional rural banks 10.90 crore, private sector banks 2.04 crore, and rural cooperative banks 0.19 crore. Dividing the published deposit stock by the published account count gives about ₹5,270 per account. That division is arithmetic on the table, not a separate average published by the Department of Financial Services, and it hides the spread across banks.

The PMJDY homepage also shows a live counter. Use the dated progress report when you cite a stock. A state table with a different “as on” date will not match this national total; the [state page](/blog/financial-inclusion-regional-view-20260330-067) uses the 11 March 2026 report on purpose.

## The survey gap

Table B.1 of the World Bank’s [Global Findex Database 2025](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/042c3272-9a73-43f3-b117-985482acd7f8/content) reports economy-level results for 2024. For India the table shows 89 percent of adults with an account, 48 percent who made or received a digital payment, 27 percent who saved formally, and 15 percent who borrowed formally. The same table shows 66 percent owning a mobile phone and 46 percent using the internet in the previous three months.

The barriers chapter of that report describes India’s account-ownership rate as 90 percent. Treat 89 as the appendix figure and 90 as the chapter’s rounding. Either way, formal borrowing sits far below account ownership. That 15 percent is a household survey result. It is not an MSME credit outstanding figure, which is why the [small-firm brief](/blog/financial-inclusion-impact-on-smes-20260330-027) refuses to treat Jan Dhan as a credit market.

Great Indian Company files each series on its own page so a quoted “inclusion number” can be traced to the release that printed it.
