# From the 1997 charter to a mobile service window

The mid-term story of India's citizen services is a sequence of institutions that stayed in place while new rails were added. Charters did not disappear when apps arrived. Cash transfers did not replace certificates. The trajectory is accumulation.

## Standards and complaints, before the apps

DARPG's [citizen charter page](https://darpg.gov.in/citizen-charter) dates the start of charter-writing to the Conference of Chief Ministers on 24 May 1997. The required contents were standards of service, time limits, and a grievance avenue, beginning with high-contact sectors such as Railways, Telecom, Posts, and the public distribution system. A consolidated charter site, goicharters.nic.in, followed on 31 May 2002. DARPG's own charter was reviewed again in February 2019. The promise to publish a time limit is almost thirty years old.

The grievance system acquired a common pipe later. DARPG's [public grievances page](https://www.darpg.gov.in/en/public-grievances) says the Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System has evolved since 2007, forwarding online and postal complaints to central ministries and to state governments. The live portal still describes a registration number, status tracking, and an appeal when the citizen rates the closure "Poor." Sevottam, DARPG's excellence framework, bundles the charter, the redress mechanism, and capability-building as three modules of one quality system. The apps did not retire that bundle.

## Cash, then a payment rule

Direct Benefit Transfer started on 1 January 2013, according to the [DBT Mission](https://dbtbharat.gov.in/static-page-content/spagecont?id=1). The Mission sat in the Planning Commission, moved to the Department of Expenditure in July 2013, and moved to the Cabinet Secretariat on 14 September 2015. The electronic payment framework of 13 and 19 February 2015 told ministries to push cash components to individual beneficiaries electronically. Jan Dhan accounts, Aadhaar, and mobile connections are the enablers the Mission names. By the [6 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635), the public description is 318 schemes of 56 ministries and over Rs 52 lakh crore transferred. The trajectory is from a 2013 reform to a Cabinet Secretariat programme with a published scheme count, not from a pilot to an unnamed app.

## Digital India as the service umbrella

PIB's 3 August 2022 reply dates the Digital India programme to MeitY and describes it as an umbrella across central and state projects. That reply already listed Common Service Centres, UMANG, e-District, and DigiLocker as service-delivery initiatives. Its counts are a baseline: more than 1,570 UMANG services, 4,671 e-District services in 709 districts, and DigiLocker at more than 11.7 crore users, more than 532 crore documents, and 2,167 issuers. A [12 August 2026 PIB note](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/aug/doc2026812952501.pdf) places UMANG's start in 2017 at 166 services and 4.6 crore transactions, and records 2,585 services and over 798 crore transactions as of 23 July 2026. DigiLocker, which a March 2026 PIB research note says launched in 2015, is recorded in the 12 August note at over 72 crore users and more than 934 crore documents as of 31 July 2026.

Read that as growth the notes themselves publish, with the 2022 and 2026 sentences kept distinct. e-District's 709-district figure has not been restated in those 2026 notes. The state portal remains the workflow. UMANG remains the window. DigiLocker remains the issuer wallet.

## The assessment catches up

NeSDA began in 2019 as DARPG's biennial study. After the 2021 round, DARPG's way-forward page describes a push to saturate mandatory e-services and to strengthen state portals and service portals. On 25 May 2026 DARPG launched the NeSDA 2025 portal. The [28 May 2026 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266244) proposes 59 mandatory services for every state and Union Territory. The trajectory's latest step is a scorecard for end-to-end online delivery, applied to a federal map the 1997 charter never had to digitise.

This path does not support a 2030 user forecast. The next documented step is the assessment cycle already underway.
