# The rules a village must meet before it is certified

Rural water and sanitation in India are governed less by a single licence and more by a sequence of declarations. A household can be on a register, a department can report that water is flowing, and a Gram Sabha can still withhold certification. JJM 2.0 adds a maintenance test to that sequence. Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) adds a third-party check for its highest village class. This brief stays with those published rules. It does not invent a drinking-water contaminant limit.

## Drinking water: entry, report, certificate, then maintenance

The [Jal Jeevan Mission dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx) publishes the three-stage meaning of Har Ghar Jal.

The household is entered only after the name and Aadhaar number of the head of household are verified. A village at 100 percent on the information system has cleared that clerical and identity test. It has not, by that fact alone, been certified.

The state water-supply department then reports that taps supply every household, school, and anganwadi centre in the unit. That is an engineering department's confirmation.

Certification is a Gram Sabha resolution, passed after the department has given the Gram Panchayat a certificate and the sabha has checked the claim.

On 10 March 2026 the Cabinet added a condition the dashboard definition, by itself, did not spell out in those words. A Gram Panchayat certifies completion and declares itself Har Ghar Jal only when adequate in-village operation and maintenance has been established by the state. The formal handover to the Gram Panchayat and the Village Water and Sanitation Committee is Jal Arpan. An annual community review, Jal Utsav, is meant to keep that maintenance visible. Water-quality surveillance is named as part of the same service-delivery shift. The note does not, in the text used here, publish the parameter list or the laboratory standard that surveillance must meet.

Separate memoranda with each state and Union Territory are the instrument that is supposed to bind timelines, sustainability, and service. A national guideline without that memorandum is not, on the Cabinet's own description, the whole compliance file.

## Money follows the scheme

The 6 August 2026 note makes scheme identity part of the control system. SNA SPARSH releases funds against individual water-supply schemes and is meant to leave an audit trail of expenditure. A works bill that cannot be tied to a Sujal Gaon or Service Area ID will sit outside the rail the mission now describes as the accountability mechanism. Geo-tagging on PM Gati Shakti is listed alongside that ID.

This is a compliance change for state accountants as much as for engineers. The reimbursement of ₹5,289.09 crore to ten states was for schemes already approved and already spent. The ₹865.87 crore released through SNA SPARSH is the pattern JJM 2.0 says will be normal: pay the scheme, not a lump.

## Sanitation: self-declaration, then a timed verification

The 8 December 2025 PIB note on Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) states the ODF Plus rule in operational language. A village that has met the criteria declares itself at a Gram Sabha. The district must ensure third-party verification within 90 days of a village's first ODF Plus declaration. That mandatory third-party verification applies to ODF Plus Model villages. Supervisory checks may still be done for Aspiring, Rising, and Model villages by block, district, and state officers.

The class rules sit underneath the timer. Aspiring is one waste stream, solid or liquid. Rising is both. Model is both, plus visual cleanliness. A village cannot be verified as Model on the strength of a toilet count from 2019.

Urban sewerage under AMRUT 2.0 is a different regulator in practice: the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, the state, and the urban local body, with projects inside an approved State Water Action Plan. Independent review and monitoring agencies engaged by that ministry review grounded projects and send observations back to the state. That is a project-monitoring rule, described in the ministry's [6 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3022_tT0QXq.pdf), not a village Gram Sabha rule.

The texts are the [JJM dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx), the [Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2237548), the [SNA SPARSH note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295509), and the [8 December 2025 SBM-G note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2200341).
