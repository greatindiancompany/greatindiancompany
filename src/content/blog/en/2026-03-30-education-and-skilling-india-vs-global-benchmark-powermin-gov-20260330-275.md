## Use the policy’s own comparison, with its date

NEP 2020 is explicit about the international yardstick it wants readers to use for vocational education. Paragraph 16.1 says the 12th Five-Year Plan (2012–2017) estimated that less than 5% of the Indian workforce aged 19–24 had received formal vocational education. The same paragraph says the comparable shares were 52% in the United States, 75% in Germany, and 96% in South Korea.

Those percentages are the Plan estimates printed in the 2020 policy. This brief does not replace them with a 2026 survey. Treating them as this year’s labour-market fact would move the date.

The policy uses the gap to argue for speed, and it gives reasons that are institutional rather than a new ranking. Vocational courses had been concentrated in Grades 11–12 and among students who left from Grade 8 upward. Students who finished school with a vocational subject often had no clear path into higher education. The National Skills Qualifications Framework, announced in 2013, is the instrument the policy names as the start of vertical mobility between vocational and general qualifications.

## The global goals the policy attaches itself to

The introduction of NEP 2020 says the global agenda is Sustainable Development Goal 4 of the 2030 Agenda, adopted by India in 2015: inclusive and equitable quality education and lifelong learning for all by 2030. The vocational chapter says the aim of exposing at least 50% of school and higher-education learners to vocational education by 2025 is aligned with Goal 4.4. This page does not report a national 2025 exposure rate.

A second domestic benchmark is higher-education participation. The policy starts from a 26.3% gross enrolment ratio in 2018, including vocational education, and aims at 50% by 2035. A Lok Sabha reply on 10 August 2026 reported a ratio of 30 for 2023-24. The distance that remains is 20 percentage points on the policy’s own scale, over the years still left before 2035. That comparison is India against its stated target, not India against an OECD table published this quarter.

## What a careful benchmark excludes

School grade-wise ratios in NEP section 3.1 (90.9%, 79.3%, and 56.5% for Grades 6–8, 9–10, and 11–12) are also internal to the 2020 text. They are not a current UNESCO league table. Public spending “around 4.43% of GDP,” set against a 6% aim, is likewise the figure the policy records, not a fresh cross-country budget ranking.
