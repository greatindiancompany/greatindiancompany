# A Dated Snapshot of State Capacity and State Finance

This page is a snapshot, not a forecast. Every figure is tied to the document that printed it. Where two documents disagree, both dates are kept. Nothing here is a Great Indian Company estimate.

## State finance

From the Reserve Bank's press release of 23 January 2026, *State Finances: A Study of Budgets of 2025-26*:

- Consolidated gross fiscal deficit: 3.3 percent of GDP in 2024-25, after three years below 3.0 percent. Budgeted at 3.3 percent of GDP for 2025-26.
- The Bank says the deficit above 3 percent mainly reflects 50-year interest-free loans under Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment, over and above the normal net borrowing ceiling.
- Capital expenditure: 2.7 percent of GDP in 2023-24 and in 2024-25. Budgeted at 3.2 percent of GDP in 2025-26.
- Consolidated outstanding liabilities: budget estimate of 29.2 percent of GDP at end-March 2026.

From the overview of the previous edition, *State Finances: A Study of Budgets of 2024-25*:

- Average consolidated gross fiscal deficit fell from 4.3 percent of GDP in 1998-99 to 2003-04 to 2.7 percent in 2004-05 to 2023-24.
- Debt fell from 31.8 percent of GDP at end-March 2004 to 28.5 percent at end-March 2024.
- The FRBM Review Committee (2017) recommended 20 percent. The 28.5 percent stock was still well above that mark.

From PIB's 1 February 2021 summary of the Fifteenth Finance Commission, for the 2021-26 award:

- Vertical devolution: 41 percent of the divisible pool, about one percentage point below the Fourteenth Commission's 42 percent, adjusted for the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
- States' share: Rs 42.2 lakh crore over 2021-26.
- Local government grants: Rs 4,36,361 crore, including Rs 2,36,805 crore rural, Rs 1,21,055 crore urban, and Rs 70,051 crore in health grants through local governments.
- Horizontal weights: income distance 45, population 15, area 15, demographic performance 12.5, forest and ecology 10, tax and fiscal effort 2.5.

From the Department of Expenditure guidelines dated 27 March 2026:

- SASCI 2026-27 allocation: Rs 2,00,000 crore, as 50-year interest-free loans, above the net borrowing ceiling.
- Part I untied: Rs 75,000 crore, of which Rs 67,000 crore is for states in proportion to Sixteenth Finance Commission tax shares, and Rs 3,000 crore is for Union Territories with legislatures.
- Part II: Rs 10,000 crore for the state share of specified central infrastructure and infrastructure schemes.
- Part III: Rs 25,000 crore for own-resource capital-expenditure growth above 10 percent. SASCI releases are excluded from that growth.
- Part IV: Rs 4,000 crore for public-finance IT, generally Rs 50 crore a component. Reform reports to the CAG by 15 December 2026.
- First installment of untied funds: 66 percent. Second: 34 percent, after 75 percent utilization and closure of Centrally Sponsored Scheme SNA accounts.

## e-service delivery

From PIB, 22 January 2026, on the December 2025 NeSDA Way Forward report. Uploads as of 31 December 2025:

- 24,090 e-services across states and UTs.
- 8,656 in local governance and utility services.
- 156 e-services added in November 2025.
- 59 mandatory e-services used as the saturation list. The note says 21 states and UTs were above 90 percent and prints a name list a reader should take from the release itself.
- Seven unified portals at 100 percent of services: Sewa Setu (Assam), e-UNNAT (Jammu and Kashmir), Seva Sindhu (Karnataka), Odisha One, MP e-Service, Apuni Sarkar (Uttarakhand), e-Sevanam (Kerala).

From PIB, 10 December 2025, for April 2023 to October 2025:

- E-services rose from 11,614 to 23,919.
- Mandatory-service saturation rose from 66 percent to 80 percent.
- No API integration, at that date, between CPGRAMS and state Right to Service systems.

From PIB, 28 May 2026, on the NeSDA 2025 portal launched 25 May 2026:

- 59 mandatory services proposed for every state and UT.
- 43 services proposed for central ministries and departments.
- The framework is DARPG's federal adaptation of the UN e-Government Survey's Online Service Index.

## Civil service and grievances

From PIB, 11 March 2026, figures as on 6 March 2026:

- More than 1.51 crore iGOT users.
- Over 4,400 courses. More than 7.7 crore completions.
- MoUs with 29 states and Union Territories.
- District-level monitoring of learning performance is not undertaken on iGOT.
- DoPT's scheme note still describes the platform as built to train about 2.0 crore employees.

From PIB, 29 July 2026, CPGRAMS report for June 2026, central ministries and departments:

- 1,96,133 cases received. 1,99,968 redressed. 78,793 pending.
- Average disposal time for 2026: 14 days.
- Fiftieth monthly report. Forty-eighth consecutive month with disposal above one lakh.
- 32,269 appeals received in June. 21,753 appeals pending.
- 83,544 new users in June. 6,262 grievances registered through Common Service Centres. The portal is available at more than five lakh CSCs.

## How to hold the snapshot

Use the January 2026 RBI release for the latest consolidated deficit, capex and liability ratios. Use the March 2026 guideline for how 2026-27 capital loans will actually be released. Use the 22 January 2026 NeSDA note for the service stock, and the 28 May 2026 note for the assessment that was just opening. Use 6 March 2026 for the size of the civil-service learning platform, and June 2026 for the central grievance clock. A sentence that mixes these dates without saying so is no longer this snapshot.
