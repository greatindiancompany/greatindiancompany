# The open doors on India's digital public rails

An opportunity, in this setting, is a door the official design has already cut. It is not a market-size estimate. India's digital public infrastructure leaves several doors open to firms and public agencies that are willing to take a defined role. The roles are different, the gates are different, and none of them is "rebuild UPI" or "become the national identity provider."

## Payments: be a participant, not a second network

UPI is the shared payment rail. NPCI's product statistics for August 2026 show 752 banks live and 24,508.96 million transactions. The government account of 29 July 2026 says the system serves more than 55.49 crore individuals and 6.5 crore merchants. The opportunity consistent with those facts is at the edge: a bank that is not yet in the 752, a merchant that is not yet in the 6.5 crore, or a workflow that uses a transfer the rail can already make. The product statistics do not show a vacant national switch waiting for a new company to operate it.

The same edge logic applies to adjacent public payment utilities only where a primary page defines them. This brief does not invent a gap in a product NPCI has not described here.

## Financial information: three seats, and most builders want one of them

The Reserve Bank's Account Aggregator directions of 28 November 2025 create a market structure with named seats.

The Account Aggregator seat is a licensed NBFC. It requires a company, a certificate of registration, net owned funds of at least ₹2 crore, and a board-approved pricing policy. It may retrieve specified financial information and present it to the customer or to a financial information user. It may not transact, may not keep the information, and may not outsource the aggregation. For most product companies this seat is the wrong opportunity. It is a narrow utility.

The financial information user seat is the one a lender, insurer, or other regulated firm can use. The user must be registered with a financial-sector regulator. The information that can move includes bank deposits, mutual fund units, equities, insurance policies, pension balances, and GST returns in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, among the items listed in the directions. The opportunity is to replace a document chase with a consented pull, inside a business the regulator already recognises.

The financial information provider seat is the bank, NBFC, fund, depository, insurer, or GSTN side: answer the pull accurately. The directions say that if the aggregator's statement and the provider's books disagree, the provider's position stands. Building a reliable response to consent requests is an operations opportunity for institutions that already hold the accounts.

Sahamati's July 2026 dashboard is the activity picture around those seats: 326.30 million cumulative accounts linked, 538.32 million cumulative consents fulfilled, and 343.78 million data deliveries in that month alone. Fulfilled consents mean data was delivered, on Sahamati's definition. The opportunity is in that repeated use, not in a one-time linking campaign.

## Documents, signatures, and government APIs

DigiLocker is the document door. As on 31 July 2026 it had more than 72.43 crore registered users and 5,437 document types or services, 4,776 of them from states. The 29 July Lok Sabha answer reported more than 936.03 crore documents accessible and over 71.66 crore users, against a disclosed allocation since inception of ₹168.58 crore. The opportunity for a department is to be an issuer whose document resolves. The opportunity for a relying party is to accept that document instead of a photocopy. The user base is already large. The long tail is the state document type that exists on the list and fails in practice.

eSign is the signature door beside it. The 29 July PIB account says 155.56 crore e-Signs had been issued by all service providers, including 44.23 crore by CDAC. A service that still asks a person to print, sign, and scan is choosing not to use a rail the government says is legally accepted in that description.

API Setu is the catalogue door. The same account says 8,904 APIs from central and state departments were published there, in support of an open API policy. Meri Pehchaan is described as the single sign-on so a person does not collect a new login for every department. UMANG is the mobile front door: 2,575 services as on 31 July 2026, more than 11.66 crore users. A department that is not among those services has a concrete onboarding task. A private firm does not get to replace UMANG. It may integrate where the API and the sign-on allow.

myScheme, in the 29 July Lok Sabha annexure, hosts over 4,750 government schemes. Discovery of eligibility is a different opportunity from delivery of the benefit. The annexure also says the National Scholarship Portal has disbursed around 8.16 crore scholarships amounting to about ₹53,300 crore through direct benefit transfer, and that direct benefit transfer nationwide has moved over ₹52 lakh crore. Those figures describe public pipes that already pay. A builder's opportunity is the last-mile experience around a pipe the state operates, not a private clone of the scholarship platform.

## Health: sandbox, then a security audit

The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission site describes a mission launched in September 2021. The ministry's reply of 24 July 2026 says applications are validated in a sandbox and undergo a web application security audit before integration. There is no central clinical repository. Exchange follows patient consent. The building blocks named on the mission site include the ABHA number, the Health Facility Registry, the Healthcare Professionals Registry, and the Unified Health Interface.

As of 20 July 2026 the stocks were 94.87 crore ABHA IDs, 5.36 lakh facilities, and 10.09 lakh professionals. The opportunity is software and operations that help a facility or a public programme join that exchange. The reply lists programmes already integrated, including PM-JAY and Nikshay, and state systems such as Gujarat's TECHO, Uttar Pradesh's eKavach, Rajasthan's pregnancy and child tracking system, and Tamil Nadu's health insurance scheme. A company that can repeat that integration for a facility still outside the 5.36 lakh is working on the open part of the map. A company that proposes a national warehouse of health records is working against the stated design.

## Commerce: network participant, not a listing on a government shop

ONDC's opportunity follows from what the Commerce Ministry says it is not. The 4 August 2026 reply says it is not a platform, an application, or a marketplace. It is a section 8 company and an open network. Buyers and sellers transact across applications. The seats are buyer-side apps, seller-side apps, logistics, and, in the domains the answer lists, mobility and financial services as well as retail and agriculture.

The July 2026 picture is over 500 million cumulative orders and about 2 lakh or more active retail merchants, roughly 60 percent small sellers. DigiDukaan, for kirana onboarding, was operating in Hyderabad and Jaipur. Public support of ₹277.35 crore runs through 2026-27 for micro and small enterprises, with a 50 percent women-owned target. A practical opportunity is to be the seller app, the logistics partner, or the field onboarding partner in places DigiDukaan has not described. It is not to "get listed on ONDC" as if a government storefront existed.

## Education: contribute to a platform states already run

DIKSHA's about page invites a wide education ecosystem, including non-government organisations, to participate on a common platform. States keep the choice of how to use it. The platform supports 36 languages. It is built on Sunbird, which the page describes as MIT-licensed open source. Use cases it names include energised textbooks, courses, quizzes, question banks, and teacher programmes such as NISHTHA. Content licensing is stated: NCERT textbooks under CC BY-NC-ND, and resources under CC BY-NC-SA. The opportunity is content and state programmes inside those licences, including material for children with special needs, which the page says is already a category. It is not an exclusive national textbook franchise.

## What is not an opportunity, on these documents

There is no published pool of "DPI market revenue" to divide. DigiLocker's disclosed allocation of ₹168.58 crore since inception is a public build cost, not a sales target. Account Aggregators cannot sit on the data and monetise it as a bureau. ONDC's order count is not a merchandise value. Aadhaar is not a dataset for sale; UIDAI's saturation report is a coverage statistic.

The doors that are open are roles: regulated information user, document issuer, API publisher, health application that clears an audit, network participant, state education programme, merchant or bank still outside the published counts. Each role has a document that says who may enter. Starting from the role is how this landscape stays real.
