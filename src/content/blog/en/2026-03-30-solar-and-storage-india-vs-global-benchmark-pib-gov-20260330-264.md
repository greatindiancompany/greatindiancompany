# How to Compare India's Solar Build Without Inventing a World Rank

A global benchmark is a comparison someone can recompute from two official series. The MNRE, SECI, and PIB pages used here publish India's own awards, commissioning figures, and scheme designs. They do not publish a table of foreign installed solar or storage on the same definition. This note refuses to fill that gap with a rank.

## What "largest" means when a press note says it

PIB release 2294208, dated 4 August 2026, calls PM Surya Ghar the world's largest rooftop solar programme and one of the fastest-growing clean energy programmes anywhere. That is the release's description of a scheme with an outlay of Rs 75,021 crore, more than 50.06 lakh beneficiary households, and 14.8 GW commissioned under the scheme. A reader can cite the sentence as what PIB wrote. A reader cannot turn it into a checked ranking against another country's household programme unless that country's statistical office is on the table too. It is not, in the documents this brief uses.

The honest domestic comparison is inside India's own papers. Cabinet release 2010133 of 29 February 2024 said the scheme would result in 30 GW of residential rooftop solar. The August 2026 release reports 14.8 GW commissioned under the scheme. One is the outcome the cabinet attached to the design. The other is commissioned capacity in that later note. The distance between them is progress against India's own scheme text, which is a benchmark a founder can actually use.

## Awarded solar against commissioned solar

SECI gives a second domestic pair, with dates that must travel with the numbers.

As of 1 May 2025, SECI's what-we-do page says it had awarded over 73.8 GW of renewable capacity, including 45.9 GW of solar. As of March 2025, SECI's solar page says it had commissioned 21.67 GW of solar. Those dates are close, and the words are not the same. Using the larger figure as "India's SECI solar" overstates what is generating. Using the smaller figure as "what SECI has contracted" understates the award book.

A third domestic pair sits in manufacturing. MNRE's PLI page sets a national outlay of Rs 24,000 crore and records awards of 8,737 MW fully integrated capacity in tranche I and 39,600 MW fully or partially integrated capacity in tranche II. That is awarded manufacturing capacity, not modules exported and not plants commissioned. Comparing it with another country's factory output would mix an award letter with a production statistic.

## Storage planning is not a world battery ranking

MNRE's energy-storage overview, last updated 21 November 2023, quotes the Central Electricity Authority's National Electricity Plan of 2023. It reports a storage requirement of 82.37 GWh in 2026-27, of which 34.72 GWh is battery storage and 47.65 GWh is pumped storage, rising to 411.4 GWh in 2031-32, of which 236.22 GWh is batteries. Those are planning requirements inside an Indian electricity plan, as restated by MNRE. They are not installed battery capacity, and they are not a comparison with another grid.

PIB release 2118325 adds a scheme size, not a world share: viability-gap support for battery projects was enlarged from 4,000 MWh to 13,200 MWh while staying inside an approved budgetary allocation of Rs 3,760 crore. A 13,200 MWh scheme next to a 236.22 GWh planning figure shows how small the funded slice is relative to the plan MNRE quotes. That ratio is an Indian policy fact. It is not a global market share.

## A comparison that survives a citation check

Use three pairs, each from Indian primary pages, and name the date.

- Scheme outcome versus later commissioning, for household rooftop: 30 GW in the February 2024 cabinet note, 14.8 GW commissioned in the August 2026 note.
- Awarded versus commissioned, for SECI solar: 45.9 GW awarded as of 1 May 2025, 21.67 GW commissioned as of March 2025.
- Planning requirement versus funded scheme, for batteries: 236.22 GWh of battery storage in 2031-32 on the MNRE page, and a VGF scheme of 13,200 MWh inside Rs 3,760 crore.

If a pitch needs India against China, the United States, or Europe, it needs those governments' series, on a stated definition. This desk will not invent the missing column.
