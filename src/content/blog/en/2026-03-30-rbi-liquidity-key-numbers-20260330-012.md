Two September publications carry the liquidity numbers that matter, and they measure different things. The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 18 September 2026 gives the corridor, the ratios and the week’s call rate. The money-market release of 21 September, for positions as on 20 September, gives the standing facilities, the reverse repo book and a stock called net durable liquidity. A Bulletin extract dated 11 September fills in the daily absorption of the first week of the month.

## The corridor on the page dated 18 September

For the week ended 11 September 2026 the supplement reports:

- Policy repo rate: 5.25 per cent
- Standing deposit facility: 5.00 per cent
- Marginal standing facility and Bank Rate: 5.50 per cent
- Fixed reverse repo rate: 3.35 per cent
- Cash reserve ratio: 3.00 per cent
- Statutory liquidity ratio: 18.00 per cent

The year-ago column, the week ended 12 September 2025, shows the repo rate at 5.50 per cent, the standing deposit facility at 5.25 per cent, the marginal standing facility at 5.75 per cent, the cash reserve ratio at 3.75 per cent, and the statutory liquidity ratio already at 18.00 per cent. The fixed reverse repo rate was 3.35 per cent then as well.

For the fortnight ended 31 August 2026 the same supplement reports a cash-deposit ratio of 3.22 per cent, a credit-deposit ratio of 80.32 per cent and an investment-deposit ratio of 26.06 per cent. The fortnight ended 5 September 2025 read 4.27, 79.43 and 28.81.

## Overnight money against the bill curve

The weighted average call rate for the week ended 11 September 2026 is 4.97 per cent. That is below the 5.00 per cent standing deposit facility printed on the same line. The primary yield on the 91-day treasury bill that week is 5.21 per cent. The 182-day yield is 5.62 per cent. The 364-day yield is 5.91 per cent. The 10-year government security par yield, from Financial Benchmarks India, is 7.05 per cent.

A year earlier, in the week ended 12 September 2025, the call rate was 5.36 per cent, the 91-day yield was 5.51 per cent, the 364-day yield was 5.67 per cent and the 10-year par yield was 6.57 per cent. Over that year the shortest bill yield fell and the one-year bill yield and the 10-year par yield rose.

Scheduled commercial banks’ published rate ranges for the week ended 11 September 2026 are a savings deposit rate of 2.50 per cent, term deposits above one year at 6.00 to 6.75 per cent, overnight MCLR at 7.80 to 8.00 per cent, and the base rate at 8.40 to 10.00 per cent.

## Absorption in the first week of September

The Bulletin extract of 11 September lists liquidity operations in crore of rupees. Outright open market sales and purchases are blank on each date from 31 August to 6 September. Three rows from that table:

- 31 August: variable rate reverse repo ₹3,84,465 crore, standing deposit facility ₹2,87,776 crore, net absorption ₹6,71,690 crore.
- 4 September: variable rate reverse repo ₹6,02,394 crore, standing deposit facility ₹2,28,290 crore, net absorption ₹8,30,561 crore.
- 6 September: variable rate reverse repo column blank, standing deposit facility ₹2,46,214 crore, net absorption ₹2,45,922 crore.

Marginal standing facility drawings on those dates are small: ₹551 crore on 31 August, ₹123 crore on 4 September, ₹292 crore on 6 September. The 4 September net absorption of ₹8,30,561 crore is the largest figure in that published window. On 5 and 6 September, the weekend, the variable rate reverse repo column is blank and net absorption drops to ₹2,64,000 crore and then ₹2,45,922 crore, with the standing deposit facility still the main balance.

A 3-day variable rate reverse repo on 18 September accepted ₹2,22,629 crore, the full amount offered, against a notified ₹2,25,000 crore. The cut-off and the weighted average rate were both 5.24 per cent.

## The stock the daily flow sits on

The money-market release for 20 September, a Sunday, shows overnight market volume at zero, which is what a Sunday print should show. The Reserve Bank lines are still filled in. That day’s standing deposit facility is ₹91,747 crore at 5.00 per cent and the marginal standing facility is ₹82 crore at 5.50 per cent. Outstanding variable rate reverse repos are ₹2,22,629 crore maturing 21 September, plus ₹52,449 crore and ₹2,39,276 crore maturing 7 October, each at a cut-off of 5.24 per cent. Net liquidity injected, outstanding operations included with that day’s operations, is an absorption of ₹6,04,964.24 crore.

The same release reports net durable liquidity as a surplus of ₹10,66,303 crore as on 31 August 2026. Scheduled commercial banks’ cash balances with the Reserve Bank on 20 September are ₹8,15,649.80 crore. The average daily cash reserve requirement for the fortnight ending 30 September is ₹8,21,989 crore. The Centre’s surplus cash balance reckoned for auction on 18 September is zero.

## Deposits, credit and money

The 11 September extract, outstanding as on 31 August 2026, reports aggregate deposits of scheduled commercial banks at ₹2,78,71,530 crore, up 17.8 per cent year on year, and bank credit at ₹2,23,87,567 crore, up 19.1 per cent. Money supply (M3) is ₹3,32,21,648 crore, up 16.7 per cent year on year. Currency with the public is ₹41,94,110 crore, down ₹13,005 crore over the fortnight and up 12.8 per cent over the year.

The durable surplus and the daily absorption are both Reserve Bank figures. They are not substitutes for each other. The ₹10,66,303 crore stock is dated 31 August. The daily net absorption in the first week of September moves from ₹8.3 lakh crore on the 4th to about ₹2.5 lakh crore on the weekend, and the 20 September release shows absorption near ₹6.0 lakh crore once outstanding operations are included.
