India's official semiconductor record is specific about nodes, partners, and product types. It does not publish a share of global wafer capacity. A comparison that invents that share is weaker than the comparison the documents already support. This brief uses the documents. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## The silicon node on the approval record

The commercial silicon fab in the approved set is Tata Electronics with Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corp of Taiwan, at Dholera. The [29 February 2024 Cabinet release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2010132) sets capacity at 50,000 wafer starts per month and names two product groups: high-performance compute chips with 28 nm technology, and power-management chips for electric vehicles, telecom, defence, automotive, consumer electronics, display, and power electronics.

On 15 July 2026 the [Semicon 2.0 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) describes the journey so far as having started with 28 nm to 110 nm, and says the first fab is scheduled to be commissioned in 2028. The research pillar then says the focus moves to more advanced nodes, with research centres in India and outside it. The commissioning date and the node range are the benchmark the Cabinet itself is using. A claim that India has an approved leading-edge logic fab, in the sense of the nodes that note still calls future work, is not what these two releases say.

Design at a finer geometry is a different fact, and the documents keep it in the design column. The [15 August 2025 PIB factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=149242&reg=48&lang=2) says India inaugurated centres for advanced 3-nanometre chip design in Noida and Bengaluru. The [7 February 2026 budget note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) says startup chips have been fabricated at advanced foundry nodes, including 12 nm, and that academic chips have been fabricated at 180 nm at the Semiconductor Laboratory in Mohali. A 3 nm design centre and a 12 nm tape-out use somebody else's fab. They do not move the Dholera approval off 28 nm, and they do not move the 2028 commissioning date.

## Packaging, compound, and display are the operating mix

As of the [22 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf), commercial production had started at three plants: Micron in assembly and test at Sanand, Kaynes Semicon at Sanand, and CG Semi, the Sanand unit approved in 2024 for CG Power with Renesas of Japan and Stars Microelectronics of Thailand. The silicon fab was still on the 2028 schedule. The benchmark this supports is sequencing. India has approved plants that package and test, and some of those have started shipping, earlier than the silicon fab.

The rest of the approved book is also easier to compare if it is named by product:

- Silicon carbide, with Clas-SiC Wafer Fab of the United Kingdom, at Bhubaneswar: 60,000 wafers a year and packaging of 96 million units, described on [12 August 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2155459) as the first commercial compound fab in the country.
- Glass-substrate advanced packaging, 3D Glass Solutions, also at Bhubaneswar: about 69,600 glass panel substrates, 50 million assembled units, and 13,200 3D heterogeneous-integration modules a year.
- Discrete devices, CDIL at Mohali, a brownfield expansion of 158.38 million units a year, in silicon and silicon carbide.
- Suchi Semicon at Surat, an OSAT for discrete semiconductors at 1,033.20 million chips a year, approved [5 May 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2258116).
- Crystal Matrix at Dholera, an integrated gallium-nitride mini and micro-LED display facility, with 72,000 square metres of panels and 24,000 sets of RGB epitaxial wafers a year. The July 2026 Cabinet note counts one integrated gallium-nitride micro-LED display fab in the set of 12. It still lists further display fabs among the categories Semicon 2.0 will seek.

That display plant is a compound-semiconductor display process. The 2021 programme also has a scheme for display fabs. The approved display project on the 2026 record is this gallium-nitride facility, together with display-module sub-assembly as a target under the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme. The releases do not describe an approved large liquid-crystal or organic light-emitting display fab.

## What official text says about the rest of the world

The 7 February 2026 PIB note says the industry is dominated by a few countries, naming Taiwan, South Korea, Japan, China, and the United States, and states that Taiwan produces over 60 percent of the world's semiconductors and nearly 90 percent of the most advanced chips. That is the global sentence in the budget backgrounder. It is not accompanied, in that note or in the Lok Sabha reply, by a matching percentage for India's wafer output. The honest benchmark is the one that can be paired: India's approved commercial silicon node is 28 nm, the first such fab is scheduled for 2028, and the same backgrounder treats the most advanced chips as concentrated in Taiwan.

Parliament's reply of 22 July 2026 records memoranda with the United States, the European Union, Japan, Singapore, and the Netherlands. It calls them an enabling platform for business-to-business supply-chain partnerships. A memorandum is not a capacity number and not a guaranteed materials contract. The technology partners already written into approvals are more concrete: PSMC in Taiwan, Renesas in Japan, Stars Microelectronics in Thailand, Clas-SiC in the United Kingdom, APACT in South Korea, and Micron.

## The electronics benchmark India does publish

On phones, the comparison is published. The 26 April 2025 PIB note calls India the world's second-largest mobile manufacturing country. The 22 July 2026 reply says about 99.2 percent of mobile phones sold in India are manufactured in the country, and that phone exports reached about ₹2.59 lakh crore in 2025-26. That is an assembly and export benchmark. It sits upstream of the chip projects. It does not convert into a wafer-share benchmark, because the phone can be assembled in India with a chip made elsewhere. The policy stack treats that difference as the reason the component scheme and the semiconductor mission exist.
