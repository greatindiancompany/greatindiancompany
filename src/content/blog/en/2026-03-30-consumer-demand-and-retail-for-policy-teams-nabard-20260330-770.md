# What policy teams should keep separate in retail demand

A policy note on “consumer demand and retail” goes wrong when it uses one series as a synonym for the others. The official record already separates them. The job is to keep that separation in the advice.

## Consumption in the accounts

MoSPI’s 31 August 2026 release is the macro line. Real private final consumption grew 7.1% in April–June 2026-27, to ₹44,74,278 crore at 2022-23 prices. Nominal PFCE was 55.6% of nominal GDP. Real GDP grew faster, at 7.8%. Real gross fixed capital formation grew faster still, at 11.9%. A claim that “demand led the quarter” has to survive that ordering. The print is provisional in the ordinary way of quarterly accounts: MoSPI says later source data revise it. The July–September estimates are scheduled for 30 November 2026.

The production-side group that contains trade grew 8.5% in real GVA. The group also contains hotels, transport, communication, and storage. It is the wrong numerator for a retail-policy target.

## Budgets, prices, and the shop

The household survey is the distributional line. In HCES 2023-24, unimputed average MPCE was ₹4,122 rural and ₹6,996 urban, and food was 47.04% of rural MPCE. Urban MPCE was about 70% higher than rural in nominal rupees. [Report 592](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/Final_Report_HCES_2023-24L.pdf) says that comparison ignores rural-urban price differences. State averages run from Sikkim at the top to Chhattisgarh at the bottom on that unimputed set. A national transfer or a national GST change does not land on one budget.

The price line is CPI. August 2026 provisional inflation was 4.82% combined, 5.23% rural, and 5.95% for food, on base 2024=100. Rural prices were the faster ones. The Reserve Bank’s 5 August resolution projected CPI inflation for 2026-27 at 5.0%, peaking in its forecast at 5.9% in the third quarter, and said demand pressures looked contained once food, fuel, and precious metals were set aside. It also flagged an uneven monsoon as a risk to rural demand. Monetary policy and retail policy are reading the same prices for different instruments.

The enterprise line is ASUSE 2025. Other retail trade was about 26% of unincorporated establishments. Registration of unincorporated establishments stood at 37.50%. Policy aimed at companies misses the majority form of the shop.

## Tax design is a third file

The 56th GST Council, in the PIB record of 3 September 2025, moved to a 5% merit rate, an 18% standard rate, and a 40% de-merit rate for a select few goods and services. Most goods changed on 22 September 2025. Specified tobacco products did not. The Council did not publish an estimate of the consumption response. Treating the recast as a measured demand stimulus writes a number the press note does not contain.

A usable policy paragraph cites the series it used, the month or quarter, and the base. PFCE for the macro share. HCES for who spends, and on food versus the rest. CPI for the price wedge, split rural and urban. ASUSE for the unincorporated shop. The Council note for the tax grid.

Those documents are the [Q1 GDP press note](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1788172583113_d65a77cf-240e-4491-82ee-59f78618fa41_Press_Note_on_GDP_Estimates_for_Q1_2026-27.pdf), the [HCES factsheet](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES%20FactSheet%202023-24.pdf), the [PIB Council note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555), and the [5 August monetary resolution](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63287).
