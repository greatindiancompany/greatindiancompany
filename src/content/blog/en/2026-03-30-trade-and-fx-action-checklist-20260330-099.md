# A reading order for India's trade and rupee releases

Open the document that matches the decision. The pages below are the ones that carried India's trade and foreign-exchange facts through 24 September 2026. Each step says what the page is for, the latest level it had settled, and the mistake it prevents. Levels will be replaced when the next release arrives. The order will not.

## 1. Today's rupee, if a conversion is the decision

Open [rbi.org.in](https://www.rbi.org.in/). Read the exchange-rate block and the timestamp. At 1:00 pm on 24 September 2026 the FBIL rates on that page were Rs 95.9099 per US dollar, Rs 109.1912 per euro, Rs 127.0295 per pound, and Rs 26.1130 per UAE dirham. The page names FBIL as the source. Since 10 July 2018, FBIL has computed the reference rate.

Use this for a live benchmark. Do not use it as the rate at which last quarter's trade was booked.

## 2. The reserve stock, if the buffer is the decision

Open the latest weekly statistical supplement. The extract dated 18 September 2026 puts reserves at US$780,782 million as on 11 September, of which foreign-currency assets were US$645,796 million and gold US$111,225 million. The week before, as on 4 September, the stock was US$785,706 million.

Read the four components and the three variations: week, since end-March, and year. The end-March variation on the 11 September stock was +US$89,674 million. A one-week decline of US$4,924 million, with gold down US$2,591 million, is a valuation-sensitive move. It is not, by itself, a statement of intervention.

## 3. The latest monthly balance of payments, if financing is the decision

Open the RBI monthly balance of payments. The 15 September 2026 release for July shows a current account deficit of US$7.0 billion, a merchandise deficit of US$31.7 billion, a services surplus of US$17.6 billion, net transfers of US$13.2 billion, and non-resident deposits of US$33.5 billion. The overall balance was +US$20.8 billion. Monetary movements of −US$20.8 billion mean reserves rose.

Check the preliminary mark before you treat the deposit cell as a run-rate. Check the sign convention printed under the table before you decide whether reserves rose or fell.

## 4. The quarterly statement, if the current-account ratio is the decision

Open [the April–June 2026 balance of payments](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_PressRelease.aspx?fn=5&prid=63493) and, beside it, the [sources of variation in reserves](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63494). The quarter's current account deficit was US$4.2 billion, 0.5 per cent of GDP. The goods deficit was US$86.1 billion. Net services receipts were US$51.6 billion. Personal transfers were US$42.9 billion. Portfolio investment was a net outflow of US$9.6 billion. Valuation was a loss of US$14.4 billion.

Use the quarterly release for GDP ratios and for the goods, services, income, and transfer split. Use the variation note for valuation versus transactions. The variation note says it uses an older format. Do not add its capital-account total to the BPM6 financial account.

## 5. The Commerce quick estimate, if the shipment is the decision

Open the latest PIB trade release from the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. The 13 August 2026 release is the model. It gives July merchandise exports of US$44.24 billion and an April–July merchandise deficit of US$118.60 billion. It also gives a services estimate, and the footnote says why: RBI services data then stopped at June.

If the footnote is still there, do not quote the services line once the RBI month in step 3 exists. July services receipts in the RBI's 31 August release were US$38,253 million.

The full-year anchor remains the 15 April 2026 release: 2025-26 merchandise exports US$441.78 billion, services exports US$418.31 billion, merchandise deficit US$333.19 billion, combined exports US$860.09 billion.

## 6. The DGCIS commodity and country tables, if a product or a buyer is the decision

Open the quick-estimate workbook, not only the PIB summary. For August 2026, provisional, merchandise exports were US$43,810.92 million and imports US$70,670.34 million. Engineering goods exports were US$12,319.81 million in the month. Readymade garments were US$1,200.73 million, down 2.74 per cent. The United States took US$8,319.99 million of August exports. The UAE took US$2,377.55 million, down 26.10 per cent on the month.

Match the row to the business. The all-India total will mislead a garment exporter and a firm shipping to the UAE.

## 7. Once a year, the software survey, if the firm sells services

The 18 September 2026 survey puts 2025-26 software exports at US$221.4 billion excluding overseas commercial presence, with the United States at 54.1 per cent and dollar invoicing at 72.6 per cent. Including foreign affiliates, the total is US$239.3 billion. This survey does not describe merchandise MSMEs. The merchandise share for MSME-related products, through 2024-25, is the separate PIB note of 11 December 2025: 48.55 per cent, up from 45.74 per cent in 2023-24.

## 8. The agreement note, if the shipment claims a preference

The 18 August 2026 PIB note on trade agreements lists 2025-26 merchandise exports to FTA partners and the certificate counts it had: 7,885 under the EFTA TEPA and 783 under the Oman CEPA. Read it for market access and origin rules. Read steps 5 and 6 for whether shipments to that partner are currently rising.

## 9. Write down the label you are about to repeat

Before a number goes into a board note, write four words beside it: the publisher, the period, the basis (customs or payments), and the status (provisional, preliminary, or estimate). "Exports were 44 billion" fails that test. "Commerce customs exports for July 2026 were US$44.24 billion, in the 13 August quick estimate" passes it.
