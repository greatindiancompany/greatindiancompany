A small firm does not bid at the variable rate reverse repo. It meets the banking system that does. The price of that meeting is in the Reserve Bank’s lending-rate footnotes, the commercial-paper print, and a credit-deposit ratio that is still high while the overnight market is in surplus. The August 2026 Governor’s statement says industrial credit has strengthened, aided by sustained credit growth in micro, small and medium enterprises and a pickup in credit to large industry. It does not publish a separate growth rate for the MSME book. What it does publish is enough to see why a liquidity surplus has not meant a steadily cheaper working-capital line.

## Fresh rates stopped passing the cut through

The statement of 5 August 2026, carried in the Bulletin dated 25 August, describes a cumulative 125 basis point reduction in the policy repo rate. From February 2025 to June 2026 the weighted average lending rate on fresh rupee loans of scheduled commercial banks fell by 80 basis points, and the rate on outstanding rupee loans fell by 91 basis points. Over the same stretch the weighted average domestic term deposit rate fell by 63 basis points on fresh deposits and by 51 basis points on outstanding deposits. The statement says transmission to fresh lending rates moderated in recent months, reflecting robust credit demand.

The previous statement, on 5 June, had carried the same cumulative exercise through April 2026: fresh lending rates down 83 basis points, outstanding lending rates down 89, fresh term deposit rates down 85, outstanding term deposit rates down 50. The later cumulative for fresh deposits, 63 basis points through June, is smaller than the 85 basis points through April. The later cumulative for fresh loans, 80 basis points, is smaller than the 83. The repo rate itself did not change between those two statements. It was 5.25 per cent at both.

For a firm rolling a cash-credit limit, the relevant fact is the fresh-rate stall, not the older stock of loans that still shows a larger cumulative decline. Outstanding loans had already repriced by 91 basis points by June. New drawing power was no longer getting cheaper at that pace.

## Market paper cheapened at the three-month point, then sat well above the repo

Larger firms that can issue paper see a different price from the overdraft. In the August statement, since the June policy the 3-month commercial paper rate averaged 7.07 per cent and the 3-month certificate of deposit averaged 6.72 per cent. Between the April and June policies those averages were 7.52 per cent and 6.67 per cent. Treasury bills at three months averaged 5.27 per cent after the June policy. The gap from the 5.25 per cent repo rate to three-month commercial paper, on those averages, is still a little under two percentage points. A firm that cannot issue paper does not receive the 7.07 per cent print. It borrows from a bank whose certificate-of-deposit funding averaged 6.72 per cent, against overnight surplus that the Bank was absorbing near the floor.

The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 18 September 2026 puts the banks’ published ranges, for the week ended 11 September, at an overnight MCLR of 7.80 to 8.00 per cent and a base rate of 8.40 to 10.00 per cent. The overnight MCLR range is the same as in the week ended 12 September 2025. The base-rate range then was 8.50 to 10.30 per cent. The policy rate over that year went from 5.50 to 5.25 per cent. The carded overnight MCLR range did not.

## Balance sheets can lend, and the deposit ratio is still tight

The Bulletin extract of 11 September 2026 reports bank credit of scheduled commercial banks at ₹2,23,87,567 crore as on 31 August, up 19.1 per cent year on year. Aggregate deposits were ₹2,78,71,530 crore, up 17.8 per cent. The supplement dated 18 September reports a credit-deposit ratio of 80.32 per cent for the fortnight ended 31 August 2026, against 79.43 a year earlier, and an investment-deposit ratio of 26.06 per cent against 28.81. Incremental ratios for that fortnight were 62.56 for credit and 18.40 for investments. The system added more credit than incremental investments, on those published ratios.

The Governor’s statement gives the regulatory liquidity buffer beside that expansion. The liquidity coverage ratio of scheduled commercial banks was 126.94 per cent at end-June 2026. The capital to risk-weighted assets ratio was 17.78 per cent in June 2026. The gross non-performing asset ratio was 1.68 per cent, against 2.22 per cent in June 2025. Net interest margin was 3.21 per cent in June 2026, against 3.26 per cent a year earlier. Non-food bank credit, on the statement’s footnote, grew 17.4 per cent year on year as on 15 July 2026, against 9.7 per cent a year before. Those are system figures. The statement describes the capital ratio as well above the regulatory minimum, the liquidity coverage ratio as robust, and the net interest margin as thinner than a year earlier, while non-food credit growth is faster than the year-ago print.

The cash reserve ratio is the share of each deposit that banks hold as a cash balance with the Reserve Bank. The notification of 6 June 2025 cut that ratio by 100 basis points in four steps, to 3.0 per cent of net demand and time liabilities from the fortnight beginning 29 November 2025. The supplement now shows 3.00 per cent, against 3.75 per cent in the week ended 12 September 2025. The Reserve Bank’s notification states the ratio and the dates. It does not, in that circular, state a rupee amount of resources freed. A lower unlent cash share is the direct consequence of the ratio. Whether a given district’s MSME book received it is not a figure the weekly supplement publishes.

The operating target remains the weighted average call rate, and the August statement says the Bank will keep liquidity sufficient to align that rate with the repo rate. In the week ended 11 September the call rate averaged 4.97 per cent, under the 5.00 per cent floor. A call rate under the floor means banks, in aggregate, were not paying up for overnight funds. It does not reset the 7.80 per cent bottom of the overnight MCLR range, and it does not publish an MSME waiver. The firm’s rate is the lending-rate survey and the card rate, read against a credit-deposit ratio of 80.32 per cent.
