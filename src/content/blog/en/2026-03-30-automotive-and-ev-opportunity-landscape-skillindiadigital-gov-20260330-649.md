# EV Segments with Open Incentive Windows After the L5 Closure

The open window is no longer "electric vehicles" as a single market. L5 three-wheelers closed on 26 December 2025. Two-wheelers were extended, at a lower rate, through 31 March 2028. Trucks, ambulances, buses, public chargers, and advanced components each have their own cap, their own certificate, and a different place on the clock.

## Two-wheelers are extended, and the rate has already stepped down

The [PM E-DRIVE portal](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/) now lists 45,79,120 electric two-wheelers and ₹2,767 crore, inside a scheme total of ₹11,900 crore. [S.O. 4424(E)](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/docs/policy_document/Gazette%20Notification%2010%2008%202026%20on%20enhancement%20of%20Outlay%2011900%20cr.pdf) sets the incentive from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2028 at ₹2,500 per kWh, capped at ₹5,000 per vehicle and at 15 percent of ex-factory price. Private and corporate registered two-wheelers qualify, as do commercial ones, if the battery is an advanced battery. The FY 2024-25 rate of ₹5,000 per kWh, capped at ₹10,000, is the closed year.

As on 22 July 2026, before this amendment, 20,56,831 two-wheelers had been reimbursed for ₹1,505.30 crore. The new vehicle cap is higher than that count. The ministry has not published how many of the extra vehicles will clear the lower cap and the ex-factory-price test. The opportunity is a longer window at a thinner per-vehicle cheque, inside a fund-limited scheme.

L5 is the segment to leave off the map. The target of 2,88,809 was reported achieved in December 2025. The July reply shows 2,60,965 L5 vehicles reimbursed for ₹769.02 crore as on 22 July 2026, and records the closure date of 26 December 2025. E-rickshaws and e-carts are a thin line beside that: a cap of 39,034 vehicles and ₹50 crore, with 5,082 reimbursed for ₹7.61 crore by 22 July 2026. Their terminal date in the March 2026 amendment ran to 31 March 2028. The August gazette's "all segments" terminal date does not reopen L5.

## Trucks, ambulances, and buses are the less-used vehicle lines

The July reply is the first full public rate card for categories that were not in the reimbursement table. Electric trucks draw the lowest of ₹5,000 per kWh, 10 percent of ex-factory price excluding the trailer, and a GVW cap. The reply prints caps of ₹2.7 lakh, ₹3.6 lakh, ₹7.8 lakh, ₹9.6 lakh, and ₹9.3 lakh across the GVW bands from above 3.5 tonnes to 55 tonnes. The portal caps eligible trucks at 5,643 and ₹500 crore, for N2 and N3 categories. The January 2026 review called the truck incentive the first time the Government of India had launched this support. A fourth phased-manufacturing amendment for N2 and N3 trucks is dated 3 September 2026. The product has to meet that schedule to be eligible.

Ambulances are capped at 3,811 vehicles and ₹500 crore. The portal sets the incentive at the lower of ₹30,000 per kWh and 35 percent of ex-factory price, for AIS 125 patient-transport, basic life support, and advanced life support vehicles. A notification for e-ambulances is dated 17 June 2026 on the ministry's list.

Buses remain the largest single vehicle line by money: 14,028 buses and ₹4,391 crore on the portal. The July reply says the incentive is ₹10,000 per kWh, capped at 20 percent of CESL-discovered cost, and further capped at ₹35 lakh, ₹25 lakh, or ₹20 lakh by length. Quantity letters covered 13,800 buses. The opportunity for an operator is a city concession that still needs a prototype approval, not a retail product.

## Components and chargers are where the certificate is the product

PLI Auto's component track pays 8 to 13 percent on other advanced-technology components and 13 to 18 percent on electric-vehicle and hydrogen fuel-cell components, after 50 percent domestic value addition. The ministry notified 103 component categories. As of 31 December 2025, ten Component Champion applicants had certificates for 37 variants. That is a short list next to 103 categories. The [January review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2214039) is the source for both numbers.

Chargers have ₹2,000 crore still in the allocation table, of which ₹689 crore was approved as on 1 July 2026. Support can cover upstream infrastructure and, in defined cases, the charger, against Bureau of Energy Efficiency benchmarks, and only if Ministry of Power guidelines and the charger's own phased-manufacturing schedule are met. The Scheme to Promote Manufacturing of Electric Passenger Cars remains a separate door: a minimum investment of ₹4,150 crore, 25 percent local content in three years and 50 percent in five, and a cap of 8,000 reduced-duty imports a year.
