# Where Land, Approvals, and the Project Account Slow a Build

Execution in Indian real estate stalls in three places the statutes already name: land title and the sanctioned plan, the time a regulator or a local body takes to say yes, and the rule that buyer money cannot fund a different project. Calling all three "red tape" hides which clock is running.

## Land and the plan come before the brochure

Section 4 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act is the application. The July 2025 [MoHUA FAQ](https://www.mohua.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/542144159b61c980253a694f478d3a5d.pdf) says the promoter files the documents and undertakings in that section, plus whatever the state rules add. Among the facts the Act is built around are the sanctioned plan, the layout, and the promoter's title in the land. A marketing team cannot lawfully get ahead of that file. Section 3 bars advertising and booking until registration.

The authority has 30 days. If the file is incomplete, the authority can ask for completion and, after a hearing, reject it. If a complete file draws silence for 30 days, registration is deemed. The bottleneck is therefore not only "the government is slow." An incomplete title or a plan that the competent authority has not sanctioned never starts the 30-day clock in a form the Act will accept.

After registration, the plan is not freely editable. Minor alterations need the competent authority and disclosure to allottees. Major alterations need two-thirds of allottees as well, with the promoter's own apartments left out of the count and one vote per allottee. A design change late in construction is an allottee-consent problem, not only a municipal one.

Occupancy and completion are different bottlenecks. Occupancy is habitability: water, sanitation, electricity. Completion is conformity with the sanctioned plan. A buyer can be waiting on one certificate while the promoter is talking about the other. States that issue a single certificate under a local name can still satisfy the Act, because both definitions say "by whatever name called." The practical task is to match the local certificate to those two tests, not to assume the name on the letterhead.

Section 16 insurance of title and of construction starts only when the appropriate government notifies it. Until that notification exists in the state, title risk is not parked in a policy the Act has already switched on.

## The project account is an execution constraint

Seventy percent of allottee receipts sit in a separate account for land cost and construction cost of that project. Withdrawals track percentage completion and require an engineer, an architect, and a chartered accountant in practice. The account is not an authority-controlled escrow. The slowdown it creates is real anyway: a promoter who has collected from buyers still cannot draw the seventy percent for a land parcel somewhere else, or for a corporate overhead the certificates will not support.

Where a development authority builds entirely from its own funds and takes nothing from buyers, the FAQ says the authority may exempt the separate account case by case. That exemption is not a general waiver for private promoters.

## PMAY has a different approval stack

Beneficiary-led and partnership projects move from the urban local body to the State Level Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee and then to the Central Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee. Central assistance on a beneficiary-led house is released 40:40:20 by direct benefit transfer against physical progress, after geo-tagging in five stages. If the beneficiary uploads the tag, the local body has 15 days before auto-approval. A house can be physically ready and still wait on a tag, a utilisation certificate, or the state's own share.

Landless households do not clear this stack until the state grants a heritable, non-transferable patta at state expense, or the family uses the partnership or interest-subsidy vertical instead. Land is the bottleneck the FAQ assigns to the state, not to the applicant's bank.

The ministry's scheme page lists reforms it will support inside a state's affordable housing policy: stamp duty and registration below 1 percent for PMAY-U 2.0 houses up to 60 square metres, extra floor-area ratio, single-window and time-bound plan approval, and relief on land-use conversion charges. Those are reforms the page says the ministry will support. They are not a certificate that every state has already granted single-window clearance. Treating the bullet as a clearance in hand is how a project schedule slips.

The ninety-day version of these clocks is in the [checklist](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-next-90-days-checklist-mca-gov-20260330-481). Great Indian Company publishes the bottleneck split so a delay review names the missing document.
