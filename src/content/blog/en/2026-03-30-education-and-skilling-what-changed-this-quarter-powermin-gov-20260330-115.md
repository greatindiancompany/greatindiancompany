The document that should replace older higher-education numbers, for anyone updating a note between July and September 2026, is the Ministry of Education release of 8 July 2026. It published the All India Survey on Higher Education (AISHE) for 2022-23 and 2023-24 together. The survey portal is [aishe.gov.in](https://aishe.gov.in/). The release is on the [Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2282525).

This is a data release, not a new policy. The National Education Policy 2020 still states the aim of lifting gross enrolment in higher education, including vocational education, from the 26.3% it recorded for 2018 to 50% by 2035. The new survey tells you how far the counted ratio has moved. It does not reset the target.

## The figures worth putting on the page

AISHE computes the gross enrolment ratio as enrolment divided by the projected population aged 18–23, using the 2011 census projection. On that definition the ratio was 23.7 in 2014-15, 29.5 in 2022-23, and 30 in 2023-24.

Female gross enrolment was 31.2 in 2023-24, up from 30.2 in 2022-23 and 22.9 in 2014-15. The gender parity index, female ratio divided by male ratio, was 1.08. The Ministry says it has stayed above 1 for seven consecutive years.

Headcount enrolment was 3.42 crore in 2014-15, 4.46 crore in 2022-23, and 4.50 crore in 2023-24, which the release describes as a 31.5% increase since 2014-15. Female enrolment was 2.24 crore in 2023-24, against 1.57 crore in 2014-15.

Scheduled Caste gross enrolment rose from 18.9 in 2014-15 to 27.8 in 2023-24. Scheduled Tribe gross enrolment rose from 13.5 to 22.8. Both remain below the all-India 30. Enrolment counts moved with them: 69.72 lakh Scheduled Caste students and 28.83 lakh Scheduled Tribe students in 2023-24, and 1.80 crore students from Other Backward Classes, up from 1.13 crore in 2014-15.

STEM enrolment was 1.02 crore in 2023-24, against 91.5 lakh in 2014-15. Women were 44% of STEM enrolment, against 38.4% in 2014-15. Total enrolment grew faster over that decade than STEM enrolment did, so a larger college system is not the same claim as a larger science-and-technology share. Faculty numbered 17.32 lakh, of whom 44.9% were women. Female faculty rose from 5.69 lakh in 2014-15 to 7.78 lakh in 2023-24.

Institutions responded in large numbers. In 2023-24, 59,533 of 64,756 registered higher-education institutions participated. The Ministry also says participation is voluntary and the returns are self-reported. Validations exist. Large-survey limits remain. Treat a one-year move from 29.5 to 30 as a small step on a long series, not as a turn in the sector.

## What this quarter did not replace

School education still rests on the UDISE+ 2024-25 report, released on 28 August 2025, not on anything dated this quarter. The [Ministry note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2161543) put the gross enrolment ratio at the middle stage at 90.3% and at the secondary stage at 68.5%, and secondary-stage retention at 47.2%. Those are NEP stage definitions, grades 6–8 and grades 9–12, and they are a year older than the higher-education release. Do not average them with the higher-education ratio of 30. The age groups and the institutions are different.

Skilling headlines that are easy to mis-date also sit outside this quarter. The Rajya Sabha reply of 1 April 2026 on [PM-SETU](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2247753) described a preparatory year and said state proposals had not yet reached the National Steering Committee. A 30 June 2026 note said more than 5.44 lakh candidates had been trained under PMKVY 4.0 in future-oriented technologies. Both are the latest official markers of their kind. Neither is a July-to-September event.

## How to update a working note

Replace any higher-education gross enrolment figure still quoted as the high 20s from an older AISHE year. Keep the denominator in the sentence: ages 18–23, 2011 projection. Keep the equity gaps in the same sentence as the national 30, because Scheduled Tribe enrolment at 22.8 is the part a state plan can still miss if it only celebrates the average.

Leave the NEP 50% aim, and the 2025 vocational-exposure aim, as policy targets rather than as descriptions of this quarter. The policy said that by 2025 at least half of learners in school and higher education should have vocational exposure. That calendar date has passed. The July release does not report that exposure rate. The Ministry of Skill Development has reported a different statistic, the share of youth aged 15–29 who are vocationally trained. Those are not interchangeable.

If a slide still pairs these education figures with a power-ministry or energy-agency link, delete the link. It was a filing error from an older template. The institutions that own these numbers are the Department of Higher Education, the Department of School Education and Literacy, and, for courses and apprenticeships, the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship.
