# Three schemes, three different kinds of support

India's food processing policy is often described as one push. The ministry runs three schemes that pay for different things, through different routes, to different sizes of firm. Mixing them up is how a small unit applies for a large-firm incentive, and how a cold-chain project is judged as if it were a micro-enterprise subsidy.

## PMKSY pays for shared and project infrastructure

Pradhan Mantri Kisan SAMPADA Yojana brought the ministry's infrastructure schemes under one umbrella on 3 May 2017. The Annual Report for 2024-25 says the original approval was Rs 6,000 crore for 2016-20, later extended through 2020-21. The point of the package, in the ministry's words, is modern infrastructure from the farm gate to the retail outlet: less waste, more processing, and a path for processed food into export markets.

For the 15th Finance Commission cycle the ministry stopped sanctioning new projects under mega food parks, creation of backward and forward linkages, and the skill-development and HACCP pieces. Those stay only for commitments already made. Components that continue for new projects are:

- Integrated cold chain and value addition, including scope for irradiation as a standalone facility
- Creation or expansion of food processing and preservation capacities
- Infrastructure for agro-processing clusters
- Food testing laboratories under food safety and quality assurance
- Human resources and institutions, limited to research and development
- Operation Greens

Mega food parks, begun in 2008, used a hub-and-spoke model: a central processing centre tied to primary processing and collection near the farm. A parliamentary reply placed on the ministry site notes that the scheme asked for at least 50 acres, which states found hard in hilly and difficult areas. That design choice is why the successor cluster and unit schemes matter more than a new park announcement.

PMKSY is a central sector scheme. Assistance is demand-driven. The ministry invites applications; it does not reserve a park for each state.

On 31 July 2025 the Cabinet raised the 15th-cycle outlay to Rs 6,520 crore, including Rs 1,000 crore aimed at 50 irradiation units and 100 NABL-accredited testing labs. That is still infrastructure money.

## PMFME formalises the unorganised unit

PM Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises is the first scheme the ministry describes as built for micro food businesses. It was launched in June 2020 under the Atmanirbhar Bharat package, with an outlay of Rs 10,000 crore, and later extended through 2025-26. The coverage target is 2,00,000 micro enterprises. The ministry's FAQ of 9 September 2025 defines a micro enterprise here as investment in plant and machinery or equipment of not more than Rs 2.5 crore and annual turnover of not more than Rs 10 crore.

The support is a credit-linked capital grant of 35 percent of eligible project cost, capped at Rs 10 lakh for an individual unit. Eligible cost is plant and machinery plus technical civil work, and that civil work must stay under 30 percent of eligible cost. Land, rent, and lease of a workshed are excluded. The subsidy is not for working capital. The applicant puts in at least 10 percent, and the rest, including the grant, is arranged as a bank loan. Common infrastructure for groups is the same 35 percent rate with a ceiling of Rs 3 crore.

Because a bank has to sanction the loan, the scheme's pace is a credit story as much as a ministry story. Expenditure is shared 60:40 between Centre and most states, 90:10 for North Eastern and Himalayan states, and fully by the Centre for Union territories without a legislature.

One District One Product is the organising idea, and the ministry has approved a product for 726 districts across 35 states and Union territories. The September 2025 FAQ is explicit that an ODOP product is not compulsory for the subsidy. Branding and marketing support is where ODOP gets priority. Seed capital, separate from the capital subsidy, goes to self-help group members engaged in food processing, up to Rs 40,000 a member and Rs 4 lakh for an SHG.

## PLI pays established manufacturers for incremental sales and brands

The Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Food Processing Industry was approved on 31 March 2021 with an outlay of Rs 10,900 crore and a life from 2021-22 to 2026-27. It is aimed at firms that already have a minimum sales base and will make a stipulated investment. The ministry's scheme page splits the support into manufacturing segments and a branding limb:

- Ready-to-cook and ready-to-eat foods, including millet products
- Processed fruits and vegetables
- Marine products
- Mozzarella cheese
- Innovative or organic products from smaller firms in those segments, including free-range eggs, poultry meat, and egg products
- Grants for in-store branding, shelf-space rental, and marketing abroad

A millet-based products component was later carved out of savings under the same scheme. Applicants file on the PLI portal and selected firms submit quarterly review reports. By the January 2026 year-end review, 170 proposals were approved.

A micro unit that fits PMFME's turnover cap is not the firm this incentive was written for. The scheme text requires minimum sales and a mandated investment in plant and machinery for the manufacturing categories.

## How the shift should be read

The policy change is a division of labour. Large common infrastructure and cold chain sit in PMKSY, with the oldest park model closed to new sanctions. The unorganised unit sits in PMFME, tied to a bank and, for marketing money, to the district product. Scale manufacturers and Indian brands abroad sit in PLI through 2026-27. Reading a headline about "food processing incentives" without naming which of the three is being funded will mis-state who can apply.
