# Food Processing In India: Three Policy Tracks, Not One Shift

## The stack is three jobs

Indian food-processing policy is not one lever. MoFPI runs support schemes for infrastructure and for micro enterprises. FSSAI sets food standards and the licence to manufacture, store, sell, distribute, or import food. APEDA promotes and regulates export of a statutory list of scheduled products. A plant can need all three, and a change in one does not rewrite the other two.

This brief does not describe a single new policy shift. It describes the tracks as the institutions publish them.

## MoFPI: infrastructure, micro units, and a production incentive

PMKSY is the umbrella MoFPI describes for modern infrastructure from the farm gate toward the retail outlet. The components the ministry page lists for implementation are integrated cold chain and value addition, creation or expansion of food processing and preservation capacities, infrastructure for agro-processing clusters, food safety and quality assurance infrastructure, human resources and institutions for research and development, and Operation Greens.

That page also says Mega Food Parks, Creation of Backward and Forward Linkages, specified human-resource promotional activities including skill development and HACCP, and a component of food safety and quality assurance infrastructure were discontinued in the 15th Finance Commission cycle, with provision for committed liability. Operation Greens began as a PMKSY vertical for tomato, onion, and potato. The page says the scope was later widened to 22 perishables, and it gives examples that include mango, banana, apple, pineapple, carrot, cauliflower, and beans.

Separately, the PM Formalization of Micro Food Processing Enterprises scheme (PMFME) is a centrally sponsored scheme. Its published aims include formalizing micro enterprises and supporting farmer producer organisations (FPOs), self-help groups, and producer cooperatives along the value chain. The common-infrastructure page names FPOs among the bodies that can seek farm-gate sorting, grading, warehousing, and cold storage, or a common processing line for the district's One District One Product (ODOP).

PLISFPI is a different instrument again: a central sector incentive for selected manufacturing segments and for branding abroad, with a published period from 2021-22 to 2026-27.

## FSSAI: the right to operate

The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 established FSSAI to lay down science-based standards and to regulate manufacture, storage, distribution, sale, and import of food. Section 31 says no person shall commence or carry on a food business except under a licence, with a registration route for petty food businesses where the regulations allow it. That is a legal condition of operating, not a scheme grant.

## APEDA: the export schedule

APEDA is a statutory body under the Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority Act, 1985 (Act 2 of 1986), in force from 13 February 1986. It registers exporters of scheduled products, fixes export standards and specifications, and is assigned inspection of meat and meat products in plants and storage. Its about page lists fruits and vegetables and their products, meat, poultry, dairy, confectionery and bakery, honey and jaggery, cocoa and chocolate, beverages, cereals, groundnuts, peanuts and walnuts, pickles, papads and chutneys, guar gum, floriculture, de-oiled rice bran, green pepper in brine, and cashew nuts and products. Basmati rice is in the Second Schedule. Marine products are not on that list, even though PLISFPI names marine products as one manufacturing segment.
