Opportunity, in the official record, is a short list of doors with published rules and published use. It is not a tour of fashionable sectors. DPIIT names the sectors it has already funded only as examples inside a wider portfolio, and those examples should not be mistaken for a ranking of what to start next.

## Selling to government is a used door

The 17 April 2026 PIB release says more than 38,600 startups had been onboarded on the Government e-Marketplace. In 2025-26, 6,540+ startups were onboarded, buyers placed 1,40,260+ orders, and the value of orders was 19,190+ crore rupees. The previous year was 5,140+ startups onboarded, 1,19,620+ orders, and 14,103+ crore rupees. Order value rose on PIB’s own table. This is procurement by recognised startups, not equity. A founder who can invoice a government buyer has a revenue path that does not wait for Fund of Funds 2.0.

## Seed and debt have published ceilings and published use

The seed fund’s ₹945 crore corpus was fully committed by the end of 2025-26, through 219 incubators, with over ₹605 crore approved to more than 3,400 startups. The live opportunity is an incubator that still has an approval to disburse, because the scheme was extended for that completion. As on 31 January 2026, incubators had approved around ₹592 crore in total, of which around ₹294 crore went to women-led startups. That women-led share is an approval figure from the 17 March 2026 PIB release, not a quota written on the recognition page.

The credit guarantee is the debt door. Cover per borrower is ₹20 crore after the 2025-26 revision, up from ₹10 crore. By year-end, more than 410 loans and over ₹1,250 crore had been guaranteed. As on 31 January 2026 the guaranteed amount was around ₹925 crore, of which around ₹39 crore was to women-led startups. The women-led slice of guaranteed credit is thin next to the women-led slice of seed approvals. A founder choosing between instruments can see that difference in the same Lok Sabha reply. The lender still has to want the credit. The guarantee does not oblige a bank to lend.

## The equity door is a fund segment, not an application to DPIIT

Fund of Funds 2.0 will not take a pitch from a company. It takes a pitch from a Category I or Category II alternative investment fund. The opportunity for a startup is to be the kind of company those selected funds are required to back.

Deep-tech funds must say in the private placement memorandum that they support deep-tech startups as G.S.R. 108(E) defines them. Their government cheque can be up to 40 percent of corpus and ₹500 crore, and they must invest at least 1.5 times the government commitment. Early-growth funds are capped at a ₹400 crore corpus, may take up to 30 percent and ₹100 crore from the scheme, must put at least half the corpus into seed or early stage, and must keep funding at or below ₹10 crore per startup. Manufacturing-oriented funds carry a 1.75 times multiplier and a ₹200 crore government cap. Agnostic funds carry a 2.5 times multiplier and a ₹180 crore cap. A company that is not a deep-tech startup under the February notification should not build a plan on the deep-tech column.

Inside the older programme, supported funds had invested around ₹25,859 crore by 31 January 2026. The women-led portion, which the same reply reports only from 2020 onward, was around ₹2,995 crore. The registry on that date had 1,02,054 recognised startups with at least one woman director or partner, out of 2,12,283. Board composition and capital received are both official, and they describe different facts.

## Intellectual property is counted as applications

PIB reports more than 19,400 patent applications filed by startups in the cumulative series it published on 17 April 2026. Filings were 2,850+ in 2024-25 and 4,480+ in 2025-26. Applications are not grants. A company citing this line should keep the word “filed”.

The first Fund of Funds portfolio, described by PIB when the cabinet approved the second fund, spans agriculture, artificial intelligence, robotics, automotive, clean tech, consumer goods and services, e-commerce, education, fintech, food and beverages, healthcare, manufacturing, space, and biotechnology, among others. That list shows where backed funds have already put money. It is not an invitation ranking, and it is not a statement that an unbacked sector is closed.
