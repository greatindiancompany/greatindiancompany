# The ten-year UDAN target, and the 2047 line the House did not adopt

India’s aviation trajectory, in the documents of July and August 2026, is three clocks. They end in different years, and only one of them is a scheme target with an outlay next to it.

## Ten years, one scheme

Modified UDAN was launched on 4 July 2026. The Lok Sabha statement of 6 August says it runs from 2026-27 to 2035-36, with an outlay of ₹28,840 crore. The target in that statement is 120 new destinations and 4 crore passengers over those ten years. The capital pieces inside the outlay are ₹12,159 crore for 100 airports developed from unserved airstrips, and ₹3,661 crore for 200 heliports in hilly, north-eastern, and aspirational areas.

Four crore passengers over ten years is 40 million passengers across the decade, not 40 million a year, and not a share of the 420 million passengers all Indian airports handled in 2025-26. The 4 crore figure is the scheme’s own passenger aim. The 168 lakh passengers already carried on RCS flights, as on 10 July 2026, are the previous scheme’s result. They are not a down payment that automatically counts toward the new 4 crore.

The minister’s airport history, given on 16 July, is the base the new target is supposed to extend. He said India had 65 airports in 2001 and 74 in 2014, nine airports in thirteen years, and that the following twelve years added 90 airports. He also said 55 unused or unserved airstrips had been revived. Seventy-four plus ninety is 164. Other official sentences in the same season use other counts, including “over 580 million” of capacity without an airport census in that sentence. Keep the minister’s 65, 74, and 90 as his 16 July account. Do not silently replace them with a later round number.

## The date the House was offered, and the date it answered

On 6 August a member asked whether the government was considering more than 350 airports by 2047, whether traffic would approach 500 million passengers before 2030 and 1 billion by 2047, and whether India intended to depend less on foreign hubs.

The answer on airports describes the instruments: the greenfield policy, UDAN, AAI upgrades, and public-private partnerships. It records 25 greenfield in-principle approvals, 14 operationalised, 6 site clearances, and 95 UDAN aerodromes. It then repeats the new scheme’s 120 destinations and 4 crore passengers. It does not adopt “more than 350” as a decision already taken.

On passengers, the answer is one sentence: Indian airports taken together are expected to handle more than 3,000 million passengers by 2046-47. That is not the 500 million, and it is not the 1 billion. A mid-term deck that still says the government told Parliament India would reach 1 billion passengers by 2047 is repeating the question.

On hubs, the ministry has an International Aviation Hub Strategy, and hub-and-spoke operations were launched on 25 June 2026. The reply does not publish a shift in the share of Indian international traffic that connects through a Gulf or Southeast Asian airport.

## The fleet clock is an expectation, dated May

A third clock was stated on 8 May 2026 at the aircraft-leasing summit in GIFT City. The civil aviation minister said the commercial fleet is expected to reach 1,100 aircraft by 2027 and more than 2,250 by 2035, and that carriers then had outstanding deliveries of 1,640 aircraft. Those are expected stocks and a delivery backlog as he described them. They are not the UDAN passenger target, and 2035 sits one year before the scheme window closes in 2035-36.

A planning note can hold all three clocks if it labels them. Scheme delivery is 120 destinations and 4 crore passengers by 2035-36, subject to states clearing the challenge. System throughput is an expectation of more than 3,000 million airport passengers by 2046-47. Fleet size is a May 2026 ministerial expectation, tied to deliveries and to leasing capacity at GIFT City.

What would falsify the near clock is operational, and it is already visible in the old scheme: of 679 routes operationalised, 348 were still flying when the ministry answered on 23 July.
