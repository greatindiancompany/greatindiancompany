# India's tap-water percentage is not a safely managed benchmark

This brief does not give India a rank against other countries. The public Indian record does not, in the pages below, publish a single "safely managed drinking water" percentage and set it next to a global table. What it does publish is a set of stages. Comparing India with anywhere else starts by refusing to treat those stages as one number.

## Three rural water facts, in the order a village actually meets them

The [Jal Jeevan Mission dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx) defines Har Ghar Jal as every household in an administrative unit having tap water supply, and then splits the declaration into three stages.

First, households are entered on the mission information system after the name and Aadhaar of the head of household are verified. When every household in the village is entered, the village is a village with 100 percent tap connections on that system.

Second, "reported" means the state or Union Territory water-supply department has confirmed that water is being supplied through taps to all households, schools, and anganwadi centres.

Third, "certified" means the Gram Sabha has passed a resolution after checking the department's claim, and after the department has certified to the Gram Panchayat that every household has supply.

The national percentage quoted in Parliament is the reported household stock, not the certified-village stock. As on 4 August 2026, the ministry told the Lok Sabha that more than 15.90 crore of 19.36 crore rural households, or 82.19 percent, were reported to have tap connections. As on 10 March 2026, the Cabinet note had put the same denominator at around 15.80 crore, or 81.61 percent. Neither sentence says that 82 percent of villages have passed a Gram Sabha resolution, and neither sentence says that 82 percent of households receive adequate, regular, potable water every day.

JJM 2.0, approved on 10 March 2026, tightens the third stage further. A Gram Panchayat declares itself Har Ghar Jal only when adequate in-village operation and maintenance is in place. The Cabinet note places assured 24×7 supply inside the 2047 vision. A benchmark that requires water to be on premises, available when needed, and free from contamination is a higher bar than a reported connection. The ministry has not, in these two documents, claimed that the 81.61 percent or the 82.19 percent already clears that bar.

The Cabinet note does cite outside estimates of health and time saved, including a World Health Organization estimate. Those sentences are impact estimates carried in a press note. They are not the administrative coverage series, and this brief does not convert them into a global league table.

## Sanitation has the same problem, with its own words

Rural India was declared open-defecation free in 2019, after the first phase of Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen). Phase II does not stop at a toilet. A Press Information Bureau note of 8 December 2025, and the mission dashboard, use three ODF Plus classes.

An Aspiring village sustains open-defecation-free status and manages either solid or liquid waste. A Rising village manages both. A Model village manages both and is visually clean, with ODF Plus messaging. The village declares itself at a Gram Sabha. For a village declared ODF Plus Model, the district must arrange third-party verification within 90 days of the first declaration.

As on 11 June 2026 the [SBM-G dashboard](https://sbm.gov.in/sbmgdashboard/) showed 5,69,020 ODF Plus villages out of 5,86,944 SBM villages, of which 5,07,634 were Model and 57,923 were still Aspiring. A national line that says "ODF Plus" is mostly true on that date, and it still hides an Aspiring remainder that has only one waste stream arranged.

## How to use the Indian numbers in a comparison

Quote the stage, the date, and the denominator. "Reported tap connection, 4 August 2026, 19.36 crore rural households" can sit in a note. "India is at 82 percent safely managed drinking water" cannot, unless a later official page actually says that. The same discipline applies to sanitation: ODF, ODF Plus, and ODF Plus Model are successive standards.

The definitions live on the [JJM dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx) and in the [8 December 2025 SBM-G note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2200341). The coverage sentences are in the [10 March 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2237548) and the [4 August 2026 Lok Sabha answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS265_f3O07i.pdf).
