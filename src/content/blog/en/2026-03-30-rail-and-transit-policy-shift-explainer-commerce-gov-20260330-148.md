# Two Policy Shifts That Now Govern Indian Rail Freight and Metro

Indian rail and urban transit are not one policy. Two documents, from two ministries, set the rules a founder or a state team actually has to meet.

## Freight terminals moved toward private capital

On 15 December 2021 the Ministry of Railways launched the Gati Shakti Multi-Modal Cargo Terminal policy. The point was additional terminals for rail cargo, sited on industry demand and cargo potential, rather than a goods shed the railway alone would build.

The early design, set out in the ministry’s Lok Sabha reply and restated in later PIB notes, is a set of cost waivers and a clock. Departmental charges are not levied on the applicant. Land licence fees are not charged for railway land used for connectivity. The cost of commercial staff is not charged. Common-user facilities at the serving station are built and maintained by the railway. Track, signalling, and overhead equipment outside the yard and the loading lines are maintained by the railway. A terminal that gives one million tonnes or more of outward traffic can recover the cost of a mid-section block station as a 10 percent freight rebate. Agencies that receive approval are expected to finish construction within 24 months.

By 7 August 2026 the same policy had a scale the 2021 launch did not: 142 terminals commissioned, an estimated handling capacity of 224 million tonnes a year, about ₹10,000 crore of private investment, and 146 million tonnes handled at those terminals in 2025-26. Location is still decided on demand, cargo potential, available railway infrastructure, and the logistics potential of the area. The policy did not hand the trunk line to private owners. It opened the terminal.

Wagon investment schemes sit beside that terminal rule. The 7 August 2026 reply said about 275 rakes of special-purpose wagons and 397 rakes of general-purpose wagons are in operation under private investment schemes, plus about 54 automobile rakes owned by industry.

## Metro assistance became a viability test

The Metro Rail Policy, 2017, issued by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, is the urban counterpart. It asks states to constitute a Unified Metropolitan Transport Authority and to plan from a Comprehensive Mobility Plan. For projects taken up with central assistance, the state commits to set up and operationalise that authority. One standard structure is an equal equity share between the Centre and the state through a special-purpose vehicle. The managing director is a state nominee, appointed with prior approval of the Centre. The ex-officio chair is a nominee of the urban affairs ministry. The policy also requires private participation, in some form, for implementation, operations, fare collection, or another unbundled activity.

A PIB note of 9 August 2025, describing that policy, adds the tests used for central financial assistance: a minimum economic internal rate of return of 14 percent, and mandatory private participation. The same note records domestic-procurement provisions of at least 75 percent of metro cars and 25 percent of key equipment and subsystems.

Those tests are not a guarantee that every sanctioned city will open on time. They are the gate. A project that cannot show the return threshold, the city authority, and a private role does not clear central assistance on the terms the ministry has published.

## What the shift is, and what it is not

The freight shift is from railway-owned sheds toward privately funded terminals on railway rules. The metro shift is from ad hoc central support toward an institutional and viability screen. Dedicated freight corridors remain a public build: the August 2026 reply describes the eastern and western corridors as commissioned ministry projects, and the Dankuni–Surat corridor as a detailed project report, not as a GCT.
