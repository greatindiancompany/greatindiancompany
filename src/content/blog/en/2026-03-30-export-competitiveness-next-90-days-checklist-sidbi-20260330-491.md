# A 90-day checklist for export trade releases and rebate rules

From 24 September 2026, ninety days runs to about 23 December 2026. The list below is only what the government has already said it publishes or reviews. It is not a prediction of rates, treaties, or market levels.

## This week: confirm which number you are still quoting

- If a July services export of US$ 35.89 billion is still in a deck, replace it. That was the Commerce estimate in the [13 August release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298878), written when RBI data stopped at June. The [31 August RBI print](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63480) puts July services exports at US$ 38,253 million, provisional.
- If an August merchandise figure is in the deck, tie it to the 15 September 2026 DGCIS quick-estimate annex: exports 43,811 million dollars, up 26.1 percent from August 2025. Do not blend that annex with the 13 August April–July cumulative and call the sum official.
- Open the [DGFT RoDTEP page](https://www.dgft.gov.in/CP/?opt=rodtep) and check that the schedule you file against is the one restored from 23 March 2026, not the 50 percent restriction of 23 February 2026.

## The September review that was already promised

The 2 January 2026 Export Promotion Mission note says interest-subvention rates under Niryat Protsahan will be reviewed in March and September. The base rate on the page is 2.75 percent, the positive list covers about 75 percent of six-digit tariff lines, and the cap is ₹50 lakh per IEC for 2025–26. Before year-end planning, look for the RBI operational guideline and for any September review note. If neither has replaced the January parameters, say so in the memo. Do not assume the FY 2025–26 cap rolled forward.

## The monthly prints that will arrive on their own calendar

Commerce has been releasing a monthly quick estimate with a short lag: the July narrative is dated 13 August, and the August statistical annex is dated 15 September. Over the next ninety days the September and October merchandise months should appear in that pattern. Read merchandise exports, merchandise imports, and the services line as three rows. If the services row is marked as an estimate because RBI has not yet published the month, wait for the RBI note before you lock a surplus.

The RBI July services release is the template: a one-page table, provisional, with growth rates revised to the balance-of-payments basis. The next pages in that series are the services check. The next quarterly BoP will cover July–September and will not match the sum of quick estimates. Leave a line in the model for that revision. Customs import revisions have already moved a previous current-account print.

## Preferential access that is already in force

The India–UK CETA and the social security agreement entered into force on 15 July 2026. A [1 September 2026 government note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2305422) says this provides zero-duty access for nearly 99 percent of India’s exports to the United Kingdom. The ninety-day task is rules of origin and proof of origin on live orders, not a celebration of the signing, which was a 2025 event. “Signed,” “concluded,” and “in force” are different legal states. The textile note of 22 April 2026 still described the UK agreement as signed in July 2025, the EFTA TEPA as in force from 1 October 2025, the Oman CEPA as a December 2025 event, the New Zealand FTA as announced on 22 December 2025, and the EU FTA as concluded on 27 January 2026. Use the later in-force sentence for the UK. Do not upgrade the EU line from “concluded” unless a later official note says it is in force.

## What not to spend the ninety days on

Pull DGCIS values if a location decision depends on them. The dated MSME export-credit design in these papers is Niryat Protsahan, issued through Commerce, the MSME ministry, Finance, and RBI guidelines.
