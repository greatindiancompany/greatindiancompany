# The First Bottleneck in Rural Insurance Is the Gram Panchayat Record

India's penetration ratio is national: 3.7 percent of GDP in 2024-25. The execution rule for going beyond large towns is local, and the [IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620) shows where that rule jammed in its first year.

## What insurers are required to cover

The March 2024 [regulatory revamp](https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/366347/Press+Note+on+Regulatory+Revamp.pdf/9ad7286a-b0a7-ea19-d51c-4c942bad43e1?t=1711121865505&version=3.0) moved rural measurement to the Gram Panchayat. The annual report spells out the obligation.

Life insurers must collectively insure 25,000 Gram Panchayats. Panchayats are allocated using mutually agreed parameters such as market share. Each life insurer must insure at least 10 percent of the population in each allocated panchayat, through individual or group policies.

General insurers, excluding stand-alone health insurers, Agriculture Insurance Company, and ECGC, must collectively cover at least 10 percent of dwellings, shops, and vehicles under fire and motor insurance in a minimum of 25,000 Gram Panchayats. Each of those general insurers must meet a 10 percent cover in the panchayats allocated to it.

Social-sector rules apply to life, general, and stand-alone health insurers, again excluding Agriculture Insurance Company and ECGC. They must cover lives in the social sector equal to 10 percent of total lives covered. The report links that book to government schemes including PMJJBY and PMSBY, and to beneficiaries of programmes such as MNREGA, Ujjwala, and PMAY, with a vocation-based route capped at 20 percent.

## Where the first year stuck

The certificate of the Gram Sachiv was meant to be the basis for reporting compliance. The Life and General Insurance Councils told IRDAI that, in the first year after notification, most insurers had trouble obtaining those certificates for administrative reasons. They asked to be excused from mandatory submission wherever that was the case for FY 2024-25.

IRDAI agreed. For FY 2024-25 it exempted mandatory Gram Sachiv certification where insurers were finding it difficult, and allowed self-certification by the chief executive, provided the statutory auditor validates the compliance. For the 25,000 Gram Panchayats allocated to life insurers, the report says examination of whether the obligations were met, and of the certification exemption, was still under process.

That is an execution bottleneck with a name, a year, and a substitute control. It is not a claim that rural cover failed. The report does not print a pass rate for the 25,000 panchayats.

## The office map makes the same point without a pass rate

Around 60 percent of life offices are in Tier I centres. General insurers have 72.18 percent of offices in Tier I and 0.42 percent in Tier VI. Stand-alone health insurers have one office in Tier V and one in Tier VI. The [PIB note of 23 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) can still report a national network of over 83 lakh distributors and 22,076 offices. Headcount at national scale and certification at panchayat scale are different tests. The 3.7 percent ratio does not distinguish them.

The office and district pattern is in the [geography brief](/blog/insurance-penetration-state-wise-patterns-ayush-gov-20260330-336). Great Indian Company treats the certificate exemption as the operating fact, so a rural rollout is planned against the control IRDAI actually accepted for 2024-25.
