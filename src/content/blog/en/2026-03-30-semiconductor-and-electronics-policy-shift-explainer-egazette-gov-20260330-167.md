India's semiconductor policy is now two instruments, and they answer different questions. The programme notified on 21 December 2021 funds fabs, display fabs, packaging, and chip design. The Cabinet decision of 15 July 2026, Semicon 2.0, sets a longer outlay and names six pillars that the first programme did not list as a set. This brief separates those two texts. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## The 2021 programme

On 29 February 2024 the Press Information Bureau recorded that the Programme for Development of Semiconductors and Display Manufacturing Ecosystem in India was notified on 21 December 2021 with an outlay of ₹76,000 crore. A [Lok Sabha reply on 22 July 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) describes the same effort as the Semicon India Programme, launched in January 2022, covering semiconductor fabs, display fabs, packaging, and a Design Linked Incentive scheme.

The design scheme has its own gazette. MeitY notification [CG-DL-E-21122021-232049](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Notification-Design-Linked-Incentive-Scheme.pdf), dated 21 December 2021, is the Design Linked Incentive. C-DAC implements it. Product-design support reimburses up to 50 percent of eligible expenditure, with a ceiling of ₹15 crore per application. Deployment support is 6 percent down to 4 percent of net sales over five years, with a ceiling of ₹30 crore per application.

A [PIB note on the Union Budget 2026-27](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839), dated 7 February 2026, describes the India Semiconductor Mission 1.0 incentive framework as fiscal support of up to 50 percent for silicon fabs, compound semiconductor facilities, assembly and testing, and chip design. That percentage sits inside the ₹76,000 crore framework. It is the support rate the note states. It is a different number from the investment commitments of the companies whose projects were later approved.

Beside the chip programme, MeitY already had a components instrument. The [Scheme for Promotion of Manufacturing of Electronic Components and Semiconductors](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/scheme-for-promotion-of-manufacturing-of-electronic-components-and-semiconductors-specs-AMxIDOtQWa) was notified on 1 April 2020 and pays 25 percent of capital expenditure on an identified list that includes components, semiconductor and display fabrication, ATMP units, specialised sub-assemblies, and capital goods. SPECS is not Semicon 2.0. Operators still meet it when a project is a component line rather than a fab or an OSAT approved under the mission.

## What the first programme had approved by mid-2026

The 22 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply states that 12 semiconductor manufacturing projects had been approved, across Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Gujarat, Punjab, Odisha, and Uttar Pradesh, with investment commitments of about ₹1.64 lakh crore. The [Cabinet note of 15 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) describes that set as one silicon fab, one silicon-carbide fab, one integrated gallium-nitride micro-LED display fab, and nine packaging units. Three companies — Micron, Kaynes, and CG Semi — had started commercial production. The same note says one more was expected to start in 2026, and it does not name that unit.

Those 12 projects are the record of the first programme. Semicon 2.0 did not re-approve them. The Cabinet presented them as progress under ISM 1.0.

## What changed on 15 July 2026

The Union Cabinet approved Semicon 2.0 with a budget outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore. The [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) sets six pillars:

1. Design, aimed at intellectual property and full chip and system designs, building on startups already using the design infrastructure.
2. Machines and materials, meaning incentives for equipment, chemicals, and gases.
3. More fabs: silicon, compound, discrete, and display.
4. A further build-out of ATMP and OSAT, including more advanced packaging.
5. Research aimed at nodes beyond the 28 nm to 110 nm range the note says the journey has started with, with the first fab scheduled to be commissioned in 2028.
6. Talent, from university design training through clean-room and fab-construction skills.

The pillar that most clearly extends the 2021 text is machines and materials. The 2021 programme pays for fabs, displays, packaging, and design. The 2026 decision says the equipment and the process chemicals are now an incentive category of their own. The research pillar is also more explicit: the Cabinet text treats 28 nm to 110 nm as the range already in view, and names more advanced nodes as work still to be done with research centres.

## Three rupee figures that are easy to merge

A reader will see three official numbers in early and mid-2026. They are not three ways of saying the same outlay.

- ₹76,000 crore is the 2021 programme outlay.
- ₹1,000 crore is the provision the 7 February 2026 PIB note records for ISM 2.0 in FY 2026-27. The same note gives the modified semiconductor and display programme a financial outlay of ₹8,000 crore for that year, with year-ahead targets for one fab scheme, nine compound and packaging units, and 30 design companies.
- ₹1,27,500 crore is the multi-year Cabinet outlay for Semicon 2.0, decided on 15 July 2026.

The about ₹1.64 lakh crore figure is neither of these. Parliament described it as the investment commitment of the 12 approved manufacturing projects. Adding it to the fiscal outlays double-counts the same factories once as private commitment and once as public support.

The 15 July release states the Semicon 2.0 outlay and the six pillars. It does not, in that text, publish a new percentage of project cost. Until MeitY notifies the implementing scheme, the operative rate a company can quote is the one already written into the 2021 framework and the Design Linked Incentive gazette, not a rate inferred from the headline ₹1,27,500 crore.

## How to use the shift

For a founder, the practical question is which door the project uses. A fabless design still reads the 2021 Design Linked Incentive and the C-DAC guidelines. A component or sub-assembly reads SPECS and the later Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme. A fab, OSAT, compound line, or the new equipment-and-materials layer reads ISM, and then checks whether Semicon 2.0 has a notified scheme yet. The Cabinet decision is the policy change. The gazette that tells an applicant the claim formula is the document that releases money.
