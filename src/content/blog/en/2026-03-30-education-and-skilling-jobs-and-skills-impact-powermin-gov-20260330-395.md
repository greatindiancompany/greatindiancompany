A skill certificate changes a person's options only if someone will employ the skill. India's official series let you see the labour market the certificate enters. They do not let you pretend every certificate has a tracked job.

## The market a young person is walking into

On the Periodic Labour Force Survey's usual status, for people aged 15 and above, unemployment fell from 6.0% in 2017-18 to 3.2% in 2023-24. The worker population ratio rose from 46.8% to 58.2%. Those figures are set out in the Directorate General of Employment's [note on PLFS](https://dge.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-04/Employment_Situation_in_the_Country.pdf).

For ages 15–29 the unemployment rate in 2023-24 was 10.2%, down from 17.8% in 2017-18. The youth worker population ratio rose from 31.4% to 41.7%. A young person is more likely to be working than in 2017-18, and still much more likely to be unemployed than an older adult in the same survey. That is the jobs backdrop for every skilling scheme aimed at "youth."

Schooling and degrees move the rates, not always in the direction a slogan suggests. Among people with secondary education and above, the worker population ratio rose from 43.2% to 52.1%. Among graduates it rose from 49.7% to 57.5%, while labour force participation rose from 60% to 66.2%. Graduates are more attached to the labour market than the average adult, and the share of them who are working has risen. The distance between "in the labour force" and "working" remains wider for graduates than for all adults aged 15 and above, where participation was 60.1% and work was 58.2% in 2023-24. Rising work and a stubborn gap can both be true.

The skill ministry has said the share of 15–29 year olds who are vocationally trained rose from 7.1% in 2017-18 to 26.1% in 2023-24. Put next to the youth unemployment rate of 10.2%, the reading is that vocational training spread while youth unemployment fell and remained high. Training spread is not the same event as unemployment falling. The Ministry presents them together as the result of several schemes. A reader can accept both numbers and still refuse a single causal sentence.

## Three different links from training to work

Long-term craft training sits in Industrial Training Institutes under the Craftsmen Training Scheme, run through the Directorate General of Training. The ITI count rose from 9,776 in 2014 to 14,688 in the skill ministry's later public count. PM-SETU proposes to upgrade 1,000 government ITIs. Most ITIs are outside that upgrade. A craftsmen certificate and a short-term certificate are not the same labour-market object.

Short-term training is PMKVY. The Ministry has been explicit that placements were tracked in the short-term component of PMKVY 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0, from 2015-16 to 2021-22, and that PMKVY 4.0 does not track placements that way. As on 7 December 2025, 27.08 lakh candidates had been trained under PMKVY 4.0. As on 30 June 2026, more than 5.44 lakh had been trained in future-oriented technologies, including artificial intelligence, drones, 5G, and green energy, with on-the-job training embedded in the course design. Embedded on-the-job training is a design feature. It is not a published placement rate.

Apprenticeship is the scheme where an employer is part of the record. Under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme, 51.30 lakh apprentices were engaged from 2016-17 up to 31 December 2025. Engagement is not completion. In 2024-25, 5.86 lakh apprentices completed on-the-job training and 2.33 lakh completed final assessment, according to the Ministry's year-end review of 23 January 2026. In 2025-26, up to 30 November 2025, the comparable figures were 4.22 lakh and 1.58 lakh. The jobs impact of apprenticeship is closer to these completion counts than to the cumulative engagement slogan.

The same review says apprenticeship trades include 266 designated trades and more than 750 optional trades. Optional trades are how a firm adds a role the designated list does not yet name. That is a real pathway into work. It still depends on the establishment staying in the contract.

## What a certificate should not be asked to prove

A PMKVY 4.0 certificate proves that a candidate was trained in an NSQF-aligned course under the current scheme rules, as far as the scheme's own records go. It does not prove a placement, because the scheme stopped publishing that field. A rise in the vocationally trained share of youth proves that training reached more young people. It does not prove that youth unemployment at 10.2% is a skills mismatch rather than a shortage of jobs, a search problem, or a measurement of people holding out for a particular kind of work. The survey does not settle that argument.

For a person choosing a course, the practical distinction is whether an employer is already on the contract, as in an apprenticeship, or whether the outcome is a certificate plus whatever the local labour market does next. For a government reading its own schemes, the impact that can be stated without stretching is: more young people are vocationally trained, more graduates are working than in 2017-18, youth unemployment is lower than in 2017-18 and still about three times the all-age rate, and the flagship short-term scheme no longer reports placements the way its earlier versions did.
