## Three routes from learning to work

Indian skilling policy describes three different routes. They are not substitutes, and they do not share one age band.

Apprenticeship is the workplace route. The National Policy on Skill Development and Entrepreneurship, 2015, which the 7 February 2025 Cabinet note cites, treats apprenticeship as a main way to build skilled manpower. Under PM-NAPS the Centre pays 25% of the stipend, up to Rs 1,500 per month per apprentice, by direct benefit transfer during training. The stated age band is 14 to 35. The note says the scheme encourages apprenticeships in manufacturing and in fields it names as artificial intelligence, robotics, blockchain, green energy, and Industry 4.0, including in MSMEs, aspirational districts, and the North-East. It does not publish how many apprentices were engaged in 2025-26.

Recognition of prior learning is the route for people who already work. On 3 August 2026 the Lok Sabha was told that, as on 30 June 2026, 3,32,137 candidates had been certified under recognition of prior learning in PMKVY 4.0. The question asked about informal workers. The answer counts candidates certified under that component. It does not further split them by informal or formal employment. A 10 August 2026 education-ministry reply adds that NCVET’s recognition-of-prior-learning guidelines allow industrial bodies to assess their own workforces, with credits based on assessed outcomes. That is a rule for credit, not a count of jobs created.

Short-term training is the classroom-plus-workplace route. The 12 August 2026 Rajya Sabha reply says on-the-job training is part of PMKVY 4.0 short-term training. Where NCVET prescribes it for a job role, it is mandatory, and the candidate is assessed only after that component. Duration follows the qualification pack. Mandatory employability-skills modules sit inside the same programmes, with further digital material on the Skill India Digital Hub.

## What these routes do not yet quantify

The Cabinet note says PMKVY 4.0’s target beneficiaries are aged 15 to 59. A different sentence, in the 3 August school-to-skill paragraph, speaks of school and college dropouts, out-of-school children, and unemployed youth aged 15 to 45. Both sentences are official. They are not one merged eligibility rule.

No reply used here gives a placement percentage, a retention rate after apprenticeship, or a change in the Periodic Labour Force Survey. The February 2025 note says the Skill India Programme is meant to stay aligned with that survey. Alignment is not a published jobs delta.
