The state Jan Dhan table is a stock of mission accounts, dated and bank-reported. It becomes misleading only when a larger stock is treated as proof that one state is more financially included than another.

The [statewise account report](https://www.pmjdy.gov.in/statewise-statistics) on pmjdy.gov.in is marked as on 11 March 2026. Its total row is 578,648,822 beneficiaries and a balance of ₹3,02,778.16 crore, with 401,731,339 RuPay cards. The country progress report dated 25 March 2026 shows 57.93 crore beneficiaries and ₹3,05,244.71 crore. Both pages are official. They are fourteen days apart, so the totals are supposed to differ. Cite the date with the number.

## Where the accounts sit

On the 11 March 2026 state table, the largest stocks are:

| State | Beneficiaries | Balance (₹ crore) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uttar Pradesh | 102,990,039 | 62,377.70 |
| Bihar | 68,118,466 | 30,170.64 |
| West Bengal | 56,340,073 | 29,174.32 |
| Madhya Pradesh | 46,708,633 | 18,238.63 |
| Rajasthan | 38,248,636 | 23,281.43 |
| Maharashtra | 37,793,120 | 20,538.48 |

These six are large because the mission enrolled large populations. Kerala’s row on the same date is 7,403,888 beneficiaries and ₹4,005.95 crore. Goa’s is 230,641 beneficiaries and ₹234.14 crore. A smaller PMJDY stock can mean the state already had other accounts before 2014, or that it has fewer adults. The table does not say which. RBI’s Financial Inclusion Index is national. It does not publish a state rank in the annual press note.

The rural and urban columns need the same care. The header is beneficiaries at rural or semi-urban centre bank branches, and at urban or metro centre branches. On 11 March 2026 the all-India split was 452,309,159 and 126,339,663. The country table of 25 March 2026 puts the same idea in crore: 45.27 and 12.66. The figure follows the branch centre that holds the account.

## What a regional gap meant in the strategy

NSFI 2025–30, looking back at 2019–2024, says some form of banking access was provided within a 5 kilometre radius of 99.9 percent of the identified villages and hamlets of 500 households. The earlier strategy had named the hard geography directly: parts of the North Eastern Region, districts affected by left-wing extremism, and aspirational districts, where access points still needed to increase. The 2025–30 document treats that access milestone as largely met and moves the regional problem toward quality of the last mile, including the share of women business correspondents.

A state government, a bank’s state-level bankers’ committee, or a founder placing agents can still use this table. It shows where the basic-account stock and the RuPay stock already are. It will not, by itself, show where credit is thin, where accounts are quiet, or where a district still lacks a reliable outlet. Those questions sit in the [household brief](/blog/financial-inclusion-impact-on-households-20260330-037), the [risk brief](/blog/financial-inclusion-risk-watch-20260330-077), and the national [scoreboard](/blog/financial-inclusion-key-numbers-20260330-017).

Great Indian Company leaves the state table on its own page so a regional claim stays tied to the date the portal printed.
