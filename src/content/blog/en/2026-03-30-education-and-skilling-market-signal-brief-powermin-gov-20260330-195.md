A market signal, for education and skilling, is not a stock price. It is a published labour or scheme figure that changes how a hiring manager, a training provider, or a founder should read demand. The figures below are the ones official series actually support. They are not a forecast of vacancies.

## The headline unemployment rate is the wrong single number

The Periodic Labour Force Survey, run by the National Sample Survey Office in the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, is the official employment series. The Directorate General of Employment's note on the [employment situation](https://dge.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-04/Employment_Situation_in_the_Country.pdf), drawing on annual PLFS rounds, reports usual-status results for people aged 15 and above. Between 2017-18 and 2023-24 the labour force participation rate rose from 49.8% to 60.1%, the worker population ratio rose from 46.8% to 58.2%, and the unemployment rate fell from 6.0% to 3.2%.

Youth aged 15–29 did not converge to that 3.2%. Over the same years their unemployment rate fell from 17.8% to 10.2%, their worker population ratio rose from 31.4% to 41.7%, and their participation rate rose from 38.2% to 46.5%. A [PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2076956) cites the same youth unemployment rate of 10.2% and the same rise in the youth worker population ratio. The signal is a tighter overall market beside a youth market that is still much looser.

Education cuts the same survey differently. For people with secondary education and above, the worker population ratio rose from 43.2% to 52.1%, and participation from 48.8% to 56.1%. For graduates, work rose from 49.7% to 57.5% and participation from 60% to 66.2%. For postgraduate and above, work rose from 57.9% to 62.2% and participation from 67.8% to 71.0%. More educated people are both more likely to be in the labour force and more likely to be working than they were in 2017-18. The gap between participation and work is still wider for graduates than for the 15-and-above population as a whole. That gap is visible in the published pair of rates. This brief does not convert it into a new unemployment percentage the survey office has not printed.

Usual status, principal and subsidiary, describes a long reference period. The same survey also publishes current weekly status. Do not mix a weekly urban print with these annual usual-status rates and call the difference a shock.

## What the skill ministry adds, and what it stopped counting

The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship has said that the share of youth aged 15–29 who are vocationally trained rose from 7.1% in 2017-18 to 26.1% in 2023-24. That sentence appears in its releases on scheme performance, including the note with figures up to 31 December 2025. It is a supply signal: a larger share of young people report vocational training. It is not a count of job offers, and it is not the National Education Policy's separate target that 50% of school and college learners should have vocational exposure by 2025.

Under the Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana, placements were tracked for short-term training in the first three versions of the scheme, through 2021-22. The Ministry has said that PMKVY 4.0, running from 2022-23, instead orients candidates toward varied career paths and does not track placements the same way. A training firm that still sells "our PMKVY placement rate" as if the current scheme published one is using a discontinued indicator.

Counts that do exist, with dates attached, are still useful as activity signals. The year-end review of 23 January 2026 said 27.08 lakh candidates had been trained under PMKVY 4.0 as on 7 December 2025, across 38 sectors, 36 states, and 732 districts, through more than 15,500 institutions including more than 7,000 skill hubs. A later note, as on 30 June 2026, said more than 5.44 lakh of the new-age and future-technology trainees sat inside that effort, and that ₹414.86 crore had been used for those job roles. Activity at that scale is real. It does not, by itself, tell you the wage or the retention of the trainee.

Apprenticeship is the closer demand signal, because an employer has to engage the person. The Ministry reported 51.30 lakh apprentices engaged under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme from 2016-17 up to 31 December 2025. The year-end review, using a slightly earlier cutoff, reported 9.85 lakh engaged in 2024-25 and 8.14 lakh more up to 30 November 2025. Completion is smaller than engagement: in 2024-25, 5.86 lakh completed on-the-job training and 2.33 lakh completed final assessment. Engagement is an opening. Assessment is a finish. Quote the one you mean.

## How to use this in a decision

If you are hiring, the useful reading is that educated participation is high and rising, youth unemployment is still about 10%, and a vocational certificate from the current flagship short-term scheme does not come with an official placement rate. Ask for the employer's own apprenticeship record or for a cohort the provider can show you, not for a national placement percentage the scheme no longer publishes.

If you are pricing a training business, separate three markets that the statistics often get rolled together: long-term craftsmen training in ITIs, short-term PMKVY courses, and apprenticeships under NAPS. They have different buyers, different regulators, and different official outcome fields. The [Skill India Digital Hub](https://www.skillindiadigital.gov.in/) is the common digital window. It is not a substitute for the labour force survey.
