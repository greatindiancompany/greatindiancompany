# Who does what in India’s agri value chain

A policy team that assigns one ministry to “agri value chains” will miss the handoffs. The official design splits price, purchase, the mandi, processing, credit, and export across institutions that do not share a single dashboard.

The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices recommends the minimum support price. The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approves it. The list is 22 mandated crops, plus toria fixed from rapeseed and mustard, and de-husked coconut fixed from copra. Since 2018-19 the announced price has had to be at least one and a half times the all-India weighted average cost, including family labour. The Commission can, and does, go higher. Wheat’s margin for Rabi Marketing Season 2026-27 is 109 percent. Common paddy’s margin for Kharif Marketing Season 2026-27 is 50 percent. The rule and the crop margins are in [how the floor is set](/blog/agri-value-chains-policy-shift-explainer-morth-nic-20260330-157).

Purchase is then agency-specific. The Food Corporation of India and designated state agencies buy cereals and coarse cereals. Wheat and paddy estimates are agreed with states before the season. Coarse grains need prior approval of a state plan. NAFED and NCCF buy pulses, oilseeds, and copra under the Price Support Scheme of PM-AASHA when the state requests it and the market price is below MSP. The Cotton Corporation of India buys cotton. The Jute Corporation of India buys jute. Budget 2025 said the whole of state production of tur, urad, and masoor would be procured for four years, up to 2028-29, and raised the pulse guarantee from ₹45,000 crore to ₹60,000 crore. That instruction sits with NAFED and NCCF. It does not move wheat buying out of FCI.

## Mandis, plants, credit, exports

States run the wholesale mandis. The Centre’s National Agriculture Market, since 2016, connects mandis that states propose. The grant is up to ₹75 lakh a mandi. As on 30 June 2026, 1,656 mandis in 23 states and 4 Union Territories were integrated. e-NAM is a trading and payment layer. It is not the procurement agency, and it is not the marketing statute.

Processing grants sit in the Ministry of Food Processing Industries: Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana for infrastructure, PMFME for micro units, and the production-linked incentive for firms that can show eligible sales. Cold chain is a Sampada component, with 409 projects approved as on 30 June 2026. Those schemes are demand-driven. A state does not receive a fixed cold-chain quota in the replies that list the projects.

Credit has two official authors. The Reserve Bank’s priority-sector rule, as the Ministry restates it, is 18 percent of adjusted net bank credit or credit-equivalent off-balance-sheet exposure for agriculture, with 14 percent for non-corporate farmers and 10 percent for small and marginal farmers. The government’s ground-level credit target for FY 2025-26 was ₹32.50 lakh crore, including ₹5 lakh crore for animal husbandry, dairying, and fisheries. NABARD refinances cooperative banks and regional rural banks from the priority-sector shortfall, through the short-term cooperative and regional-rural-bank funds and the long-term rural credit fund. In 2023-24 that short-term refinance disbursement was ₹1,83,152.53 crore. The Reserve Bank’s sectoral release is a third product: outstanding bank credit, which grew 17.0 percent for agriculture and allied activities as on 31 July 2026. Target, refinance, and outstanding growth are three different policy dials.

Exports sit with the Department of Commerce, APEDA for its scheduled products, and the Export Inspection Council for the health certificate. The June 2026 Commerce briefing is the place that names residue, aflatoxin, and phytosanitary conditions. APEDA’s 2024-25 report is the place that splits scheduled products from all-agri exports. Neither document sets the MSP.

A policy review that asks “did the value chain work this season” has to pick a dial. Price discipline is the CACP margin. Farmer reach is the procurement beneficiary count, 1.84 crore in the July 2024 to June 2025 foodgrain year. Market plumbing is e-NAM’s traded value and the unfinished assaying kit. Processing is MoFPI sanctions versus releases. Credit is the 18 percent target versus the 17 percent growth print versus NABARD’s refinance year. Export credibility is the certificate and the residue test, not the MSP circular.
