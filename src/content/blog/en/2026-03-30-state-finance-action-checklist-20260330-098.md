# A checklist for reading one state budget

This is a reading order for one Indian state, using documents that already exist. It assumes you have the state's budget for the year in front of you, and the Reserve Bank's *State Finances: A Study of Budgets of 2025-26* (released 23 January 2026) as the consolidated cross-check. It does not ask you to score the state. It asks you to leave with the lines that change a decision.

## Before the speech

Name the column. Accounts are audited or closed outcomes. Revised estimates are the year's mid-course picture. Budget estimates are the plan for the year about to start. The Reserve Bank study uses 2023-24 accounts, 2024-25 revised or provisional numbers, and 2025-26 budget estimates. A budget speech that quotes only the coming year's plan should be put next to last year's account before it is believed.

Write down two denominators and keep them on the page. Consolidated ratios in the Bank's press release use national GDP. State rows often use GSDP. The gross fiscal deficit of 3.3 per cent of GDP in 2024-25, budgeted again at 3.3 per cent for 2025-26, is the all-state GDP figure. Your state's GSDP ratio is a different cell. Chart II.2 is that cell for 2025-26 budget estimates.

## The seven lines

1. Revenue deficit or surplus, Statement 2. This is the current account. A surplus means revenue receipts cover revenue expenditure. A deficit means the state is borrowing for current spending.

2. Gross fiscal deficit, Statement 3, and its decomposition, Statement 4. This adds capital spending to the gap. Ask how much of the gap is capital outlay and how much is a revenue gap. The Bank says the all-state step through 3 per cent of GDP mainly reflects 50-year interest-free capital loans above the normal borrowing ceiling. Check whether your state's deficit includes that loan, and whether the loan is funding the capital line you care about.

3. Own-tax revenue, Chart II.4 and Statement 14, set beside devolution and grants in Statement 17. Own tax is the base the state controls. Devolution is the state's share of Union taxes. For 2026-27 to 2030-31 the accepted share of the divisible pool is 41 per cent, which is then split across states by the Finance Commission's formula. Grants are the third receipt. A project that depends on a grant should name the grant.

4. Capital expenditure against revenue expenditure. All-state capital spending was 2.7 per cent of GDP in 2023-24 and 2024-25, and is budgeted at 3.2 per cent for 2025-26. Your state's capital outlay, Chart II.7's components, is the line for roads, irrigation, and buildings. Revenue expenditure, Chart II.5, holds interest, salaries, pensions, and subsidies. Statement 13, Statement 29, and Statement 36 are those three committed or quasi-committed heads.

5. Financing, Statements 5 to 10. Split market borrowings from loans from the Centre, from the capital-investment scheme, and from other items. Market borrowings are the state development loans. Chart II.16 is the yield and the spread over central securities. Read the chart in the study. Do not carry in a spread from memory.

6. Debt stock and maturity. Statements 18 to 20 are outstanding liabilities. The all-state budget estimate is 29.2 per cent of GDP at end-March 2026. Statements 23 and 24 are the maturity profile of state government securities. A heavy redemption year is a refinancing year.

7. Guarantees, Statement 28. These can sit outside the deficit until they are called. The Finance Commission asked states to bring off-budget borrowing onto the budget. The government's memorandum accepted the borrowing-ceiling quantum in principle and left the off-budget recommendation for separate examination. Until a later document says the practice has stopped, read the guarantee statement.

## After the consolidated study

Open the state's own finance act and budget volumes for tax-rate changes, new schemes, and the capital projects by name. Then open the Comptroller and Auditor General's finance accounts and the audit report on that state's finances for the last closed year. The audit is slower than the budget speech and closer to cash.

For 2026-27, add two Union documents. The explanatory memorandum on the Sixteenth Finance Commission is the 41 per cent devolution and the 3 per cent of GSDP ceiling excluding capital-investment loans. The Department of Expenditure guidelines of 27 March 2026 are the Rs 2,00,000 crore interest-free loan window for that year, and the conditions attached to it.

Stop when you can say, in one paragraph, whether the state is borrowing for capital or for the current account, what share of receipts it raises itself, when its bonds mature, and whether a guarantee sits beside the bond. That paragraph is the reading. The rest is annexure.
