Execution problems in this sector are not mysterious. The official series already show where a learner, a teacher, or a rupee drops out. The bottlenecks below are those drops, not a general complaint that "implementation is hard."

## The secondary stage is the school bottleneck

The UDISE+ 2024-25 note of 28 August 2025 is unusually clear. At the foundational stage, retention was 98.9% and the pupil-teacher ratio was 10. At the secondary stage, retention was 47.2%, dropout was 8.2%, and the gross enrolment ratio was 68.5%. Transition from middle to secondary was 86.6%, better than 83.3% the year before, and still the point where a national "near universal" story stops being true.

Dropout has fallen at every stage the note reports. Preparatory dropout went from 8.7% in 2022-23 to 2.3% in 2024-25. Middle dropout went from 8.1% to 3.5%. Secondary dropout went from 13.8% to 8.2%. Improvement and a remaining bottleneck are compatible. The stage that still loses the most students is the one the policy needs for its 2030 universal-enrolment goal.

Basic facilities are no longer the main national bottleneck the census used to describe. Drinking water was reported in 99.3% of schools, girls' toilets in 97.3%, electricity in 93.6%. Computers were in 64.7% and internet in 63.5%. Ramps with handrails were in 54.9%. A digital or inclusive programme will still stall in the third of schools without a computer, or in the schools without a ramp. That is a facilities bottleneck at the margin, not at the core of water and toilets.

Single-teacher schools fell by around 6% in the reporting year and zero-enrolment schools by around 38%. The Ministry presents both as progress from a conscious rationalisation. The fact that single-teacher schools were still numerous enough to be reported as a national indicator means deployment, not just headcount, is unfinished. Teachers overall crossed 1,01,22,420. A one-crore teaching force with single-teacher schools left is a posting problem.

## Trainers and ITI buildings are the skill bottleneck

Short-term training has scale. PMKVY 4.0 had trained 27.08 lakh candidates as on 7 December 2025, through more than 15,500 institutions. Scale without trainers is a bottleneck the Ministry has already priced: the year-end review earmarks ₹200 crore under PMKVY 4.0 for a national pool of trainers and assessors, with procedures and certification through NCVET, hosted on the Skill India Digital Hub. Earmarking the money recognises the constraint. It does not mean the pool exists at the size the courses require. New-age courses, more than 5.44 lakh trainees as on 30 June 2026, need equipment and instructors that a standard short-term centre may not have. The common-norms ceiling of ₹49 per hour is the Ministry's answer on cost. Centres that cannot recover equipment inside that ceiling will not run the course, whatever the job-role list says.

The long-term bottleneck is physical and procedural. PM-SETU is supposed to upgrade 1,000 government ITIs and five National Skill Training Institutes. As of 1 April 2026, state proposals had not reached the national committee, and funds for upgradation wait on a special purpose vehicle. The 2025-26 revised estimate for the ministry's centrally sponsored schemes, ₹367 crore against a ₹3,049.90 crore budget estimate, is the financial trace of that wait. You cannot teach a semiconductor technician course, NSQF 4.5, in a lab that the upgrade has not built. The course can be notified before the building exists. Notification is not execution.

## Money released late is an execution bottleneck

Samagra Shiksha's 2025-26 revised estimate was ₹38,000 crore against a budget estimate of ₹41,250 crore. PM SHRI was revised from ₹7,500 crore to ₹4,500 crore. A state implementing agency cannot run the timetable in the original estimate if the revised estimate is what arrives. The bottleneck is often described as "state capacity." Sometimes it is the Centre's own revision. Both can be true. The school department's sponsored schemes as a group were revised from ₹62,660 crore to ₹53,730.02 crore. That is a system-sized gap, not a single delayed file.

Higher education's version is the sponsored line revised from ₹1,815 crore to ₹800 crore in 2025-26, while the stock of students reached 4.50 crore. Capacity pressure and a cut in the sponsored scheme can coincide. PM-USHA's multi-year outlay through 2025-26 does not remove the annual revision.

## Outcome rules that make execution look better than it is

PMKVY 4.0's decision not to track placements the way earlier versions did removes a bottleneck from the dashboard without removing it from the learner's life. A scheme can report training numbers cleanly and still not know whether the trainee worked. Apprenticeship keeps the outcome harder to fake, and therefore shows the bottleneck in the open: many more apprentices are engaged than complete final assessment. In 2024-25 those assessment completions were 2.33 lakh.

The execution review, reduced to the hand-off points, is: children between middle and secondary school, teachers between a national headcount and a single-teacher school, ITI capital between a budget estimate and a special purpose vehicle, and trainees between a certificate and a recorded job. Each of those hand-offs has an official number. That is enough to manage against. A composite "execution score" would only hide which hand-off is stuck.
