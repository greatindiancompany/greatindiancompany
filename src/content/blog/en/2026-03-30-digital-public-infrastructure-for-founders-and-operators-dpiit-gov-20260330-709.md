# India DPI for Founders: Which Public Rail Does What

## Pick the rail before the pitch

Founders do not build India's digital public infrastructure. They build on pieces of it. The Press Information Bureau research note of 6 March 2026, and the Digital India page for India Stack Global, name the pieces. The practical map is which job each piece already does. This page does not add pricing, uptime, or a sandbox rule the note does not contain.

## Payments

UPI is the retail real-time rail. The note's January 2026 reading is 21.70 billion transactions, over ₹28.33 lakh crore, 691 banks live, and 81 percent of domestic retail payment volume. The footnote is NPCI. A product that takes money from people in India is, on these figures, meeting customers who already pay on that rail. The note does not publish what a startup is charged to use it.

## Documents and government APIs

DigiLocker, launched in 2015, is the consent-based document wallet: 67.63 crore users as of 5 March 2026 and over 950 crore documents issued. Use it when the job is fetching a document an issuer has placed there. It is not, in the note, a private file store for records the government never issued.

API Setu, started by MeitY in March 2020, is the catalogue for government APIs. As of March 2026 the note records 8,036 APIs, 2,559 publishers, 6,592 consumers, and 10,530 organisations. That is the place to check whether a government function is already an API before building a private equivalent.

## Commerce and procurement

ONDC, launched in 2022, is an open network. The note's seller count is 1.16 lakh-plus retail sellers in 630-plus cities and towns as of December 2025. The design point is interoperability across platforms. It is not a store a founder has to win.

Government eMarketplace is the public-procurement channel. As of November 2025 the note records more than 1.67 lakh buyer organisations and over 24 lakh sellers, with micro and small enterprises at 44.8 percent of cumulative order value. A founder selling to government has a named channel and a named buyer count. The note does not promise an order.

## Consented financial data

The Department of Financial Services page says account aggregators are NBFCs under a Reserve Bank master direction dated 2 September 2016. They move financial information only with explicit consent, and joining is voluntary for the customer. As of 31 March 2026 the page lists 17 licensed aggregators. The progress figures are credited to Sahamati. A lending or personal-finance product that wants bank or investment data is in this framework. The page does not describe a private scrape as an alternative.

## What India Stack Global will not settle

The Digital India showcase lists 15 platforms, including U-WIN, Aarogya Setu, DIKSHA, eSanjeevani, and e-Courts alongside the rails above, and says the material is in all UN languages. A name on that list is not an integration guide. Uptime, dispute handling, and per-API terms remain outside both the list and the March note.
