# The multi-year shape of India's manufacturing incentives

A mid-term view of Indian industrial policy has to follow the life of the instrument. Make in India is a standing mandate from 2014. PLI is a set of sector schemes with claims, reviews, and an outlay that later official text has restated. Corridors are construction. None of the documents used here publishes a single year in which all of this ends.

## What has already compounded

DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 dates Make in India to 25 September 2014 and describes a later focus on 27 sectors and 24 sub-sectors. That is a decade of facilitation, procurement preference, and investment outreach, not a scheme that expires on a claim form. The report is the [DPIIT Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/3d9c9c2daeefb97bb9ce964370938b71.pdf).

The same report says PLI schemes for 14 sectors, with an incentive outlay it states as Rs. 1.97 lakh crore, are under implementation, and that improvement in production, skills, employment, growth, and exports is expected "over the next five years and more." The phrase is the report's horizon from its own vantage point. It is not a calendar end date, and it is not the outlay to quote in 2026. The 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf) states the approved outlay as ₹1.91 lakh crore.

That July reply is also the results stock: as on 31 March 2026, 892 approved applications, actual investment over ₹2.40 lakh crore, production and sales over ₹22.66 lakh crore, exports over ₹15.2 lakh crore, employment over 14.15 lakh, and incentives disbursed of ₹35,354 crore. The 21 July note, [PIB PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008), says cumulative exports reported under the schemes were ₹4 lakh crore as on 31 March 2024, ₹6.5 lakh crore in the FY 2024–25 column, and ₹15.2 lakh crore as on 31 March 2026. Read those as the note's cumulative export path. Do not redraw them as a promise that the next year repeats the last step.

## Reviews can change the path without a new scheme

The 21 July note says the Empowered Group of Secretaries, chaired by the Cabinet Secretary, reviews PLI implementation, and that modifications have been made in certain schemes after feedback. Rationalisation of guidelines, relaxation of some eligibility conditions, and tighter project monitoring are the measures the note lists. A mid-term plan that assumes every original guideline is frozen will miss those changes. The note does not attach the revised guidelines. The administrative ministry's text is the document that binds an applicant.

## Corridors run on a civil clock

The corridor institutions go back to 2008. The Cabinet approval reported on 30 August 2024, [PIB PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136), is a new set of nodes at an estimated ₹28,602 crore, plus a statement that four projects were already complete and four were under implementation. Plug-and-play land is the stated aim. Approval in 2024 is not allotment in 2026. The export target of USD 2 trillion by 2030 in that note is a government target attached to the programme. It is not a trajectory this brief extends with its own math.

## The domestic output series stops earlier

The annual report's manufacturing growth rates, -3 percent in FY 2019–20 and 9.9 percent in FY 2023–24 on the 2011–12 index, are the long official output comparison in that document. They stop at FY 2023–24. They should not be drawn forward to stand in for PLI sales of ₹22.66 lakh crore, which are scheme sales, not the index.

The honest mid-term statement is modest. Incentives already paid are a fraction of the approved outlay. Reported investment and sales are large and concentrated in a handful of sectors. Corridor cities are still a mix of completed projects, projects under implementation, and 2024 approvals. The next official stock, when Parliament or PIB publishes one after 31 March 2026, is what moves this picture.
