# Execution bottlenecks in India's manufacturing trade diversification

Announcements are not the bottleneck. The stall is usually one of four handoffs: from a national scheme to a line ministry, from an anchor plant to a vendor, from a trade agreement to a certificate, or from a corridor map to the last 50 kilometres.

## Ministries do not move as one scheme

The 2 August 2023 PIB note is explicit. PLI schemes for all 14 sectors had been notified, and they were "in various stages of implementation" by the ministries and departments concerned. DPIIT can describe the architecture. It does not run every sector's claims. A firm that waits for a single PLI window will wait past the sector that is already inviting applications, and rush the sector whose guidelines are still being settled. The same reply said the Cabinet had not approved additional sectors as of that date. Treating "PLI" as a live programme for an unlisted product is an execution error, not a policy change.

The vendor handoff is the bottleneck the note itself predicts. Anchor units are expected to create a supplier base, mostly MSMEs. At that date, 176 of 733 selected applications were MSMEs in a subset of sectors. If those vendors cannot tool up, the anchor imports the sub-assembly and the localization claim fails in the bill of materials. That is a shop-floor bottleneck. It does not show up in the Rs 1.97 lakh crore outlay.

## Paper bottlenecks at the border

Two paper paths stall physical diversification. Quality Control Orders can stop a substitute input for the domestic market. The FTP 2023 exemption for Advance Authorisation, EOUs, and SEZs is real and narrow: it is for export production, with conditions against leaking the input into the domestic tariff area. A plant that did not separate its export and domestic inventories will look "stuck in customs" when it is stuck in its own process design.

Preferences stall the same way. An agreement that is not in force, or a shipment that misses the origin rule, or a missing certificate from an agency in DGFT Appendix 2B, ships at the ordinary rate. The buyer then concludes India "did not work," when the bottleneck was documentation. Commerce's own CEPA note for the UAE shows what the unstuck version looks like: an agreement in force since 1 May 2022, with duty elimination specified at tariff-line level.

## The physical bottleneck is often not the shed

PIB's 27 November 2025 note reports logistics cost at 7.97 percent of GDP for 2023-24, Rs 24.01 lakh crore in total, and higher costs for smaller firms. It flags the first and last 50 kilometres of a roughly 600-kilometre journey. A corridor can be genuinely faster — the same note says Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor wagon turnaround fell from 15-16 days to 2-3 days — and the plant can still miss a sailing if the approach road, the truck terminal, or the container visibility layer is weak. ULIP and the Logistics Data Bank are the official attempts to make that layer visible. They do not remove a missing approach road.

State capacity is the other physical variable. A PIB note on the National Logistics Policy says 26 states and Union Territories had notified state logistics policies. LEADS, released for 2025 on 13 May 2026, is how DPIIT compares the rest. A supplier cluster in a state that has not aligned policy or infrastructure will not inherit the national corridor's average speed.

Critical-mineral processing is a slower bottleneck of the same type. The National Critical Mineral Mission provides for parks and recycling. Until those parks run, a localized cell or electronics plant still queues for an imported intermediate.

## What to escalate

Escalate the handoff you actually control this quarter: the line ministry's guideline, the vendor's tool-up, the origin file, or the last-mile survey. Leave the national outlay in an appendix.
