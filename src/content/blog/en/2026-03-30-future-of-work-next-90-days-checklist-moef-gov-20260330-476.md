# A 90-day watchlist for labour rules, payroll, and e-Shram

This list covers the ninety days from 24 September 2026. It is not a calendar of events that have been announced for those days. It is the set of official documents worth opening, because each one can change a decision a firm, a state department, or a worker organisation would otherwise make on stale paper.

## Gazette and ministry notifications

The four labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025. The Ministry said then that rules, regulations, and schemes would be framed in consultation, and that existing Acts and rules would continue during the transition. The Economic Survey 2025–26 said, early in 2026, that rules were expected over the following months. In this quarter, look for:

- Rules under each of the four codes, and whether a given rule says it replaces a named older rule. If it does not, keep the older instrument in the file.
- A notification that sets the aggregator contribution rate under section 114(4) of the Code on Social Security. The statute allows a rate from 1 percent to 2 percent of annual turnover, excluding central tax, levy, and cess, capped at 5 percent of amounts paid or payable to gig and platform workers. The band is not the rate.
- A separate notification of the commencement date under section 114(5). Without that date, do not treat the contribution as having started.
- Any amendment of the Seventh Schedule under section 152. That schedule is the list of aggregator categories, from ride sharing and delivery through e-marketplaces, professional services, healthcare, travel, and content.

Do not fill a blank Gazette with an assumption. If the notification is not there, the checklist item is "not found," which is itself a finding.

## State gig-worker notifications

Karnataka's 2025 Act is deemed in force from 30 May 2025. Within six months of commencement the state was to notify the welfare-fee percentage, inside a band of 1 to 5 percent of payout per transaction. Ninety days from late September 2026 is time to confirm whether that notification exists and which category rates it sets, and whether the Payment and Welfare Fee Verification System is operating. Until that system is operational, the Act provides a quarterly self-reporting route for payouts. Also check whether the welfare board has been appointed. The Act establishes it from a date the state government chooses.

Rajasthan's 2023 Act commences on a date the state notifies, and its fee is a notified percent of transaction value excluding tax. The check is the commencement notification first, the fee notification second. The 12 percent yearly interest on delayed fees matters only once a fee is actually due. Bihar's Act 8 of 2025 has the same kind of commencement check, with room for different dates for different provisions. Telangana's Act 21 of 2026 was appointed to come into force on 2 June 2026, so the check there is the rules and the fee notification under that Act, not whether the Act itself has started.

## Payroll

Open the provisional payroll page on the EPFO site and read the latest month together with the revision of the month before it. The June 2025 release is the benchmark already in hand: a provisional net addition of 21.89 lakh members and about 10.62 lakh new subscribers, described then as the highest net addition since the series began in April 2018. Later files can rewrite that month. In the next ninety days, record three numbers from whatever release is current: new subscribers, exits, and rejoins. Net alone hides churn. Ignore any commentary that calls the net figure "jobs created." EPFO's own notes say the data are provisional, can include temporary employees, and must not be summed into a year-end headcount.

## The household survey

The National Statistics Office releases PLFS press notes on mospi.gov.in. The next one should be filed by concept before it is filed by headline. Write down whether the note is usual status or current weekly status, whether it is all-India or urban, and which ages it covers. The anchors already published are a usual-status unemployment rate of 3.2 percent for July 2023–June 2024, a current-weekly-status all-India unemployment rate of 4.9 percent for calendar 2024, and an urban weekly-status rate of 6.7 percent in that same calendar year. A new note that moves one of these is news. A new note that quotes a different concept is not a reversal.

## The register and the pension rail

e-Shram stood at 31.89 crore registrations as on 20 August 2026, with women at 54.28 percent. The previous official count in this set was 31,78,31,749 as on 14 July 2026. In the next ninety days, look for the following print and for any change in the fifteen schemes the 20 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply said were already mapped, or in the links to the National Career Service, the Skill India Digital Hub, and Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maandhan. A higher registration total without a change in scheme mapping is a wider list, not a wider benefit.

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority launched the NPS e-Shramik (Platform Service Partner) model on 29 October 2025, by the Economic Survey's account, to bring platform workers into the National Pension System. The ninety-day check is whether a subsequent PFRDA circular or ministry note reports how onboarding is proceeding. The launch date alone is not a coverage figure.

## What not to spend the ninety days on

Do not wait on a new estimate of the gig workforce. The NITI Aayog figures of 77 lakh workers in 2020–21 and 2.35 crore by 2029–30 are a 2022 model. They will not be updated by a routine circular. Do not treat a ministry speech as a substitute for the Gazette items above. The speech can tell you what the government intends. The notification tells you what a firm is required to pay, and from when.
