# State Finance and the 2026-27 Capital Investment Loan Cycle

State capital spending in India is no longer a line that appears once, on budget day. For 2026-27 it is a cycle of certificates, releases, utilization and a second release, written down by the Department of Expenditure on 27 March 2026. The capacity question is whether a state can move a project through that cycle inside the year.

## The loan and the ceiling

The guidelines, F. No. 44(1)/PF-S/2026-27 (CAPEX), allocate Rs 2,00,000 crore. Assistance is a 50-year interest-free loan to state governments and to Union Territories with legislatures. It sits over and above the normal net borrowing ceiling for 2026-27. Funds are to be used within 2026-27 unless a specific clause allows otherwise. The Ministry of Finance has run this special assistance since 2020-21. The March 2026 letter calls the 2026-27 round a redesigned scheme, continued because states asked for it and because the Centre wanted higher capital investment.

The Reserve Bank's 23 January 2026 study of state budgets had already told readers how this loan shows up in the accounts. The consolidated gross fiscal deficit was 3.3 percent of GDP in 2024-25, and budgeted at 3.3 percent again for 2025-26, after three years below 3 percent. RBI says the excess over 3 percent mainly reflects these 50-year loans, which are outside the normal ceiling and inside the deficit. Capital expenditure was 2.7 percent of GDP in 2023-24 and 2024-25, budgeted at 3.2 percent for 2025-26. The 2026-27 loan is the next turn of that wheel, at a larger notified allocation.

## How a rupee actually moves

States file every certificate and every project list on the PFMS module. There is no manual path. The signatory is the senior-most officer in the finance department. Ongoing projects that can finish inside 2026-27 get priority, and states must upload photographs of completed works and inaugurate them. For focus, a state prioritises at most five sectors.

Two mandatory conditions apply before any part pays out. The state certifies that Centrally Sponsored Schemes use their official names, including a correct local-language rendering, and follow Government of India branding instructions. The state also files a consolidated utilization certificate in Form 12-B of the General Financial Rules, 2017, for funds released in 2025-26 or earlier, through PFMS. A few named categories are outside that second condition, including unity malls, iconic tourist centres, working-women hostels, police housing, children and adolescent libraries and digital infrastructure, and the additional 2025-26 allocation for severe natural disasters.

Part I, untied, is Rs 75,000 crore. Rs 67,000 crore goes to states in proportion to their Sixteenth Finance Commission shares of central taxes. Rs 3,000 crore is for Union Territories with a legislature. Rs 5,000 crore clears balances on unity malls, police housing, libraries and digital infrastructure, iconic tourist centres, and working-women hostels. The first installment is 66 percent. The second, 34 percent, needs utilization of at least 75 percent of the first, closure of all Single Nodal Agency bank accounts of Centrally Sponsored Schemes, deposit of the unspent central share and interest into the Consolidated Fund of India, and upload of SASCI projects from 2020-21 to 2024-25. Unspent 2025-26 SASCI money is adjusted against the first installment, with the same named exceptions. Reconstruction funds released in 2025-26 for disasters of that year may submit utilization certificates up to 31 December 2026.

Part II is Rs 10,000 crore for the state or UT share of central infrastructure, including rail, metro, highway, power and airports, and of schemes such as AMRUT, PMGSY and Jal Jeevan Mission. It follows the same 66/34 and 75 percent pattern. States may also use Part I money for that share if they need to.

Part III is Rs 25,000 crore, shared in proportion to Part I, for states that grow capital expenditure by more than 10 percent. Half of it looks at 2025-26 over 2024-25. Half looks at the first six months of 2026-27 over the same months of 2025-26. SASCI releases are excluded. Only the state's own resources count. Where finance accounts are unavailable, the guideline points to the Comptroller and Auditor General's monthly key indicators.

## Why this is a capacity cycle

A state that cannot certificate, cannot spend 75 percent of the first slice, or cannot close scheme bank accounts will not see the second slice on the timetable the guideline describes. A state that parks its entire capex inside SASCI will also miss Part III, because that incentive ignores SASCI money by design. The Public Finance (States) Division is the office that already watches state borrowing under Article 293(3) and the Finance Commission path. The capex loan is additional. It is still administered as a conditional release.

The Fifteenth Finance Commission's 2021-26 design, with 41 percent vertical devolution, remains the backdrop of untied tax shares. The 2026-27 untied loan uses Sixteenth Finance Commission proportions. Readers watching the cycle should follow installments and utilization, not the February speech alone.
