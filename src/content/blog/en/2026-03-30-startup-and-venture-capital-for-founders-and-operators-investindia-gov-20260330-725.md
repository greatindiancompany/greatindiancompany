A DPIIT certificate is a key to named benefits. It is not a valuation, a round, or a three-year tax holiday. Founders who treat it as all three will discover the difference in a data room. The checks below use the Startup India recognition page, the 5 February 2026 PIB note on G.S.R. 108(E), the 13 February 2026 Rajya Sabha reply, and the Fund of Funds 2.0 guidelines.

## Before you apply

Confirm the legal form. The recognition page allows a private limited company, a registered partnership, an LLP, or a cooperative society. PIB’s 5 February note is explicit that multi-state cooperatives and state or Union Territory cooperatives can qualify. A sole proprietorship is not on that list.

Confirm age and turnover against the current notification, not against an old brochure. Ordinary recognition runs up to 10 years from incorporation, with turnover below ₹200 crore in any previous financial year. Deep-tech recognition runs up to 20 years and below ₹300 crore. The work has to be innovation or improvement, with a potential to generate employment or create wealth. An entity formed by splitting or reconstructing an existing business is out. Invest India’s brochure stored under a June 2019 file path still says turnover not exceeding ₹100 crore. That PDF is historical. The live test is on the Startup India page, which cites G.S.R. 108(E).

File the form yourself on the National Single Window System, as “Registration as a Startup”. The page says DPIIT has appointed no agency, representative, or franchise to issue the certificate, and that the ministry charges no fee. The toll-free line printed there is 1800 115 565, from 10:00 am to 5:30 pm. The page tells the startup to file in its own name. It does not describe a paid franchise for the certificate.

The application is self-certified. A false innovation claim is your fact pattern, not a detail DPIIT has pre-audited in the releases used here.

## After the certificate, open a second file for tax

The recognition page offers a further application under section 80-IAC. On that page the holiday is three consecutive financial years out of the first ten since incorporation. Only a private limited company or an LLP can apply, and only if incorporation is after 1 April 2016. A recognised partnership or cooperative does not clear this limb. A deep-tech firm recognised for 20 years does not, on this page, receive a 20-year holiday.

The same page also links to an exemption application under section 56, labelled angel tax. Use that link as the portal’s application route. Read the current Income-tax Act before you tell an investor the allotment is exempt. The portal and the Act can diverge; the Act is the one the assessing officer will use.

If you need Companies Act accommodations, the 13 February reply lists what MCA had extended to startups: optional cash-flow statement, two board meetings a year, sweat equity up to 50 percent of paid-up capital, ESOPs for promoters and certain directors, member deposits without the ordinary 35 percent cap, and a convertible note of ₹25 lakh or more that is convertible into equity or repayable within ten years and is not treated as a deposit. Match the instrument in your round to that note if you are relying on the deposit exemption. A smaller note is not described there.

## When the certificate and the money use different clocks

Recognition ends when the age or turnover test fails. Fund of Funds 2.0 guidelines say that if a fund committed an amount while the company was still recognised, the unpaid balance of that commitment can still be released afterwards. Ask the fund which date it uses. Do not assume the certificate and the contribution agreement expire together.

You do not apply to DPIIT for the equity. You become eligible for funds that DPIIT has backed. Those funds are SEBI-registered Category I or II alternative investment funds. Deep-tech treatment inside Fund of Funds 2.0 depends on the definition in G.S.R. 108(E), which the fund’s memorandum has to cite. If your company is a cooperative, check whether the fund’s documents can hold that form; the guidelines speak of DPIIT-recognised startups, and the fund’s own memorandum may be narrower.

GeM onboarding, the seed-fund incubator, and the credit guarantee are separate files. More than 38,600 startups had been onboarded on GeM by the April 2026 PIB account. The guarantee ceiling is ₹20 crore per borrower after the 2025-26 change. None of those facts replaces the certificate check above.
