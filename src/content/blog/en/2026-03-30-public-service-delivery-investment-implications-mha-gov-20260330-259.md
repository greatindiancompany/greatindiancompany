# What an investor can underwrite in India's service rails

Public service delivery in India does not sit in one capital-expenditure line that an investor can annualise. The [6 August 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635) of a Lok Sabha reply says Digital India is an umbrella of projects run by central ministries, departments, and states, each with its own budget. It also says the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology implements eight Central Sector sub-schemes under the programme, and that those sub-schemes have no state-wise allocation. A model that treats "MeitY digital India spend" as the market for state certificate portals is reading the wrong appropriation.

## Separate the public stock from the private wedge

The public stock is already named. DigiLocker, in that August reply, has over 71.66 crore registered users, more than 936.03 crore documents, and 2,822 issuers. UMANG offers 2,575 services for individuals. Direct Benefit Transfer covers 318 schemes of 56 ministries, with over Rs 52 lakh crore transferred. Common Service Centres numbered 5.01 lakh as of May 2026. These are operating platforms. They are not a pipeline of greenfield concessions.

The private wedge, where one exists, is integration and operations around those platforms. DigiLocker's [own note](https://blog.digilocker.gov.in/digilocker-an-initiative-towards-paperless-governance/) describes a consent-based exchange between an issuer and a requester, with real-time fetch from the issuer. A company that helps a board, a transport department, or a hospital become an issuer, or that verifies an issued document with consent, is working on a rail the government has already defined. A company that stores user-uploaded scans and calls them DigiLocker documents is working against that definition.

## Cash transfer is scheme money

The [DBT Mission](https://dbtbharat.gov.in/static-page-content/spagecont?id=1) places the programme in the Cabinet Secretariat. The cumulative transfer figures in 2026 PIB notes, including "over Rs 52 lakh crore" in the August Lok Sabha reply, measure benefits credited under schemes. They are not revenue of a payments company. What an investor can underwrite is the operational layer the Mission's materials already flag: bank accounts that can receive the credit, correct seeding on the Aadhaar Payments Bridge, and a business correspondent or branch that can pay cash out. Wrong seeding produces wrong credits. The Mission's standard operating procedure for the bridge says the bank is liable to make good those credits to the originating department.

Aadhaar is preferred on the Mission's about page and is not mandatory in DBT schemes as a class. Individual schemes funded from the Consolidated Fund can still require it under section 7 of the Aadhaar Act, as UIDAI's DBT FAQ states. Underwriting "Aadhaar is always required" or "Aadhaar is never required" both misread the texts.

## State implementation is the variance

e-District workflows are implemented by states. The August 2026 reply says certificates, pensions, and welfare schemes are delivered through state portals and mobile applications, and it names Tamil Nadu's e-Sevai portal as an end-to-end example. UMANG's 17 Tamil Nadu services in the same reply show that national mobile coverage and state catalogue depth are different investments. A national app integration can be live while the legally meaningful status still sits on the state system.

DARPG's NeSDA 2025 cycle, launched on a portal on 25 May 2026, will assess state, UT, city, and central portals, and a second class of service portals ([PIB, 28 May 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266244)). The release proposes 59 mandatory services for every state and UT. That assessment is a quality score. It is not a guarantee of procurement.

## What to refuse in a model

Refuse a total addressable market built by multiplying crore-scale user counts by an assumed fee. The platforms are public. CSC filing fees, where they exist, are a narrow assisted-access charge; CPGRAMS itself says the government does not charge to file a grievance. Refuse a single national capex forecast. Ask which budget, which state workflow, which issuer, and which failure (rejected upload, unmapped account, portal that informs but does not deliver) the company is paid to fix.
