# Rural and urban India set the pattern in women’s work

State rankings travel further than the table they came from. The split MoSPI prints in the national press note, and that any state row has to respect, is rural against urban. In calendar 2025, on usual status for women aged 15 and above, rural labour force participation was 45.9 percent and urban participation was 27.7 percent. The all-India rate of 40.0 percent is a weighted combination of those two, not a third kind of economy.

## The same women, two job structures

Participation is higher in rural areas. Regular wage employment is higher in urban areas. Among rural women workers in usual status, 70.7 percent were self-employed, 9.3 percent were in regular wage or salaried work, and 20.0 percent were in casual labour. Among urban women workers, 40.4 percent were self-employed, 50.9 percent were in regular wage or salaried work, and 8.7 percent were in casual labour.

A State that looks “high participation” on the usual-status rate can still have a small regular-wage share if its women workers are in rural self-employment. A city that looks “low participation” can have most of its women workers on a regular wage. Those are both official descriptions. A single state rank on participation hides the second fact.

Unemployment follows the same geography. Rural female unemployment on usual status was 2.1 percent in 2025. Urban female unemployment was 6.4 percent. The worker population ratio was 44.9 percent for rural women and 25.9 percent for urban women. High rural participation and low rural unemployment can coexist with self-employment and casual labour. Higher urban unemployment can coexist with a majority of urban women workers being on a regular wage.

## Weekly status narrows the rural lead, and does not remove it

On the current weekly status for calendar 2025, rural female participation was 38.3 percent and urban female participation was 25.9 percent. The rural lead shrinks when the window is seven days, which is what subsidiary and seasonal work would do. It does not reverse. In the April–June 2026 quarterly bulletin, also on the weekly status, Figure 3 places rural women at 37.2 percent and urban women at 25.0 percent. The previous quarter was 39.2 and 25.4. The quarter’s movement was rural. Urban stayed near 25 percent.

The older July–June design showed the same geography. In July 2023–June 2024, rural female usual-status participation was 47.6 percent and urban was 28.0 percent. That pair belongs to the pre-2025 sample. It should be compared with earlier July–June years, not subtracted from the 2025 calendar pair as if the survey had been held constant. MoSPI has said the 2025 estimates may not be strictly comparable with earlier annual reports.

## How to read a state table when you open the bulletin

The April–June 2026 quarterly bulletin says it provides state-level estimates of labour force participation, the worker population ratio and unemployment, on the weekly status, for selected States. The annual report carries a fuller state break. This brief does not reprint a state ranking from a secondary summary. The press note’s prose gives the all-India rural and urban cuts above, and those cuts are the pattern a state row has to be read against.

When a state figure is used, four labels have to travel with it.

- Status: usual (365 days, including subsidiary work) or current weekly (seven days). The 2025 usual-status all-India female rate is 40.0 percent. The weekly rate is 34.3 percent. A state can move by a similar gap with no change in its economy.
- Age: the headlines here are 15 and above. An all-ages rate is lower because children are in the denominator.
- Sector: rural and urban inside the State, not only the combined rate.
- Design year: July–June reports through 2023–24 are one series. Calendar 2025 is the redesigned survey. Quarterly bulletins before April–June 2025 do not contain rural areas.

A district programme, a plant location, or a state scheme that quotes one female participation rate without those four labels is quoting an average of unlike rows. The operational difference is already visible at the all-India rural and urban level: rural work is mostly self-employment and casual labour; urban women workers are mostly on a regular wage; the weekly rural rate moves with the quarter; the urban weekly rate has stayed near 25 percent.
