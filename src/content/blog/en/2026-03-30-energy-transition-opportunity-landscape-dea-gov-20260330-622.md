# The Opportunity Landscape in India's Energy Transition

An opportunity in this sector is a buyer with a rulebook, not a gap between today's capacity and a 2030 slogan. The lanes below are the ones the Government has actually funded or regulated. Several are already crowded. A few are still early, which is a different kind of opportunity and a different kind of risk.

## Households, through a portal

The buyer is a residential consumer with a distribution-company connection. The scheme is PM Surya Ghar, outlay ₹75,021 crore, implementation through 31 March 2027, target of one crore households and up to 300 units a month. Central financial assistance for households is the ₹65,700 crore core of that outlay. The household applies on pmsuryaghar.gov.in, picks a registered vendor, and the utility inspects the plant. MNRE publishes benchmark equipment prices on the portal so that the subsidy does not simply inflate the quote.

As of 12 August 2026, PIB reported 51.58 lakh households benefiting, ₹28,024 crore of subsidy transferred, and 14.8 GW commissioned under the scheme. National grid-connected rooftop of all kinds, not just this scheme, was 30.74 GW on 31 July 2026. The open commercial question is the second half of the one-crore target, in a scheme window that closes at the end of FY 2026-27. Vendors, lenders, and utility inspection teams are the businesses this lane actually needs. Another module brand is not the scarce piece. Module nameplate is already about 172 GW.

## Farms, through KUSUM

The buyer is a farmer or a distribution company solarising agricultural supply. Budget 2026-27 provides ₹5,000 crore for PM-KUSUM, and the scheme has been extended to 31 March 2027. MNRE's year-end review counted 25 lakh pumps installed or solarised, 13.93 lakh of them in FY 2025-26, when KUSUM added 7.67 GW. The product is pumps, feeder solarisation, and the service network around them. It is a rural electrical business with a dated subsidy window.

## Firms buying for their own meters

In FY 2025-26, MNRE put commercial, industrial, and captive solar at about 15 GW, roughly a third of solar added that year, alongside about 15 GW of power-purchase-agreement projects. The Green Energy Open Access Rules let a consumer with 100 kW of contracted load buy green power, and they set no load floor for captive plants. The renewable consumption obligation, 35.95 percent in 2026-27, gives designated consumers a reason to sign even when the voluntary premium is thin. Virtual power purchase agreements and a 500 MW contract-for-difference pilot are extra tools, not the main market. The buyer here is a company compliance officer as much as a discom tender committee.

## Utility parks, now a mature lane

Ground-mounted solar is 122.57 GW of the 164.59 GW solar stock. Rajasthan alone has 38.66 GW of it. This lane has developers, SECI and state tenders, and a transmission plan aimed at the same districts. The opportunity that is less mature is the one the Government just funded: the Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana, approved on 31 July 2026 with ₹5,070 crore, for 5,000 MW of floating solar and 10,000 MWh of co-located storage, to be sanctioned through FY 2030-31. Floating solar with a storage condition is a new specification. It is not another copy of a Rajasthan park.

Wind's open stock is the 28 GW the Government says is under implementation, on top of 58.14 GW installed. Offshore wind is a 1 GW viability-gap scheme of ₹7,453 crore. Small hydro has a newer scheme, approved in March 2026, of ₹2,584.60 crore through FY 2030-31, aimed at about 1,500 MW in projects of 1 to 25 MW, with an emphasis on the hills and the North-East. Himachal Pradesh already has 1,013 MW of small hydro and 11,571 MW of large hydro. The scheme is a top-up to a hydro map, not a blank slate.

## Factories, where modules are ahead of cells

Selling an undifferentiated module into a 172 GW nameplate industry is a hard version of this opportunity. Eight of twelve PLI manufacturers had started production somewhere in the chain by April 2026. The production-linked incentive pays for five years after commissioning on modules that are made and sold. The scarcer official targets are upstream: the ingot-and-wafer Approved List pathway announced for June 2028, and cells. For storage cells, ₹18,100 crore is the advanced-chemistry-cell envelope for 50 GWh, 40 GWh already awarded and 10 GWh bid out for grid-scale use, with capital-goods duty relief through March 2028.

## Molecules and gas from waste

Green hydrogen's buyer, in the Mission design, is industry, transport, and eventually export. The incentive pool is SIGHT, inside a ₹19,744 crore Mission. The installed production base PIB cited for February 2026 was about 8,000 tonnes a year. The opportunity is real and early. It should be sized off that stock and off whatever offtake contract exists, not off the 5 million tonne aim alone.

Compressed biogas is further along in registrations than in commissioning. Of 1,929 plants registered as of 13 August 2026, 217 were commissioned and 357 were under construction. GOBARdhan, approved on 6 August 2026 with ₹23,731 crore through FY 2035-36, is the new central scheme for that lane. Feedstock aggregation and plant completion are the work. A registration is not a plant.

## A way to choose

Match the lane to a buyer you can name: a household on the national portal, a farmer under KUSUM, a commercial consumer under open access, a discom tender, a PLI auction, or an industrial hydrogen offtaker. If the only buyer in the model is "India's 500 GW target," the landscape has not been read yet.
