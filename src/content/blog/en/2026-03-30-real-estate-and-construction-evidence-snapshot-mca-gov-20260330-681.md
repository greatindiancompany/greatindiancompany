# Which Official Series Actually Measure Indian Housing

A housing brief is only as good as the series it names. India publishes several. They answer different questions, on different calendars, with different coverage. This snapshot says what each one is for, and what this page refuses to copy.

## Registered prices at the Reserve Bank

The House Price Index is quarterly, built from registration-authority transactions, base year 2022-23, eighteen cities. The [24 August 2026 release](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63436) put the all-India index at 117.5 in Q1 2026-27, up 1.1 percent on the quarter and 3.6 percent on the year. The annual pace matched the year-earlier quarter. City history is on [DBIE](https://data.rbi.org.in/DBIE/#/dbie/home) under Statistics, Real Sector, Prices and Wages. The index does not measure listing prices, under-construction quoted rates, or any city outside the eighteen.

## Assessment, registration, and under-construction prices at NHB

[NHB RESIDEX](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/Dashboard/About) is the National Housing Bank's family of indices. It began in July 2007. Coverage grew to 26 cities by March 2015. Base years have been 2007, then FY 2012-13, then FY 2017-18 from April 2018 through the December 2025 quarter, then FY 2024-25 from the quarter ended March 2026, with backward linking so the older series can still be read. The about page puts current geographical coverage at 50 cities, including 18 capitals and 37 smart cities, and says the set is meant to expand past 100.

Three housing indices use three sources. Registered prices come from sub-registrar offices. Assessment prices come from lender valuations. Under-construction market prices come from a survey of developer stock in the primary market and exclude resale. The live dashboard computes assessment-price changes as a four-quarter moving average and labels that view with base year FY 2024-25. This snapshot does not freeze a city table from the dashboard. Open [residex.nhbonline.org.in](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/) for the quarter NHB is currently showing.

The same brand includes, in its stated scope, a housing rental index, land price indices, and building-materials price indices. Scope is not the same as a number in hand. Do not cite a land or cement level from this page.

NHB says the national composite, weighted by population, was built so RESIDEX could be compared internationally. That is a purpose statement. It is not a cross-country table.

## Bank credit, with a definition break

[Sectoral deployment for July 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63478), released 31 August 2026, covers 41 banks and about 95 percent of non-food credit. The highlights that touch property are: personal loans up 16.2 percent, with housing still in double-digit growth; services up 22.9 percent, supported by NBFCs, trade, and commercial real estate; industry up 20.0 percent, with infrastructure named among buoyant industries. Non-food credit itself was up 19.1 percent. The construction-industry percentage is not in the highlight list. It is in the statements.

From 31 December 2025, year-on-year rates compare end-of-month data with the previous year's older fortnight definition. Any chart of these rates needs that sentence in the caption.

## Mission progress and the project register

PMAY-U 2.0 progress is an administrative system: applications, sanctions by the state and central committees, geo-tagged stages, releases, and utilisation. The FAQ describes the system. The ministry dashboard is where the counts of sanctioned and occupied houses live. This page does not substitute a round number for that dashboard.

A project register is different again. Each state Real Estate Regulatory Authority publishes the projects and agents registered with it, the quarterly updates Section 11 requires, and the orders it passes. There is no single stock of "registered projects in India" in the July 2025 central FAQ, and this snapshot does not invent one. The FAQ does fix the rules a register entry is supposed to reflect: registration before sale, a separate account for seventy percent of allottee money, and an audit within six months of year-end.

## What not to mix

Do not place 117.5 next to an NHB index and call the gap a spread. Do not add housing-loan growth to commercial-real-estate growth and call it construction capex. Do not treat an application count as a completion count. The [price brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-market-signal-brief-mca-gov-20260330-201) stays with the RBI print alone. Great Indian Company publishes this snapshot so a citation is a series, a date, and a coverage line.
