## Skills are a published programme, jobs are not a single corridor census

Trade corridors need drivers, terminal operators, marine staff, planners, and compliance clerks. India's official record publishes a training and education build under the National Logistics Policy, and a coastal-community pillar under Sagarmala. It does not publish one national headcount of jobs "created by trade corridors." Treat a viral jobs figure as unsupported unless it names a release.

## What DPIIT has counted

The logistics policy, launched on 17 September 2022, included skill development and the place of logistics in higher education as part of its purpose. The Cabinet note at launch said training on PM GatiShakti and logistics was going onto the iGOT platform with the policy itself.

The 16 September 2025 stocktake is specific. More than 100 universities and institutes offer logistics-related courses. The Centre of Excellence for City Logistics at SPA Bhopal has trained more than 100 officials. More than 65,000 professionals were trained between 2023 and 2025, with new qualification packs approved. A memorandum with Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya opened undergraduate and postgraduate logistics programmes. More than 250 workshops have been held for government officials, with digital courses on iGOT.

On 3 January 2025 the commerce minister launched a 15-hour Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya course, "PM GatiShakti Concept for Efficient Infrastructure Planning and National Development," to be hosted on iGOT and the UGC SWAYAM portal and built into central and state training institutes. The same event gave the LEAPS 2024 education award to the Indian Institute of Management, Mumbai. LEAPS also named operating firms in rail freight, maritime freight, multimodal transport, warehousing, and logistics technology. Those are award categories, not employment totals.

DPIIT and GIZ have a study on women's participation in logistics. As of the September 2025 note, findings were under review. The commerce minister, releasing LEADS 2024, had already asked the sector to widen women's participation. The study is the formal next step. It is not yet a participation rate.

## Ports and the coastal workforce

Sagarmala's five pillars include coastal community development: skill development and livelihoods in fisheries, coastal tourism, and related work. Grant-in-aid under the scheme covers coastal skill development alongside fishing harbours, port modernisation, and urban water transport. The Sagarmala Startup Innovation Initiative, launched on 19 March 2025, supports startups in green shipping, smart ports, maritime logistics, shipbuilding technology, and sustainable coastal development. That is a skills-and-firm channel for maritime technology, with funding and mentorship named as the tools.

The March 2025 Sagarmala note also sets Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision targets of 4 million gross registered tonnage of shipbuilding capacity and 10 billion metric tonnes of annual port handling, with an aim to be among the top five shipbuilding nations by 2047. Those targets imply a future marine workforce. They are not a current payroll.

## Terminals hire when cargo shows up

Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals are private investments in handling points. By 7 August 2026, 142 were commissioned and had handled 146 million tonnes in 2025-26, with mechanised loading and unloading cited in the earlier March 2025 reply as a reason transit time can fall. Mechanisation changes the job mix inside a terminal. The releases do not say how many people each terminal employs.

For a founder, the practical map is the qualification packs and the 65,000-person training total, the iGOT and SWAYAM courses for public officials who clear projects, the maritime startup channel, and the terminal locations where cargo is already moving. For a policy team, the open item is the women's participation study and the nine state logistics policies that were still in draft in September 2025, because industry status and state policy are what make local hiring incentives real.
