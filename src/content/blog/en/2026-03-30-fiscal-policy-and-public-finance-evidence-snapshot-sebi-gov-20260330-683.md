This is a sheet, not a story. Every figure is an estimate, a revised estimate, or an unaudited provisional actual, and the column header is part of the figure. Mixing them is how fiscal writing goes wrong.

## Deficit and debt, percent of GDP

From the Medium-term Fiscal Policy Statement placed with the Union Budget in February 2026:

| Indicator | 2025-26 revised | 2026-27 Budget |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Fiscal deficit | 4.4 | 4.3 |
| Revenue deficit | 1.5 | 1.5 |
| Primary deficit | 0.8 | 0.7 |
| Gross tax revenue | 11.4 | 11.2 |
| Non-tax revenue | 1.9 | 1.7 |
| Central government debt | 56.1 | 55.6 |

The fiscal deficit in rupees for 2026-27 is ₹16.96 lakh crore. Central debt net of NSSF special securities issued for the states is 55.1 percent of GDP in the Budget estimates and 55.5 percent in the revised estimates. Nominal GDP underlying the Budget estimates is assumed to grow 10.0 percent.

## Where the Budget rupee comes from

The Key Features booklet’s receipt graphic adds to 100 percent:

| Source | Share |
| --- | ---: |
| Borrowings and liabilities | 24% |
| Income tax | 21% |
| Corporation tax | 18% |
| GST and other taxes | 15% |
| Non-tax revenue | 10% |
| Union excise duties | 6% |
| Customs | 4% |
| Non-debt capital receipts | 2% |

Gross tax revenue in the fiscal statement is ₹44.04 lakh crore, of which direct taxes are ₹26.97 lakh crore (61.2 percent) and indirect taxes ₹17.07 lakh crore. CGST is estimated at ₹10.19 lakh crore. Net tax revenue of the Centre, after devolution, is ₹28.67 lakh crore. States’ share of the divisible pool stays at 41 percent.

| Net tax revenue | 28,66,922 | 6,36,576 | 22.2% |

| Fiscal deficit | 16,95,768 | 3,07,833 | 18.2% |
| Revenue deficit | 5,92,344 | 2,578 | 0.4% |

The same page warns that the deficit to date is not the annual deficit.

## GST collections, provisional, as on 31 August 2026

From the GST system’s [monthly report as on 31 August 2026](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/news/aug_2026_gst_revenue_report_final_for_publishing_monthly.pdf). Amounts are ₹ crore. The report says the figures may change slightly on finalisation.

| | August 2025 | August 2026 | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gross domestic revenue | 1,25,570 | 1,37,249 | 9.3% |
| Gross import revenue | 48,546 | 62,604 | 29.0% |
| Total gross GST | 1,74,116 | 1,99,853 | 14.8% |
| Total refunds | 18,935 | 31,795 | 67.9% |
| Total net GST | 1,55,181 | 1,68,057 | 8.3% |

April–August gross GST was ₹10,42,757 crore, 11.0 percent above ₹9,39,724 crore a year earlier. Net GST for those five months was ₹8,89,523 crore, up 9.0 percent. Gross is not what the Centre keeps. Refunds, settlement to states, and the CGST/SGST/IGST split all sit between the gross number and the Budget’s CGST estimate.

## A few full-year expenditure anchors

Also from the fiscal statement, 2026-27 Budget estimates: total expenditure ₹53.47 lakh crore; revenue expenditure ₹41.25 lakh crore; capital expenditure ₹12.22 lakh crore, of which state capital assistance is ₹2.0 lakh crore; effective capital expenditure ₹17.15 lakh crore; interest ₹14.04 lakh crore; major subsidies ₹4.11 lakh crore; pensions ₹2.96 lakh crore. Gross dated market borrowing is ₹17.20 lakh crore and net is ₹11.73 lakh crore.

State governments are not in this sheet. Their consolidated gross fiscal deficit was 3.3 percent of GDP in 2024-25 provisional accounts and in 2025-26 budgets, in the Reserve Bank’s January 2026 study. That study is a different year and a different issuer. It is omitted here so this page does not pretend to be general government.

If a later monthly account or a final GST print revises a cell, replace the cell. Do not average it with the estimate.
