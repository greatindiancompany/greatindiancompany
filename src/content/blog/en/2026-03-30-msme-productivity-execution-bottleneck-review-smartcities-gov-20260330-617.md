# Where MSME productivity programmes stall

India's MSME productivity programmes are built. The stalls show up in the official funnels, where a large top and a smaller finished count are both published. The bottleneck is the conversion, and each conversion depends on an institution that is not the firm.

## A guarantee still needs a bank

CGTMSE can cover collateral-free credit to micro and small enterprises up to ₹10 crore on guarantees approved on or after 1 April 2025. The trust was set up by the Ministry of MSME and SIDBI. The member lending institution sanctions the loan. The 26 June 2026 Ministry note records a large approval flow — 29.03 lakh guarantees and ₹3.77 lakh crore between 1 January and 30 November 2025 — which shows the window is used. It does not show how many applications a given bank declined, or how many sanctioned limits were drawn for equipment rather than rolled working capital. Anyone managing a state credit target has to ask the bank those questions. The scheme page will not answer them.

Udyam Assist was built so informal micro enterprises could be seen for priority-sector lending. A certificate that banks are allowed to use is not a sanction. The stall, where it exists, is at the branch after the certificate.

## A filing is not a payment

Samadhaan routes delayed-payment applications to Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Councils. As on June 2026 the Ministry counted 2,56,892 applications claiming ₹55,244.29 crore, and 58,148 cases disposed. Disposal is the council's act. The residual stock is the execution fact. The 14 May 2026 note says 161 councils exist, and that an Online Dispute Resolution portal opened in June 2025 to shorten the path. A portal launch moves the queue onto a screen. It clears the queue only as councils decide.

The Trade Receivables Discounting System is the other cash path. Factoring on it counts as priority sector. Budget 2026-27, in the 14 May note, mandates it for central public sector enterprises. A mandate on the buyer side still needs financiers, onboarded sellers, and invoices that buyers accept. States that sign platform agreements, as several have described under RAMP, are doing that onboarding work. The agreement is the start of the stall's removal, not the end.

## Certification and clusters finish slowly

ZED registrations, as on May 2026, stood above 93.61 lakh. Certifications stood above 6.68 lakh. Lean registrations stood above 65,647 and certifications near 18,961. The productivity effect the schemes describe — less waste, cleaner process, audited quality — sits with the certified group. Outreach that stops at registration has not finished the scheme.

Cluster infrastructure has a similar shape. Of 612 Micro and Small Enterprises Cluster Development Programme projects approved as on June 2026, 364 were completed. In 2025-26, up to 20 November 2025, 11 projects costing ₹253.23 crore were approved and two were completed. Common facility centres raise productivity when they operate. An approved project is a future facility.

## Reimbursement trails the state plans

RAMP is where centre and state execution meet. Plans from all 36 states and Union Territories were evaluated. The 26 June 2026 note says 398 proposals worth ₹3,211.75 crore were approved and more than 55 lakh MSMEs had been reached, while reimbursement against completed deliverables stood at 50 percent of the programme target. The stall to watch into the end of the 2022–27 programme window is that reimbursement ratio, alongside how many approved projects are actually delivered.

The direction of these programmes, as distinct from the stalls, is in [the mid-term path](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/msme-productivity-mid-term-trajectory-smartcities-gov-20260330-537).
