# Where payments capital sits once UPI is a public rail

The investable question in Indian financial digitalization is not whether UPI is large. The Press Information Bureau's [August 2026 factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?id=150962&lang=1) already puts July 2026 at 2,365.8 crore transactions and ₹29.87 lakh crore. The question is which layer a company is allowed to own, and which layer is a shared system with a rulebook instead of an equity story.

## The rail is not the cap table

UPI is run by the National Payments Corporation of India. MeitY presents it as public digital infrastructure on the [Digital India UPI page](https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/initiative/unified-payment-interface-upi/). Banks and apps connect to it. A startup does not buy the switch. Returns, if they exist, sit in distribution, merchant service, credit, or data, under a licence that matches the activity.

That split is why a payments pitch and a lending pitch should not share one set of metrics. NPCI's [monthly UPI table](https://www.npci.org.in/product/upi/product-statistics) measures transfers. It does not measure net interest margin, loss given default, or the fee a payment aggregator is allowed to take.

## Three balance-sheet floors that are written down

A non-bank payment aggregator, under the [Regulation of Payment Aggregators Directions, 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896) of 15 September 2025, needs a minimum net worth of ₹15 crore when it applies and ₹25 crore by the end of the third financial year after authorisation. That net worth is maintained on an ongoing basis. The applicant is a company incorporated in India. A bank does not need this authorisation to carry on the same business. A payment gateway that only routes a transaction and never handles funds is defined separately from an aggregator. Capital is required where customer money is aggregated and then settled to merchants, and those funds sit in an escrow account with rules on what the aggregator may do with them.

An NBFC-account aggregator, under the [Account Aggregator Directions, 2016](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=10598), updated as on 6 September 2024, needs net owned funds of not less than ₹2 crore. The leverage ratio cannot be more than seven. The company does no other business. It does not support customer transactions. Customer financial information does not reside with it. It cannot hire a third party to perform the aggregation itself. From an investment view, this is a consent utility with a narrow revenue right, not a data warehouse and not a lender. NBFC-account aggregators stay in the base layer of the scale-based regulatory structure.

A default loss guarantee in digital lending, under the [Digital Lending Directions, 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12848), cannot exceed five per cent of the amount disbursed from a fixed, identifiable set of loans. The cover is cash, a lien-marked fixed deposit, or a bank guarantee. Once invoked, it is not reinstated by later recovery. A regulated entity that provides the guarantee deducts the outstanding amount from its own capital. The lender still recognises non-performing assets loan by loan. Five per cent is a contract cap, not a business model in which a platform's equity silently warehouses the credit risk.

## What a diligent investor checks before the narrative

Ask which legal person disburses the loan and which bank account receives it. If the answer is the platform, the structure is outside the directions. Ask whether a multi-lender screen, required since 1 November 2025, shows every matching offer or only the preferred one. Ask whether a digital lending app on a website is the same app certified on the Reserve Bank's Centralised Information Management System. The Reserve Bank does not verify that filing. A logo that implies otherwise is a disclosure problem, not a moat.

Ask whether credit on UPI is a bank's pre-sanctioned line. The [4 September 2023 circular](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12532), updated on 12 February 2025, allows scheduled commercial banks, including small finance banks after that update, to let a customer pay from such a line. The bank's board sets the terms. It is not a licence for a non-bank to invent a limit inside a UPI handle.

The Digital Rupee does not change this map into a new float business. In the retail pilot announced on 29 November 2022, e₹-R does not earn interest and converts into deposits. It is a central-bank token distributed by banks. Holding it is not a yield strategy.
