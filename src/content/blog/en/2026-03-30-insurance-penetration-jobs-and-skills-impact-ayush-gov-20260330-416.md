# India's Insurance Distribution Grew, and Life-Agent Churn Stayed High

Penetration depends on who can actually sell and service a policy. IRDAI counts that workforce in the [Annual Report 2024-25](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620). The counts are large, and in life insurance they turn over quickly.

## Three agent books, not one headcount

On 31 March 2025, individual life agents numbered 31.23 lakh, against 28.95 lakh a year earlier, a rise of 7.87 percent. The public-sector life insurer had 14.87 lakh of them, up 5.10 percent. Private life insurers had 16.37 lakh, up 10.53 percent. Women were 34.42 percent of life agents. Men were 65.58 percent.

During 2024-25, life insurers appointed 11.15 lakh agents and terminated 8.87 lakh. Net growth and heavy replacement are both in that pair of figures. A skills story that cites only the year-end stock of 31.23 lakh misses the replacement the report prints directly.

General insurers had 7,73,107 agents on 31 March 2025, up 12.17 percent. IRDAI says specialised insurers have no agents. Of the general-insurer agents, 75 percent are men and 25 percent are women.

Stand-alone health insurers had 15.49 lakh individual agents, up 18.66 percent. Of those, 71 percent are men and 29 percent are women.

Add those three individual-agent stocks and you still do not reach the distribution figure PIB uses. The [23 April 2026 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) says the distribution network grew from about 48 lakh in FY 2020-21 to nearly 83 lakh in FY 2024-25, and that 74 insurers now work with a network of over 83 lakh agents, point-of-sale persons, and institutional partners. The 83 lakh figure includes categories the agent tables do not. Quote them side by side. Do not collapse them into one jobs number.

## Offices are the other workplace

Life offices were 12,210 on 31 March 2025. General insurers operated from 8,078 offices. Stand-alone health offices were 1,788. PIB's combined office count is 22,076, up from 21,338. Around 60 percent of life offices and 72.18 percent of general-insurance offices sit in Tier I towns. The people who sell insurance are more numerous than the offices, and both are thicker in large population centres. The rural obligation is a Gram Panchayat cover rule, described in the annual report, not a published census of rural agents.

## What this means for hiring and training

The official record supports three operating facts. The agent stock is growing fastest at stand-alone health insurers. Life distribution replaces a large share of its agency each year. Women are roughly a third of life agents and a smaller share of general and health agency forces. The record does not publish wages, pass rates, or a standard training hour-count. A workforce plan that needs those inputs has to look past this brief, at insurer disclosures and the regulations on intermediaries, rather than at a national penetration ratio.
