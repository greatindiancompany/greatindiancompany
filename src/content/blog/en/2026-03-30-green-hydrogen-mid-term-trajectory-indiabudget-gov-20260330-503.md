# India’s 2030 Green Hydrogen Aim, Set Next to Capacity Already Awarded

The mid-term marker India has published is 2030. The path from a 2026 award to that marker is a set of smaller official numbers. It is not a smooth ramp, and adding the numbers together without labels will invent one.

## The aim, as restated in 2026

On 17 June 2026, Minister Pralhad Joshi said the Mission aims to establish 5 million tonnes of green hydrogen production capacity, supported by 125 GW of renewable capacity, catalysing investment exceeding ₹8 lakh crore, creating over 6 lakh jobs, and reducing annual carbon emissions by 50 million tonnes. The Press Information Bureau note of 12 November 2025 states the same 5 million tonne aim and the same 125 GW and investment language, and adds an expectation that fossil-fuel imports fall by more than ₹1 lakh crore. These sentences are aims. A minister repeating them in 2026 does not convert them into capacity already built. The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) states the objective as a global hub for production, use, and export, and states the outlay. It is the later notes that attach the 5 million tonne figure to 2030 in the form used here.

## The award that can be filed

The same June speech says that under SIGHT, incentives have been awarded for 8,62,000 tonnes a year of green hydrogen production. That is 0.862 million tonnes set next to an aim of 5 million. The November 2025 note had described 862,000 tonnes a year allocated to companies as of May 2025. The tonne figure is stable across those two statements. It remains an incentive award. It is the number a planner can file as “support awarded,” and it is well below the aim.

Narrower slices sit beside it, and they should stay labelled. The 11 August 2026 refinery list is 30,000 tonnes a year at Panipat, Bina, Vizag, and Numaligarh. That is industrial offtake under Mode-2B. It is not an extra 5 million tonnes, and it should not be added to the 8,62,000 unless a document says the 30,000 sits outside that award. This brief does not have that document, so the figures stay in separate sentences. JSW’s 3,600 tonnes a year, cited in the June speech as commissioned in November 2025, is smaller again, and it is the rare figure described as commissioned.

Electrolyser manufacturing is the equipment path toward the aim: 15 companies, 3,000 MW a year of capacity, on the June speech and the November note. Factory capacity awarded is the means. It is not tonnes of hydrogen.

## Hub figures for 2030, in the units Parliament used

The 5 August 2026 Lok Sabha annex says Pudimadaka’s planned hydrogen output for 2030 is 600 tonnes a day, written as 0.21 million tonnes a year. Tuticorin’s three land allotments carry expected capacities of 0.1, 0.22, and 0.5 million tonnes a year. Paradip’s 4.0 million tonnes a year is the handling capacity of a jetty, not hydrogen production. Adding a jetty’s cargo rating to a production aim inflates the trajectory. The [annex](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2987_27NWXF.pdf) is careful enough to keep those units apart. A chart should be equally careful.

## What would have to move

The tools on the mission page that could bend the path are a year-wise minimum consumption share for designated consumers, decided by the Empowered Group; competitive procurement; the manufacturing awards; and port clusters so that production sits near use or export. The [schemes list](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/) shows the guidelines those tools use. Whether they close the distance from under 1 million tonnes of awarded production incentives to 5 million tonnes is the mid-term question. The documents do not show it as already closed.

Great Indian Company keeps 5 million tonnes labelled as the aim, and 8,62,000 tonnes labelled as incentives awarded, so a 2030 chart has two lines.
