# From Make in India to production-linked incentives

India did not replace one industrial policy with another on a single day. The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) still runs several instruments side by side. Treating "Make in India" and "PLI" as synonyms is the mistake this note is for.

## Make in India is a facilitation mandate

DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 says Make in India was launched on 25 September 2014 to facilitate investment, foster innovation, build infrastructure, and make India a hub for manufacturing, design, and innovation. The report says the programme is now focused on 27 sectors under Make in India 2.0, with ministries and state governments working on 24 sub-sectors chosen for competitive strength, import substitution, export potential, and employability. The listed sub-sectors include furniture, air-conditioners, leather and footwear, auto components, electronics, steel, textiles, ethanol, toys, drones, and medical devices, among others. The report is the [DPIIT Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/3d9c9c2daeefb97bb9ce964370938b71.pdf).

That list is not an application window. It is a coordination map. Investment outreach runs through ministries, state governments, and Indian missions, with Invest India doing identification and handholding. Project Development Cells in ministries were approved to build a pipeline of investible projects with the states.

## Procurement is a second tool

The Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) Order, 2017 was issued on 15 June 2017 under the General Financial Rules. The annual report says it was last amended on 19 July 2024. It applies to goods, services, and works bought by central government procuring entities. Class-I local suppliers are those with at least 50 percent local content. Class-II local suppliers are those with at least 20 percent and less than 50 percent. For tenders below Rs. 200 crore, a global tender enquiry needs approval. Purchase preference for Class-I suppliers is set out in the order, including a 20 percent margin.

The annual report also says a manufacturer of an item under a PLI scheme is treated as a deemed Class-II local supplier for that item unless local content already meets the Class-I threshold. The sentence in the report continues into a proviso. Read the order itself before using the deemed status in a bid.

## PLI pays on production, and another ministry usually runs it

The 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf) says PLI schemes for 14 sectors were approved with an outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore. The stated aims are manufacturing capability, investment, exports, employment, and lower import dependence. DPIIT coordinates and monitors. The administrative ministry implements the scheme in its sector.

That is the structural change from a branding-and-facilitation mission to a claim-based incentive. The annual report, written earlier, states an outlay of Rs. 1.97 lakh crore for 14 sectors. The July 2026 reply is the later official outlay. Quote the date with the number.

A 21 July 2026 PIB note, [PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008), says implementation is reviewed by the Empowered Group of Secretaries, chaired by the Cabinet Secretary, and that some schemes have been modified after feedback. Modification is part of the design, not a sign that the scheme list is closed or open.

## Corridors are a fourth track

Industrial land and trunk infrastructure sit in the National Industrial Corridor Development Programme, under DPIIT through the National Industrial Corridor Development Corporation. The 30 August 2024 PIB note, [PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136), describes node approvals and state joint ventures. A PLI approval does not allot a plot in Dholera, AURIC, or any other node.

If you only remember one distinction: Make in India tells the system to attract and facilitate factories. The procurement order changes who can win a central tender. PLI pays approved applicants against production rules written by a line ministry. Corridors try to put serviced land on the map.
