The outlook is a stack of instruments with different legal force. Reading only the newest press note makes the stack look like one regime. It is not.

## Already binding, as of 24 September 2026

**CERT-In, 28 April 2022.** Directions under section 70B of the IT Act. The named entities report the incidents in Annexure I within six hours of noticing them or being told of them. Annexure I includes attacks or suspicious activity affecting systems related to artificial intelligence and machine learning, and it includes data breach and data leak. Logs of ICT systems are kept for a rolling 180 days inside Indian jurisdiction. Failure to furnish information can be dealt with under section 70B(7). This is cybersecurity direction. It is not the DPDP penalty Schedule.

**Reserve Bank, 6 April 2018.** Payment-system data stored only in India, with the foreign-leg exception and the FAQ's 24-hour return if processing happens abroad. This binds system providers under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act. It is the payments rule, explained against section 16 in the [cross-border note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-india-vs-global-benchmark-meity-gov-20260330-270).

**SEBI, 2019 reporting.** A SEBI board memorandum of December 2024 records that circulars already required reporting of AI and machine-learning systems offered to investors: stock brokers and depository participants (4 January 2019), market infrastructure institutions (31 January 2019), and mutual funds and their trustees and asset managers (9 May 2019). That is a reporting duty for those regulated persons. On 20 August 2024 SEBI issued the Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework. Regulated entities remain accountable for third-party services, including confidentiality, integrity, and availability of their data and logs. A clarification circular followed on 31 December 2024.

The same December 2024 memorandum describes a consultation paper of 13 November 2024 that would make a regulated person solely responsible for the privacy, security, and integrity of investor data when it uses AI tools, including third-party tools, and for the output. The memorandum says the amendments were to be notified after the due process. This brief does not treat that proposal as a regulation in force. Check the SEBI website for a notification later than this reading before you act as if it is.

**MeitY advisory, superseding the 1 March 2024 note.** The advisory MeitY published in supersession of the 1 March 2024 due-diligence note tells intermediaries and platforms that use of an AI model, a large language model, or generative AI on their computer resource should not permit unlawful content under rule 3(1)(b) of the IT Rules, 2021. Under-tested or unreliable models are to be made available to users in India only after labelling the possible fallibility of the output. A consent popup, or an equivalent, may be used to say that explicitly. The advisory says it is in addition to the advisory of 26 December 2023. It is an advisory under the due-diligence heading. It is not a section of the DPDP Act, and it is not a licence to launch a model.

## In force as machinery, not yet as a fiduciary duty

From 13 November 2025, the DPDP Act's definitions, the provisions that allow the government to establish the Data Protection Board, the rule-making power, the TRAI Act amendment that names TDSAT as the appellate tribunal, and the substituted RTI clause are in force. Rules 17 to 21, on how the Chairperson and members are searched for and how the Board meets, are in force. Section 18 still says the Board is established from a date the Central Government notifies. This set of briefs does not cite a notification that names the Chairperson or the headquarters. Do not describe the Board as open for complaints until that paper is in hand.

## Not yet the general duty

Notice, consent, legitimate uses, security safeguards, children's data, Significant Data Fiduciary obligations, cross-border transfer, and the penalty chapters wait until 13 May 2027. Consent Manager registration waits until 13 November 2026. PIB's 14 November 2025 note calls the package full operationalisation with an eighteen-month phase. The gazette is the phase. The [bottleneck note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-execution-bottleneck-review-meity-gov-20260330-590) stays on that difference.

## Not statutes

The India AI Governance Guidelines, unveiled on 5 November 2025, set out seven principles, recommendations across six pillars, a short, medium, and long-term action plan, and practical guidelines. PIB records the Principal Scientific Adviser describing the spirit as "Do No Harm." The guidelines say the seven principles were adapted from the Reserve Bank's FREE-AI committee report. That report was placed on the Reserve Bank's website on 13 August 2025. The press release describes seven sutras and 26 recommendations under six pillars. It is a committee report. It is not a direction to banks. The guidelines are not a direction to every company.

A founder who needs the live list in operating order should use the [founder note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-for-founders-and-operators-meity-gov-20260330-710).
