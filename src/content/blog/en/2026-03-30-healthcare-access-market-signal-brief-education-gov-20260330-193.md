## The purchaser is buying listed admissions

Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY is a public purchaser of hospitalization, not a general subsidy for every service a private hospital sells. The National Health Authority’s about-page states a cover of ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary care, cashless at the point of service, across empanelled public and private hospitals. The expenses wrapped into that cover, on the same page, include examination, medicines and consumables, ward and ICU care, diagnostics, implants where needed, accommodation, food, complications, and follow-up for up to 15 days after discharge. Pre-hospitalization is included for up to three days. Pre-existing conditions are covered from day one. There is no family-size cap.

That bundle is the product. A private hospital that empanels is agreeing to deliver listed procedures at listed rates and to collect from the scheme, not from the eligible patient at the counter. Outpatient primary care sits on the other side of Ayushman Bharat, at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, and is described as free to users in the catchment. A hospital business built on walk-in consultations is reading a different market from the one PM-JAY is signalling.

## Package lists on the official page do not match each other

The Authority’s about-page says services include approximately 1,929 procedures. Further down, the same page says the scheme covers more than 1,300 packages whose prices the Authority has fixed. Both sentences are on the official page. They are not a licence to quote either number as the live fee schedule. The page also says states may expand the package list and revise prices within a limit, because disease profile and costs differ. Anyone pricing a hospital should read the current package master for that state, then read which procedures the state has reserved for public facilities.

Reservation is an explicit signal. The Authority says some conditions are kept for public hospitals so that private providers do not take work government facilities can do, and that states may revise that reserved list. A private hospital can be empanelled and still be barred from a procedure. Public hospitals, the page says, are reimbursed at par with private hospitals. The scheme is trying to put a public provider and a private provider on the same rate card for the work both are allowed to do.

## Portability and the 70-plus extension

Benefits are portable: an eligible person can use an empanelled hospital in another state. For a hospital in a city that draws patients from elsewhere, the relevant fact is whether the transaction system accepts an out-of-state beneficiary and which state agency settles the claim. The about-page describes national portability as an information-system feature. Settlement still has two governments in it. The [state-pattern note](/blog/healthcare-access-state-wise-patterns-education-gov-20260330-313) covers trust, insurance, and mixed modes, which decide who actually pays the hospital.

The Cabinet decision of 12 September 2024, recorded on the Authority’s homepage, extends cover to every citizen aged 70 or above irrespective of income. The senior-citizen FAQ caps that benefit at ₹5 lakh on a family basis for those members. Households that were outside the SECC 2011 bottom 40 percent can now appear as PM-JAY patients once someone in the family crosses 70. That is a demand signal aimed at age and case mix. It is not a forecast of occupancy.

## What the signal does not say

The about-page argues that purchased care could pull private capacity toward smaller cities, and that public hospitals could earn scheme revenue to fill service gaps. Those are aims. They are not a census of new hospitals. The page also carries older National Health Accounts 2015 figures, including about 1.2 percent of GDP and 62 percent out-of-pocket spending. Those sentences are labelled 2015 on the page. They are a poor stand-in for this year’s payer mix.

Empanelment can be withdrawn. The Authority’s revised empanelment and de-empanelment guideline, issued in 2021, places hospital approval with state agencies under national rules, and allows the Authority to empanel directly in specified cases. A market that includes de-empanelment is a contract market. Quality failures and claim disputes are part of the commercial terms, which the [regulatory note](/blog/healthcare-access-regulatory-outlook-education-gov-20260330-433) separates from clinical-establishment law.
