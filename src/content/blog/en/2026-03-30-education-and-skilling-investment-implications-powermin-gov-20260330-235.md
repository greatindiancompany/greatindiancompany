Private capital in Indian education and skilling sits next to a public budget, not instead of it. The useful question is which public line is paying for a service a private operator might deliver, which line is building a public asset, and which announced sum was revised down before it was spent. The documents are the Union Budget 2026-27 expenditure statements on [indiabudget.gov.in](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/sumsbe.pdf).

## The education ministry's shape

Net of receipts and recoveries, the Ministry of Education's 2026-27 budget estimate is ₹1,39,289.48 crore. The Department of School Education and Literacy is ₹83,562.26 crore. The Department of Higher Education is ₹55,727.22 crore. School education is the larger cheque.

Inside school education, the [Expenditure Profile](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/vol1.pdf) shows centrally sponsored schemes at ₹63,010.02 crore for 2026-27, after a 2025-26 budget estimate of ₹62,660 crore that was revised to ₹53,730.02 crore. Samagra Shiksha, the main school scheme, is ₹42,100 crore in the 2026-27 budget estimate in [Statement 12](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/stat12.pdf), against a 2025-26 budget estimate of ₹41,250 crore and a revised estimate of ₹38,000 crore. PM SHRI, the exemplar-school programme, is ₹7,500 crore for 2026-27, after a 2025-26 budget estimate of ₹7,500 crore that was revised to ₹4,500 crore. Actual spending in 2024-25 was ₹3,504.30 crore.

The pattern matters more than any one line. A budget estimate is an intention. The revised estimate is what the year actually supported. In 2025-26 both Samagra Shiksha and PM SHRI were revised down. A private partner pricing a multi-year school contract off the budget-estimate speech is pricing the wrong column.

Central schools are a separate public system, not a residual. Statement 12 puts the Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan at ₹10,129.41 crore and the Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti at ₹6,025 crore in the 2026-27 budget estimate. PM POSHAN, the meal programme, is ₹12,749.99 crore. These are operating systems with their own scale. They are not a market waiting to be "disrupted" by a generic app.

Higher education's centrally sponsored line, the annual channel associated with PM-USHA, moves from a 2025-26 budget estimate of ₹1,815 crore to a revised estimate of ₹800 crore, then to ₹1,850 crore in 2026-27. A Lok Sabha reply records a separate multi-year PM-USHA outlay of ₹12,926.10 crore for 2023-24 to 2025-26, including committed liabilities of the earlier RUSA phase. Do not add that multi-year outlay to the annual budget estimate and call the sum "the market."

## The skill ministry is smaller, and lumpier

The [Demand for Grants of the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/sbe92.pdf) shows a net total of ₹9,885.80 crore for 2026-27. That compares with a 2025-26 budget estimate of ₹6,100.10 crore and a revised estimate of ₹2,703.54 crore. Actual net expenditure in 2024-25 was ₹2,774.70 crore. The ministry's own gross figures are higher because of recoveries, including transfers through the Madhyamik and Uchchtar Shiksha Kosh. Quote the net line unless you are specifically discussing the gross.

Two scheme facts sit inside that net. The Skill India Programme, which the demand describes as PMKVY 4.0, the apprenticeship promotion scheme, and Jan Shikshan Sansthan, is ₹2,800 crore in the 2026-27 budget estimate, ₹2,700 crore in the 2025-26 budget estimate, and ₹2,000 crore in the revised estimate. Centrally sponsored schemes, dominated by PM-SETU, were revised from ₹3,049.90 crore to ₹367 crore in 2025-26, and are estimated at ₹6,140.50 crore for 2026-27. A Rajya Sabha reply on 1 April 2026 said PM-SETU's 2025-26 phase was preparatory, fund release depends on a special purpose vehicle, and state proposals had not yet reached the national committee. The revised estimate is what that preparatory year looked like in money.

PM-SETU's design, in that reply, is an upgrade of 1,000 government ITIs in a hub-and-spoke model, 200 hubs and 800 spokes, plus capacity work at five National Skill Training Institutes. An earlier release put the ITI count at 14,688, against 9,776 in 2014. Upgrading 1,000 government ITIs is a capital project inside a much larger training network. It is not a purchase of the whole network.

## What this implies for a private balance sheet

School delivery at national scale is financed as a centrally sponsored scheme shared with states, plus central school organisations. A company can sell textbooks, technology, meals logistics, teacher development, or school-complex services into that system. It should assume tender cycles, state co-financing, and revised estimates that come in below the speech.

Short-term training and apprenticeships are a smaller public purse with sharper year-to-year cuts. Revenue that exists only while a PMKVY target is approved is scheme revenue. The common norms rate cited for the highest new-age courses, ₹49 per hour as on 30 June 2026, is a cost ceiling in a public scheme, not a market price you can mark up at will.

Higher education is the mixed zone. Public universities and public grants still anchor the system. Private universities and colleges exist inside UGC and AICTE rules, and inside AISHE's self-reported census. An investment case that needs a national "edtech market size" will not find one in these budget documents. What it will find is the public line item the customer is actually spending, and the column, budget or revised, that the customer managed to use.
