# State Patterns in India's Energy Transition

India's energy transition is concentrated. A national total of 291,725.64 MW of renewable capacity, including large hydro, as on 31 July 2026, hides five different state systems. The figures below are location-based installed capacity from MNRE's state sheet of that date. They describe where the plant stands, not which distribution company buys the power.

## The western and southern build-out

Five states hold most of the variable renewable fleet.

Rajasthan's renewable total is 50,335.34 MW, and 44,135.33 MW of that is solar. Ground-mounted solar alone is 38,661.99 MW. Rooftop solar is 2,315.60 MW, and the solar component of hybrids is 2,283.30 MW. Wind is 5,541.24 MW. Rajasthan is a ground-mounted solar state with a secondary wind fleet.

Gujarat's renewable total is higher, at 52,610.17 MW, with a broader mix: 33,763.96 MW solar, 16,613.06 MW wind, and 1,990 MW large hydro. Rooftop solar inside Gujarat's solar total is 7,518 MW, the largest rooftop stock on the sheet. Hybrid solar is 1,531.18 MW. Gujarat is the state where utility solar, wind, and rooftops are all large at once.

Maharashtra's renewable total is 33,194.82 MW: solar 20,347.66 MW, of which rooftop is 5,975.80 MW, wind 6,415.91 MW, bio-power 2,999.97 MW, and large hydro 3,047 MW. The bio-power row is mostly bagasse cogeneration, at 2,907.30 MW. Maharashtra is what a large industrial and agricultural state looks like when several technologies scale together.

Tamil Nadu's renewable total is 29,802.32 MW. Wind is 12,299.49 MW, still one of the two great wind fleets, beside Gujarat. Solar is 14,121.46 MW, almost all of it ground-mounted (12,409.75 MW) rather than rooftop (1,635.80 MW). Large hydro is 2,203.20 MW.

Karnataka's renewable total is 27,441.74 MW: solar 11,635.52 MW, wind 8,915.24 MW, large hydro 3,689.20 MW, and small hydro 1,284.73 MW. Andhra Pradesh follows at 16,675.43 MW, with solar 8,144.32 MW, wind 4,461.58 MW, and large hydro 3,290 MW. Madhya Pradesh is at 12,748.71 MW, with solar 6,544.09 MW and wind 3,686.45 MW.

Add Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka and the renewable total is about 193.4 GW, roughly two-thirds of the national renewable stock including large hydro. The transmission plan for 2030 was drawn around this geography: renewable-energy zones such as Fatehgarh, Bhadla, and Bikaner in Rajasthan, Khavda in Gujarat, and zones in Andhra Pradesh, plus offshore wind off Gujarat and Tamil Nadu.

## Hydro states are a different transition

Himachal Pradesh's renewable total is 12,979.97 MW, of which large hydro is 11,571.02 MW and small hydro is 1,013.46 MW. Solar is 385.29 MW. Uttarakhand's sheet is also hydro-led, with large hydro of 5,035.35 MW inside a 6,322.53 MW renewable total. Sikkim's renewable total is 2,344.67 MW against solar of 7.56 MW.

These states already run on renewable electricity in the capacity sense MNRE uses, because large hydro is inside the renewable total. Their policy problem is not "how to reach a first solar park." It is how to operate hydro as flexibility while solar grows elsewhere, and how the distributed-renewable component of the consumption obligation applies. The Ministry of Power's 2025 renewable consumption obligation gives hilly and North-Eastern states, along with Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand, half the distributed component that other states carry.

## Rooftop geography is not park geography

Kerala's solar total is 2,626.70 MW, and 2,259.50 MW of it is rooftop. Ground-mounted solar is 342.27 MW. Delhi's solar total is 461.30 MW, of which 450 MW is rooftop. The national rooftop stock is 30,742.55 MW. Gujarat, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Kerala account for a large share of it. Uttar Pradesh's solar total on the location sheet is 6,024.43 MW, with rooftop at 2,486.10 MW.

Household schemes follow people and distribution networks, not irradiation maps alone. A Lok Sabha reply listed PM Surya Ghar installations as on 16 July 2026. The largest installation counts were Gujarat (7,51,644), Uttar Pradesh (6,77,363), and Maharashtra (6,75,518). Karnataka, a top-tier utility-solar and wind state, had 22,383 installations. Applications nationally were 76,00,788 against 39,03,615 installations on that date. The gap between a filed application and a commissioned meter is a state-level execution fact.

The later national update, in the PIB factsheet of 13 August 2026, says 51.58 lakh households were benefiting and 14.8 GW had been commissioned under the scheme. That national figure will not be evenly spread. A state energy department that copies Rajasthan's ground-mounted story into a rooftop target, or copies Gujarat's installation count into a wind target, is using the wrong map.

## What a reader should pull from the PDF

For any state, read four cells before the total: ground-mounted solar, rooftop solar, wind, and large hydro. The total renewable number adds them to bio-power and small hydro. A state can rank high on the total because of dams commissioned decades ago. That is a real resource. It is a poor guide to where the next solar tender will be built.
