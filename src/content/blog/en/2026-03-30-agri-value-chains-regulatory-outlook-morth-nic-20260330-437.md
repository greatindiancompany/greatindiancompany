# Which rules govern India’s agri chain now

The chain is not under one licence. A mandi, a pulse purchase centre, a cold store, a crop loan, and an export packhouse each answer to a different instrument. The official descriptions published in 2025 and 2026 say what those instruments currently do.

Wholesale marketing still runs through state mandis. The Centre’s digital piece is e-NAM, in operation since 2016. The 17 March 2026 Lok Sabha reply describes it as an online platform that integrates physical wholesale mandis so that bidding, price discovery, and payment can happen electronically. It does not describe e-NAM as a replacement for the state marketing law. The Centre’s money in the reply is a grant of up to ₹75 lakh per mandi for assaying laboratories, electronic weighing, computers, cleaning, grading, packaging, software, and compost units. Integration happens when a state or Union Territory proposes a mandi. From January to June 2026, 134 mandis in five states and Union Territories were added, and the stock on 30 June 2026 was still 1,656 mandis. The grant follows the proposal. It does not create a mandi on its own.

## Who may buy at MSP

MSP itself is a Cabinet price, recommended by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, for 22 mandated crops. The buying rules differ by crop family. Cereals and coarse cereals are procured by the Food Corporation of India and designated state agencies. Estimates for wheat and paddy are fixed with the states and FCI before the season, from production, marketable surplus, and cropping pattern. Coarse grains enter the central pool only with prior approval of a state plan. Pulses, oilseeds, and copra are procured under the Price Support Scheme of PM-AASHA when the state asks and the market price is below MSP. The agencies named for that scheme are NAFED and NCCF. Cotton is bought by the Cotton Corporation of India. Jute is bought by the Jute Corporation of India.

Two digital layers sit on top of that buying. For the Price Support Scheme, NAFED’s e-Samriddhi and NCCF’s e-Samyukti register farmers with Aadhaar, land records, bank details, and crop details, then schedule a purchase-centre slot and pay MSP into the bank account. For cotton, the Cotton Corporation’s Kapas Kisan app handles self-registration, slot booking, and quality and payment updates. These are payment and scheduling systems. They do not change which agency is allowed to buy.

Budget 2025 added a quantity instruction for three pulses: 100 percent of state production of tur, urad, and masoor is to be procured for four years, up to 2028-29, and the PM-AASHA guarantee for pulses was raised from ₹45,000 crore to ₹60,000 crore. That is a procurement commitment inside PM-AASHA. It is not a change to the mandi statute.

## Credit rules and the export gate

Banks’ agriculture obligation is the priority-sector target restated by the Ministry: 18 percent of adjusted net bank credit or credit-equivalent off-balance-sheet exposure, whichever is higher, with 14 percent for non-corporate farmers and 10 percent for small and marginal farmers. NABARD refinance against the shortfall is the wholesale backstop. It is not a licence for an FPO to borrow. FPO borrowing still has to pass the lender, which is why the credit-guarantee cover and the equity grant are separate scheme facts.

Export plants face a different regulator. The Department of Commerce, in its 10 June 2026 briefing, put sanitary and phytosanitary compliance at the centre of market access: antibiotic residues in marine products, pesticide residues and aflatoxin in rice, food safety in spices and tea, and phytosanitary controls on fruit and vegetables. The Export Inspection Council is shifting toward risk-based inspection frequency, a laboratory information system, and faster screening. Recognised laboratories stood at 89 in FY 2025-26. An export approval is not implied by an e-NAM lot number or by an MSP purchase receipt.
