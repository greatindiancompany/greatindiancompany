# Insurance for All by 2047 Is a Coverage Goal, Not a Penetration Target

On 25 November 2022, IRDAI published a press note, [Insuring India by 2047](https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/366347/IRDAI+Press+Note+-+Insuring+India+by+2047-+New+Landscape+for+Insurance+Sector.pdf/b315159b-dd0b-2164-77e7-b649d1d1f77e?download=true&t=1669388065990&version=1.1). The commitment is specific. By 2047 every citizen should have an appropriate life, health, and property cover, and every enterprise should be supported by an appropriate insurance solution. The same note says the Indian insurance sector should be globally attractive.

That is a coverage statement. It is not a published target for premium as a percentage of GDP. Anyone who attaches a 2047 penetration percentage to this note is adding a number IRDAI did not print.

The [Press Information Bureau's 23 April 2026 note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) uses the same coverage language: adequate life, health, and property insurance for every citizen, and suitable risk protection for every enterprise.

## Three pillars, then a rulebook

The 2022 note organises the work around three pillars: policyholders, insurers, and distributors. The practical aims it lists are the right product for the right customer, a working grievance path, easier operations, a rulebook that matches the market, and a shift toward principle-based regulation with more room for innovation and distribution.

On 19 March 2024, at its 125th meeting, IRDAI approved a set of consolidated regulations and described them in a [regulatory revamp press note](https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/366347/Press+Note+on+Regulatory+Revamp.pdf/9ad7286a-b0a7-ea19-d51c-4c942bad43e1?t=1711121865505&version=3.0). The note says the exercise replaced 34 regulations with six consolidated ones and introduced two new regulations, after the expenses-of-management regulation notified in January 2024. The new set covers rural, social-sector, and motor third-party obligations; the Bima Sugam electronic marketplace; registration and capital; corporate governance; insurance products; foreign reinsurer branches and Lloyd's India; actuarial, finance, and investment functions; and policyholder protection.

Bima Sugam is described there as a digital public infrastructure and a one-stop marketplace for customers, insurers, intermediaries, and agents, tied explicitly to Insurance for All by 2047.

## What Parliament changed after the regulations

The regulatory revamp is not the last policy layer. PIB records the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025, which amends the Insurance Act, 1938, the Life Insurance Corporation Act, 1956, and the IRDA Act, 1999. The foreign direct investment cap in Indian insurance companies moves from 74 percent to 100 percent. Intermediaries get one-time registration. The IRDAI approval threshold for share transfers rises from 1 percent to 5 percent. The net owned fund requirement for foreign reinsurers is reduced from ₹5,000 crore to ₹1,000 crore. The Act provides for a Policyholders' Education and Protection Fund, gives IRDAI a disgorgement power, and raises the maximum penalty for non-compliance from ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore.

PIB also records a GST exemption, from 22 September 2025, on individual life policies, individual health policies including family floaters, and the related reinsurance. The note says this removes 18 percent GST from those premiums.

## How this sits next to the 3.7 percent

The coverage goal and the penetration ratio answer different questions. Penetration, in the 2024-25 annual report, is 3.7 percent of GDP. Insurance for All asks whether a person or a firm has a suitable cover. A country can add covered lives through term, health, property, and government schemes while the premium-to-GDP ratio stays flat, if GDP and the mix of low-premium covers move together. The official documents do not convert one measure into the other.
