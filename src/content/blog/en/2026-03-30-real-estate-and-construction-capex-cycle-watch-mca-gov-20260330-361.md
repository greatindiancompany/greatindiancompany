# How to Read the Housing and Construction Credit Cycle

Capex in Indian housing is not one cycle. A mission envelope, a home-loan stock, a commercial-property loan, and an infrastructure-industry loan can all rise in the same month for different reasons. Adding them produces a number no official release has published.

## The mission envelope is not a quarterly print

The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs says [PMAY-U 2.0](https://www.mohua.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/pradhan-mantri-awas-yojana-urban-MjNzYjMtQWa), in force from 1 September 2024, involves an investment of ₹10 lakh crore and central assistance of ₹1.6 lakh crore for one crore additional households. That is the scheme's stated size. It is not a measured flow of cement poured in July 2026.

The same page gives two physical estimates tied to the earlier urban mission, and it labels them as estimates. On a standard consumption factor for EWS, LIG, and MIG houses, about 631 lakh metric tonnes of cement and 143 lakh metric tonnes of steel "would have been consumed." Those sentences are not production data from the steel ministry. They are the housing ministry's back-of-the-build arithmetic. Use them as that.

A third estimate on the page, from a National Institute of Public Finance and Policy study, says investment under PMAY-Urban would have created more than 357 lakh jobs, 112 lakh direct and 245 lakh indirect. Again, the verb is the page's. It is not a quarterly payroll.

## Three credit lines that must stay apart

The RBI [credit release of 31 August 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63478) is the cycle print, and it is carefully partitioned.

Industry credit grew 20.0 percent year on year as on 31 July 2026, against 6.5 percent a year earlier. Large and medium industry accelerated. Among major industries, the release names infrastructure, basic metals, engineering, chemicals, petroleum and coal products, and textiles as buoyant. It does not name construction in that sentence, and it does not give infrastructure its own percentage. Anyone who needs the construction-industry row has to open Statements I and II on the [data page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/Data_Sectoral_Deployment.aspx). Applying 20 percent to "builders" is not what the highlight says.

Services credit grew 22.9 percent, against 10.2 percent, supported by NBFCs, trade, and commercial real estate. Commercial real estate here is a bank-credit category. It is not the order book of a listed contractor, and it is not a REIT's rent roll.

Personal loans grew 16.2 percent, against 11.9 percent. Housing loans sustained double-digit growth. That is household mortgage credit, including priority-sector housing in the Bank's usual presentation of this table. It finances purchase and construction by families. It is not the developer's working-capital line.

A project can touch all three and still be one building: a contractor drawing an industry or services facility, a commercial sponsor drawing a commercial-real-estate loan, and an allottee drawing a housing loan. Summing the growth rates counts the building three times.

## Materials, land, and the price of the asset

NHB RESIDEX has room in its design for land price indices and building-materials price indices, alongside housing prices and a rental index. The [about page](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/Dashboard/About) describes those families. It does not, in the text used here, publish a July 2026 cement or steel index level. A capex note that needs material inflation should cite the series that prints it, not borrow the housing-price index as a proxy. The RBI house price index answers a different question: what registered residential prices did in eighteen cities.

There is also a break in the credit comparison. From 31 December 2025 the year-on-year rate uses end-of-month data against the older fortnight definition for the base month. The 19.1 percent non-food rate, and the sector rates under it, carry that footnote. A cycle chart that ignores it will show a cliff or a surge that is partly a definition.

## What to watch next

Watch the next sectoral statement for whether commercial real estate is still named as a support for services, and whether housing is still described as double-digit, and read the construction row in the statement rather than the industry headline. Watch PMAY releases for sanctions and utilisation, which move the ₹10 lakh crore envelope from a design figure toward a flow. Do not watch them as if they were the same series.

How those credit lines differ from a listed trust is in the [investment brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-investment-implications-mca-gov-20260330-241).
