# What policy teams must align for manufacturing trade diversification

Policy teams do not lack instruments. They lack a single owner for the handoff between instruments. Manufacturing diversification in India is simultaneously a DPIIT question, a Commerce and DGFT question, a line-ministry question, a state question, and, for some inputs, a mines question. Optimizing one of those and ignoring the others produces an anchor plant that cannot clear an input, or a trade agreement no factory can use.

## Who holds which piece

DPIIT holds the FDI rulebook. The Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2020 puts manufacturing, including contract manufacturing under a legally tenable contract, on the automatic route, and puts investors and beneficial owners from land-border countries on the government route. DPIIT also holds the logistics division: the National Logistics Policy launched on 17 September 2022, the industrial corridor frame, LEADS, and the cost study. A PIB note of 27 November 2025 reports that study's 2023-24 result as 7.97 percent of GDP and Rs 24.01 lakh crore, and describes SMILE city plans and NICDC parks.

The Department of Commerce holds agreements and the trade ledger. The India-UAE CEPA, in force from 1 May 2022, is the worked example of preference at tariff-line level. On 13 May 2026 the Commerce and Industry Minister said four of nine recent agreements were operational, five were expected within 12 months, and exports in the year ending 31 March 2026 were USD 863 billion, with an aspirational USD 1 trillion aim. DGFT holds the machinery under those agreements: FTP 2023 since 1 April 2023, the Importer-Exporter Code, Appendix 2A for the agreement list, Appendix 2B for origin certificates, and Appendix 2Y for which quality orders can be waived on the export path.

Line ministries hold PLI implementation. The 2 August 2023 PIB note said all 14 schemes had been notified and were at various stages, with an outlay of Rs 1.97 lakh crore, and that the Cabinet had not added sectors as of that reply. A commerce-ministry slide that implies one PLI window is mis-assigning the work.

The Ministry of Mines holds the mineral chain through the National Critical Mineral Mission, approved on 29 January 2025. The 24 March 2025 PIB note records overseas assets via KABIL, Rs 500 crore for processing parks, and Rs 1,500 crore for recycling, under an empowered committee chaired by the Cabinet Secretary. A DPIIT team localizing batteries still depends on that committee. States hold the last mile: a PIB progress note said 26 states and Union Territories had notified logistics policies, and LEADS 2025 is the comparison DPIIT released on 13 May 2026.

## The failure mode

The failure mode is a coherent press note and an incoherent shipment. An FTA is in force and the bill of materials fails origin. A PLI anchor is selected and the MSME vendor layer the 2023 note expected, 176 MSMEs among 733 selections, never tools up. A mineral asset is signed abroad and the processing park provision is not a plant beside the factory. A corridor cuts wagon time, as the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor example in the November 2025 note describes, and the state's last 50 kilometres do not move. Land-border capital sits in a government-route queue while the construction contract assumes an automatic close.

## A minimum alignment

A policy team that wants one view can keep a single page with five dates: the FDI circular in force, the PLI sector list and the implementing ministry, the agreement's entry into force, the QCO appendix version, and the state's logistics-policy and LEADS status. Add the mineral mission only if the bill of materials needs it. Publish the page when a date changes. That is more useful than a new vision statement.
