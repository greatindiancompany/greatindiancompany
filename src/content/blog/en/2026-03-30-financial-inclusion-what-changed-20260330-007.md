The change worth tracking in India’s financial inclusion is no longer the opening of the next basic account. On 16 July 2026 the [Reserve Bank of India](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63164) reported that its Financial Inclusion Index stood at 70.0 for March 2026, compared with 67.0 for March 2025. The release said growth showed up across the sub-indices, and that the improvement was mainly an uptick in Usage.

That sentence is the shift. When RBI introduced the index on 17 August 2021, the figure for March 2021 was 53.9, against 43.4 for March 2017. The Bank’s annual report for that period showed Access already well ahead of Usage: Access had moved from 61.7 to 73.3, while Usage had moved from 30.8 to 43.0 and Quality from 48.5 to 50.7. Access had done the early work. The later press notes keep pointing at use.

## The policy frame moved with the index

The [Department of Financial Services](https://financialservices.gov.in/pradhan-mantri-jan-dhan-yojana-pmjdy) records the same turn inside Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana. PMJDY was launched on 28 August 2014 as a mission to give every household a basic bank account. DFS states that, with effect beyond 14 August 2018, the focus of account opening moved from “every household” to “every unbanked adult.”

RBI then replaced the 2019–2024 National Strategy for Financial Inclusion. On 1 December 2025 the Governor released NSFI 2025–30, approved by the FSDC Sub-Committee. The press note says the earlier five years saw gains in access, usage, and quality, and that the new strategy is about consolidating those gains. The document itself says the next push is last-mile consistency and effective use, including for households and micro enterprises, and it sets five objectives — the Panch-Jyoti — with 47 action points.

One episode in that document shows why the rails mattered once they existed. NSFI 2025–30 records that in 2020–21 the government delivered ₹5.53 lakh crore digitally across 319 schemes and 54 ministries under the PM Garib Kalyan package, and that business correspondents were a channel for getting that money to the doorstep. That is a completed transfer episode, described by RBI, rather than a claim about this year’s benefit flow.

## What to carry into the next release

A July FI-Index note that again attributes the gain to Usage is continuity. A note that attributes it to Access would be a different story. The index has no base year; RBI designed it as a 0–100 cumulative reading, with Access weighted 35 percent, Usage 45 percent, and Quality 20 percent, across banking, investments, insurance, postal services, and pensions.

The account stock still matters, and the [Jan Dhan figures](/blog/financial-inclusion-key-numbers-20260330-017) are the place to read it.
