# Future of Work in India: Mid-Term Labour and Skilling Path

## What is already settled

The multi-year path starts from statutes. Since 21 November 2025 the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 have been in force. A Lok Sabha reply from the Ministry of Labour and Employment on 20 July 2026 says Central Rules were notified on 8 May 2026. Appointment letters, a floor wage, fixed-term parity with gratuity after one year, and a legal definition of gig and platform work are features of that settlement.

## State rules converge slowly

Because labour is concurrent, the same reply says states and Union Territories frame rules. Most had finalised or published them as of 20 July 2026, and a few were still in process. The mid-term question for a multi-state employer is how those rulebooks line up, establishment by establishment. An Aadhaar-linked Universal Account Number, which the ministry describes for gig and platform benefits, is meant to reduce one friction for workers who migrate. Wage rules and safety rules can still differ by state while that identity travels.

## From a definition to a scheme people can use

The 20 July 2026 reply says gig and platform workers were defined so schemes can provide life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity benefits, and old-age protection. The 21 November 2025 note sets the funding architecture: 1 to 2 percent of aggregator turnover, capped at 5 percent of amounts paid or payable to those workers. Administration of those schemes, including through EPFO and ESIC where the government assigns that role, is a multi-year task. e-Shram, launched on 26 August 2021, is the registry the ministry has pointed unorganised and platform workers toward. This page does not date a notification that has not been issued.

## Skilling after a closed guideline window

PMKVY 4.0 guidelines from the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship schedule that phase for FY 2022-2026, with Skill India Digital as the training-lifecycle platform, the National Council for Vocational Education and Training as the standards body, and the National Skill Development Corporation in an implementation role. That window ended in March 2026. The next skilling path is the text MSDE publishes after it, read against the National Skills Qualification Framework.

NITI Aayog's June 2022 report, *India's Booming Gig and Platform Economy*, recommended that PLFS identify gig workers so the category is not left to a one-off estimate. PLFS continues as the household survey. Its annual report for calendar 2025 was issued on 27 March 2026. Later rounds will move the employment picture. This brief does not project them.
