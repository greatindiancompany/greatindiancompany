# FRBM, Article 293, and the state fiscal ceiling

"FRBM" is often used as a nickname for every fiscal rule in India. The statute with that name is narrower. The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2003, is a law of Parliament for the Union government. The Department of Economic Affairs publishes the Act with the FRBM Rules, 2004. It requires the Union to lay fiscal-policy statements before Parliament and to manage the Union deficit and Union debt on the targets Parliament has set. State budgets are governed by a different pair of instruments: each state's own fiscal responsibility law, and the Union's consent when the state wants to borrow.

## Article 293 is the operational gate

Article 293 of the Constitution deals with borrowing by the states. Clause (3) says that a state which still owes money to the Government of India may not raise a fresh loan without the consent of the Government of India, and that consent may be given subject to conditions. Because states carry such debt, the annual net borrowing ceiling is issued as that consent. It is a limit on fresh borrowing for the year, expressed against the state's GSDP, and it can carry conditions.

The ceiling is a ceiling on the ordinary window. The Reserve Bank's state-budget study of 23 January 2026 says the consolidated gross fiscal deficit moved to 3.3 per cent of GDP in 2024-25, and is budgeted at 3.3 per cent for 2025-26, after three years below 3.0 per cent. The same release says the excess over 3 per cent mainly reflects 50-year interest-free loans under Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment, which are over and above the normal net borrowing ceiling. A state can be inside its ordinary ceiling and still print a consolidated deficit above 3 per cent of GDP because the capital loan is classified outside that ceiling and inside the deficit.

## What the Sixteenth Finance Commission asked the Union to do

The Commission's award period is 2026-27 to 2030-31. Paragraph 26 of the government's explanatory memorandum, published with the Union Budget, records the recommendation in plain terms. States' fiscal deficit should be capped at 3 per cent of their respective GSDP, excluding loans under the capital-investment scheme. The cap should be enforced under Article 293(3). States should stop off-budget borrowing and bring those borrowings onto the budget. State fiscal-responsibility laws should be amended so that they are uniform and lined up with that path. The Union, on the Commission's recommendation, would reduce its own fiscal deficit to 3.5 per cent of GDP by the end of the award period.

The memorandum then splits the government's response. It accepts in principle the quantum of the states' net borrowing ceilings, as a per cent of GSDP. It says the other recommendations, including off-budget borrowing, amendment of state fiscal-responsibility laws, and the Union deficit, will be examined separately. As of that document, the 3 per cent of GSDP ceiling excluding the capital loans is the accepted borrowing quantum. A uniform rewrite of state FRBM-style laws is a recommendation still under examination.

## Where the capital loan sits in the rule

The Ministry of Finance has run Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment since 2020-21. The loans are for 50 years and interest-free, and they are provided over and above the normal net borrowing ceiling. The Department of Expenditure guidelines dated 27 March 2026 allocate Rs 2,00,000 crore for 2026-27 on those same terms, for states and for Union territories with a legislature. The scheme guidelines are the yearly rulebook for which projects and which reforms qualify. They are an expenditure-department instrument. They are separate from the FRBM Act.

A reader who wants the legal stack in order can use this sequence.

1. The FRBM Act, 2003, for the Union's own targets and statements.
2. The state's fiscal responsibility law, for the targets the state has written into its own statute.
3. The annual net borrowing ceiling under Article 293(3), for the borrowing the Union will consent to.
4. The SASCI guidelines, for the interest-free capital loan that sits outside that ceiling and inside the gross fiscal deficit.
5. The Reserve Bank study, for how those pieces added up in the latest budgets.

Off-budget borrowing, meaning loans raised by state entities that the budget does not show, is the gap the Commission asked states to close. The memorandum does not say that closure has already been completed. Guarantees, reported in Statement 28 of the Reserve Bank study, are a related exposure: a promise that may become a budget line later. They are part of reading the ceiling honestly, because a ceiling on on-budget loans can be skirted by a guarantee until the guarantee is called.
