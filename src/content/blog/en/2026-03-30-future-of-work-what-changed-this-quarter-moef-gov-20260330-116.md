# What moved in India's work data from July to September 2026

The quarter that ends in September 2026 does not contain a new labour code. The four codes — the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 — were made effective on 21 November 2025. They rationalised 29 central labour laws. That was last year's statute. This quarter's official movement is in the register of unorganised workers.

## The registration print

The Ministry of Labour and Employment launched the e-Shram portal on 26 August 2021 as an Aadhaar-seeded National Database of Unorganised Workers. Registration produces a Universal Account Number on a self-declaration basis. The portal covers unorganised workers, including gig workers, platform workers, and migrant workers.

A written reply in the Lok Sabha, released by the Press Information Bureau on 20 July 2026, put registrations at over 31.78 crore as on 14 July 2026. The exact annexure total is 31,78,31,749.

On the portal's fifth anniversary, the Ministry reported 31.89 crore registrations as on 20 August 2026. Women were 54.28 percent of the register and men 45.72 percent. By age, 0.08 percent were 16–18, 55.21 percent were 18–40, 24.18 percent were 40–50, and 20.53 percent were above 50.

On the rounded crore totals the Ministry published, 31.89 crore against 31.78 crore is a difference of about 11 lakh registrations. That is a real addition on the figures as released. It is also a small step on a base that was already above 31 crore. It is not evidence that about 11 lakh jobs were created in five weeks. The July figure is an exact annexure count. The August figure is the Ministry's rounded crore total, so the difference should not be quoted as an exact headcount. e-Shram records a self-declaration that a person does unorganised work. It does not record a hire, a wage, or an hour worked.

## What the same reply shows the register is for

The 20 July 2026 reply also describes what the database is being wired to. The Ministry launched e-Shram as a "One-Stop-Solution" on 21 October 2024, after a Budget 2024–25 announcement, so that a registered worker can see social-security schemes in one place. Fifteen schemes of central ministries had been integrated or mapped by the date of the reply. Named examples include PM SVANidhi, PMSBY, PMJJBY, the Viksit Bharat rural employment guarantee, PMAY-Gramin, Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY, PMAY-Urban, PMMSY, PM-KISAN, One Nation One Ration Card, and PMMVY.

On 14 July 2026 the Ministry reported scheme-enrolment counts among e-Shram registrants, including 24.46 crore under One Nation One Ration Card, 15.10 crore under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY, and 8.54 crore under PMSBY. These rows overlap. One person can sit in several schemes. Adding them does not produce a count of people newly protected this quarter.

The same reply records linkages to the National Career Service for jobs, the Skill India Digital Hub for skilling, and Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maandhan for pensions. State and Union Territory microsites, launched on 29 January 2025, are meant to handle local registration, updates, verification, and analytics.

## What this quarter is not

Two misreadings are easy, and both are wrong.

First, a larger e-Shram number is not a fall in unemployment. The Periodic Labour Force Survey is the household survey for labour-force participation, the worker population ratio, and unemployment. Its latest full usual-status year in the releases used here is July 2023–June 2024, when the usual-status unemployment rate for persons aged 15 and above was 3.2 percent. That survey and this register answer different questions. Neither was redesigned in July–September 2026 in any release cited here.

Second, the quarter is not the moment gig workers started receiving aggregator-funded benefits. The Code on Social Security defines gig and platform work and sets a contribution band for specified aggregators, but the code also says the date on which aggregator contributions commence is a date the Central Government notifies. The 21 November 2025 press note said that, during the transition, relevant provisions of the existing labour Acts and their rules continue. The Economic Survey 2025–26, presented in early 2026, recorded that the codes had been notified and that rules were expected over the following months. This page does not treat a later rule as issued unless a Gazette reference is in the sources above.

## How to use the quarter

If you follow India's work story week to week, file July–September 2026 as a registry quarter. The useful sentences are narrow. The unorganised-worker register crossed five years on 26 August 2026. It stood at 31.89 crore on 20 August, up from 31.78 crore on 14 July. A majority of the people on it are women, and a majority are under 40. The register is being mapped to existing schemes and to the career-service and skill portals. It is not a jobs release, and it is not a substitute for the labour-code rules that were still the open implementation question at the start of 2026.
