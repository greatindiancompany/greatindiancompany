# How India's Digital Public Infrastructure Policy Fits Together

## Three vision areas, not one app

Digital India is a Government of India programme launched on 1 July 2015. The programme page, published under MeitY, states three vision areas: digital infrastructure as a utility to every citizen, governance and services on demand, and digital empowerment of citizens.

That is the policy frame. Digital public infrastructure, in the Press Information Bureau research note of 6 March 2026, is the population-scale architecture that grew inside and alongside that frame. The note says the United Nations describes these systems as foundational digital infrastructure for interaction among people, businesses, and governments. India's version, in the same note, connects identity, payments, and data exchange as interoperable public rails.

## The base the note names

The note calls the base the JAM trinity.

- Jan Dhan, launched in August 2014, is the account layer. The note says accounts rose from 14.72 crore in 2015 to 57.71 crore as of March 2026.
- Aadhaar is the biometric identity layer. The note says more than 144 crore Aadhaar numbers had been generated as of March 2026.
- Mobile connectivity is the access layer. The note says 85.5 percent of households owned at least one smartphone, and that wireless subscribers stood at 125.87 crore at the end of December 2025.

The policy claim in the note is direct delivery. Welfare can move to an authenticated account with fewer intermediaries. It states that the Public Financial Management System was mandated in December 2014 for Direct Benefit Transfer payment, accounting, and reporting. It reports savings of more than ₹4.31 lakh crore between 2015 and March 2024, and cumulative transfers crossing ₹49.09 lakh crore as of January 2026. Those are the note's figures.

## Shared rails after the base

The March note's design point is reuse. Open APIs and public digital goods let ministries share identity, data, and payments instead of rebuilding separate portals. The Digital India pillars page organises the original programme into nine pillars: broadband highways, universal access to mobile connectivity, public internet access, e-governance, e-Kranti, information for all, electronics manufacturing, IT for jobs, and early harvest programmes. Those pillars are the programme's organising list. They are not restated here as a new cabinet decision.

India Stack Global, on the Digital India site, is the later showcase of operating platforms, including Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, API Setu, UMANG, and sector systems for health, learning, and procurement.

## What is background, not a 2026 decision

The note says the G20 New Delhi Leaders' Declaration of 2023 recognised digital public infrastructure, and that the Global DPI Repository was launched under India's presidency. That is background in a 2026 note. This brief does not treat it as a policy change dated to this year.
