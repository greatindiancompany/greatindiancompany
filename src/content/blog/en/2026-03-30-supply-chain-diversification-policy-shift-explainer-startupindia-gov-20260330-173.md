# The policy shift behind India's manufacturing trade diversification

Global buyers say "China-plus-one." Indian notifications say something more specific, and less dramatic. The shift is from a general hope that factories will arrive, to a named set of manufacturing sectors, a trade-policy machinery for preferences, and a screening rule for capital from countries that share a land border with India.

## Localization is an incentive design

The Press Information Bureau record of 2 August 2023, drawn from a Lok Sabha reply by the Minister of State for Commerce and Industry, is the cleanest official statement of the Production Linked Incentive design. Schemes for 14 sectors were announced with an outlay of Rs 1.97 lakh crore, framed against the Atmanirbhar goal of stronger manufacturing and exports. The stated purpose is to attract investment and technology, build scale, and make Indian manufacturers more competitive.

The 14 sectors are mobile manufacturing and specified electronic components; critical key starting materials, drug intermediates and active pharmaceutical ingredients; medical devices; automobiles and auto components; pharmaceutical drugs; specialty steel; telecom and networking products; electronic and technology products; white goods (air conditioners and LEDs); food products; textiles in the man-made fibre segment and technical textiles; high-efficiency solar PV modules; advanced chemistry cell batteries; and drones and drone components.

That list is the policy shift. It is not a blanket subsidy for every factory that leaves another country. The same reply said the sectors were chosen for technologies where India could gain on employment, exports, and wider economic benefit, after vetting by NITI Aayog, and that the Cabinet had not approved extra sectors as of that date. Anchor units were expected to pull a vendor base, much of it in MSMEs. Out of 733 selected applications, 176 were MSMEs in sectors such as bulk drugs, medical devices, pharma, telecom, white goods, food processing, textiles, and drones.

## Trade policy is the other half of the shift

A factory that only substitutes an import for the home market has not diversified a buyer's supply chain. The buyer still needs a way to land the good in a third country on a preference, or at least on a predictable rule. That is the Department of Commerce and DGFT half of the shift.

FTP 2023, in force from 1 April 2023, is the operating manual: what may be imported, how an exporter is identified, and how a certificate of origin is issued. Agreements such as the India-UAE CEPA, in force from 1 May 2022, are where preference becomes a tariff line. Under that agreement the UAE eliminated duties on 97.4 percent of its tariff lines, corresponding to 99 percent of imports from India. India's own immediate duty elimination covered over 80 percent of its tariff lines, corresponding to 90 percent of India's export value, with labour-intensive goods named in the Commerce note: oil seeds and oils, beverages, cotton, fish, textiles, clothing, gems and jewellery, leather, footwear, pharmaceuticals, and many engineering products.

## Capital is screened, not banned

DPIIT's Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2020, effective 15 October 2020, puts foreign investment in manufacturing on the automatic route. Manufacturing may be the company's own plant or contract manufacturing in India under a legally tenable contract. A manufacturer may sell those India-made goods wholesale or retail, including by e-commerce, without a separate government approval.

Paragraph 3.1.1(a) of the same circular is the border rule. An entity of a country that shares a land border with India, or an investment whose beneficial owner is situated in or is a citizen of such a country, can invest only under the government route. The rule is written for every land-border country, not as a China-only clause. Buyers who say "China-plus-one" often mean a second factory. The Indian rule answers a different question: whose capital is on the government route.

## How to use the shift

Read three documents before you adopt the slogan. The PLI sector list tells you whether the product is inside an incentive. The FTA and the certificate of origin tell you whether a preference exists.
