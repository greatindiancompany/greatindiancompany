The risk in India’s startup numbers is misclassification. A recognised entity is not an operating success, a funded company, or a job that will still be there. The official sources already separate those states. Scenarios that collapse them will be wrong even when every input is real.

## Closure is published, and it is smaller than folklore and larger than zero

PIB release PRID 2241313 records a Lok Sabha reply using Ministry of Corporate Affairs data. As on 31 January 2026, DPIIT had recognised 2,12,283 entities. Of the recognised startups, MCA categorised 6,789 as closed, meaning dissolved or struck off. Of those closed entities, 2,950 had at least one woman director or partner. On the same date, 1,02,054 recognised startups had at least one woman director or partner.

Six thousand seven hundred and eighty-nine is 3.2 percent of 2,12,283. That ratio is division of the two official counts on one date. It is not a lifetime failure rate. It excludes firms that stopped trading without being dissolved or struck off. It excludes firms that were never recognised. It is also a snapshot before the February 2026 widening of the definition and before the June stock of 2,40,092. A later closure print may use a larger denominator. Until MCA and DPIIT publish it, 6,789 remains the dated closure stock.

Women’s share of closures is not the same object as women’s share of the registry. The reply gives 2,950 and 1,02,054. It does not, in the text used here, offer a reason.

## Concentration is a balance-sheet risk for anyone underwriting “India startups”

On 30 June 2026, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, and Gujarat held 1,32,499 of 2,40,092 recognised startups, or 55.2 percent. A shock to company formation or to fund activity in those five jurisdictions moves the national series. Lakshadweep’s 3, Sikkim’s 21, and Ladakh’s 25 show the other tail: a state average will describe a place that does not exist. Policy that “scales what Maharashtra did” is scaling a stock of 41,440 recognised entities, not a template the annex says every Union Territory already has.

Capital is more concentrated than recognition. At the end of 2025-26, alternative investment funds backed by the first Fund of Funds had invested in over 1,420 startups. The seed fund had approved money for more than 3,400. The guarantee covered more than 410 loans. A registry of 2.23 lakh at 31 March 2026 contained all three programmes and still left the great majority of names outside them. The risk of a “startup winter” headline is that it can be true for the 1,420 and irrelevant for the rest, or the reverse.

Jobs add a reporting risk. The national total of more than 23.36 lakh direct jobs as on 31 March 2026 continues a series the Rajya Sabha annexure called self-reported. If closed firms remain inside a cumulative jobs claim, the jobs number will not fall when the company register does. The publications used here do not show a netting of jobs against the 6,789 closures. A lender should not assume they have been netted.

## Three scenarios that stay inside the evidence

First, the registry keeps growing under the wider February 2026 test. April–June already added 16,334 recognitions. That scenario raises the headline count. It does not, on the programme sizes above, raise the funded subset in proportion.

Second, Fund of Funds 2.0 stays a rulebook for longer than readers hope. The guidelines name SIDBI as the initial agency and leave a second agency to be selected. They require half-yearly review and an annual report. A delay in letters of intent is a capital-timing risk for managers who priced a 2026 first close on a government anchor. It is not a recognition risk. The portal does not depend on that anchor.

Third, credit remains the thin instrument. Cover per borrower is now up to ₹20 crore, and the guaranteed stock at year-end was over ₹1,250 crore across more than 410 loans. A scenario in which banks treat the guarantee as a mass product has to explain why a scheme open since 1 April 2023 had reached only that stock by March 2026. The documents do not supply the explanation. They do supply the stock, which is the constraint.
