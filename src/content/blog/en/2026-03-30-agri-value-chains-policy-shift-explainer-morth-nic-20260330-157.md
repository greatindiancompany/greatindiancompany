# From a price floor to a chain of institutions

India's agricultural policy still has a price and a cheque. It also now has a company of farmers, an electronic mandi, a loan for a warehouse, and a grant for a cold chain. Those are different instruments. Treating them as one "value chain scheme" hides which problem each one was built to touch.

## The price and the cheque

The minimum support price remains a Cabinet price, not a market. On 13 May 2026 the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs raised MSPs for 14 kharif crops for marketing season 2026-27. The rule it restated is the Union Budget 2018-19 line: MSP at least one and a half times the all-India weighted average cost of production. Common paddy is Rs 2,441 a quintal, a margin of 50 per cent over a cost of Rs 1,627. The release says the margin is highest for moong (61 per cent), then bajra and maize (56 per cent), then tur (54 per cent). The same release records a decade of procurement: 8,418 lakh metric tonnes of paddy bought during 2014-15 to 2025-26, and Rs 16.08 lakh crore paid to paddy growers. Procurement on that scale is a public buyer standing in the chain. It is concentrated in the crops and states where the buying machinery actually operates. The price list itself does not buy the crop.

PM-KISAN is the other old instrument, continued rather than replaced. The Cabinet on 31 July 2026 extended it from 2026-27 to 2030-31 with an outlay of Rs 3.15 lakh crore. The payment is still Rs 6,000 a year in three instalments to an eligible landholding family. By that note, more than Rs 4.47 lakh crore had moved through 23 instalments. A transfer of that size changes a household's cash. It does not grade, store, or export a tonne.

## The institutions added around the crop

Three programmes, all launched before this quarter, are the chain itself.

The National Agriculture Market has run since 2016. A Lok Sabha reply on 17 March 2026 said 1,656 mandis in 23 states and 4 Union territories were on the portal. As on 28 February 2026 the platform had 1.80 crore farmers, 2.72 lakh traders, and 4,724 farmer producer organisations. Trade since inception was 13.22 crore metric tonnes, worth Rs 4,82,350 crore. The Centre's grant for a mandi's integration is up to Rs 75 lakh. e-NAM does not replace the Agricultural Produce Market Committee. It puts an auction, an assay, and a bank credit on top of a mandi the state already regulates.

The Central Sector Scheme for formation and promotion of 10,000 farmer producer organisations was launched in 2020 with a budgetary outlay of Rs 6,865 crore. As on 1 March 2026, 10,000 FPOs were registered. The support is specific: up to Rs 18 lakh over three years for management, a matching equity grant of up to Rs 2,000 per member with a ceiling of Rs 15 lakh per FPO, and a credit guarantee of up to Rs 2 crore of project loan. As on 31 December 2025 the equity grant had reached 6,557 FPOs (Rs 430.77 crore) and the guarantee had covered 2,671 FPOs (Rs 662.71 crore). Registration finished first. The balance-sheet support is still partial.

The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund, begun in July 2020, is a loan window, not a grant. The ministry's August 2025 note sets a facility of Rs 1 lakh crore, an interest cap of 9 per cent, a 3 per cent subvention for up to seven years on the first Rs 2 crore, and credit guarantee through CGTMSE and NABSanrakshan. The scheme is described as operational from 2020-21 to 2032-33. A Cabinet expansion on 28 August 2024 made integrated primary-and-secondary processing eligible, and left standalone secondary processing outside. As on 26 January 2026, loans of Rs 80,224.15 crore had been sanctioned for 1,50,431 projects.

Food processing sits in a second ministry. The Ministry of Food Processing Industries runs Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana, with an outlay of Rs 6,520 crore up to 2025-26, and the production-linked incentive for food processing, with an outlay of Rs 10,900 crore from 2021-22 to 2026-27. Exports of scheduled products sit with APEDA, under the Commerce Ministry, under the APEDA Act of 1985.

## What the shift is, and what it is not

The shift is that a grower now meets the state in more than two places. There is a support price where procurement happens, a cash transfer if the land record qualifies, a collective if the FPO is real, a mandi auction if the local market is on e-NAM, and a loan or a grant if the asset fits the guideline. Each door has its own ministry, its own eligibility, and its own dated stock of progress.

The shift is not a measured rise in the farmer's share of the consumer rupee. None of the documents above publishes that share. The Third Advance Estimate of 27 May 2026 does show a large crop behind the chain: 376.563 million tonnes of foodgrain in 2025-26. A large crop makes storage, mandis, and processing matter more. It does not, by itself, prove the new institutions are clearing it.
