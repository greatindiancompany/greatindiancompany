# How India’s MSP rule sets the crop floor

India’s minimum support price is not a market forecast. It is a pre-announced purchase price for a fixed list of crops, built from a cost formula and a minimum margin.

The government announces MSP for 22 mandated crops on the recommendation of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, after hearing states and central departments. Toria is then fixed from the rapeseed and mustard MSP, and de-husked coconut from the copra MSP. CACP’s brief, as the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare restates it, covers cost of production, demand and supply, domestic and world prices, inter-crop parity, the terms of trade between agriculture and the rest of the economy, and a minimum margin of 50 percent over cost.

The cost itself is uniform across crops and states. It includes hired labour, animal or machine labour, rent on leased land, seed, fertiliser, manure, irrigation, depreciation, interest on working capital, fuel for pumps, miscellaneous paid-out costs, and the imputed value of family labour. Family labour is inside the formula. A state with higher local wages does not get its own MSP.

## The rule that has governed every season since 2018-19

The Union Budget for 2018-19 said MSP would be set at least one and a half times the all-India weighted average cost of production. That is a 50 percent margin over cost. It is a floor on the margin, not a cap. Crops the government wants to encourage are given more.

For Kharif Marketing Season 2026-27, announced on 13 May 2026, the Cabinet puts the margin at 61 percent for moong, 56 percent for bajra, 56 percent for maize, and 54 percent for tur. Every other kharif crop in that decision, including common paddy, is at 50 percent. Common paddy MSP is ₹2,441 a quintal against a cost of ₹1,627. The absolute increases are largest in sunflower seed (₹622), cotton (₹557), nigerseed (₹515), and sesamum (₹500). The price signal toward oilseeds, cotton, and selected pulses is in the margin table, not in the paddy headline.

Rabi Marketing Season 2026-27, announced on 1 October 2025, shows how far above 50 percent the same rule can go. Wheat’s margin is 109 percent (MSP ₹2,585, cost ₹1,239). Rapeseed and mustard are at 93 percent (₹6,200 against ₹3,210). Lentil is at 89 percent (₹7,000 against ₹3,705). Gram is at 59 percent (₹5,875 against ₹3,699). Barley is at 58 percent (₹2,150 against ₹1,361). Safflower is at the 50 percent minimum (₹6,540 against ₹4,360), even though safflower received the biggest rabi rupee increase, ₹600.

The moong rupee MSP for 2026-27 is not restated in the Cabinet prose that gives the 61 percent margin. The 2025-26 moong MSP, in the Ministry’s later MSP note, was ₹8,768 a quintal. Use the season that matches the crop in front of you.

## What a policy reader should not add

The 1.5-times rule does not oblige the Centre to buy the entire crop. It obliges the announced price to clear that margin over the CACP cost. Cereal buying is done by the Food Corporation of India and state agencies. Pulses, oilseeds, and copra are bought under the Price Support Scheme of PM-AASHA when the concerned state asks and the market price is below MSP. Cotton goes through the Cotton Corporation of India. Jute goes through the Jute Corporation of India. A high margin on wheat has coincided with large wheat buying. A high margin on moong does not, by itself, produce a wheat-sized procurement book.
