# Three Capital Cycles in India's Energy Transition

Capital spending in this transition is not one cycle. Plants, factories, and transmission lines are sanctioned under different laws, paid on different triggers, and show up in different statistics. Watching only installed generation capacity will make manufacturing look late, or early, for the wrong reason.

## Generation capital: a plan, then a stock

The National Electricity Plan notified in May 2023 estimates the fund requirement for generation-capacity addition at ₹14,54,188 crore during 2022-27 and ₹19,06,406 crore during 2027-32. PIB described the sum as about ₹33.60 lakh crore. The second period excludes spending pulled forward for plants commissioned after March 2032.

That estimate covers the whole mix the plan expects to be standing in 2031-32: 900,422 MW, of which conventional capacity is 304,147 MW, including 259,643 MW of coal, and renewable-based capacity is 596,275 MW, including 364,566 MW of solar and 121,895 MW of wind, plus 47,244 MW of battery storage. It is an engineering-planning number from 2023. It is not a tally of money spent so far.

The stock that exists can be set beside the near year of that plan. For the end of 2026-27 the plan's likely installed capacity is 609,591 MW, including 185,566 MW of solar and 72,895 MW of wind. On 31 July 2026, with eight months of the fiscal year still ahead, MNRE shows solar at 164.59 GW and wind at 58.14 GW, and PIB describes the whole power system as around 552 GW. Solar is within reach of the plan's end-year figure if the recent addition rate holds. Wind has a wider gap. The plan's solar number for 2031-32, 365 GW, is a later hill. None of this tells you the tariff at which the next gigawatt gets financed.

Coal stays inside the cycle. The plan still carries 235.1 GW of coal in 2026-27 and 259.6 GW in 2031-32, with average plant load factors of about 58 percent, and domestic coal requirements of 866.4 million tonnes and 1,025.8 million tonnes in those two years. Generation capex in the official plan is a both-and cycle.

## Factory capital: nameplate first, integration later

Solar module manufacturing capacity reached about 172 GW by 31 March 2026, MNRE said, from about 2.3 GW in 2014. About 98 GW of that nameplate was added in FY 2025-26. The production-linked incentive that helped pull this forward has an outlay of ₹24,000 crore. Tranche II is ₹19,500 crore. Letters of award went to 11 bidders in April 2023 for 39,600 MW of fully or partly integrated capacity. The incentive is paid for five years after commissioning, on manufacture and sale. In April 2026 MNRE said 8 of 12 PLI manufacturers had started production in at least one part of the chain.

The next factory cycle is upstream and in cells. MNRE has pointed to an Approved List pathway for ingot and wafer capacity with a June 2028 marker. For storage, the advanced-chemistry-cell incentive is ₹18,100 crore for 50 GWh, of which 40 GWh has been awarded and 10 GWh is reserved for grid-scale storage. Basic customs duty exemption on capital goods for lithium-ion cell manufacturing for battery storage runs from 2 February 2026 to 31 March 2028. Wind turbine manufacturing capacity was about 24 GW at the end of March 2026.

A module factory at 172 GW of nameplate capacity can be "ahead" of the 164.59 GW of solar plants installed by July 2026. That is not spare generation. It is the ability to supply modules, including for replacement and export, if the lines run and if buyers are there. PLI pays on sale, so unsold nameplate does not earn the incentive.

## Wire capital: planned early because plants are faster

The Central Electricity Authority's transmission report for integrating renewable capacity by 2030 says the point in plain language. Wind and solar plants are built faster than the lines that evacuate them, so the wires have to be planned first. The report plans the inter-state system for about 537 GW of renewable capacity and estimates 50,890 circuit kilometres of additional lines and 4,33,575 MVA of substation capacity, at ₹2,44,200 crore. It includes corridors for renewable zones in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, and Ladakh, and transmission for offshore wind off Gujarat and Tamil Nadu. Inter-regional transfer capacity was 1,12,250 MW in that report, with a path toward about 1,50,000 MW.

This year's Budget line for the Green Energy Corridor is ₹599.99 crore, described as central assistance toward intra-state lines, against a cumulative 6,000 circuit kilometre programme. Intra-state assistance and the inter-state ₹2.44 lakh crore estimate are both "transmission capex." They do not fund the same towers.

Storage is the capital item that connects the three cycles. The Electricity Plan calls for 8,680 MW / 34,720 MWh of batteries by 2026-27 and 47,244 MW / 236,220 MWh by 2031-32, and for pumped storage to rise from 7,446 MW in the 2026-27 plan to 26,686 MW in 2031-32. MNRE's July 2026 sheet already counts 7,425.6 MW of pumped storage inside large hydro. It does not publish a national battery row. Battery capital is still mostly a plan, a PLI reservation, and a customs exemption, not a line on the monthly achievement table.

## What to watch if you are timing capital

Watch module sales and the ingot-wafer list, not just module nameplate. Watch wind commissioned against the 28 GW the Government says is under implementation. Watch whether battery capacity acquires a public stock figure. And keep the coal and transmission numbers in the same notebook. The official plan never took them out.
