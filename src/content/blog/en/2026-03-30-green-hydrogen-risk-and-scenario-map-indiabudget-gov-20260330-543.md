# Green Hydrogen Risks Already Written into India’s Mission Design

A risk map that invents probabilities is not useful. The risks worth naming are the ones the schemes already admit in plain language.

## Distance

The Press Information Bureau’s release of 10 October 2025, on recognition of the Deendayal, V.O. Chidambaranar, and Paradip port areas, says the Mission uses a cluster model because long-distance hydrogen transport is logistically and technically difficult. A scenario that makes hydrogen in one district and burns it unchanged in a far-off plant, with no pipeline and no conversion to ammonia or methanol, fights that design. The official scenario is production and use in the same region, or a derivative a port can handle. Kandla’s methanol bunkering, Paradip’s handling jetty, and Tuticorin’s methanol tanks, as described in the 5 August 2026 Lok Sabha annex, are that scenario in hardware.

## Recognition is not a grant

The same October 2025 release says Component B2 of the 27 June 2025 guidelines lets MNRE recognise a hub without direct financial assistance. Recognition lets projects point at other central or state benefits. It does not, on that text, fund the jetty. Paradip’s ₹797.17 crore jetty is a separate approval, on a public-private basis, in the August annex. A financial model that books “hub status” as Mission capex will not match the guideline. The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) still carries a ₹400 crore line through 2025-26 for hubs and other projects, from the original design. That line and Component B2 recognition are different instruments. Using both as if they were one cheque double-counts public support.

## Money released, and plants still dated in the future

Of about ₹410.77 crore allocated for 12 vehicle pilots, ₹41.582 crore had been released when the Lok Sabha was told so on 29 July 2026. Release lag is not, by itself, a failed pilot. A small release against a large sanction means the 70 vehicles and 16 stations were not, on that date, a finished network. The answer asks agencies to report efficiency, fuel use, safety, and utilisation, which is the language of a trial still meant to generate evidence.

At Kandla, the August annex dates contractual completion of the 5 MW plant, scalable to 10 MW, to 31 December 2026. Until a later document reports completion, the honest status is “contracted.” The 1 MW demonstration plant is the one the annex describes as complete.

## The label can fail after the plant is built

The green claim is a well-to-gate average of not more than 2 kg of CO2 equivalent per kg of hydrogen, over 12 months, under the 19 August 2023 standard. The [certification scheme notice](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/green-hydrogen-certification-scheme-of-india/) is the April 2025 instrument, and the November 2025 note says a final certificate is mandatory for a facility that takes a central or state incentive or sells or uses the hydrogen in India. A project-finance model that assumes the incentive and skips the certificate has a condition it has not priced. The mission page’s enabling framework, a waiver of interstate transmission charges for renewable power used in production, banking, and time-bound open access, shows that power delivery is the constraint the Mission designed around. A plant that cannot show renewable origin inside the boundary can miss the label even if the electrolyser runs.

## Readers mixing documents

Tuticorin’s pilot is described one way in the November 2025 note and another way in the August 2026 annex, including the rupees, the month, and whether the 10 Nm³ figure is a stock or a rate. Ammonia offtake is 7.24 lakh tonnes a year at ₹55.75 per kg in the November note, and 6.7 lakh tonnes a year to 11 plants in the June 2026 speech. Choosing the larger number because it is larger is a scenario error.

Water use and a government rupee-per-kilogram cost of hydrogen are absent from the pages this brief relies on. They matter to engineers. They are questions here, not inputs.

Great Indian Company maps these as document risks. A scenario that needs a number the document does not contain is a guess.
