# Why the same certificate looks different on state portals

Birth, caste, income, and residence certificates are a national service idea and a state workflow. The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology funds the e-District pattern. The state, or an agency it designates, runs it. Anyone comparing "India's portal" as if it were one site will misread both the citizen's experience and a vendor's deployment.

## The national pattern, stated once

A Lok Sabha reply published on [3 August 2022](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1847837) said e-District had been implemented at district and sub-district level in all states and Union Territories. The services it named include birth, caste, death, income, and local-resident certificates, old-age, disability, and widow pensions, and selected electoral, revenue, tax, labour, and skilling services. The count in that reply is 4,671 e-services in 709 districts. Later national notes have not republished a replacement national total. Keep the date on the number.

The [6 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635) confirms the operating fact that still matters: certificates, pension-related services, and welfare schemes are delivered through state government portals and mobile applications. UMANG can be a window onto some of those services. It does not dissolve the state system of record.

## Two official state descriptions

The same August 2026 reply names the Tamil Nadu e-Governance Agency and the e-Sevai portal, [tnesevai.tn.gov.in](https://www.tnesevai.tn.gov.in/), as enabling end-to-end online delivery without manual intervention. It also says UMANG includes 17 services integrated by the Government of Tamil Nadu, inside a national catalogue of 2,575 services for individuals. Seventeen is not a judgment of Tamil Nadu's own portal. It is evidence that the mobile catalogue and the state portal are different inventories.

Tripura's Directorate of Information Technology describes the same Mission Mode Project in local terms on its [e-District page](https://dit.tripura.gov.in/e-district). The page says MeitY funds the project and the state implements it, that a District e-Governance Society coordinates it in each district, and that the state portal supports an end-to-end workflow, a digitally signed certificate, online verification of that certificate, email output, SMS updates, online status, and payment through eGRAS. The page states that 47 services are available on the Tripura portal. That is the department's figure on its page, not a NeSDA rank.

## How DARPG compares states without this brief ranking them

NeSDA, DARPG's biennial study, assesses states and Union Territories separately from central ministries. The [28 May 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266244) says the 2025 cycle proposes 59 mandatory services for every state and Union Territory, and that it scores both government portals and service portals. Parameters include end service delivery, integrated service delivery, and status tracking. A state can have a strong information homepage and a weak certificate workflow. Those are separate scores. This brief does not turn partial public material into a state ranking.

Assisted access also varies inside a state. The August 2026 reply says India had 5.01 lakh functional Common Service Centres as of May 2026, including 3.91 lakh at gram panchayat level. In Tamil Nadu it records 18,240 functional centres, 12,112 of them at gram panchayat level, and in Dharmapuri district 498 centres, 412 at gram panchayat level. The pattern is a national assisted channel with a local density, sitting beside a state portal the citizen may reach from home or only through that centre.

## What to ask in any new state

Ask which department signs the certificate, whether the output is digitally signed and verifiable, whether DigiLocker is fed by the issuer or only by a citizen upload, whether UMANG deep-links into the state status page, and whether a District e-Governance Society or an equivalent body actually runs the counter. Those questions follow from the Tripura, Tamil Nadu, and NeSDA documents. A single "digital maturity" label does not.
