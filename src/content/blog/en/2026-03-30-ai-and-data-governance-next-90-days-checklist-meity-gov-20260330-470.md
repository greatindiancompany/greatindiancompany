Ninety days from 24 September 2026 runs to 23 December 2026. One DPDP date falls inside that window. A long list of duties does not.

## On 13 November 2026, open these and only these

G.S.R. 843(E) brings into force, one year after 13 November 2025:

- section 6(9), which says every Consent Manager shall be registered with the Board on the technical, operational, financial, and other conditions prescribed;
- clause (d) of section 27(1), which gives the Board the function of inquiring, on an intimation, into a breach of a Consent Manager's registration conditions, and of imposing a penalty as provided in the Act.

Rule 4 of the DPDP Rules starts the same day. It is the registration procedure. The First Schedule requires an applicant to be a company incorporated in India, with net worth not less than two crore rupees, and it sets conflict-of-interest and audit conditions. Part B says the Consent Manager must not be able to read the personal data it enables a person to share, and it must keep a record of consents for at least seven years.

Before treating registration as open in practice, look for a notification under section 18 that establishes the Board from a stated date, and for any order that names the Chairperson and members. Sections 18 to 26 and Rules 17 to 21 have been in force since 13 November 2025, so the government can appoint. This series has not cited the appointment order. Registration is "with the Board." A search committee in the Rules is not itself a counter that accepts applications.

Also open MeitY's corrigendum of 16 December 2025 before you build a form from the 13 November text alone. The ministry lists it. This checklist does not paraphrase it.

## What does not start on 13 November 2026

Do not switch on, that morning:

- the standalone consent notice in Rule 3;
- security safeguards, breach intimation to the person and the Board, or the one-year log inside Rule 6 and Rule 8;
- verifiable parental consent, Rule 10;
- the Significant Data Fiduciary audit and the in-country restriction in Rule 13(4);
- the cross-border order power in Rule 15;
- the 90-day grievance rule in Rule 14;
- the penalty chapters, sections 28 to 34, and therefore the Schedule ceilings.

Those are in the eighteen-month set. They fall on 13 May 2027, which is outside this 90-day window. The rest of section 27, other than clause (d) of sub-section (1), is also on that later date. Clause (d) arriving alone does not mean the Board can already levy the Schedule on an ordinary Data Fiduciary.

Section 43A of the IT Act is omitted by section 44(2), which is also on the May 2027 list. Do not treat 13 November 2026 as the day the old compensation section disappears.

## What is already true, so it is not a future item

CERT-In's six-hour report and 180-day logs have been in force for years. The Reserve Bank's payment-data rule has been in force since 2018. The MeitY labelling advisory that replaced the 1 March 2024 note is already on the intermediary. None of these waits for November. If they are not in your operating checklist today, November will not be the reason.

IndiaAI publishable stocks, GPU hours, and AIKosh counts do not have a statutory date inside these 90 days. Read them when PIB or a parliamentary reply updates them. Do not expect the Mission outlay to be re-approved on 13 November.
