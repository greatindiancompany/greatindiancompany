# India’s Pharma Volume Rank and the API Import Gap

International comparison of Indian pharma goes wrong when one rank is asked to do three jobs. Volume of finished medicines, exports of bulk drugs, and dependence on imported intermediates are different measurements. The Government of India’s own approval notes keep them in separate sentences. A benchmark memo should do the same.

## What the government has been willing to say about scale

The cabinet note of 21 March 2020 says the Indian pharmaceutical industry is the third largest in the world by volume. The Press Information Bureau repeated that rank on 1 September 2022 when it announced in-principle approval of bulk-drug parks in Himachal Pradesh, Gujarat, and Andhra Pradesh. Use the rank as the government’s stated position in those documents. Pair it with the date. A fresh league table needs a fresh official publication.

The same September 2022 note gives the export side of the comparison for one year. In FY 2021–22, pharmaceutical exports were ₹1,75,040 crore, including bulk drugs and drug intermediates. Bulk drugs and drug intermediates alone were ₹33,320 crore. That split is the useful benchmark: formulations and intermediates are both in the trade number, and the intermediate line is much smaller than the headline export line. Anyone comparing India with a country that exports mostly patented specialties is comparing unlike baskets. The notes above do not publish that other country’s number. Leave the other country out unless its statistical office is cited for the same year.

## The gap the schemes name

The 2020 cabinet note is explicit about the weakness behind the volume rank. India depends on imports of bulk drugs used to produce medicines. In some specific bulk drugs the import dependence is 80 to 100 percent. The concern written next to that range is drug security: a disruption in supplies of those inputs can affect affordable healthcare. The scheme response was domestic manufacturing of a defined list, plus shared infrastructure in three parks, so that the cost of making those inputs could fall.

The 6 February 2026 Lok Sabha reply keeps the same problem statement for the bulk-drug PLI: avoid disruption in critical ingredients for which there are no alternatives, where dependence on a single source is the risk. By that date the reply is counting 41 identified products and 48 approved greenfield projects covering 33 bulk drugs, inside a ₹6,940 crore outlay. A benchmark that stops at “third by volume” has skipped the sentence the money was appropriated to answer.

The pharmaceuticals PLI, outlay ₹15,000 crore, is the other half of the official comparison with global product mix. The February 2026 reply says it is meant to move production toward high-value goods: biopharmaceuticals, complex generics, patented drugs or drugs nearing patent expiry, autoimmune drugs, anti-cancer drugs, and inputs other than the bulk-drug notified list. That is a product ambition. It is not a measured share of world biopharmaceutical revenue. No such share appears in the reply.

## Quality is a domestic schedule plus a foreign inspector

Manufacturing benchmark language often jumps from an export rank to a claim that Indian plants match a particular foreign GMP regime. The domestic legal instrument is Schedule M of the Drugs Rules, 1945. On 28 December 2023 the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare notified G.S.R. 922(E), substituting Schedule M with requirements titled Good Manufacturing Practices and Requirements of Premises, Plant and Equipment for Pharmaceutical Products. CDSCO, under the Directorate General of Health Services, describes its own job as approval of new drugs, clinical trials, drug standards, quality of imported drugs, and coordination of state drug controllers so the Drugs and Cosmetics Act is enforced with some uniformity.

A buyer in another jurisdiction can still inspect a site under that buyer’s law. The Indian gazette does not retire that inspection. Revised Schedule M is the standard a domestic licensee has to meet. WHO prequalification, a US or European inspection, and a CDSCO review are separate proceedings. Cite the one that actually happened to the plant.

## A biotech comparison uses a different department

The Department of Biotechnology’s National Biotechnology Parks Scheme note discusses parks and incubators as places for pilot work and scale-up. It is not a volume ranking of the formulation industry. The cabinet approval of the BioE3 policy on 24 August 2024 sets thematic sectors for biomanufacturing, from bio-based chemicals and enzymes to precision biotherapeutics. Those sectors are a forward industrial list. They are not a current export share.

For the dated trade figures alone, the [market-signal brief](/blog/pharma-and-biotech-market-signal-brief-mnre-gov-20260330-194) keeps the FY 2021–22 lines in one place. Great Indian Company uses official ranks with their limits attached, so a comparison survives a second reading: [greatindiancompany.com](https://greatindiancompany.com).
