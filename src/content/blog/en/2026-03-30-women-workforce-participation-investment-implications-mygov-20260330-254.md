# Women’s earnings and job type as an investment input

An investment view of women’s work in India starts from the composition of jobs and the earnings MoSPI prints beside them. Participation can be stable while the type of job, and the pay attached to that type, still moves. The Annual Report for January–December 2025 is the current all-India account. MoSPI warns that the revamped sample means 2025 levels may not be strictly comparable with earlier annual reports. The comparisons below are the ones the press note itself draws, and they carry that warning.

## The stock of women workers

On usual status, 20.0 crore women aged 15 and above were employed on average during 2025, out of 61.6 crore workers. The female worker population ratio was 38.8 percent. The female labour force participation rate was 40.0 percent, so unemployment among women in the labour force was 3.1 percent, the same rate MoSPI prints for men. Urban women are the exception inside that average: their usual-status unemployment rate was 6.4 percent, down from 6.7 percent in the 2024 calendar reading the report cites.

A 3.1 percent unemployment rate next to a 40.0 percent participation rate means most women aged 15 and above are outside the labour force, not unemployed inside it. Among women outside the labour force, 44.4 percent gave child care or personal commitments in home-making as the main reason. Among men outside the labour force, 69.8 percent gave continuing studies as the main reason. The constraint an investor is looking at, on MoSPI’s own reason code, is unpaid responsibility in the household.

## Job type decides which earnings line applies

Among women workers in usual status, self-employment was 64.2 percent in 2025, against 66.5 percent in the 2024 comparison in the press note. Regular wage or salaried employment was 18.2 percent, against 16.6 percent. Men’s regular-wage share was 26.5 percent. Rural women workers were 70.7 percent self-employed and 9.3 percent regular wage. Urban women workers were 40.4 percent self-employed and 50.9 percent regular wage. Casual labour was 20.0 percent of rural women workers and 8.7 percent of urban women workers.

The earnings lines follow that split. For regular wage or salaried work, MoSPI reports women’s average earnings at ₹17,126 in 2024 and ₹18,353 in 2025, about 7.2 percent nominal growth. Men’s regular earnings moved from ₹22,891 to ₹24,217, about 5.8 percent. For self-employment, women’s earnings moved from ₹5,861 to ₹6,374, about 8.8 percent, while men’s moved from ₹16,893 to ₹17,914, about 6.0 percent. For casual labour other than public works, women’s earnings moved from ₹299 to ₹315, about 5.4 percent, while men’s stayed at ₹456 and ₹455.

The press note states the levels and the growth rates. The reference period for each series is the one in the annual report’s earnings table. The levels are far apart: a woman in regular wage work is reported at ₹18,353 and a woman in self-employment at ₹6,374. Because self-employment is the status of 64.2 percent of women workers, the lower line is the line that covers most women who are counted as employed. Faster percentage growth on the smaller base does not close that gap in one year.

Hours point the same way. Urban self-employed men worked about 17.5 hours more per week than urban self-employed women. In rural areas the gap was about 12.3 hours. In regular wage work the gap was about 7.9 hours a week, and in casual labour about 6.9 hours. Earnings per person and hours per week are both lower for women inside the same status. An investment memo that uses the regular-wage line as “women’s wages” describes the 18.2 percent, and in rural areas the 9.3 percent.

## What the formal-payroll flow adds

EPFO’s 26.9 lakh net female subscribers in 2024–25 is the flow into covered establishments. It sits next to the regular-wage share, not next to the self-employed majority. A business whose customers or whose workforce are urban and on payroll is looking at a different pool from a business whose workers are rural helpers and own-account workers. PLFS gives the size of each pool. EPFO gives a covered-establishment flow. Neither figure is a valuation.

Nominal wage growth of 7.2 percent, 8.8 percent and 5.4 percent is not a real-wage calculation. MoSPI does not deflate these three series in the press note. A real-earnings claim needs a price index the note does not apply.
