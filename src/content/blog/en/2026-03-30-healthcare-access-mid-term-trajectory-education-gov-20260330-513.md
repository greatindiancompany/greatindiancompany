## The target year is behind the programmes

The National Health Policy, 2017, put dates on its money targets. Government health expenditure was to move from 1.15 percent of GDP to 2.5 percent by 2025. States were asked to spend more than 8 percent of their budgets on health by 2020. The share of households facing catastrophic health spending was to fall by 25 percent from then-current levels by 2025. This brief was updated on 24 September 2026. Those dates have passed. This page does not declare the targets met or missed. A health-accounts or budget series has to do that, with a definition that matches the policy.

What can be read now is the delivery design built in response, and the hinges that still decide whether access is real.

## What was actually stood up

Ayushman Bharat was the policy’s operating answer. In February 2018 the government announced 1,50,000 Health and Wellness Centres, now Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, to deliver comprehensive primary care, including free essential drugs and diagnostics. The ministry’s reply of 25 March 2025 reported 1,76,573 centres operationalised till February 2025. Later Press Information Bureau notes reported 1,81,873 as on 30 November 2025 and 1,82,944 as on 31 December 2025. The design target has been passed on the official count. Passing a count is a trajectory fact. It is not a quality fact.

PM-JAY launched on 23 September 2018 with a ₹5 lakh family cover for listed hospitalization. In September 2024 the Cabinet extended cover to every person aged 70 and above irrespective of income, on the family rules in the Authority’s FAQ. The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission launched in September 2021 with a 14-digit health account and facility and professional registries. PM-ABHIM carried a published outlay of ₹64,180 crore for 2021–22 to 2025–26, with approvals that include critical care blocks and district laboratories. The infrastructure window in that published text closed with FY 2025–26. The mid-term question is whether those assets are commissioned and staffed, which the approval list does not answer.

## The hinges that remain

The first hinge is follow-up. Mandirs are supposed to screen and manage non-communicable disease and to refer onward. The ministry can report crores of hypertension and diabetes screenings. Screening without drugs, without a repeat visit, and without a place to send a deteriorating patient leaves the hospital scheme as the first door that actually opens. The 2017 policy wanted two-thirds or more of resources on primary care so that this would not happen. The budget share is a finance question. The patient path is visible in a district.

The second hinge is the district hospital. The policy wants most secondary care there, inside the golden hour, against an aspiration of two beds per 1,000. Indian Public Health Standards 2022 are the benchmark. PM-JAY pays public hospitals at par with private ones and allows states to reserve packages for public facilities. If the district hospital cannot perform the package, portability and private empanelment move the patient to a city. That can be access. It can also be a long journey that the district design was meant to avoid. Both descriptions fit the rules. Only the local facility decides which one is true.

The third hinge is who is inside the hospital cover. SECC 2011 remains the floor on the Authority’s page. States may add families at their own cost. People at older ages were added nationally in 2024 without an income test. Households that are poor on a later survey and young on the age rule can still sit outside the ₹5 lakh cover unless the state has added them. Primary-care mandirs, by contrast, are for residents of the catchment. The trajectory of “access” splits: clinic access can widen while hospital-cover access stays targeted.

## What would count as a next official chapter

A health-accounts release that can be set beside 2.5 percent of GDP. A mandir report that separates operational buildings, filled teams, and stocked drugs from a cumulative screening total. A PM-ABHIM statement of blocks commissioned, not only approved. A current state table of trust, insurance, and mixed mode. Until those appear, the mid-term story is the design above, read with the dates attached.
