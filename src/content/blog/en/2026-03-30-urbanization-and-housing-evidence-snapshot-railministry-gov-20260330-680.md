# Urban Housing Figures, Each with a Date and a Document

## How to read the list

Every figure below is tied to a document and a date. Where two official pages disagree, both are shown and the dates are kept. This page does not update a December 2024 stock to September 2026 by assumption.

## Population and towns

MoHUA’s urban-growth page, reproducing Census 2011 as on 1 March 2011: total population 1,210.2 million; urban population about 377.1 million; net urban addition over the decade 91.0 million. The table prints the urban share as 31.16 percent in 2011 and 27.81 percent in 2001. The prose on the same page says 31.6 percent and an increase of 3.35 percentage points. Urban decadal growth, 2001-2011, is 31.8 percent. New towns: 2,774, of which 242 were statutory and 2,532 were census towns. Million-plus cities and urban agglomerations: 35 in 2001, 53 in 2011.

A Ministry of Home Affairs Lok Sabha reply of 7 May 2013 gives the unrounded urban count as 377,106,125 in a population of 1,210,569,573. State shares from that reply are in the state-pattern brief, not repeated here.

## First PMAY-Urban, as printed in December 2024

PIB background note, document dated in the December 2024 file: houses sanctioned 1.18 crore in 4,618 urban local bodies; grounded 1.14 crore; completed or delivered 88.32 lakh. Verticals among sanctions: Beneficiary Led Construction about 75 lakh, partnership and in-situ slum redevelopment about 18 lakh, Credit Linked Subsidy Scheme about 25 lakh. Investment ₹8,07,393 crore. Central assistance sanctioned about ₹2 lakh crore. Central assistance released ₹1.66 lakh crore. About 89 lakh houses in a woman’s name or joint ownership. Estimated materials: 570 lakh metric tonnes of cement and 130 lakh metric tonnes of steel. Estimated work: 917 crore person-days.

PIB, 24 July 2025, on the same closed subsidy: up to ₹2.67 lakh a house; middle-income window ended 31 March 2021; economically weaker and low-income window ended 31 March 2022; ₹58,885 crore released to 25,04,220 beneficiaries through the National Housing Bank, State Bank of India, and Housing and Urban Development Corporation.

## PMAY-Urban 2.0

Cabinet approval 9 August 2024, in the December 2024 note. Effect from 1 September 2024, in the July 2025 reply, for 1 crore additional beneficiaries over five years. Government assistance ₹2.30 lakh crore and investment of ₹10 lakh crore, in the December 2024 note. Verticals: Beneficiary Led Construction, Affordable Housing in Partnership, Affordable Rental Housing, Interest Subsidy Scheme. Unit assistance under the first two: ₹2.50 lakh. Central share: ₹2.25 lakh (90 percent) for the North-East, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Jammu and Kashmir, Puducherry, and Delhi; ₹2.50 lakh (100 percent) for other Union Territories; ₹1.50 lakh (60 percent) for other states.

Interest Subsidy Scheme, July 2025 reply: maximum ₹1.80 lakh; income up to ₹9 lakh; loan up to ₹25 lakh; property up to ₹35 lakh; carpet area up to 120 square metres; 4 percent on the first ₹8 lakh; tenure up to 12 years; five yearly instalments; loan active and more than half the principal outstanding.

As of the December 2024 note only: in-principle approval for more than 6 lakh houses from 29 States and Union Territories that had signed memoranda. No later national sanction total is used on this page.

## AMRUT 2.0, guidelines release of 27 October 2021

Indicative outlay ₹2,99,000 crore, including ₹22,000 crore for the ongoing first mission, from 2021-22 to 2025-26. Central share ₹86,760 crore, including ₹10,000 crore for that ongoing mission. Household taps in 4,700 statutory towns. Sewerage or septage in 500 AMRUT cities. Proposed connections: 2.68 crore taps and 2.64 crore sewer or septage connections. Release pattern 20:40:40. Ten percent of funds for million-plus cities in public-private partnership. First AMRUT mission: June 2015, 500 cities.

## Smart Cities

Launch 25 June 2015, 100 cities, central outlay ₹48,000 crore. Originally approved to 31 March 2020, extended four times to 31 March 2025. MoHUA mission page, physical progress as on 1 December 2025: 8,064 projects worth ₹1,64,811 crore with work orders, 7,741 (96 percent) worth ₹1,55,386 crore completed. The page also records claims of ₹47,458 crore by financial closure and utilisation of ₹46,093 crore, described there as 97 percent of central share released, and states that no new city will be taken up.

Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 3148, answered 6 August 2026, as reported as on 31 March 2026: 7,790 projects worth ₹1,56,257 crore completed out of 8,064 worth ₹1,64,811 crore; central assistance utilised ₹46,377 crore; 197 partnership projects worth ₹9,277 crore taken up, 194 worth ₹9,190 crore completed; command centres in all 100 cities. Advisory No. 27 is dated 10 June 2025.

The December 2025 page and the March 2026 parliamentary snapshot are both official. The later data date is 31 March 2026. Neither is a September 2026 count.

## Left out on purpose

No 2026 urban population. No national house-price index. No count of projects on state RERA registers. No PLFS unemployment rate. No claim that every state met the March 2025 affordable-housing-policy deadline. Census 2027’s Cabinet outlay of ₹11,718.24 crore, from the PIB note of 25 April 2026, is a census operation cost, listed so it is not mistaken for a housing-scheme outlay.
