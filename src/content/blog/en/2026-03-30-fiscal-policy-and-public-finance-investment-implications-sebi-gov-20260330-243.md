Public investment in the Union Budget is a line in the capital account, plus a second line that accounting calls revenue but the Finance Ministry treats as asset creation. An investor who reads only “fiscal deficit 4.3 percent” misses both.

## Two definitions, both official

Capital expenditure in the 2026-27 Budget estimates is ₹12.22 lakh crore, 3.1 percent of GDP. That figure includes capital support to states through the Special Assistance as Loan to States for Capital Expenditure (SASCI), with an outlay of ₹2.0 lakh crore. The loan is the Centre’s capital disbursement. The asset, if it is built, sits on a state’s project list.

Effective capital expenditure adds grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets. Those grants are booked as the Centre’s revenue expenditure, because a grant is not the Centre’s own asset, and they are still spent on assets. The allocation for that grant line is ₹4.93 lakh crore, 1.3 percent of GDP. Effective capital expenditure is therefore ₹17.15 lakh crore, 4.4 percent of GDP.

Using the smaller number understates the asset impulse the Budget claims. Using the larger number as if it were all Union-owned capex overstates what the Centre itself will build. The ₹2.0 lakh crore SASCI piece and the ₹4.93 lakh crore grant piece both depend on states and implementing agencies.

## Is the borrowing paying for assets?

The fiscal statement publishes a direct test. Capital expenditure divided by the fiscal deficit is 72.1 percent in the 2026-27 Budget estimates, against 70.3 percent in the 2025-26 revised estimates and 66.8 percent in 2024-25. Effective capital expenditure divided by the fiscal deficit is 101.1 percent, against 90.1 percent and 84.1 percent. Above 100 percent means the asset-side total, including grants that are accounted as revenue, exceeds the deficit. Borrowing is not the only funder of that total; revenue receipts cover a large share of ordinary spending.

The revenue account is still in deficit. Revenue expenditure is ₹41.25 lakh crore and revenue receipts are ₹35.33 lakh crore, a revenue deficit of 1.5 percent of GDP. Receipts cover 85.6 percent of revenue expenditure. If the capital grants are set aside as capital in nature, the statement says the coverage rises to 97.3 percent. The quality claim is that ratio. It is a Budget estimate, not an audited outcome.

## What the first quarter did with the capital line

Through June 2026, CGA reports capital expenditure of ₹3,40,258 crore, 27.8 percent of the ₹12,21,821 crore estimate, ahead of the 24.5 percent pace a year earlier. Loans disbursed, the slice that includes on-lending such as state capital support, were ₹1,00,579 crore, 36.1 percent of that sub-estimate. A fast loan line in the first quarter is not the same thing as kilometres commissioned. It does mean the Centre had started moving the capital disbursement earlier than last year.

## What this does not tell an investor

The macroeconomic framework in the same Budget papers says gross fixed capital formation rose 7.8 percent in 2025-26 and has stayed near 30 percent of GDP for three years. That is economy-wide investment, public and private. The Budget does not split how many points of that 30 percent are the Union’s ₹12.22 lakh crore. Treating the Union capital line as the whole investment cycle double-counts once state capex and private capex are added, and under-counts if those are ignored.

Nominal GDP for the estimates is assumed to grow 10.0 percent over the first advance estimates of 2025-26. The deficit and capex ratios move if that nominal base is wrong. A lower nominal GDP raises the deficit ratio even when the rupee deficit is unchanged.

Interest is the competing claim. The Budget estimates interest payments at ₹14.04 lakh crore, larger than the ₹12.22 lakh crore capital line. A portfolio that likes public capex still has to underwrite a coupon bill bigger than the capex bill. The speech’s case for the debt path is that a falling debt ratio slowly relieves that coupon. The 2026-27 numbers do not show the relief yet: interest is higher than the previous year’s Budget estimate of ₹12.76 lakh crore.

For a project sponsor, the actionable public line is narrower than “capex.” It is whether the relevant ministry’s demand, the SASCI release to that state, or the capital grant in a centrally sponsored scheme is actually sanctioned. The Union ratio says the Budget intends to keep asset spending large relative to the deficit. The sanction file says whether a given project is in that intention.
