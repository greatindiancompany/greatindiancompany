## Two money figures that are not one cycle

Education capital and skill-scheme spending are easy to merge and wrong to merge.

NEP 2020, in its financing chapter, says public expenditure on education in India has not come close to 6% of GDP. That 6% level was envisaged in the 1968 policy, reiterated in 1986, and reaffirmed in the 1992 review. The 2020 policy records current public expenditure, Centre and states together, at around 4.43% of GDP, from an analysis of budgeted expenditure. It then says the Centre and the states will work together to reach 6% of GDP at the earliest. The 4.43% figure is the level written into NEP 2020. This page does not have a later official ratio for 2025-26 or 2026-27, so it does not update the percentage.

The Skill India Programme is a different instrument. On 7 February 2025 the Cabinet approved an overlay outlay of Rs 8,800 crore for 2022-23 to 2025-26 for a central-sector scheme combining PMKVY 4.0, PM-NAPS, and Jan Shikshan Sansthan. A central-sector outlay is not the education budget, and it is not a state capital plan for school buildings.

## What has been reported as used

Inside PMKVY 4.0, a Rajya Sabha reply on 12 August 2026 said Rs 414.86 crore had been utilized as on 30 June 2026 for future and new-age job roles, including drone technology, artificial intelligence, and green-energy maintenance. The same reply said the highest Common Norms training cost then prevalent, Rs 49 per hour, is provided for those new-age courses so providers can meet infrastructure, tools, and trainer requirements. That hourly rate is not a general school-construction cost.

A school-side capital item that is specified in units rather than rupees is the skill-lab project in the 3 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply: 1,200 vocational labs in 400 Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas and 200 Eklavya Model Residential Schools, funded through the National Skill Development Fund, with two labs and two trained skill teachers per school. The reply states the lab count. It does not state the rupee cost of the labs.

## What a capex watch can check next

A reader tracking spending can keep three lines separate: the NEP 6% aim and its 4.43% baseline, the Rs 8,800 crore Skill India window, and the Rs 414.86 crore new-age utilization. Samagra Shiksha remains the school scheme named in NEP 2020. Its latest approved outlay is not in the documents this brief uses, so no Samagra rupee figure is given here.
