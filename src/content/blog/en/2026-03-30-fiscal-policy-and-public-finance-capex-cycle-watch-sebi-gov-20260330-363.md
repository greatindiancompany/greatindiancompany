A capex cycle is visible only if capital spending is placed next to revenue spending, interest, and the deficit. In the Union Budget for 2026-27 the capital line is large. It is still the smaller of the two expenditure accounts.

## The Budget mix

Total expenditure is estimated at ₹53.47 lakh crore, 13.6 percent of GDP, 7.7 percent above the 2025-26 revised estimate of ₹49.65 lakh crore. Inside that total:

- Revenue expenditure is ₹41.25 lakh crore, 10.5 percent of GDP, down from 10.8 percent in the revised estimates.
- Capital expenditure is ₹12.22 lakh crore, 3.1 percent of GDP.
- Of the capital line, ₹2.0 lakh crore is capital support to states under SASCI, the special assistance scheme for state capital expenditure.

The revised estimates for 2025-26 had already put capital expenditure at ₹10.96 lakh crore and revenue expenditure at ₹38.69 lakh crore. The new Budget raises both. Capital rises by more in percentage terms. Revenue remains more than three times the capital line.

Effective capital expenditure, which adds ₹4.93 lakh crore of grants for creating capital assets, is ₹17.15 lakh crore, or 4.4 percent of GDP. Those grants are not extra cash on top of the ₹53.47 lakh crore. They already sit inside revenue expenditure. Adding ₹17.15 lakh crore to ₹41.25 lakh crore double-counts them.

## What revenue spending is made of

Three rigid revenue items set the floor under the cycle.

Interest payments are estimated at ₹14.04 lakh crore, compared with ₹12.76 lakh crore in the 2025-26 Budget estimates. Interest is larger than the entire capital line. Any “capex boom” reading that ignores the coupon bill is incomplete.

Major subsidies are estimated at ₹4.11 lakh crore: food ₹2.28 lakh crore, fertiliser ₹1.71 lakh crore, petroleum ₹0.12 lakh crore. That is 1.0 percent of GDP and about 10 percent of revenue expenditure.

Pensions, civilian and defence together, are estimated at ₹2.96 lakh crore, 0.8 percent of GDP, against ₹2.87 lakh crore in the 2025-26 revised estimates. Pensions do not step down because a capital project is announced.

Together those three are about ₹21 lakh crore before salaries, defence revenue, and transfers are counted. The capital line of ₹12.22 lakh crore has to be financed in a budget where these claims are already booked.

## The early-year pace

Through June 2026 the accounts show capital expenditure of ₹3,40,258 crore, 27.8 percent of the annual estimate, against 24.5 percent at the same point a year earlier. Revenue expenditure was ₹10,16,818 crore, 24.6 percent of its estimate, almost the same pace as last year’s 24.0 percent. The first quarter was a slightly faster capital draw, not a collapse in revenue spending.

Loans disbursed were 36.1 percent of their estimate. That sub-line is where state capital loans show up. A high percentage in June can mean the Centre released loans early. It does not yet mean the state has booked the asset.

In the previous year, April–December 2025, the fiscal statement says capital expenditure grew 15 percent year on year, and the deficit stood at 54.5 percent of the Budget estimate by December. The cycle’s habit, visible in that nine-month print, is that capital can run ahead for a while and still have to fit inside the annual deficit. The June 2026 page carries the same warning in the Controller General’s own note: the deficit to date is not the year’s deficit.

## What to watch on the next prints

Four ratios tell you whether the cycle is the one the Budget described.

1. Capital expenditure as a share of its ₹12.22 lakh crore estimate, compared with revenue expenditure as a share of ₹41.25 lakh crore. If revenue sprints and capital stalls, the mix has changed even if the deficit is unchanged.
2. The loan sub-line against project commissioning. Releases to states are not kilometres.
3. Interest payments against the ₹14.04 lakh crore estimate. A capex cycle financed at a higher yield is a different cycle.
4. Effective capital expenditure only after you have subtracted the grant piece from revenue, so the same rupee is not counted twice.

The Budget’s own quality ratios — capital expenditure at 72.1 percent of the fiscal deficit, effective capital expenditure at 101.1 percent — are estimates. The monthly accounts are the test.
