# A Risk Map for Project Delay, Fund Use, and Slow Price Gains

Risk in Indian housing is not a single crash scenario. The official record supports a shorter list: delay against a declared completion date, buyer money leaving the project, a price index that rises slowly while a few cities do the work, a credit comparison that changed definition, and a rental law that still depends on the state. This map does not assign probabilities. The releases do not either.

## Delay and the allottee's choice

Section 18 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, as the July 2025 [MoHUA FAQ](https://www.mohua.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/542144159b61c980253a694f478d3a5d.pdf) states it, gives the allottee a choice if possession is not handed over as agreed. The allottee may leave and take a refund with interest and compensation, or stay and take interest for every month of delay, plus compensation in the circumstances the section names. The interest rate is the rate in the state rules, and it is the same rate if the allottee is the one in default.

The promoter's room to extend registration is narrow. Force majeure means war, flood, drought, fire, cyclone, earthquake, or another natural calamity affecting the works. Reasonable circumstances without the promoter's fault can extend registration by not more than one year. A demand slump, a sales pause, or a dispute with a contractor is not on the force majeure list.

If the authority revokes registration, the association of allottees has the first right of refusal to finish the project. Revocation is described as a last resort, after a hearing. The residual risk for a lender is a half-built project whose promoter is no longer the person in control of the account.

## Money that is not free cash

Seventy percent of amounts received from allottees goes into a separate account for that project, usable for land cost and construction cost. Withdrawals follow percentage completion and three certificates. The audit within six months of year-end is supposed to catch a diversion. The account is self-maintained. The FAQ is clear it is not an escrow that needs the authority's nod for each withdrawal. The control is therefore documentary. A risk process that only asks whether an escrow bank has countersigned the cheque is looking for a control the Act did not create.

Upfront collection is capped in a different place. More than 10 percent before the agreement for sale is not permitted. A booking plan that treats a large token as unrestricted cash is already outside the FAQ.

## Price and credit, without a drama the data do not show

The RBI House Price Index for Q1 2026-27 stood at 117.5. The quarter's rise was 1.1 percent. The year's rise was 3.6 percent, the same as a year earlier. The release attributes the move to a subset of its eighteen cities, including Chandigarh, Lucknow, Jaipur, Kanpur, and Thiruvananthapuram. The risk is not a number this release does not contain. The risk is using 3.6 percent as the price path of a city the release did not name, or of under-construction asking rates, which this index does not measure.

On credit, the 31 August 2026 release still describes housing growth as double-digit and names commercial real estate as one support for faster services credit. It also says credit-card outstanding and gold-jewellery loans decelerated. Those are different products. A scenario that borrows the gold-loan slowdown and pastes it onto housing contradicts the sentence the Bank wrote. The scenario the footnote does support is analytical: year-on-year rates from December 2025 compare a new end-of-month definition with an old fortnight. A model that ignores the break will invent a cycle.

## Rental and mission locks

Affordable rental housing can be built under PMAY-U 2.0 whether or not a state has adopted the Model Tenancy Act. The July 2022 parliamentary list of four adopting states is stale as a census. A sponsor who needs landlord-tenant enforcement is exposed to the state statute, not to the model.

Separately, a house built with PMAY assistance is not to be sold or rented by the beneficiary for five years from completion. That is a lock on the assisted stock. It is a different risk from a private project, where the allottee's right is timely possession, not a ban on later sale.

Where these risks show up as stalled approvals and a stuck project account, see the [execution brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-execution-bottleneck-review-mca-gov-20260330-601). Great Indian Company publishes the map so a risk register cites the section, not a mood.
