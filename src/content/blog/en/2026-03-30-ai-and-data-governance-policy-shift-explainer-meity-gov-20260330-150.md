The policy shift is real, and it is not the same thing as a switch that has already flipped. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023) received the President's assent on 11 August 2023. Section 1(2) says it comes into force on the date the Central Government appoints, and that different dates may be appointed for different provisions.

## What the Act changes, once a provision is in force

The Act is about digital personal data. A Data Fiduciary decides the purpose and means of processing. A Data Principal is the individual. A Data Processor processes on the fiduciary's behalf. Consent, in section 6, has to be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data necessary for the specified purpose. Section 7 lists certain legitimate uses, including employment and specified state functions, that are not the same thing as consent.

Section 8 makes the fiduciary responsible even if a contract says otherwise, requires reasonable security safeguards, and requires intimation of a personal data breach to the Board and to each affected person. Section 9 covers children, defined as anyone who has not completed eighteen years. Section 10 lets the Central Government notify a Significant Data Fiduciary by volume, sensitivity, risk to the person, sovereignty, electoral democracy, security of the State, and public order. The Data Protection Officer of such a fiduciary has to be based in India.

Section 16 is a restriction power, not a list of approved countries. The Central Government may notify countries or territories to which a transfer for processing is restricted. Any Indian law that is stricter still applies. That is the hinge on which the Reserve Bank's payment-data rule continues to sit. It is explained in the [cross-border note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-india-vs-global-benchmark-meity-gov-20260330-270).

The Schedule, read with section 33, sets monetary ceilings. Failure to take reasonable security safeguards may extend to two hundred and fifty crore rupees. Failure to intimate a breach, and failure of the children's duties, may each extend to two hundred crore. Significant Data Fiduciary duties may extend to one hundred and fifty crore. A Data Principal's breach of section 15 may extend to ten thousand rupees. Any other breach may extend to fifty crore. These are ceilings, not automatic fines.

## What had already changed by 24 September 2026

G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025 split the Act into three dates.

On publication, the government brought in the definition section, the Board's institutional provisions (sections 18 to 26), a block of miscellaneous powers including the power to make rules, and sub-sections (1) and (3) of section 44.

Section 44(1) is already in force. It amends the TRAI Act so that the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal is also the appellate tribunal under this Act. Section 44(3) is also already in force. It substitutes clause (j) of section 8(1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005, so that the clause reads "information which relates to personal information." That RTI change is not waiting for 2027.

Section 44(2) is not in that immediate list. It is in the eighteen-month list. Section 44(2)(a) omits section 43A of the Information Technology Act, 2000. Until 13 May 2027, that omission has not taken effect. The compensation section that sat under the IT Act is still on the statute book as of 24 September 2026. The [bottleneck note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-execution-bottleneck-review-meity-gov-20260330-590) is the place to read that split against PIB's sentence on "full operationalisation."

## What the Rules add, when their own clock says so

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified the same day, G.S.R. 846(E). PIB's note of 14 November 2025 describes an eighteen-month phased timeline, consent notices that stand on their own, Consent Managers that are Indian companies, breach intimation in plain language, and a maximum of 90 days for a fiduciary to respond to a person. Those descriptions match the Rules. The Rules' own commencement clause does not turn all of them on at once. Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 started on publication. Rule 4, the Consent Manager rule, starts one year later. Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 start eighteen months later.

PIB also says the Act follows a SARAL design: Simple, Accessible, Rational, and Actionable. That is the ministry's description of the drafting style. It is not a statement that every duty is already enforceable.

The next date that actually changes the legal position is 13 November 2026, and it is a narrow one. The [ninety-day checklist](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-next-90-days-checklist-meity-gov-20260330-470) says what to open that morning.
