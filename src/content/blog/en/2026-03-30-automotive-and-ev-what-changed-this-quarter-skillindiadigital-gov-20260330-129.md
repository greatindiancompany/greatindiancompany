# What Changed in India's EV Schemes Since July 2026

The useful change this quarter is a pair of official texts that do not say the same thing, because the second one came later. On 28 July 2026 the Ministry of Heavy Industries told Parliament that PM E-DRIVE was a ₹10,900 crore scheme, that the two-wheeler incentive ran only to 31 July 2026, and that L5 electric three-wheelers had already closed on 26 December 2025. On 10 August 2026 the same ministry published gazette S.O. 4424(E), which raises the scheme outlay to ₹11,900 crore and sets 31 March 2028 as the terminal date for every segment still open, including registered electric two-wheelers.

## What the July reply still records

The [28 July 2026 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696) is the latest disbursement table in this set. As on 22 July 2026, OEMs had been reimbursed ₹2,281.94 crore for 23,22,878 vehicles: 20,56,831 electric two-wheelers (₹1,505.30 crore), 5,082 e-rickshaws and e-carts (₹7.61 crore), and 2,60,965 L5 three-wheelers (₹769.02 crore). Those counts sit against the outlay and the vehicle caps that were in force that week. They are not a September tally, and they are not a share of the later ₹11,900 crore.

The same reply says no state-wise demand-incentive allocation exists, so the ministry does not keep a state table for those vehicles. It also says no assessment has been done of the scheme's effect on fuel use or emissions. What it does state is a penetration figure: electric vehicles moved from 0.7 percent of vehicles in FY 2019-20 to 8.2 percent in FY 2025-26, with FAME-II, PM E-DRIVE, and other policies named as support.

Charging and buses were still mid-process. Of the ₹2,000 crore kept for public charging stations, ₹689 crore had been approved as on 1 July 2026 for 6,562 chargers, assigned to three oil-marketing companies and nine states. The reply does not name the states. For buses, 14,000 had been allocated, letters of confirmation of quantity had gone to bidders for 13,800, and Surat and Hyderabad had signed concession agreements for 600 and 915 buses. Prototype approval and deployment were still ahead.

## What the August gazette changes

[S.O. 4424(E)](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/docs/policy_document/Gazette%20Notification%2010%2008%202026%20on%20enhancement%20of%20Outlay%2011900%20cr.pdf) amends the 29 September 2024 notification. The scheme runs from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2028, with EMPS-2024 (1 April to 30 September 2024) subsumed. The payout stays fund-limited: if a sub-component's money runs out before 31 March 2028, that sub-component closes. The last date to submit any claim is 31 December 2027.

For registered electric two-wheelers the annexure now reads 45,79,120 vehicles and ₹2,767 crore. From 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2028 the incentive is ₹2,500 per kWh, capped at ₹5,000 per vehicle, and also capped at 15 percent of ex-factory price. The FY 2024-25 rate in the same annexure remains ₹5,000 per kWh, capped at ₹10,000. L5 stays closed from 26 December 2025. The ministry portal's allocation table matches the new totals: scheme support of ₹11,900 crore, including ₹55 crore for administration. An older "About" paragraph on that same [portal](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/) still describes the original two-year design. The gazette and the allocation table are the later record.

## What September adds, and what it does not

On 3 September 2026 the ministry listed a fourth phased-manufacturing amendment for N2 and N3 electric trucks and another for M2 and M3 electric buses on its [notifications page](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/policy_document). This brief does not paraphrase those two PDFs. Anyone buying or supplying those vehicles should open them before treating an older localisation schedule as current.

SIAM's year-end sales release is from 14 April 2026, so it is not a July-to-September change. Use it for the FY 2025-26 base, not as news from this quarter.
