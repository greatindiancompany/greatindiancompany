# Where regional routes and inbound receipts have already broken

A risk map for Indian aviation and tourism should start from breaks that are already in the official tables, and only then say what a repeat would look like. The breaks are a route network that shrank after it was announced, a domestic-traffic year that stalled after a strong May, and a tourism-earnings year that fell in rupees while remaining above 2019.

## The route break

On 23 July 2026 the Ministry of Civil Aviation told the Lok Sabha that 679 RCS routes had been operationalised and 348 were still operational. The annexure counts 165 routes discontinued before they completed their viability-gap tenure. Uttar Pradesh accounts for 34 of those 165, Uttarakhand 17, Assam 15, Madhya Pradesh 14, Karnataka 12, and Gujarat and Rajasthan 11 each.

Six hundred and seventy-nine minus 348 is 331 routes no longer operating. One hundred and sixty-five is the subset discontinued early. The reply does not label the other 166. Some will have finished a three-year tenure and continued without support, some will have finished and stopped, and the paper does not say which. Treating all 331 as failures overstates the break. Treating 679 as the live network hides it.

Funding has not tapered to zero while that selection happened. Airlines received ₹807.02 crore of viability-gap funding in 2023-24, ₹628.42 crore in 2024-25, and ₹738.13 crore in 2025-26, the last year provisional. Support can rise in a year when the live network is 348 routes.

## The traffic break inside 2026

DGCA’s year-to-date line for domestic passengers carried is almost flat: 984.03 lakh in January–July 2026, against 977.79 lakh a year earlier, growth of 0.64 percent. Inside that line, May was 153.91 lakh and July was 119.99 lakh. July was also 4.8 percent below July 2025. The minister’s 16 July description of about five lakh domestic passengers a day belongs to the period that included the May peak. It is a poor description of July.

The concentration risk sits in the same DGCA tables. In June, IndiGo carried 89.20 lakh of 134.64 lakh passengers. A disruption at one carrier moves the national month because of that share. The papers here do not quantify a future disruption. They show the share.

## The inbound break

Ministry of Tourism foreign-exchange earnings for January–December 2025 were ₹2,73,638 crore, down 6.62 percent from ₹2,93,033 crore in 2024. May and June 2025 were the sharp months, down 21.35 percent and 22.2 percent. The year was still 26.4 percent above 2019’s ₹2,16,467 crore. A sector can be above its pre-pandemic rupee earnings and still have had a down year. Both describe 2025.

Foreign tourist arrivals and international arrivals are different series. The down year in rupee earnings should be checked against both before anyone calls it a collapse in “tourists.” The 2024 compendium levels were 9.95 million foreign tourists and 20.57 million international arrivals.

## Three readings, labeled as readings

If the new five-year, tapered support works as the 23 July reply hopes, more of the next 120 destinations will still be flying in year five, and the early-exit list will not grow like the 165. If airlines again bid beyond the aircraft they have, the 16 July rule — no permit, no bid; miss the window, lose the route — will show up as re-bids rather than as a longer route list. If domestic months keep the May-to-July shape and rupee tourism earnings stay below 2024, airport projects sized only to the May peak or to the 2024 earnings year will be early.

None of the three is a probability. Each is a way to recognise the next official table when it arrives.
