# Why India Publishes Housing Prices Without a Global League Table

A slide that ranks Indian housing against another country needs a method both statistical offices would sign. The official Indian releases a reader can open today do not contain that ranking. They contain domestic series that are already hard to compare with each other. Inventing the cross-country step on top of that gap does not make the slide more serious.

## Two Indian series that are not interchangeable

The Reserve Bank's House Price Index for Q1 2026-27, released on 24 August 2026, stood at 117.5 on a 2022-23 base. It rose 1.1 percent on the quarter and 3.6 percent on the year. It covers eighteen cities and uses registration-authority transactions. That is a closed description. It says nothing about London, Singapore, or a global city index.

National Housing Bank RESIDEX was built, the Bank writes, so that a national composite could be set beside international series. The [about page](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/Dashboard/About) says composite indices were computed with population weights "to provide comparison of NHB RESIDEX at international level." That sentence is a design aim. It is not a published table of India versus a named foreign index. This note does not supply the missing crosswalk.

Inside India, NHB already warns that one city can have three prices. Registered prices come from sub-registrar offices. Assessment prices come from valuations at primary lending institutions. Under-construction market prices come from a market survey of developer stock, primary market only, and they exclude resale. From the quarter ended March 2026 the published series uses FY 2024-25 as the base, linked back to the FY 2017-18 series. Coverage is 50 cities, including 18 capitals and 37 smart cities. The RBI index and the NHB registered-price index can both be called "registration based" and still differ in city set, base year, and compiler.

Until someone documents those differences, a foreign house-price series that mixes listings, valuations, and transactions is not a benchmark for 117.5. It is a different object with a similar name.

## What India did standardise

Where India has written a common rule, it is about the unit of sale and the public register, not about a global price level.

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act requires a project to be registered before it is advertised, and it requires a separate account for seventy percent of allottee receipts. MoHUA's July 2025 FAQ treats carpet area, completion, and occupancy as defined terms in the Act. The PMAY-U 2.0 FAQ uses its own plain sentence for carpet area: the area enclosed within the walls, not including the thickness of the inner walls, and it caps support at 45 square metres for beneficiary-led and partnership houses, 60 square metres for affordable rental, and 120 square metres for the interest subsidy, with a floor of 30 square metres. Those two documents should not be quoted as if they were one definition. A global "price per square foot" that does not say whether the foot is carpet, built-up, or super built-up is not comparable to either.

The mission is also a poor stand-in for a price benchmark. PMAY-U 2.0 is central assistance for eligible families who do not own a pucca house, for five years from 2024 to 2029. It is not a statement that Indian urban prices are high or low by world standards. The ministry's investment figure of ₹10 lakh crore on the scheme page is the mission's stated envelope, not a measured share of GDP placed next to another country's housing budget.

## A comparison that can be defended

A defensible note has three sentences. State which Indian series is used, and its base and coverage. State that NHB's international-comparison language is an aim recorded on its about page, not a result reproduced here. State the domestic rule that does travel: sale of a registrable project is gated by the state Real Estate Regulatory Authority, and assistance under the urban mission follows the income and ownership tests in the PMAY FAQ.

Readers who need the latest domestic print, without the global leap, should use the [Q1 2026-27 price brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-market-signal-brief-mca-gov-20260330-201). Great Indian Company would rather withhold a league table than print one the Reserve Bank and the National Housing Bank have not published.
