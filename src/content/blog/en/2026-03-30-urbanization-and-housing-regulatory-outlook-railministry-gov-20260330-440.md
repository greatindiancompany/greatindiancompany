# RERA Is the Urban Housing Rulebook, and States Run It

## The statute MoHUA administers

Urban housing projects that are sold to the public sit under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016. India Code records it as Act 16 of 2016, under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs. The long title is precise: establish a Real Estate Regulatory Authority, make the sale of a plot, apartment, or building efficient and transparent, protect consumers, provide adjudication, and establish an appellate tribunal.

MoHUA’s RERA page records the parliamentary path. The Rajya Sabha passed the Bill on 10 March 2016, the Lok Sabha on 15 March 2016, and the President assented on 25 March 2016.

## Commencement was split on purpose

Notification S.O. 1544(E), dated 26 April 2016, appointed 1 May 2016 as the date for section 2, sections 20 to 39, sections 41 to 58, sections 71 to 78, and sections 81 to 92. India Code states the same limit: enforcement on 1 May 2016 except sections 3 to 19, 40, 59 to 70, and 79 and 80. Sections 20 to 39 are the authority. Sections 3 to 19 are prior registration of projects, the application, the agent, and the duties that make a sale checkable. Those sections were not in the 2016 notification. MoHUA publishes a separate document for commencement of the remaining sections in 2017. The regulatory system a buyer uses, the public register, is the 2017 layer on top of authorities that could be constituted from May 2016.

Section 4 requires seventy percent of the amounts realised from allottees, from time to time, to be kept in a separate account in a scheduled bank and used only for construction cost and land cost. That is a use-of-funds rule. It sits in the registration chapter, so it attaches to projects that have to register. Section 6 deals with extension of registration. Section 43 establishes the appellate tribunal. Disputes are designed to move through the authority and the tribunal, not through a MoHUA helpdesk.

## States write the working rules

The Act tells each appropriate government to make rules and to establish the authority. MoHUA’s page lists state and Union Territory authority sites, including Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat, Telangana, Delhi, and others. A compliance check is a check of that state’s register and that state’s rules. A project registered in one state is not registered for another. Carpet area, timelines, and the agreement for sale travel through those rules. Reading only the central Act and skipping the state rules leaves the operative form unread.

On 31 July 2026 the ministry’s Housing Division issued an advisory, hosted as a PDF on mohua.gov.in, to the chairpersons of all Real Estate Regulatory Authorities. It points to Section 6, which allows extension of registration for force majeure, including war, and to Section 7(3), which lets an authority keep a registration in force on terms it considers appropriate for allottees. The advisory cites a Department of Expenditure office memorandum of 29 April 2026 that treats the West Asia situation as war for force majeure. It advises authorities that they may extend registration and the corresponding completion timelines by four months for registered projects whose completion date, revised completion date, or extended completion date falls on or after 28 February 2026, and that they may do so by a common order rather than project-by-project applications. The advice is to the authorities. It is not itself the extension. A buyer or lender still needs the order of the state authority that actually grants it.

## Where RERA stops and the missions start

PMAY-Urban 2.0’s Interest Subsidy Scheme, as PIB reported on 24 July 2025, uses carpet area up to 120 square metres as an eligibility cap. Carpet area is also the Act’s measure. A lender and a developer who still sell on super built-up area are crossing a statutory definition and a subsidy definition at the same time. The subsidy’s other caps, income of ₹9 lakh, loan of ₹25 lakh, and property value of ₹35 lakh, are scheme guidelines. They are not sections of the Act. A house can be lawful under RERA and ineligible for the subsidy, and the reverse error is also possible if a subsidised unit is sold without the registration the Act requires.

Affordable rental housing and beneficiary-led construction raise different registration questions depending on whether a promoter is selling a unit to an allottee. The Act’s trigger is that sale. A rental arrangement or a household building on its own land is not automatically a registered project. State authority guidance is the place to resolve the boundary. This brief does not invent a national exemption.

The practical next step is the state register, the July 2026 advisory if an extension is in play, and the subsidy caps if the unit is also a PMAY file.
