# Why Indian Housing Delivery Still Depends on the State

A national housing number is a sum of state files. The Centre writes the Act and the mission. The state notifies rules, stands up the Real Estate Regulatory Authority, signs the PMAY memorandum, sanctions projects, and decides whether a model tenancy law becomes its own statute. Reading India as one housing market skips the layer where delay and delivery actually sit.

## Assistance is already a state schedule

The [PMAY-U 2.0 FAQ](https://pmaymis.gov.in/PMAYMIS2_2024/faq.html) publishes three central-assistance rates for beneficiary-led construction and affordable housing in partnership.

- North-Eastern states, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir, Puducherry, and Delhi: ₹2.25 lakh from the Centre, and at least ₹0.25 lakh from the state or territory.
- Other Union Territories: ₹2.50 lakh from the Centre.
- Other states: ₹1.50 lakh from the Centre, and at least ₹1 lakh from the state.

The same geography gets a technology innovation grant on affordable rental housing of ₹3,000 per square metre from the Centre and ₹2,000 per square metre from the state, on the special-category row of that table. The interest subsidy, up to ₹1.80 lakh, is a central-sector payment and does not follow the state-share column.

States join the mission by signing a memorandum of agreement with the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs. Newly notified towns enter only after a state gazette and ministry approval. Landless families become eligible for beneficiary-led construction only if the state, at its own expense, gives heritable and non-transferable land rights. None of that is a central field office deciding a ward.

## Application share is not completion

The [ministry scheme page](https://www.mohua.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/pradhan-mantri-awas-yojana-urban-MjNzYjMtQWa) says that about 56 lakh applications have come through the unified portal, and it lists leading shares: Uttar Pradesh 41 percent, Bihar 13 percent, Madhya Pradesh 8 percent, Karnataka 7 percent, Telangana 5 percent, Assam 4 percent, Maharashtra 4 percent, Haryana 3 percent, and Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Andhra Pradesh 2 percent each. Those are the shares the page prints. They do not add to 100, because the page is naming leaders, not closing a table. They are applications, not occupied houses. The dashboard of sanctioned houses, utilisation certificates, and occupation stage is the progress record. This note does not replace it with the application list.

Coverage on that page is 5,494 towns and urban development authorities, against an original list of 4,041 Census 2011 statutory towns. Inclusion of a town is not delivery in that town.

## The regulator is also state-shaped

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, the appropriate government for a state is the state government. It notifies rules, establishes the authority and the appellate tribunal, and sets the interest rate, which must be the same for promoter and allottee. A project is registered with that state's authority. An agent, including a web portal, needs a separate registration in each state where it sells. Fees, the agreement-for-sale form, and the website fields beyond the Act are rule-making, which is why two state registers do not look identical.

The July 2025 central FAQ says many authorities already prescribe different project fees, and that a state may waive or cut fees for slum rehabilitation. It does not publish a league table of which state has done so.

## Tenancy adoption has a date stamp

The Model Tenancy Act was circulated to every state and Union Territory on 7 June 2021. On 25 July 2022, a written reply in the Rajya Sabha, reported by [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1844644), said Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, and Assam had revised tenancy Acts on those lines, on information then with the ministry. Using that sentence as a 2026 map overstates what the reply claimed on the day it was given. The text circulated is the [Model Tenancy Act PDF](https://www.mohua.gov.in/pdf/modeltenancyact2021.pdf). Whether a given state has enacted it since July 2022 is a question for that state's current law, not for this brief.
