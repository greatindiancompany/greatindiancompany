# Public corridor spending and private PLI investment

People watching an "India capex cycle" often add every manufacturing number they can find. The official record keeps at least two capital stocks apart. This note is about that split, as published, not about a forecast of the next quarter's private capex.

## Public capital in corridor nodes

On 30 August 2024 the Press Information Bureau reported Cabinet approval of 12 industrial-node proposals under the National Industrial Corridor Development Programme, at an estimated investment of ₹28,602 crore. The note is [PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136). The money is for industrial areas and cities along corridors, developed with state governments through special purpose vehicles. It is trunk infrastructure and land development, not a subsidy paid per unit of factory output.

The same note describes how the institutional machinery was built. The Delhi Mumbai Industrial Corridor Development Corporation was set up on 7 January 2008 under DPIIT. In December 2016 the trust's mandate was widened into the National Industrial Corridor Development and Implementation Trust. In February 2020 the company was renamed the National Industrial Corridor Development Corporation. That history matters for capex timing: these are multi-year civil projects. The August 2024 note is an approval. It says four projects had been completed and four were under implementation. It does not say the twelve new nodes were built.

Dholera's SPV structure, AURIC's 10,000 acres, Greater Noida's 50:50 venture, and Vikram Udyogpuri's 1,096 acres show the asset. They do not show how much of the ₹28,602 crore had been spent by September 2026. That spend figure is not in the documents used here.

## Private capital reported under PLI

The other stock is in the 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf). As on 31 March 2026, actual investment under the 14 PLI schemes was over ₹2.40 lakh crore. The sector annexure of 21 July 2026, [PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008), totals ₹2,40,138 crore. That is investment reported by approved applicants. It is not the government's incentive, which the same Lok Sabha reply puts at ₹35,354 crore disbursed against an approved outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore.

An earlier checkpoint, the Rajya Sabha reply of 27 March 2026 ([PRID 2246089](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246089)), put cumulative investment over ₹2.16 lakh crore as on 31 December 2025. The move from that figure to the March figure is the latest official step in the series. It is one quarter of reported stock, not proof of a cycle turning.

Inside the March annexure the capital is uneven. Solar PV modules are ₹64,873 crore, pharmaceutical drugs ₹45,158 crore, automobiles and auto components ₹44,326 crore, specialty steel ₹23,896 crore, and large-scale electronics ₹20,580 crore. Advanced chemistry cell batteries are ₹4,570 crore with 1,245 people in the employment column. A capex watcher who only tracks job announcements will miss the battery and solar lines. A watcher who only tracks those lines will miss food products, where the employment column is 3,29,200 and investment is ₹9,207 crore.

## What not to call a cycle

DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 records manufacturing growth in the index of industrial production, base 2011–12, at 9.9 percent in FY 2023–24 after -3 percent in FY 2019–20. That is an output index through FY 2023–24. It is not a 2026 capex print, and it is not the corridor estimate.

No release used here gives a single "India manufacturing capex" total for FY 2025–26, a private-sector guidance number, or a split of the ₹2.40 lakh crore by greenfield and brownfield. Until those exist in an official table, the disciplined watch is the next parliamentary update of PLI investment past 31 March 2026, and any later status note on the twelve nodes.
