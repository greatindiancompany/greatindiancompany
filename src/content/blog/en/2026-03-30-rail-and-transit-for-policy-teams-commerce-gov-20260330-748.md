# Railways and Urban Affairs Split India's Transit System

A policy note that says “rail and transit” and then cites one budget has already mixed two ministries. Indian Railways and the metro programme report to different rules, different budgets, and different city institutions.

## What the Ministry of Railways actually holds

The railway book, in the releases of April to August 2026, includes freight loading of 1,670 million tonnes in 2025-26, the commissioned Eastern and Western dedicated freight corridors, Vande Bharat (162 chair-car services and 2 sleeper services as of 23 July 2026), the Gati Shakti terminal policy, wagon schemes, passenger fares, and recruitment against 1,61,889 non-gazetted vacancies. Gross budgetary support in Budget Estimates 2026-27 is ₹2,78,030 crore. The sanctioned construction book as on 1 April 2026 is 514 projects, 40,000 km, and about ₹8.31 lakh crore.

Passenger fares are a social policy as well as a tariff. The 11 March 2026 Lok Sabha reply put the passenger subsidy at ₹60,239 crore in 2024-25, about 43 percent on average. A state government cannot reprice that subsidy. It can ask for a train, and the ministry has said new Vande Bharat sleeper services still depend on a path, stock, and maintenance infrastructure.

## What the urban affairs ministry holds

Metro rail is the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs. The Metro Rail Policy, 2017, requires a Unified Metropolitan Transport Authority and a Comprehensive Mobility Plan, and it routes central assistance through an equal-equity special-purpose vehicle with the state. A PIB note of 9 August 2025 describes a 14 percent economic internal-rate-of-return test and mandatory private participation for that assistance, plus domestic procurement shares for metro cars and key equipment.

The network PIB reported on 15 March 2026 was about 1,095 km across 26 cities, including 55 km of the Delhi–Meerut regional rapid transit system. The metro budget in that note is ₹29,550 crore for 2025-26, against ₹5,798 crore in 2013-14. Since 2014, 38 projects covering 1,051 km have been sanctioned at about ₹3.44 lakh crore. An August 2025 note had stated a 2025-26 metro budget of ₹34,807 crore. Policy teams should cite the March 2026 figure and keep the earlier note in the file, not blend them.

Namo Bharat, at 160 km/h on Delhi–Meerut, is part of that urban and regional system. It is not a zonal railway train. Kolkata’s underwater metro and Kochi’s water metro, described in the same March note, are city projects.

## Where the two systems are supposed to meet

The 15 March 2026 note says metro projects are being placed inside the PM GatiShakti National Master Plan, and that the Network Planning Group reviews metro and aviation projects together. That is a planning forum. It does not merge the railway gross budgetary support line with the metro budget. Gati Shakti cargo terminals, despite the shared name, are a Ministry of Railways freight policy dated 15 December 2021. Using “Gati Shakti” as a single scheme will brief the wrong instrument.

A usable policy split is: corridors, freight yield, fares, and railway hiring on one page; metro assistance tests, city authorities, and the 26-city network on another. The rule text is in the [policy-shift explainer](/blog/rail-and-transit-policy-shift-explainer-commerce-gov-20260330-148). The assistance gates are in the [regulatory outlook](/blog/rail-and-transit-regulatory-outlook-commerce-gov-20260330-428).

Great Indian Company keeps the two ministries apart so a briefing note cannot spend a railway rupee on a metro line, or the reverse.
