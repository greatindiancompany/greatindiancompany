# How policy teams should read India’s export trade numbers

A policy note on export competitiveness goes wrong in a predictable way: one paragraph adds a customs export, a BoP credit, a national-accounts growth rate, and a scheme outlay, then calls the sum a result. The reading order below is how to keep those lines apart.

## Four series, four questions

Customs quick estimates, from the Commerce statistics division and DGCIS, answer what left or entered on a shipping bill that month. The 13 August 2026 narrative is the July merchandise print: exports US$ 44.24 billion, imports US$ 76.22 billion. The 15 September annex is the August merchandise print: exports US$ 43,811 million, imports US$ 70,670 million. Services in the July narrative were labelled as an estimate.

The RBI monthly services release answers a different question, on a BoP basis, and it arrives later. July 2026 services exports are US$ 38,253 million in the 31 August provisional print. Replacing the Commerce estimate with this figure is a correction, not a revision of merchandise.

The quarterly balance of payments answers how the goods deficit, services, income, and remittances netted. April–June 2026–27 shows a goods deficit of US$ 86.1 billion, net services of US$ 51.6 billion, and a current account deficit of US$ 4.2 billion, 0.5 percent of GDP. Customs months will not add up to that goods line, and a later customs revision can move it. The RBI has already revised one recent quarter for that reason.

MoSPI’s national accounts answer a volume question. Real exports of goods and services grew 12.0 percent in April–June 2026–27. That rate is not “exports grew 12 percent in dollars.”

## Four instruments, four verbs

RoDTEP remits embedded duties and taxes on eligible goods. The live event is the 23 March 2026 restoration of rates and caps after a 50 percent cut. RoSCTL does that job for apparel, garments, and made-ups, and it was extended, with RoDTEP, beyond 31 March 2026. Niryat Protsahan cheapens or de-risks rupee export credit for MSMEs, at a stated 2.75 percent subvention and a ₹50 lakh IEC cap for 2025–26, on a pilot. EPCG and Advance Authorisation remove duty on inputs and capital goods against an export obligation. A sentence that calls all four “incentives” will mis-brief a minister the first time a trading partner asks about subsidies.

FTA language needs the same discipline. In force (UK CETA from 15 July 2026; EFTA TEPA from 1 October 2025) is not signed, announced, or concluded (New Zealand announced 22 December 2025; EU concluded 27 January 2026, in the April textiles note). Zero-duty for nearly 99 percent of India’s exports to the UK is a government description of the agreement that is in force. It is still subject to origin rules the note does not reprint.

## Counts that are not values

Logistics cost at about 7.97 percent of GDP is the DPIIT–NCAER estimate launched on 20 September 2025. It replaced a widely repeated 13–14 percent. It is not a monthly KPI. District coverage — committees in all 36 states and Union territories, draft plans for 590 districts, 249 notified, as of the 24 March 2026 Lok Sabha reply — is not a state export share. State shares come from DGCIS. If the share is not in the extract, leave it out.
