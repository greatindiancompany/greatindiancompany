If the startup system had a clogged front door, the recognition stock would stall. It has not stalled. The clog is after the certificate: getting onto the books of a SEBI-registered fund, an incubator with remaining allocation, or a lender using the credit guarantee.

## The front door is a self-certified form

Startup India’s recognition page takes applications through the National Single Window System. A Lok Sabha reply says the process is self-certification with simplified documentation. DPIIT charges no fee. In April–June 2026 the system accepted 16,334 recognitions and the stock reached 2,40,092. Financial year 2025-26 had already accepted more than 55,200. A process that clears tens of thousands of entities a year is not the binding constraint on capital.

The February 2026 widening of the test makes the front door wider still: turnover up to ₹200 crore, a deep-tech path to 20 years and ₹300 crore, and cooperatives. More eligible entities will not, by themselves, create more cheques.

## The back door is three small stocks

PIB’s year-end account for 2025-26 puts Fund of Funds disbursements at more than ₹7,000 crore to over 135 alternative investment funds, and those funds’ investments at more than ₹26,900 crore in over 1,420 startups. In the year itself, disbursement to funds was 1,039+ crore rupees. Set that beside 55,200+ new recognitions in the same year. The average new recognised startup was not a draw on that disbursement. A Lok Sabha reply dated in the 17 March 2026 PIB release gives the 31 January 2026 selection stock: 1,382 startups under the Fund of Funds, 3,311 under the seed fund, and 281 under the credit guarantee.

The seed-fund corpus of ₹945 crore was fully committed to 219 incubators by the end of 2025-26. Approved funding had crossed ₹605 crore for more than 3,400 startups. The scheme was extended so approved work could be disbursed and finished. An extension for completion is an execution fact. It is not a fresh ₹945 crore. Incubators without an existing approval are waiting on a committed corpus, not on a new appropriation announced in these releases.

The guarantee has been open since 1 April 2023. By 31 January 2026, loans of around ₹925 crore had been guaranteed. By the end of March 2026 the stock was more than 410 loans and over ₹1,250 crore, after the per-borrower ceiling rose from ₹10 crore to ₹20 crore. Hundreds of loans, against a registry in the lakhs, means the product has not become ordinary bank credit. PIB says the cover fraction was enhanced and the fee cut for identified sectors. It does not say lenders have used the new ceiling at scale. The Startup India scheme page remains the operational rulebook for a bank that wants to.

## Failures inside the schemes are not the clog

The same 31 January reply asks MCA how many scheme beneficiaries were dissolved or struck off. Seventeen Fund of Funds startups and 26 seed-fund startups were in that closed category. Against 1,382 and 3,311 selected, those closure counts are small. The reply’s CGSS column shows 281 startups selected; the public text does not clearly print a matching closed count, so none is stated here. The registry-wide closure stock is a different and larger figure: 6,789 of 2,12,283 recognised entities. The bottleneck for capital is admission to the schemes, not a wave of failures among those already admitted.

## The new fund still has an empty seat in its own guidelines

Fund of Funds 2.0 tells SIDBI to start, and tells DPIIT to select another domestic implementation agency in due course. Until that agency is named in an order, capacity to screen funds is the capacity SIDBI already has, plus a committee. The guidelines also slow deployment on purpose: a 50 percent ceiling on all government fund-of-funds money inside one alternative investment fund, segment caps, and multipliers that force private capital to show up before the government cheque can do its job. Those rules are the design. They are also why a ₹10,000 crore corpus will not arrive as ₹10,000 crore of startup bank balances in a single year.

A policy team that wants a dashboard for this clog should count letters of intent, drawdowns, and the second agency, not another recognition milestone.
