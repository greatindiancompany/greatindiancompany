# Jobs and skills in India's food processing

Food processing is one of the larger employers in Indian manufacturing, and most of the people in it are not on a registered factory roll. Scheme reviews add a third kind of number: jobs a project is expected to support, or jobs beneficiaries report. Keeping those counts apart is the useful part of reading this labour market.

## Two official employment pools

The Annual Report for 2024-25, using the Annual Survey of Industries 2022-23, says registered food processing engaged 22.96 lakh persons. That was 12.41 percent of the 184.95 lakh persons in registered manufacturing. The Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises for the same year put 46.57 lakh workers in unincorporated food processing, 15.21 percent of employment in unregistered manufacturing.

The Year End Review of 9 January 2026 cites a later survey year for the organised share only. It says food processing accounted for 12.83 percent of employment in the registered or organised sector in ASI 2023-24. It does not publish a new headcount. The 22.96 lakh figure stays tied to 2022-23.

Women's employment is stated in the same annual report, separately from the factory total. The ASI for 2022-23 counted 2.17 lakh women workers in registered food-processing units. The unincorporated survey for 2022-23 counted 11.25 lakh women workers in food processing. The report is plain that ministry schemes are project-based and do not set a quota by gender. A gender budget cell exists in the ministry. That cell is an internal budget practice, not a reserved share of cold-chain grants or micro-enterprise subsidies.

## Scheme job figures answer a different question

When the ministry says each cold-chain project helps create direct employment for about 100 persons and indirect employment for about 500, it is describing a project type in the cold-chain chapter, not adding a new national survey. The same chapter says each such project links about 9,552 farmers. Multiply those planning ratios by all 394 approved projects only if the note is explicit that the result is the ministry's planning arithmetic, not a payroll census.

The Year End Review's PMKSY total is in the same family of language. Approved projects, on operationalisation, are expected to result in more than 7.22 lakh direct and indirect jobs. "Expected" and "direct/indirect" both belong in the sentence.

PLISFPI is reported differently. The review says investment reported under the scheme has generated employment opportunities of 3.4 lakh. That figure comes from scheme reporting by beneficiaries. It is not a line in the Annual Survey of Industries, and it should not be added to the 22.96 lakh registered-factory count.

Inside mega food parks that are already running, the annual report is more concrete and much smaller: about 114 units in 24 operational parks, providing employment to more than 27,000 people directly, plus a larger franchise and dealership count. Direct jobs on a park gate and indirect jobs in a scheme expectation should not be added together.

PMFME's main labour effect is formalisation of units that often already exist. The coverage target is 2,00,000 micro enterprises. By the January 2026 review, 1,72,707 credit-linked loans had been sanctioned. A sanctioned micro unit is not automatically a new factory job of the ASI kind.

## Skills routes that exist on paper

The Human Resources and Institutions component of PMKSY no longer sanctions new skill-development projects in the 15th Finance Commission cycle. What remains under that head for new work is research and development. Training has not disappeared. It sits in other institutions.

The ministry runs an internship of 6 to 12 weeks for undergraduate, postgraduate, and PhD students, with an intake of 15 a quarter, and a certificate from the ministry. The National Institute of Food Technology, Entrepreneurship and Management at Kundli and the institute at Thanjavur are the ministry's academic bodies. Their annual-report tables record women students in B.Tech, M.Tech, and PhD batches. Those tables are campus participation, not sector hiring.

For a working plant, the binding training rule is the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India's. Food businesses holding a central or state licence are told to have at least one FoSTaC-certified food safety supervisor for every 25 food handlers, or part thereof, on each premises. PMFME's own machinery is district resource persons, who help prepare the project report and the bank file, plus incubation centres: 76 approved and 23 commissioned by the January 2026 review, for product development on district-product and allied lines.

A skills plan for this sector should say which pool it means. A supervisor certificate, a campus degree, a micro-unit subsidy, and a 3.4 lakh scheme-reported PLI figure are four different outcomes.
