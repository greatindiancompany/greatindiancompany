# Future of Work in India: Benchmarking the Labour-Code Design

## A benchmark of legal design

On 21 November 2025 the Ministry of Labour and Employment said many Indian labour laws dated from the 1930s to the 1950s, that major economies had consolidated labour regulation, and that India had still been operating under 29 central labour laws. The four codes brought into force that day — the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 — are the consolidation.

That comparison is about statute design. Unemployment rates, participation rates, and gig-worker shares against other countries belong in the studies that publish them. This brief keeps the comparison on the legal choices the ministry wrote down.

## Written proof of employment

The ministry's before-and-after note says appointment letters are required. Its audio-visual sector note says the letter states designation, wages, and social-security entitlement. A multinational benchmarking India against its home-country practice can ask whether the India letter carries those elements. The answer is in the personnel file.

## Platform work inside social security

The Code on Social Security defines gig work, platform work, and aggregators. The ministry's note sets an aggregator contribution of 1 to 2 percent of annual turnover, capped at 5 percent of amounts paid or payable to those workers, with an Aadhaar-linked Universal Account Number for portability across states. NITI Aayog's June 2022 report, *India's Booming Gig and Platform Economy*, discussed social-security practice and recommended that the Periodic Labour Force Survey identify gig workers. The benchmark question is whether the worker is visible in the statute and, when the survey adds the category, in PLFS. A platform's headcount in another country is a different benchmark.

## One code text, many rulebooks

A Lok Sabha reply on 20 July 2026 records Central Rules notified on 8 May 2026 and says states and Union Territories must frame rules because labour is concurrent. A global handbook that assumes a single national rulebook misreads India even when the code text is uniform. The honest benchmark asks which appropriate government applies to the establishment.

## IT and BPM conditions

The ministry's IT and ITES section addresses salary by the 7th, equal pay, night work for women with consent and safety measures, and social security through fixed-term employment and appointment letters. Those are the India employment conditions a global delivery-centre comparison can use. Utilisation and hiring plans remain commercial facts, outside this legal benchmark.
