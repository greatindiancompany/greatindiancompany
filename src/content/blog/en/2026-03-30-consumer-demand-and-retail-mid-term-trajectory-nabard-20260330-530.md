# The consumption path already written into the new series

A medium-term reading of Indian consumer demand has to start from the break in the series. MoSPI released the new annual and quarterly GDP estimates, base year 2022-23, on 27 February 2026, and provisional estimates for 2025-26 on 5 June 2026. The 31 August 2026 quarterly note then placed April–June 2026-27 on that base, and pointed readers to National Accounts Statistics 2026 for the revised annual years and the updated quarters back to 2022-23.

On that base, real PFCE in the first quarter grew 7.1%, after 6.8% in the first quarter of 2025-26. Nominal PFCE was 55.6% of nominal GDP, against 55.8% a year earlier. The trajectory those two quarters support is steady real growth in household consumption and a roughly stable nominal share, while real capital formation (11.9%) ran ahead. Two quarters are not a five-year path. They are the path the new series has actually printed.

## The household survey underneath the base

The new CPI base is 2024, and the national-accounts base is 2022-23. Both updates sit downstream of the back-to-back Household Consumption Expenditure Surveys. The HCES 2023-24 factsheet, for August 2023–July 2024, put unimputed average MPCE at ₹4,122 rural and ₹6,996 urban, with food still 47.04% of the rural budget and 39.68% of the urban budget. That composition is the medium-term fact about what Indian households buy. It moves more slowly than a quarterly growth rate.

## The inflation path the MPC has already published

The 5 August 2026 resolution projected CPI inflation for 2026-27 at 5.0%, with Q2 at 4.7%, Q3 at 5.9%, Q4 at 5.5%, and Q1 of 2027-28 at 5.3%. Core inflation was projected at 4.3%. The committee said headline inflation would peak in the third quarter on food and fuel, and that core inflation excluding precious metals still pointed to contained demand pressures. It also said the continuing effect of GST rationalisation should keep supporting urban demand, while an uneven south-west monsoon and El Niño conditions posed a risk to agriculture and rural demand.

The medium-term sentence, using only those publications, is this. Real consumption entered 2026-27 growing a little faster than it had a year earlier, at a nominal share just under 56%. The price path the MPC published rises into late 2026 because of food and fuel. Rural budgets, on the last household survey, still devote nearly half of per-capita spending to food, so that price path lands on a large share of the rural basket.
