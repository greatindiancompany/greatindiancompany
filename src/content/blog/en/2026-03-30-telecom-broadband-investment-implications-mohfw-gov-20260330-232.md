# Three Broadband Cash Flows That Should Stay Separate

An investment note on Indian telecom goes wrong when it adds a Cabinet outlay, a quarter of industry revenue, and a recommended spectrum price into one "capex cycle." The Department of Telecommunications, TRAI, and the Press Information Bureau publish them as different objects. This brief leaves them apart.

## Public programme outlay

PIB's 21 December 2024 note on BharatNet records the Amended BharatNet Programme, approved in August 2023, at a cost of Rs 1,39,579 crore. The design is optical fibre from block to gram panchayat in ring topology for 2.64 lakh gram panchayats, fibre to the remaining non-gram-panchayat villages (about 3.8 lakh) on demand, an IP-MPLS network, ten years of operation and maintenance against a service-level agreement, power backup, and remote fibre monitoring. PIB's 12 February 2026 parliamentary reply describes Phases I and II, the network already built, as nearly 2.14 lakh gram panchayats online out of 2.56 lakh, at an approximate cost of Rs 42,000 crore. The same reply calls the amended programme a $16.9 billion initiative. That dollar phrase is the PIB wording. The rupee outlay to use for the design is the Rs 1,39,579 crore figure.

This money sits on the public side, largely through Digital Bharat Nidhi, the renamed Universal Service Obligation Fund. It is a reason a rural fibre crew gets paid. It is a poor proxy for the capital expenditure of a listed access provider.

## Industry revenue, one quarter

For January–March 2026, TRAI reported gross revenue of Rs 1,05,118 crore, up 2.58 percent on the previous quarter, and adjusted gross revenue of Rs 86,716 crore, up 2.90 percent. Applicable gross revenue was Rs 98,638 crore. Public-sector undertakings accounted for 3.12 percent of access adjusted gross revenue. Monthly wireless ARPU was Rs 196.04. Revenue realisation on wireless data was Rs 7.51 per GB, on usage of 26.70 GB per wireless data subscriber per month.

That is the operating pool from which spectrum instalments, tower rents, and fibre builds are paid. A 2.90 percent quarterly rise in adjusted gross revenue does not, by itself, say whether network spending rose.

## Spectrum that is recommended, and cash that is not yet bid

On 24 February 2026 TRAI released recommendations on the auction of spectrum in the bands identified for international mobile telecommunications. DoT had asked, by letter dated 15 May 2025, for reserve prices and auction conditions in the 800, 900, 1800, 2100, 2300, 2500, 3300 MHz, and 26 GHz bands, and for advice on 600 MHz and the upper 6 GHz ranges. TRAI's press note recommends that the available spectrum in 600, 800, 900, 1800, 2100, 2300, 2500, 3300 MHz, and 26 GHz be put to auction, with a 20-year validity, on a circle or metro basis, using a simultaneous multiple-round auction.

The same note recommends a coverage scheme: after the auction, a winning bidder could opt for a reduction of up to 10 percent of the auction-determined price, and in return deploy new base-station sites in coverage holes identified by DoT, using 4G or 5G. That scheme is a recommendation to DoT. It is not a completed auction, and this brief does not treat the reserve-price table as a bill already issued. The upper 6 GHz ranges (6425–6725 MHz and 7025–7125 MHz) are recommended to stay out of the forthcoming auction until after the next World Radiocommunication Conference.

A practical reading for capital planning: budget the public fibre programme on the Cabinet figure, budget industry cash flow on the quarterly revenue series, and budget spectrum only after DoT issues a notice inviting applications. The spending shape of the public programme is in the [capex note](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/telecom-broadband-capex-cycle-watch-mohfw-gov-20260330-352).
