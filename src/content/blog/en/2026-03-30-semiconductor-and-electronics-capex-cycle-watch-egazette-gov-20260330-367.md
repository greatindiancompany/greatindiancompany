The semiconductor capex cycle India can document is a sequence of Cabinet approvals, not a published series of quarterly spend. Each approval states an investment commitment and a product. The releases used here do not state how many rupees have been drawn. This brief follows the sequence and then separates the three different capital subsidies an equipment invoice might meet. It is current to 24 September 2026.

## Approvals, in the order the record gives them

June 2023. The [29 February 2024 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2010132) says the Union Cabinet had approved Micron's unit at Sanand, Gujarat. The [15 August 2025 factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=149242&reg=48&lang=2) lists the investment at ₹22,516 crore and describes an assembly-and-test facility with a phased ramp-up. By 22 July 2026 this plant was one of three in commercial production. In capex terms it is the project that has moved furthest from approval to output, and it is a packaging plant.

29 February 2024. Three units were approved together, and the release said construction on all three would start within 100 days. Tata Electronics with PSMC at Dholera, ₹91,000 crore, 50,000 wafer starts a month. Tata Semiconductor Assembly and Test at Morigaon, ₹27,000 crore, 48 million chips a day. CG Power with Renesas and Stars at Sanand, ₹7,600 crore, 15 million chips a day. The only one of these three that the July 2026 texts place in commercial production is the Sanand specialised-chip unit, later called CG Semi. The Dholera silicon fab's commissioning is scheduled for 2028 in the [15 July 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784). The 100-day construction start was a commitment in February 2024. A later percentage of capital installed is not in the releases used here.

September 2024. The factsheet dates Kaynes Semicon at Sanand to this month, at ₹3,307 crore and 6.33 million chips a day. Kaynes is the second of the three plants Parliament said had started commercial production by 22 July 2026. The capex pattern so far is packaging lines reaching production while the large silicon fab is still inside its build.

May 2025. The factsheet dates the HCL-Foxconn joint venture at Jewar to this month, at ₹3,700 crore and 20,000 wafers a month.

12 August 2025. Four projects, around ₹4,600 crore combined, taking the count to 10 and the cumulative commitment to around ₹1.60 lakh crore. The capital is spread across a silicon-carbide fab and packaging line, a glass-substrate packaging plant, a discrete-device expansion, and a system-in-package unit. The factsheet's project-level investments are ₹2,066 crore, ₹1,943 crore, ₹117 crore, and ₹468 crore. The release's own number for the day is the ₹4,600 crore group.

5 May 2026. Two Gujarat projects, around ₹3,936 crore combined and 2,230 skilled jobs, taking the count to 12 and the cumulative commitment to around ₹1.64 lakh crore. Crystal Matrix is an integrated compound and assembly investment at Dholera. Suchi Semicon is an OSAT investment at Surat. After this date, the [15 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) decision changes the fiscal envelope. It does not add a thirteenth project commitment.

## Three ways capital expenditure is supported

A tool, a clean room, or a deposition system can fall under different schemes, and the schemes do not stack by default.

Under the 2021 mission, the [7 February 2026 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) describes fiscal support of up to 50 percent for silicon fabs, compound facilities, assembly and test, and chip design. That support is appraised project by project against the ₹76,000 crore programme. It is the relevant capital support for the 12 approved manufacturing units.

SPECS, notified on 1 April 2020, pays 25 percent of capital expenditure on electronic components, semiconductor and display fabrication, ATMP, specialised sub-assemblies, and the capital goods used to make them. The [guidelines](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/SPECS-Guidelines-01062020.pdf) closed the original application window on 31 March 2023. MeitY's scheme page lists a later addendum and a later amendment. A company with an acknowledged SPECS application can still be inside a five-year investment window counted from acknowledgement. A company that never applied cannot assume the 2020 window is open.

The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme, notified on 8 April 2025, pays a capital-expenditure incentive of 25 percent on selected bare components and on the supply-chain and capital-equipment segments. Display-module and camera-module sub-assemblies in that scheme are on a turnover incentive, not a capex incentive. The minimum investments are written segment by segment in the [26 April 2025 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620), from ₹10 crore for capital goods up to ₹1,000 crore for high-density and flexible boards.

PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware adds a boundary. Its [guidelines](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/03/Guidelines_PLI-Scheme-ITHW-min.pdf) say that investment which has already taken a SPECS incentive is not counted again toward the PLI incremental-investment threshold. Capex that has been subsidised once does not reappear as fresh skin-in-the-game for a second scheme.

## The year-ahead capital targets

The February 2026 budget note projects, for 2026-27, ₹4,000 crore of investment during the year under the modified scheme for semiconductor fabs, and ₹11,000 crore under the modified scheme for compound semiconductors, sensors, discrete fabs, and ATMP or OSAT. It also says one fab and nine compound or packaging units are to be supported in that year. "To be supported" is a support target for the year. It is not a statement that nine new units were approved in the quarter, and it sits alongside a book of 12 units already approved by May 2026.

Semicon 2.0's machines-and-materials pillar, approved on 15 July 2026, is the next capital category: equipment, chemicals, and gases. The Cabinet text announces that companies in that layer will be incentivised. It does not, in the release, publish the capex percentage or the first list of approved equipment projects.
