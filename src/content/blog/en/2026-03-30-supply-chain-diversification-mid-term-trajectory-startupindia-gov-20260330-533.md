# The mid-term trajectory of India's manufacturing trade corridors

The mid-term path for India's manufacturing diversification is institutional. It is not a forecast of export growth this site is willing to invent. Four official clocks are already running: logistics policy out to 2030, production-linked incentives over a multi-year production window, a critical-mineral mission with dated budget provisions, and a set of trade agreements moving from text to operation.

## Logistics has a target and, now, a baseline

The National Logistics Policy was launched on 17 September 2022 to sit beside PM Gati Shakti. A PIB progress note states the aims as a lower logistics cost, a Logistics Performance Index place among the top 25 countries by 2030, and data-driven decisions. The Comprehensive Logistics Action Plan under that policy covers digital systems, standards, human resources, states, and export-import logistics.

The baseline those aims can now be judged against is the DPIIT-NCAER assessment for 2023-24, reported by PIB on 27 November 2025: 7.97 percent of GDP, 9.09 percent of non-services output, and Rs 24.01 lakh crore. The trajectory to watch is whether later editions of that study move, and whether state and city plans under SMILE and LEADS change the first and last mile, where the same note says a large share of a 600-kilometre haul's pain sits. "Top 25 by 2030" remains a target. It is not a claim that the rank has been reached.

Corridor investment is part of the same clock. The Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor figures in that November 2025 note — shorter wagon cycles and shorter transit — show what "mid-term" looks like when a spine is actually open. NICDC's plug-and-play parks are the manufacturing nodes those spines are meant to feed.

## Manufacturing incentives run on a production window

The 2 August 2023 PIB note said PLI schemes for 14 sectors, with an outlay of Rs 1.97 lakh crore, had the potential to lift production, employment, and growth over about five years, and that implementation was at different stages by ministry. The mid-term test is not whether the outlay is still quoted. It is whether anchor units have a vendor base, which that note expected mostly among MSMEs, and whether any later official note adds sectors. As of that August 2023 reply, the Cabinet had not approved new ones.

## Inputs and preferences have their own clocks

Critical minerals will not localize on the PLI clock alone. The National Critical Mineral Mission, approved on 29 January 2025, is written as a chain from exploration to recycling. The 24 March 2025 PIB note records KABIL's exploration agreement for five lithium brine blocks in Argentina's Catamarca province, covering 15,703 hectares, and an MoU with Australia's Critical Mineral Facilitation Office on lithium and cobalt. It also records Rs 500 crore for processing parks and Rs 1,500 crore for recycling. Over the mid-term, the question is whether processing exists in India, not whether a block was signed abroad.

On trade, the 13 May 2026 remarks of the Commerce and Industry Minister are the near edge of the mid-term. He said four of nine recent agreements were operational and five were expected to enter into force within 12 months, alongside older agreements with Japan, Korea, and ASEAN. He also stated an export level of USD 863 billion for the year ending 31 March 2026 and an aspirational USD 1 trillion target for the then-current year. Aspirational is the right word to keep. Preferences arrive shipment by shipment, after entry into force and a certificate of origin.

## What the trajectory is not

It is not a promise that every component now imported will be made in India by 2030. It is a set of clocks an operator can audit: the next logistics-cost study, the next PLI implementation note, the mineral-processing parks, and which agreements have a start date.
