# The India trade and rupee figures that answer different questions

India publishes several "trade" numbers in the same month. They are all official. They are not substitutes. This note is a card: one question, one figure, one document. Levels are taken from the release named beside them. Nothing here is a forecast.

## How wide is the current account?

The current account is the RBI's summary of goods, services, income, and transfers.

For April–June 2026 the current account deficit was **US$4.2 billion, 0.5 per cent of GDP**. For April–June 2025 it was US$3.4 billion, 0.4 per cent of GDP. Source: RBI, [Developments in India's Balance of Payments during the First Quarter of 2026-27](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_PressRelease.aspx?fn=5&prid=63493), 1 September 2026. The data are marked preliminary.

For July 2026 the current account deficit was **US$7.0 billion**. For April–July 2026 it was **US$11.2 billion**. Source: RBI, [monthly balance of payments for July 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63596), 15 September 2026, also preliminary.

## How wide is the customs goods gap?

Use Commerce and DGCIS when the question is what left and entered the customs frontier.

The [Commerce release of 13 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298878) puts July 2026 merchandise exports at US$44.24 billion and imports at US$76.22 billion. For April–July 2026-27 it puts exports at US$173.78 billion, imports at US$292.38 billion, and the merchandise trade deficit at **US$118.60 billion**. A year earlier that April–July deficit was US$96.66 billion.

DGCIS quick estimates for August 2026, provisional, put merchandise exports at **US$43,810.92 million** and imports at **US$70,670.34 million**. For April–August 2026-27 the same table puts exports at US$215,912.45 million, up 17.85 per cent, and imports at US$363,000.93 million, up 18.21 per cent. The difference between the August lines is US$26,859.42 million. The difference between the April–August lines is US$147,088.48 million. A year earlier those April–August lines differed by US$123,879.93 million. Source: DGCIS quick-estimate tables carried on the PIB site, values in million US dollars, August 2026 provisional.

July customs exports of US$44.24 billion and July payments exports of US$45.1 billion are both on this card because they are different measurements.

## How large is services?

Two services figures are current, and they cover different months.

RBI quarterly balance of payments: services exports **US$106.2 billion** and net services receipts **US$51.6 billion** in April–June 2026. A year earlier, US$97.4 billion and US$47.9 billion.

RBI monthly services, provisional, [31 August 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63480): July 2026 receipts US$38,253 million, up 13.4 per cent, and payments US$20,606 million, up 19.1 per cent. The same table gives April US$37,021 million, May US$33,355 million, and June US$36,371 million of services exports. Growth rates in that release are over the corresponding month of the previous year and have been revised on a balance-of-payments basis.

RBI July balance of payments rounds the July services numbers to exports of US$38.3 billion, imports of US$20.6 billion, and a net of US$17.6 billion. April–July services exports are US$144.5 billion and the net is US$69.3 billion.

Commerce estimated a services surplus of US$69.17 billion for April–July 2026-27 in its 13 August release, and it labelled the July services line an estimate because RBI data then stopped at June. Once the RBI July print exists, that is the services figure to use for July.

The annual anatomy of software, which is the largest piece of services, is in the RBI survey released on 18 September 2026: software services exports of **US$221.4 billion** in 2025-26, up 8.2 per cent, excluding sales through overseas commercial presence. Including foreign affiliates, the total is US$239.3 billion.

## How large are remittances?

Personal transfer receipts in April–June 2026 were **US$42.9 billion**, against US$33.2 billion a year earlier. That line sits inside secondary income. It is not the whole secondary-income net, which was US$40.8 billion in the same quarter.

In the July monthly balance of payments, net transfers were US$13.2 billion, and US$53.9 billion for April–July 2026.

## Which number to pick up

Pick the current-account ratio when the question is whether India is living beyond its external receipts. Pick the customs deficit when the question is ports, commodities, and destination markets. Pick the services survey when the question is software. Pick the weekly supplement when the question is the reserve stock. Pick the homepage rate when the question is the rupee today.
