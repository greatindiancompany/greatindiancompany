# India's Insurance Market Grew in Rupees While Penetration Stood Still

Two official pictures of the same year can be true together. The market is large and still growing in rupees. The penetration ratio is not rising.

The [IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620), citing Swiss Re, says India remained the 10th largest insurance market by nominal premium in 2024, with a 1.8 percent share of global premium. The [PIB note of 23 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) repeats that rank and share, and puts FY 2024-25 activity at 41.84 crore policies, premiums of ₹11.93 lakh crore, claims paid of ₹8.36 lakh crore, and assets under management of ₹74.44 lakh crore as on 31 March 2025.

Penetration in that same year was 3.7 percent of GDP, unchanged from 2023-24. Life was 2.7 percent, down from 2.8 percent. Non-life was 1 percent.

## The mix inside the rupee total

PIB says life insurance is about 74 percent of total premium income and 91 percent of assets under management. Inside non-life, health is the leading line, at 41 percent of gross domestic premium, ahead of motor.

IRDAI's reproduction of Swiss Re premium volumes for 2024 puts India at USD 141.16 billion, of which life is USD 104.84 billion and non-life is USD 36.32 billion. The report prints life as 74 percent of that India total and non-life as 26 percent. The world book in the same table is the reverse shape: life 41 percent and non-life 59 percent of USD 7,799.33 billion.

A market signal that says only "premiums are up" misses the composition. India's premium stock is still a life-savings market. The non-life book, where health now leads, is the smaller dollar pool.

## Real growth was slower than the world in 2024

Swiss Re figures carried in the IRDAI report put 2024 real premium growth in India at 2.3 percent for life, 0.2 percent for non-life, and 1.7 percent overall. The world rates in that table are 6.1 percent, 4.7 percent, and 5.2 percent. Nominal rupee collections and real growth are different units. PIB's four-year nominal table, from FY 2020-21 to FY 2024-25, shows total premium income from ₹8.30 lakh crore to ₹11.90 lakh crore, which the note prints as 43.37 percent growth. Life in that table goes from ₹6.30 lakh crore to ₹8.86 lakh crore (40.63 percent). Non-life goes from ₹2.02 lakh crore to ₹3.10 lakh crore (53.46 percent). The narrative sentence in the same note uses ₹11.93 lakh crore for FY 2024-25 collections. Use the sentence and the table as printed. Do not average them.

## What a market reader should not infer

A 10th-place ranking is a volume rank. It is not a depth rank. Depth is the 3.7 percent ratio and the USD 97 density PIB reports for the same year. Household balance sheets are a third signal, and PIB attributes it to the Economic Survey 2025-26: the share of insurance and pension funds in household financial assets rose from 28.6 percent in FY 2018-19 to 29.6 percent in FY 2024-25. That share mixes insurance with pensions. It is not the penetration ratio.
