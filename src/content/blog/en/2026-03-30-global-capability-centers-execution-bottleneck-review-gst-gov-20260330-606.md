# Execution Bottlenecks for Captive Hubs in India

The work of a captive hub is execution: hiring, delivery location, data flows to a parent, and the permits required to exist. Official series are blunt on all four. They are blunt in a useful way. They show where the constraint is structural, and where a team is waiting for a document the government has not issued.

## People already deliver from India

In the Reserve Bank survey released on 18 September 2026, off-site services were **91.7 percent** of software services exports, **US$ 203.0 billion** of **US$ 221.4 billion**. On-site services were **US$ 18.4 billion**. Presence of natural persons, on the wider total of **US$ 239.3 billion**, was **7.7 percent**, down from **8.7 percent** a year earlier.

A new captive is not inventing the off-site model. That model is the export stock. The execution constraint is hiring and keeping the people who make the 91.7 percent possible, in the cities where the export rupees already sit. The Bank does not publish attrition or a wage index, so this brief does not quote one. The smaller official pipe is the movement of people across borders. Visa administration for foreign specialists sits outside the survey. Mode 4 is the export trace of that movement, not a consular statistic.

## Smaller cities have centres, not export mass

STPI runs **67** centres, **59** in Tier II and Tier III cities, and in 2024-25 it brought Koraput and Jajpur into operation, with a further list that includes Agra, Gorakhpur, Bareilly, Amritsar, Jamshedpur, and Balasore. That is a real public platform: **17 lakh sq ft** of IT-grade space, **411** incubatees, and a Next Generation Incubation Scheme capped at **₹25 lakh** per eligible startup.

The export table for the same year, marked tentative, does not follow the centre map. Karnataka alone is **₹4,58,669.50 crore** of **₹10,69,270.59 crore**. Bihar is **₹5.61 crore**. A mandate from a parent to "open in a smaller city" can find an STPI centre and an incubation seat. It cannot find, in that table, an existing software-export cluster comparable to Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Mumbai, Chennai, or the National Capital Region. The bottleneck is density of specialised teams and suppliers, which the rupee table reflects and the centre count does not. The two maps are set out in the [state brief](/blog/global-capability-centers-state-wise-patterns-gst-gov-20260330-326).

## The data rule teams need is not finished

A hub that ships employee data, client data, or logs to a parent needs a stable control list. Rule 15 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules allows transfer out of India subject to later government orders. Rule 13(4) can freeze specified personal data and related traffic data for a notified Significant Data Fiduciary, and it commences on **13 May 2027**. The rule does not name the datasets. Execution teams that block a launch on "final localisation guidance" are waiting on a specification the Gazette has not made. The practical bottleneck is the inventory they can build now, not a missing statute. The statute and the rules are published. The category list is the open item. Detail is in the [regulatory brief](/blog/global-capability-centers-regulatory-outlook-gst-gov-20260330-446).

## There is no official GCC index to manage against

RBI does not split captives from third-party exporters. MoSPI does not split IT out of a group that also contains finance, real estate, and dwellings. DPIIT's computer-software equity line of **USD 13,946 million** in 2025-26 is a sector bucket, not a centre dashboard. An operating review that pauses until "the GCC number" is updated will pause indefinitely. The workable review uses the activity line the hub actually resembles — engineering services were **US$ 12.0 billion** — and the company's own books.

## Approvals have a front door, not a single municipal key

The consolidated FDI policy is the entry text, and Make in India lists IT and BPM at 100 percent on the automatic route. The Finance Ministry's 20 July 2026 Lok Sabha answer lists the National Single Window System among facilitation measures. DPIIT's annual report describes the system as the online interface for permissions to start an industry, and as a portal used where FDI still needs government approval. That is a central front door. It does not, in those documents, collapse state and municipal permissions for a campus into one clock. A bottleneck log should separate FDI reporting, which is largely automatic for this activity, from land, power, and building permission, which these national tables do not time.
