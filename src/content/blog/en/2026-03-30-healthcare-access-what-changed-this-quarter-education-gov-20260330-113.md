## Three records, three different clocks

A quarter in healthcare access is easy to fake with a dashboard screenshot and hard to read from a scheme that moves on several clocks at once. Hospital cover, primary-care facility counts, and capital approvals do not update on the same day. This note keeps three official records separate, and it stops where those records stop. It does not fill July–September 2026 with a change that no release on this desk states.

## Hospital cover for people aged 70 and above

The National Health Authority homepage records a Cabinet decision of 12 September 2024: health coverage for every senior citizen aged 70 and above, irrespective of income, under Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB-PMJAY). The Authority’s senior-citizen FAQ states the benefit as free treatment up to ₹5 lakh. The same FAQ says the ₹5 lakh is a family amount. Two grandparents in one family share that limit. In a family that already has PM-JAY cover, the additional ₹5 lakh applies to members aged 70 or above. The rest of the family stays on the existing ₹5 lakh. The card name used on the Authority’s homepage for this group is Ayushman Vay Vandana.

That is a change in who can be entitled. It is not a count of admissions in the latest quarter. Eligibility without an income test, at older ages, changes the case mix a hospital should expect. It does not, by itself, tell you how many of those admissions have been billed.

The design of the base scheme is still the one on the Authority’s PM-JAY page: ₹5 lakh a year per family for listed secondary and tertiary hospitalization, cashless at empanelled public and private hospitals, portable across states, with pre-existing conditions covered from day one. The current about-page describes eligibility as the bottom 40 percent, “over 12 crore” families and about 55 crore people, identified from SECC 2011 plus families that were on the older Rashtriya Swasthya Bima Yojana and missing from that census. A November 2021 empanelment guideline from the same Authority said “about 10.74 crore households.” Both documents are official. They do not agree. A quarterly note should show that disagreement instead of averaging it.

## Primary-care counts after the 1.5 lakh target

The Ayushman Arogya Mandir site, the renamed Health and Wellness Centre programme, records the February 2018 announcement of 1,50,000 centres, built by converting sub-centres and primary health centres. The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare’s written reply of 25 March 2025 says states had reported 1,76,573 centres operationalised till February 2025. The same reply gives 107.10 crore hypertension screenings, 94.56 crore diabetes screenings, and 5.06 crore wellness sessions including yoga as on 28 February 2025.

Later Press Information Bureau notes continue the facility series: 1,81,873 centres as on 30 November 2025, and 1,82,944 as on 31 December 2025. Those are state-reported operational counts on successive dates. A screening total counts tests. It does not count distinct people, and it does not show whether the person received medicine, a referral, or a hospital bed. For how to keep those series apart, see the [evidence snapshot](/blog/healthcare-access-evidence-snapshot-education-gov-20260330-673).

## Capital approvals are a five-year list

The Ministry’s update on the Pradhan Mantri Ayushman Bharat Health Infrastructure Mission states an outlay of ₹64,180 crore for 2021–22 to 2025–26. It also states administrative approvals of ₹33,081.82 crore across those five financial years, covering 10,609 building-less Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, 5,456 urban mandirs, 2,151 block public health units, 744 district integrated public health laboratories, and 621 critical care hospital blocks. Approval is a Centre–state decision on a proposal. Commissioning is a later fact. The published scheme window runs through FY 2025–26, which had ended before this page was updated on 24 September 2026. A successor line in a later budget is a document to open, not a number to guess. The build list is unpacked in the [capital-cycle note](/blog/healthcare-access-capex-cycle-watch-education-gov-20260330-353).

## What to open next

For a live quarter, use the Authority’s PM-JAY page and dashboard for hospital-cover transactions, the Ministry’s mandir portal for a facility total newer than 31 December 2025, and the Ministry’s PM-ABHIM update for whether the approval figures above have been restated. Compare each new release with the dated figures on this page. A movement in one series is not a movement in the other two.
