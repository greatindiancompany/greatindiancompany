# What changed in rural water supply after the March 2026 Cabinet decision

The useful change in India's rural drinking-water programme is dated, and it is not a new slogan for pipes. On 10 March 2026 the Union Cabinet approved a restructured Jal Jeevan Mission, called JJM 2.0, running to December 2028. On 6 August 2026 the Ministry of Jal Shakti said it had released ₹6,154.96 crore to states in FY 2026-27. Between those two dates, the ministry also put a later household count on the parliamentary record.

This page stops at those documents. It does not estimate what the live dashboard shows after 4 August 2026.

## The decision

The Cabinet note, issued by the Press Information Bureau the same afternoon, says the Ministry of Jal Shakti will reorient the mission "from infrastructure creation to a service delivery", with drinking-water governance and an institutional setup for sustainable rural piped supply.

The money in that note has two layers. The total outlay rises to ₹8.69 lakh crore. Central assistance rises to ₹3.59 lakh crore, from ₹2.08 lakh crore approved in 2019-20, which the note describes as an additional central share of ₹1.51 lakh crore. A follow-up [PIB note on 13 March 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2239620) describes the total financial outlay as having moved from ₹3.60 lakh crore to ₹8.69 lakh crore. Those are different bases. The first pair is the central share. The second pair is the full outlay. Adding them together would double-count the Centre.

The same Cabinet note sets the near goal and the long goal in different sentences. By December 2028, JJM 2.0 is to help certify every Gram Panchayat as Har Ghar Jal and provide a tap connection to all 19.36 crore rural households identified by the states and Union Territories, through separate memoranda with those governments. Assured 24×7 rural drinking water is attached, in that note, to the Viksit Bharat @2047 vision. December 2028 is the certification and connection horizon written there. It is not written as the date by which every village receives round-the-clock supply.

## The count, on two dates

The Cabinet note gives the stock as on 10 March 2026. From a 2019 baseline of 3.23 crore rural households with tap connections, which the note rounds to 17 percent, more than 12.56 crore additional households had been provided connections. Of 19.36 crore rural households, around 15.80 crore, or 81.61 percent, were reported to have tap connections.

A later written answer in the Lok Sabha ([AS265](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS265_f3O07i.pdf)), giving the position as on 4 August 2026, uses the same household denominator of 19.36 crore and a more precise baseline: 3.23 crore, or 16.72 percent, at the start of the mission in August 2019. It then says more than 15.90 crore households, or 82.19 percent, were reported to have tap connections. Both figures are state-reported mission statistics, not a census of water flowing that day. The year-wise and state-wise series the ministry points to is the Jal Jeevan Mission dashboard.

The two dates can be read side by side. They should not be turned into a monthly run-rate. "Around 15.80 crore" and "more than 15.90 crore" are differently worded, five months apart.

## The money that actually moved

The 6 August 2026 release splits the ₹6,154.96 crore into two actions.

The Centre reimbursed ₹5,289.09 crore as central share to 10 states. The ministry's reason is specific: during the gap between the end of the earlier mission period and the approval of JJM 2.0, several states kept implementing already approved schemes with their own funds. The reimbursement recognises that spending. The largest lines in the published table are Uttar Pradesh at ₹2,711.52 crore, Karnataka at ₹795.74 crore, Maharashtra at ₹594.44 crore, and Rajasthan at ₹537.72 crore. The full ten-state table is in that PIB note. This brief does not restate every row.

Separately, ₹865.87 crore had been released through the SNA SPARSH platform across 13 states in the same financial year, as on that date. SNA SPARSH is the scheme-level payment rail JJM 2.0 uses so that a release can be tied to an individual water-supply scheme.

## What did not change

Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen), AMRUT 2.0, and Namami Gange were not rewritten by this Cabinet decision. Rural sanitation remains a Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation programme with its own dashboard. Urban water and sewerage sit with the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs under AMRUT. Sewage treatment along the Ganga sits with the National Mission for Clean Ganga. A reader who treats "water and sanitation" as one budget line will mix four implementation clocks.

The live national page for rural taps remains [the JJM India dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx).
