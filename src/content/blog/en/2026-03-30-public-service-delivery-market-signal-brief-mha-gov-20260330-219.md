# The market signal in UMANG, DigiLocker, and DBT

The useful signal in India's citizen-service stack is architectural. Official notes describe who issues a document, who runs the workflow, who stands at the counter, and which bank account may receive cash. They do not publish a private market size for "govtech."

## One front door, many owners

[UMANG](https://web.umang.gov.in/landing/) is an initiative of the National e-Governance Division in the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. The page describes it as a gateway to central, state, and local schemes and services, on a mobile app, a website, a chatbot, and a voice bot. Documents such as Aadhaar, driving licence, PAN, and vehicle registration are pulled from DigiLocker. The service owner remains the department. UMANG is the window.

The [6 August 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635) of a Lok Sabha reply says the platform offers 2,575 services for individuals, including 17 integrated by the Government of Tamil Nadu. That pair of numbers is the signal: a national catalogue can be large while a given state's integration is still a short list. A firm that assumes "listed on UMANG" means the state workflow is finished will misread the channel.

## Issued documents are the product, uploads are not

DigiLocker's own description, on its [paperless-governance note](https://blog.digilocker.gov.in/digilocker-an-initiative-towards-paperless-governance/), says it is a MeitY flagship under Digital India. Issued documents are fetched from the issuing agency. The note says those issued documents are deemed at par with original physical documents under Rule 9A of the Information Technology (Preservation and Retention of Information by Intermediaries Providing Digital Locker Facilities) Rules, 2016. The same note describes DigiLocker as a document exchange between a trusted issuer and a trusted requester, with the citizen's consent, and as a verification module that lets an agency check data with the issuer after consent.

The commercial implication is narrow and practical. A requester integration is worth building when the document comes from the issuer repository. A scan the user uploaded into personal storage is a different object. Railway circular CC/33/2018, hosted on DigiLocker, already drew that line for train travel: Aadhaar or a driving licence shown from Issued Documents can be identity; a file in Uploaded Documents cannot.

The 6 August 2026 reply states 2,822 issuers and more than 936.03 crore documents on the platform. Issuer onboarding is ongoing public work. It is not a licence to invent a remaining-addressable-market figure.

## Cash moves on a mapped account

The [DBT Mission](https://dbtbharat.gov.in/static-page-content/spagecont?id=1) says Jan Dhan accounts, Aadhaar, and mobile connections are the enablers, and that the electronic payment framework covers schemes with a cash component to an individual. The last-mile institutions in the Mission's own materials are banks, including the Aadhaar Payments Bridge operated with NPCI, and the business-correspondent network that can pay out in cash. A product that "does DBT" without knowing which account is seeded in the mapper will send an operator into failed credits. The Mission page also says Aadhaar is preferred and is not mandatory in DBT schemes. Scheme rules can still require it where section 7 of the Aadhaar Act applies. Read the scheme, not the slogan.

## Assisted access is part of the market

The same August 2026 reply says Common Service Centres deliver over 800 services, with 5.01 lakh centres functional as of May 2026, including 3.91 lakh at gram panchayat level. Village-level entrepreneurs are the stated operators. The CPGRAMS portal adds a cost signal: government does not charge a fee to file a grievance, and money the public pays at filing goes to the CSC. Self-serve apps and assisted counters are both official channels. A service design that only counts app installs misses the counter the government still funds.

## Grievance volume is a service signal

[CPGRAMS](https://pgportal.gov.in/) is the redress window, including inside UMANG. A PIB note of [15 June 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?ModuleId=3&NoteId=158902&id=158902&lang=1&reg=1) says approximately 6 lakh grievances were resolved between January 2025 and February 2026, and that 69.8 percent were rated satisfactory by complainants. That is a satisfaction reading for a stated window. It is also evidence that service failure still produces a recorded queue. Firms that integrate a department's form own part of that queue when their outage or bad status message is what the citizen complains about.
