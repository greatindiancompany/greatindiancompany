The capital cycle a reader can actually watch is the IndiaAI compute pool. It is public, subsidised, and empaneled. It is not the private capex of every data centre in the country, and the official notes do not support adding every GPU sentence into one total.

## The sequence, in the order the notes were issued

**7 March 2024.** The Mission is approved with an outlay of Rs. 10,371.92 crore over five years. That date and figure are restated in the Lok Sabha reply of 5 August 2026. The outlay is the budget envelope. It is not a GPU count.

**6 March 2025.** The Compute Portal opens. PIB says it will initially provide access to 10,000 GPUs, with 8,693 more to be added, and that eligible users get up to 40 percent subsidy on cloud AI compute. The Minister named classes of hardware on offer, including NVIDIA H100, H200, A100, L40S and L4, AMD MI300X, Intel Gaudi 2, and AWS Trainium and Inferentia. Those names are the launch list. They are not a statement that every later GPU is one of those parts. The Secretary said nearly 45 percent of mission funding sat in this pillar.

**November 2025.** The India AI Governance Guidelines say over 38,231 GPUs are being made available at subsidised rates, and that a secure cluster is being constructed to house 3,000 next-generation GPUs for sovereign and strategic applications. The 3,000 are described as a cluster under construction. They are not a subset the reader should subtract from 38,231, and they are not a subset to add on top, unless a later commissioning note says so. This series has not cited that commissioning note.

**25 March 2026.** A Lok Sabha reply says that as on that date, 38,231 GPUs had been onboarded from 14 empaneled providers. The average rate is approximately Rs 65 per GPU per hour, except for select high-end GPUs.

**13 March 2026.** A separate PIB note says more than 38,000 GPUs had been empaneled through 14 providers, and that capacity was being expanded by adding 20,000 GPUs, then "currently under process." "Under process" is not "onboarded." The 10 February 2026 note had already said more than 38,000 were available at ₹65 per hour, and it added 1,050 TPUs. The TPU figure is in that February note. Later GPU totals in this series do not silently include or exclude it.

**June and August 2026.** The 13 August 2026 backgrounder says shared compute had reached 45,000+ GPUs as of June 2026, and that by August 2026, 237 projects had used 93.18 lakh GPU hours.

## What not to do with the arithmetic

Do not add 10,000 and 8,693 and call it the 2026 stock. The March 2025 sentence was an opening offer, and later notes report a larger empanelled pool.

Do not add 38,231 and the 20,000 "under process" and call the sum 58,231, or call it the June figure of 45,000+. The June note states 45,000+ directly. If the 20,000 were fully onboarded, a later note would have to say so. The June wording is "expanded shared compute capacity to 45,000+," which is consistent with growth and is not an equation.

Do not add the 3,000-GPU sovereign cluster to the shared pool. Different sentence, different purpose, and still described as being constructed in the November 2025 guidelines.

Do not read GPU hours as GPUs. 93.18 lakh hours is consumption by 237 projects. It is the best official sign that the subsidy is being drawn. It is not a count of chips.

## Where this sits against data-protection spending

DPDP compliance spending is not in these notes. The Rules will, from 13 May 2027, require security safeguards, logs, and in specified cases a data-protection impact assessment. They do not authorise a rupee estimate of industry spend, and this brief will not supply one. The public capital a reader can date is the mission outlay and the empaneled pool.
