April–June 2026 is the first full quarter after the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) widened who can be recognised as a startup. The quarter’s official record is a larger registry, a higher count of women directors and partners, and the operating rules for a second fund of funds. It is not a new national tally of venture rounds.

## The registry at 30 June 2026

A Lok Sabha reply states that DPIIT had recognised 2,40,092 entities as startups as on 30 June 2026. Of that stock, 16,334 were recognised between 1 April and 30 June 2026. The same reply ties the flow to notification G.S.R. 108(E) of 4 February 2026, which expanded eligibility, and to a fully digital application on the Startup India portal and the National Single Window System. Recognition rests on self-certification.

The previous official milestone is the Press Information Bureau release of 17 April 2026: recognised startups had crossed 2.23 lakh as on 31 March 2026. Financial year 2025-26 added more than 55,200 recognitions, against 36,400+ in 2024-25. PIB states that recognitions rose 51.6 percent year on year. Startup India itself was launched on 16 January 2016.

Sixteen thousand three hundred and thirty-four recognitions in one quarter, set beside a prior full year of more than 55,200, is a fast opening. Dividing those published totals gives a rough pace of about 4,600 recognitions a month in 2025-26 and about 5,400 a month in April–June 2026. That division is arithmetic on the printed figures. It is not a DPIIT forecast.

## What moved inside the quarter, and what had already moved

The gazette change is dated 4 February 2026, so it belongs to the previous quarter. What fell inside April–June was the machinery for capital.

On 25 April 2026, PIB reported that DPIIT had issued operational guidelines for Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0. The corpus is ₹10,000 crore. The money does not go straight to companies. It is committed to SEBI-registered Category I and Category II alternative investment funds, which then invest in DPIIT-recognised startups. The Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI) is the initial implementation agency. DPIIT is to onboard another domestic agency. The guidelines were published during this quarter; they are not a disbursement scorecard for the quarter.

Women in the registry also moved. PIB put the 31 March 2026 figure at more than 1.07 lakh recognised startups with at least one woman director or partner, about 48 percent of the stock. A later Lok Sabha reply puts the 30 June 2026 figure at more than 1.15 lakh. Both are director-or-partner counts, not a census of women founders in operating roles.

## What this quarter does not update

The 17 April PIB release is still the national jobs print: more than 23.36 lakh direct jobs as on 31 March 2026, with 4,99,400+ of those reported for 2025-26 against 3,66,870+ in 2024-25. A parliamentary annexure earlier labelled the underlying series self-reported. No later national jobs total, dated after 31 March 2026, is used here. Karnataka-specific figures appear in the state brief.

The same 17 April release is also the latest scheme stock used here for the original Fund of Funds: more than ₹7,000 crore disbursed to over 135 alternative investment funds by the end of 2025-26, and more than ₹26,900 crore invested by those funds in over 1,420 startups. That is a year-end figure. It should not be read as capital deployed during April–June 2026.

## How to use the quarter

A founder reading only the 16,334 should treat it as applications accepted under a wider test, not as 16,334 financed companies. An investor comparing India with a global “deals closed” series should keep the DPIIT stock and the fund-of-funds portfolio in separate columns. A policy reader should ask the next Parliament or PIB print for the 30 September 2026 stock, and for whether Fund of Funds 2.0 has moved from guidelines to letters of intent.
