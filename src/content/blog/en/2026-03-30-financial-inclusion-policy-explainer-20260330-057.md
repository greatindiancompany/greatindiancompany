India’s inclusion system is a stack of institutions, and each layer has a job. Mixing the layers is how a product description starts promising credit, cash, and insurance from a single account opening.

## The account

The Department of Financial Services launched Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana on 28 August 2014. The scheme page says an unbanked person can open a basic savings bank deposit account at a branch or at a business correspondent outlet, the Bank Mitra. After 14 August 2018 the mission’s account-opening focus moved from every household to every unbanked adult. The account is the government’s basic bouquet: no minimum balance, a RuPay card, accident cover, eligibility for an overdraft of up to ₹10,000, and eligibility for the insurance and pension schemes and for MUDRA.

## The correspondent

A Bank Mitra is the bank’s agent, not a separate bank. NSFI 2025–30 defines a fixed-point business correspondent outlet as a banking outlet manned by the correspondent, offering deposit acceptance, cheque encashment or cash withdrawal, or lending, for at least four hours a day on at least five days a week. The strategy’s retrospect says that during 2019–2024 some form of banking access was provided within a 5 kilometre radius of 99.9 percent of the identified villages and hamlets of 500 households. The same document sets a delivery goal inside the correspondent network itself: raise the share of women business correspondents from around 15 percent to at least 30 percent by December 2028, through voluntary plans adopted by banks at the state-level and union-territory bankers’ committees.

## The payment

[NPCI](https://www.npci.org.in/product/aeps) describes the Aadhaar Enabled Payment System as a bank-led model. A customer uses Aadhaar authentication at an AePS touchpoint to reach an Aadhaar-enabled account. NPCI lists cash withdrawal, cash deposit, fund transfer, balance enquiry, mini statement, and BHIM Aadhaar Pay among the services, in both inter-bank and intra-bank modes. The inputs are the bank name, the Aadhaar number, and biometrics. The correspondent’s micro-ATM is the usual counter. AePS moves cash and small transfers for someone who already has an account. It does not open the account, and it does not decide a loan.

## The strategy and the index

RBI wrote the measurement and the forward plan. The Financial Inclusion Index, introduced on 17 August 2021, compresses banking, investments, insurance, postal services, and pensions into one 0–100 value, weighted Access 35 percent, Usage 45 percent, and Quality 20 percent. On 1 December 2025 the Governor released the [National Strategy for Financial Inclusion 2025–30](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=61712). Its five objectives, the Panch-Jyoti, are: a usable bouquet of services for households and micro enterprises; a gender-sensitive approach and differentiated products for underserved households; livelihood and skill linkages; financial education for financial discipline; and stronger customer protection and grievance redress. Forty-seven action points sit under those objectives. The emphasis in the document is usage and quality, with last-mile access still in the frame.

A founder building on this stack can now place a feature: account origination belongs to the bank under PMJDY rules, cash-in and cash-out belongs to AePS at a due-diligence-cleared touchpoint, and credit for a micro enterprise belongs to the separate work NSFI names.
