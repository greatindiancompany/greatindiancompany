# India’s Green Hydrogen Map: Four Named Hubs, and State Policies Left Unnamed

State rankings of green hydrogen are easy to invent, because many states have hosted a conference. The geography in the documents used here is narrower, and it splits into hubs, unnamed policy counts, and road-trial corridors.

## Four places Parliament named

On 5 August 2026, Minister of State Shripad Yesso Naik answered Lok Sabha question 2987. The [annex](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2987_27NWXF.pdf) lists Deendayal Port at Kandla in Gujarat, V.O. Chidambaranar Port at Tuticorin in Tamil Nadu, and Paradip Port in Odisha as recognised hubs, and records MNRE’s endorsement of NTPC’s proposal for a hub at Pudimadaka in Andhra Pradesh. The Press Information Bureau had reported the three port recognitions on 10 October 2025, under revised guidelines dated 27 June 2025. Those guidelines are on MNRE’s [schemes list](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/). The August annex is the later text, and it carries the project detail.

## Gujarat: leased land and a dated plant contract

At Kandla, the annex says Deendayal Port has allotted 3,400 acres for green hydrogen, derivatives, and allied infrastructure on a 30-year lease, through an e-tender-cum-e-auction. It reports a 1 MW demonstration plant completed at ₹14.1 crore. It reports a contract for a 5 MW plant, scalable to 10 MW, at ₹62.80 crore, with completion dated 31 December 2026. It reports a 5-tonne-a-day bio-methanol plant at ₹59.71 crore, timed to 31 January 2027. It reports a memorandum of 29 January 2026 with Assam Petro-Chemicals for a 150-tonne-a-day e-methanol facility, with the feasibility study still in progress, and methanol bunkering at port readiness level 6, with a move toward level 7 underway. An earlier PIB note, in November 2025, described a Kandla facility at about ₹13 crore with nearly 140 tonnes a year. This brief uses the annex for the later account and does not add the two descriptions into two separate plants.

## Tamil Nadu: a small pilot and larger expectations

At Tuticorin, the annex says a 10 Nm³ pilot was commissioned in April 2025 with port funding of ₹3.88 crore, and green-methanol bunkering and refuelling facilities at ₹38.12 crore, of which the MNRE grant is ₹35 crore, with a capacity notation of 2×750 m³. It then lists three allotments and expected capacities: Renew E-fuels, 10 acres, 0.1 million tonnes a year; Green Infra Renewable Energy Farms, 163.97 acres, 0.22 million tonnes a year; Amplus Ganges Solar, 145.47 acres, 0.5 million tonnes a year. Expected capacity on an allotment is not output. The November 2025 PIB note described a Tuticorin pilot as a ₹25 crore, 10 Nm³ per hour facility commissioned in September 2025. The date, the rupee figure, and the unit do not match the annex. Quote the document you mean.

## Odisha and Andhra Pradesh

Paradip, in the annex, is common infrastructure for other people’s plants: a 4.0 million-tonne-a-year green hydrogen and green ammonia handling jetty and ancillary facilities at ₹797.17 crore, approved for public-private partnership, with a 280-metre berth for manufacturers in the hinterland. Pudimadaka is one endorsed proposal: investment committed of ₹85,000 crore excluding renewable projects; 4,300 tonnes a day of green ammonia, 1,200 tonnes a day of green methanol, and 600 tonnes a day of sustainable aviation fuel; planned hydrogen output for 2030 of 600 tonnes a day, stated as 0.21 million tonnes a year.

## Policies counted, states not named

On 17 June 2026, Minister Joshi said six states had notified dedicated green hydrogen policies, seven had integrated hydrogen into existing industrial or renewable frameworks, and four were still finalising. The [release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2274212) does not name them. This brief will not guess the seventeen.

Road pilots are a different map. The 29 July 2026 vehicle annex includes routes touching Gujarat, Odisha, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, and Madhya Pradesh. A bus trial is not a production hub. The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) still says the Mission will develop regions capable of large-scale production or use. The places it has named, through the August answer, are the four above.

Great Indian Company treats the unnamed policy count as a count, and the port annex as the map.
