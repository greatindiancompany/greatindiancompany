There is no state-wise liquidity adjustment facility. Banks in every region borrow and lend overnight in one rupee market, and the weighted average call rate is a national target. The regional fact the Reserve Bank does publish each week is the cash position of governments, because the Bank is their banker and government cash is one of the frictional drains on that national pool.

## One line moved by ₹24,824 crore in a week

The Bulletin’s Weekly Statistical Supplement extract dated 11 September 2026 reports loans and advances on the Reserve Bank’s balance sheet, in crore of rupees. Central government loans and advances are zero on 5 September 2025, on 28 August 2026 and on 4 September 2026. State government loans and advances are ₹44,070 crore on 5 September 2025, ₹3,116 crore on 28 August 2026 and ₹27,940 crore on 4 September 2026. The week’s variation is ₹24,824 crore. The year’s variation is a decline of ₹16,130 crore. The extract marks the figures provisional.

The supplement does not name the states inside the ₹27,940 crore. A reader cannot tell from this table whether one large state drew ways and means advances or several did. What the table does show is a national swing: state drawings went from ₹3,116 crore on 28 August to ₹27,940 crore on 4 September, and that later stock was still below the ₹44,070 crore of a year before.

The money-market release for 20 September adds the Centre’s side in a different unit. The Government of India’s surplus cash balance reckoned for auction, as on 18 September 2026, is zero. A zero on that line means the Centre’s surplus was not, on that reckoning date, a cash pile being counted for the auction. It does not locate cash across state capitals.

## Why a state drawing changes the national pool

The Reserve Bank’s account of its role as banker to government sets the mechanism. Under the arrangements it describes, the Central Government maintains a minimum cash balance with the Bank. Civil, railway, post, telecommunication, defence and departmentalised ministry accounts are maintained in regional offices, and the principal account of those accounts is at the Central Accounts Section in Nagpur.

Under Section 21A of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, the Bank may agree to conduct a state government’s banking business, including the deposit, free of interest, of its cash balances. The Bank states that it is banker to all states and Union territories except Sikkim. Each state maintains a minimum balance that varies with the size of its budget and its economic activity. Temporary mismatches are met with three facilities the Bank lists: a Special Drawing Facility, normal ways and means advances, and overdraft.

The abbreviation collides with the liquidity corridor. In the money market, SDF is the standing deposit facility, the floor at 5.00 per cent where banks place surplus reserves. In the state-government account, SDF is the Special Drawing Facility, a way for a state to bridge a cash gap. They are different instruments. A bank placing ₹91,747 crore in the standing deposit facility on 20 September 2026, which is the figure in that day’s money-market release, is not a state drawing its Special Drawing Facility.

When a state draws on the Bank, loans and advances to state governments rise. That is the line that jumped by ₹24,824 crore in the week to 4 September. When governments spend balances they were holding at the Bank, those balances leave the Bank’s government accounts and enter the banking system. The Governor’s statement of 5 August 2026 lists “the usual return of currency during the monsoon season” and “drawdown of government cash balances” among the flows expected to aid banking-system liquidity in the near term. The weekly loan figure is the published trace of the state half of that government-cash channel. The Centre’s auction reckoning on 18 September happened to be zero.

## What a regional reader still cannot rank

For the week ended 11 September 2026 the supplement dated 18 September shows a national corridor of 5.00 / 5.25 / 5.50 per cent, a cash reserve ratio of 3.00 per cent and a statutory liquidity ratio of 18.00 per cent. Those settings apply to the whole system. The weekly statistical pages used here do not split the standing deposit facility, the marginal standing facility or the variable rate reverse repo by state or by regional office of the Bank. Currency with the public, ₹41,94,110 crore as on 31 August 2026 in the same extract, is also a national stock. Festival and harvest demand for notes is geographically uneven, and the extract does not publish that geography.

The operational reading is therefore narrow and concrete. Watch the state-government loan line for the weekly injection or repayment. Watch the Centre’s surplus cash balance on the money-market release. Treat both as frictional government cash around a corridor that is set once, in Mumbai, for the whole system. For the week covered by the 11 September extract, the state loan book rose by ₹24,824 crore while central government loans stayed at zero. That is the regional fact the national liquidity statistics actually contain.
