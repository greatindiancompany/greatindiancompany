# What Counts as Green Hydrogen in India, and Who Must Certify It

Regulation of green hydrogen in India turns on a product definition, a certificate, and equipment rules for tenders. It is not a single licence with the title “green hydrogen permission.”

## The product rule

On 19 August 2023, MNRE notified the Green Hydrogen Standard for India. The [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1950421) says green hydrogen has a well-to-gate emission of not more than 2 kg of CO2 equivalent per kg of hydrogen. The headline states that figure as a 12-month average. The boundary in the release includes water treatment, electrolysis, gas purification, drying, and compression. Electrolysis and biomass are both inside the scope. The release says a detailed methodology for measurement, reporting, monitoring, on-site verification, and certification would be specified by MNRE, and that the Bureau of Energy Efficiency would be the nodal authority for accrediting the agencies that monitor, verify, and certify projects.

The August 2023 text defines the molecule’s claim. It does not say that meeting the threshold replaces factory, environmental, or explosives permissions. Those sit with the regulators that already license an industrial plant.

## The scheme and the portal

MNRE’s [notice page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/notice/green-hydrogen-certification-scheme-of-india/) lists the Green Hydrogen Certification Scheme of India with a start date of 29 April 2025. The PIB note of 12 November 2025 describes the effect. The scheme is the national framework for labelling hydrogen green by assessing greenhouse-gas emissions across the production cycle. A final certificate is mandatory for any green hydrogen production facility in India that receives subsidies or incentives from the central or state governments, or that sells or uses the hydrogen domestically. The Bureau of Energy Efficiency accredits the agencies that monitor and certify projects.

On 17 June 2026, Minister Pralhad Joshi launched the Green Hydrogen Certification Portal during an MNRE workshop, so that this compliance has a system. In the same speech he said standards for green ammonia and green methanol had been finalised in recent months. Derivatives matter because fertiliser offtake and port bunkering, in the other official notes, are ammonia and methanol trades as much as hydrogen-gas trades.

The November 2025 note states the mandatory cases as incentives and domestic sale or use. A facility that does neither is outside the cases that paragraph lists. An exporter in that position still faces the importing country’s standard. The Indian portal does not retire that document.

## Tenders add an equipment rule

The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) says eligibility for competitive bidding for green hydrogen and its derivatives will require equipment approved by the Government of India on specified quality and performance criteria. A plant can meet the emissions threshold and still fail a tender if the stack is outside that approval. Operators who intend to bid should read the equipment rule as a second gate, beside the certificate.

## What is still with the Empowered Group

The mission page assigns the year-wise minimum share of consumption for designated consumers to the Empowered Group. The later releases used here launch a portal, record offtake agreements, and record pilot sanctions. They do not reprint a binding national quota. Until that trajectory is published as the Group’s decision, producers model contracts, pilots, and the label. They do not yet model a statutory domestic share from these pages.

The outlook from September 2026 is administrative. The standard is three years old. The scheme notice is from April 2025. The portal is from June 2026. Ammonia and methanol standards were described as finalised. The work for a compliance officer is to match a plant to the mandatory cases and to the Bureau’s accredited agencies, not to wait for a new target.

Great Indian Company points that officer at the August 2023 standard, the April 2025 scheme notice, and the June 2026 portal, in that order.
