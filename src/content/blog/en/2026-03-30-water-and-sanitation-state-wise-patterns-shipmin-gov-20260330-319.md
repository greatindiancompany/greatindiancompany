# State rows, not the national average, are the water and sanitation record

The national tap-connection rate is a sum of state returns. It is a poor guide to where work remains, because the states do not move together, and because the Centre does not implement the schemes. This brief explains how to read the state record. It does not publish a league table. A ranking copied from memory would go stale the next time a state updates the dashboard, and this page will not invent one.

## Drinking water is reported by the state that builds it

The Jal Jeevan Mission dashboard is updated by states and Union Territories. The ministry has said so in Parliament: the state-wise count of functional household tap connections is the dashboard, and the financial progress by state and year is a separate public report.

Two national prints show why the date on a state row matters. On 10 March 2026 the Cabinet note said around 15.80 crore of 19.36 crore rural households (81.61 percent) were reported connected. On 4 August 2026 the ministry told the Lok Sabha the reported stock was more than 15.90 crore (82.19 percent) of the same 19.36 crore. Any state share of that movement has to be read off [the India dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx) for a named day, not inferred from the national delta.

The dashboard's own status words also differ by village, inside a single state. A village can be fully entered on the information system, reported by the engineering department, or certified by the Gram Sabha. A state average of households with a connection can rise while many villages in that state are still short of certification. JJM 2.0 makes that last step wait on in-village maintenance. A state that is high on reported connections can still be early on Jal Arpan handovers.

The financial companion is the [state-wise allocation, release, and expenditure report](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/JJM/JJMReports/Financial/JJMRep_StatewiseAllocationReleaseExpenditure.aspx). Allocation is not expenditure. A state can appear well funded on one column and slow on another.

## The FY 2026-27 reimbursement is a map of who bridged the gap

On 6 August 2026 the Centre said it had reimbursed ₹5,289.09 crore to ten states for central share against approved schemes those states had carried on their own books during the transition into JJM 2.0. The largest published lines were Uttar Pradesh ₹2,711.52 crore, Karnataka ₹795.74 crore, Maharashtra ₹594.44 crore, and Rajasthan ₹537.72 crore. A further ₹865.87 crore had gone out through SNA SPARSH across 13 states.

This is not a ranking of who is closest to Har Ghar Jal. It is a map of who spent during the interregnum and who had begun to draw scheme-level releases. Uttar Pradesh's reimbursement is more than half of the ten-state total. That tells you where transition cash was concentrated. It does not, by itself, tell you where taps are missing.

## Sanitation is a state subject, and the classes are the pattern

A Press Information Bureau note on 12 February 2026 says sanitation is a state subject, and that Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) Phase II runs from 2020-21 to 2026-27 with the aim of sustaining open-defecation-free status and reaching ODF Plus Model villages. States report the classes on the mission information system.

The [SBM-G dashboard](https://sbm.gov.in/sbmgdashboard/) as on 11 June 2026 is the place to see which states still hold the Aspiring stock. Nationally that stock was 57,923 villages, against 5,07,634 Model villages and 3,463 Rising villages, inside 5,86,944 SBM villages. A state can be almost entirely Model and still have a block of Aspiring villages. The national 5.69 lakh ODF Plus figure, repeated in a PIB feature of 17 September 2026, will not show that block.

Urban water and sewerage follow a third map. AMRUT 2.0 projects are chosen and implemented by states and urban local bodies inside State Water Action Plans. The ministry's project browser is state and city specific. A rural Har Ghar Jal percentage says nothing about whether that state's cities have awarded their sewerage projects.

Read, in this order, the village stage, the state financial column, and the programme. The pages are the [JJM dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx), the [financial report](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/JJM/JJMReports/Financial/JJMRep_StatewiseAllocationReleaseExpenditure.aspx), the [SBM-G dashboard](https://sbm.gov.in/sbmgdashboard/), the [6 August 2026 reimbursement note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295509), and the [12 February 2026 SBM-G note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2226999).
