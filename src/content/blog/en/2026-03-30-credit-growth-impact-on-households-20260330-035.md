# Households still take most of India’s bank credit

Households are still the centre of the loan book. They are no longer the part of the book that is speeding up.

On the annual Basic Statistical Return for March 2026, borrowings by the household sector rose **14.3%** year on year. Households held **58.6%** of bank credit and accounted for about three-fifths of the credit added during 2025-26. Individuals alone were **47.8%** of bank credit. Within individual credit, the share of women borrowers was **24.7%**, up from **23.8%** a year earlier. The same return adds a district-level series on credit to individuals by gender. The press note publishes the national share, not a district table.

Personal loans, defined there as housing, education, vehicle, credit cards, consumer durables, and other personal loans, grew **12.9%**. That was below overall BSR credit growth of **14.1%**. The Reserve Bank says personal loans had outpaced the overall book for several years before this. Their share was still large, at **30.7%**.

## The latest sectoral print is a different personal-loan series

The monthly sectoral release is not the BSR. As on 31 July 2026, the personal-loans segment of 41 major banks grew **16.2%** year on year, against 11.9% a year earlier. It had also been 16.2% on 31 March 2026, against 11.7% then. Over those four months, industry moved from 15.0% to 20.0% and services from 19.0% to 22.9%. The household segment held its pace while firms and services pulled the headline non-food rate from 15.9% to 19.1%.

Inside the July personal-loan book, housing and vehicle loans stayed in double digits. Credit-card outstanding and loans against gold jewellery decelerated. In March, gold-jewellery loans had still been described as robust, and housing as steady. The retail mix cooled at the unsecured card end and at gold, and it did not cool at housing and vehicles.

The price of the wider March book moved with the easing cycle. Loans carrying interest below 9% were **64.2%** of outstanding credit at end-March 2026, against **43.9%** a year earlier. That is the whole BSR book, not a household-only rate.

## Debt is higher, and still mostly non-housing retail

The June 2026 Financial Stability Report puts household-sector debt, from bank and non-bank loans, at **45.5% of GDP** at end-September 2025. The report’s chart title describes that level as relatively low, and says the rise has been driven mainly by non-housing retail loans. Those loans were **58.4%** of household outstanding borrowings. Loans taken for consumption were nearly half of household borrowings, **49.7%** as at end-March 2026. The report’s categories separate consumption (personal loans, cards, consumer durables, and similar credit), asset creation (housing and vehicle loans), and productive borrowing (individual agriculture and business loans, and education).

This GDP ratio is not the 58.6% share of bank credit. One measures household debt against national output, including non-bank lenders. The other measures how much of the banking system’s loan book sits with households. Both say the household is the main borrower. Only the GDP ratio speaks to leverage.

## Unsecured loans slowed after earlier rules, and the risk did not vanish

The same stability report says demand for unsecured personal loans had been strong enough, in past years, to draw regulatory steps aimed at excess growth. Growth in that segment later moderated. The unsecured form of the loan remains a borrower vulnerability. A survey of ten large banks, covering **89%** of personal loans in the third quarter of 2025-26, looked at originations and fresh non-performing accounts across nine quarters from the third quarter of 2023-24. Originations and impairments both concentrated in borrowers with income below ₹10 lakh. The share of the below-₹5 lakh band in originations was declining. Prime and prime-plus rating tiers dominated originations.

For a household, the practical reading is narrower than “retail is booming.” Most of the bank book is still household credit. The newest acceleration is industrial and services credit. Cards and gold loans have cooled inside an otherwise double-digit personal-loan rate. The borrowers showing up most often in new unsecured loans, and in the loans that sour, are in the lower income bands the survey names.
