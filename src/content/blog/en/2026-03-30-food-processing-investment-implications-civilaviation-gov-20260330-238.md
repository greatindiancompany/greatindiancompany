# What MoFPI support changes for an investment memo

An investment note on Indian food processing should separate four piles of money: the promoter's equity, the bank loan, the ministry grant or incentive, and any foreign equity. The ministry's schemes change the size of the third pile. They do not replace the first two, and they do not turn a memorandum of understanding into commissioned capacity.

## Match the instrument to the asset

A cold-chain or processing-capacity project lives under PMKSY. The Annual Report for 2024-25 says the integrated cold-chain grant is capped at Rs 10 crore a project and is paid only on technical civil works and eligible plant and machinery. In general areas the promoter's equity must be at least 20 percent of project cost and the term loan at least 20 percent. For SC or ST promoters and the North Eastern region those floors are 10 percent. The implementation clock is 24 months in general areas and 30 months in difficult areas, with a reduction in the approved grant for delay, up to 5 percent of the instalment for each month past the timeline.

As on 31 December 2024 the ministry had taken up 394 cold-chain projects. Their combined project cost was Rs 11,466.38 crore, of which private investment was Rs 8,495.53 crore and grant-in-aid was Rs 2,970.85 crore. Private capital is the larger cheque. A memo that models the grant as most of the capital structure is not describing this scheme.

PLISFPI is a different contract. The firm invests and sells; the incentive follows eligible incremental sales, and a separate limb supports branding abroad. The Year End Review of 9 January 2026 says reported investment under the scheme is Rs 9,702 crore and incentives disbursed are Rs 2,162.553 crore across 161 cases, against 170 approved proposals. Disbursement is about a fifth of reported investment. Model the incentive as a recovery over the scheme life, which runs through 2026-27, not as day-one equity.

PMFME is smaller and stricter about what the grant may buy. The September 2025 FAQ caps the individual credit-linked grant at 35 percent and Rs 10 lakh, requires at least 10 percent from the beneficiary, and refuses land and working capital. A micro-unit model that uses the subsidy to fund inventory will not match the scheme.

## Budget lines are the cash the Centre has voted

Demand No. 45 for 2026-27 provides Rs 915 crore for PMKSY, Rs 1,200 crore for the food-processing PLI, and Rs 1,700 crore for PMFME, inside a ministry budget estimate of Rs 4,064.16 crore. Those are appropriations, not sanctions in a promoter's name. The ministry's own statement for 2025-26 shows why the distinction matters for PLI's predecessor year and for PMFME: PMFME's 2025-26 budget estimate was Rs 2,000 crore, the revised estimate Rs 1,500 crore, and actual expenditure Rs 1,296.53 crore. A memo should test returns against release, not against the opening budget estimate alone.

## Foreign equity has two routes

The annual report records two long-standing permissions. Food processing itself can receive 100 percent FDI on the automatic route, subject to sectoral regulation. Trading, including through e-commerce, in food products manufactured or produced in India can receive 100 percent FDI on the government approval route. A foreign investor buying into a factory and a foreign investor building a marketplace for Indian-made food are not in the same approval lane.

The stock of equity inflow the ministry cites for April 2014 to March 2025 is US$7.33 billion. That is a cumulative sector figure. It is not a run-rate a single plant should paste into a year-one cash flow.

## What to leave out of the base case

World Food India 2025 produced MoUs the ministry valued at more than Rs 1,02,000 crore. Put that in a market-interest paragraph. Do not add it to sanctioned project cost. Expected employment and farmer linkage in PMKSY press material is likewise tagged as expected on operationalisation. The cold-chain book, where private investment and grant are both stated as sanctioned totals, is the firmer capital figure, and it is dated 31 December 2024.

Loans to food and agro-based processing units and to cold chain are classified under agriculture for priority sector lending, in the list attached to the Reserve Bank's Master Directions of 4 September 2020, as the annual report notes. Cold chain and food parks also sit on the Harmonised Master List of Infrastructure. Both facts can change the lender's treatment of the loan. Neither fact is a guarantee of sanction.
