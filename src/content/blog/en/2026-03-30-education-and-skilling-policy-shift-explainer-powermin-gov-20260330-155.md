## The policy that is still in force

The National Education Policy 2020 is India’s first education policy of the 21st century. It says it will revise the structure, regulation, and governance of education so the system lines up with Sustainable Development Goal 4, which India adopted with the 2030 Agenda in 2015. Goal 4 seeks inclusive and equitable quality education and lifelong learning opportunities for all by 2030.

The policy text still describes the Ministry of Human Resource Development. That ministry is now the Ministry of Education. The aims below are taken from the policy, not from a later amendment.

## The school redesign

NEP 2020 says the 10+2 structure will be modified to a 5+3+3+4 design covering ages 3 to 18:

- Foundational Stage: three years of Anganwadi or pre-school plus Grades 1–2, ages 3–8.
- Preparatory Stage: Grades 3–5, ages 8–11.
- Middle Stage: Grades 6–8, ages 11–14.
- Secondary Stage: Grades 9–12, ages 14–18, in two phases.

Children aged 3–6 were outside the old 10+2 structure because Class 1 began at age 6. The new design brings early childhood care and education into the school structure.

The policy’s own account of access says Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan, now Samagra Shiksha, and the Right to Education Act brought near-universal enrolment in elementary education. It then records a drop at later grades. In the figures printed in the policy, the gross enrolment ratio was 90.9% in Grades 6–8, 79.3% in Grades 9–10, and 56.5% in Grades 11–12. The 75th round NSSO survey of 2017-18, as cited there, counted 3.22 crore out-of-school children aged 6 to 17. The stated goal is a 100% gross enrolment ratio from pre-school to secondary level by 2030. Those grade-wise ratios are the 2020 text’s figures, not a 2026 release.

## Vocational education inside the same policy

NEP 2020 says vocational education had been treated as inferior to mainstream schooling and had been concentrated in Grades 11–12 and among dropouts from Grade 8 upward. It requires vocational programmes to be brought into mainstream institutions in a phased way, with every child learning at least one vocation. It sets an aim that, by 2025, at least 50% of learners in school and higher education have exposure to vocational education. This September 2026 brief does not have a later official percentage that says whether that 2025 exposure aim was met.

In higher education, the policy aims to raise the gross enrolment ratio, including vocational education, from 26.3% in 2018 to 50% by 2035. A 10 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply later reported a ratio of 30 for 2023-24. That later level is a progress marker. It is not a change to the 2035 aim.
