The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship delivers most of its public training through four tracks, under the Skill India Mission launched in 2015. A Rajya Sabha reply on 4 February 2026, from Minister of State (Independent Charge) Jayant Chaudhary, is the cleanest official map of what those tracks had produced by the end of 2025.

The four tracks are the Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana, the Jan Shikshan Sansthan scheme, the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme, and the Craftsman Training Scheme in Industrial Training Institutes. The ministry says they are aligned to the National Skills Qualifications Framework, and that mobilisation runs through the Skill India Digital Hub.

## What each track had counted by 31 December 2025

PMKVY, introduced in 2015–16, had trained and certified over 1.64 crore candidates up to 31 December 2025. Jan Shikshan Sansthan had trained 34.14 lakh persons from 2018–19 to 31 December 2025. Apprentices engaged under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme totalled 51.30 lakh from 2016–17 to 2025–26, up to 31 December 2025. The number of ITIs rose from 9,776 in 2014 to 14,688.

These are throughput counts: people trained, certified, or engaged. The reply is explicit that placement was tracked only in the short-term training component of PMKVY 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0, from 2015–16 to 2021–22. Under PMKVY 4.0 the stated focus is to orient candidates for a career path they choose. The Skill India Digital Hub is the tool named for connecting candidates with jobs and apprenticeships. A trained-and-certified total is not a placement rate.

The ministry still cites older evaluations, and the dates matter. NITI Aayog's October 2020 study of PMKVY found that about 94% of employers surveyed said they would hire more PMKVY-trained candidates, and that 52% of candidates placed in full-time or part-time work, or oriented under recognition of prior learning, received or expected a higher salary than uncertified peers. A 2020 evaluation of Jan Shikshan Sansthan reported that training nearly doubled household incomes for beneficiaries, with women at 79% of participants. An ITI tracer study published in 2018 found that 63.5% of pass-outs were employed, of whom 6.7% were self-employed. A 2021 evaluation of the apprenticeship scheme said on-the-job training improved employability. The reply says the later version of that scheme pays the government's stipend share by direct benefit transfer to the apprentice's bank account. None of these studies is a 2025 placement census.

## Training inside the labour-force survey

The same reply says the share of youth aged 15–29 who are vocationally trained rose from 7.1% in 2017–18 to 26.1% in 2023–24, and attributes that rise to the combined schemes. That percentage is a characteristic of young people in the labour-force survey years. It is not the unemployment rate, and it is not a count of PMKVY certificates. The survey rates for the population aged 15 and above are in the [key-numbers brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-key-numbers-20260330-020).

On digital roles, the reply says 5,10,732 candidates had been trained or oriented in AI, IoT, and Industry 4.0 job roles, and 3,59,659 had been certified. The courses sit on qualification packs agreed with sector skill councils, under the National Council for Vocational Education and Training.

## Approvals on the books

The Cabinet has approved the Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability through Upgraded ITIs (PM SETU), with an estimated outlay of ₹60,000 crore over five years. The design is 1,000 ITIs in a hub-and-spoke model: 200 hubs and 800 spokes. The reply records that approval and that design. A count of institutes already upgraded is not part of the reply.

Separately, the Ministry of Labour and Employment told the Lok Sabha on 1 December 2025 that the Employment Linked Incentive scheme, the Pradhan Mantri Viksit Bharat Rozgar Yojana, has an outlay of ₹99,446 crore and aims to incentivize the creation of more than 3.5 crore jobs over two years, with a focus on manufacturing. That is the scheme's stated aim. It is not a tally of jobs already created, and it is not an EPFO payroll print. How payroll differs from a job count is in the [EPFO brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-impact-on-smes-20260330-030).

An employer looking for an apprentice, and a reader looking for the unemployment rate, are using two ministries and two kinds of number. The useful sentence names which one it is, and the date the count runs to.
