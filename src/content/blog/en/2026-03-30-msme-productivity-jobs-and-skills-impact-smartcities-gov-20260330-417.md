# Jobs, pay, and skills in the MSME productivity record

Employment and productivity answer different questions, and India's official pages use different universes for each. The page that counts workers in unincorporated non-farm establishments is ASUSE. The pages that train or certify firms sit with the Ministry of MSME.

## The worker count that has a denominator

ASUSE 2025, released 24 March 2026, estimates about 12.81 crore workers in unincorporated non-agricultural manufacturing, trade, and other services during January–December 2025. Employment rose 6.18 percent from ASUSE 2023-24, and more than 74.52 lakh jobs were added. Other services had the highest employment growth in the note, 7.40 percent, and about 35 lakh of the new jobs.

Gross value added per worker rose 4.54 percent in current prices, to ₹1,56,539. Emoluments per hired worker rose 3.88 percent, from ₹1,41,071 to ₹1,46,550, with the strongest rise in trade. Jobs grew faster than pay per hired worker and faster than value added per worker. The sector added people. It added less output per person than the raw gross-value-added headline of 10.87 percent might suggest, because that headline is not a per-worker figure and it is not adjusted for prices.

Women-owned proprietary establishments were 27.0 percent of proprietary establishments, up from 26.2 percent. That is an ownership share inside the survey, not a count of women workers.

Two later Press Information Bureau notes speak of MSME livelihoods at a much larger scale: about 32.8 crore people in the 14 May 2026 note, and more than 38.9 crore people employed in the 26 June 2026 note. Those figures are not ASUSE's 12.81 crore. Quote them as Ministry statements with a date. Do not average them with the survey.

## Skills programmes with published funnels

The MSME Champions scheme has two shop-floor pillars with counts in the 26 June 2026 note, both as on May 2026.

Lean manufacturing: more than 65,647 MSMEs registered, and nearly 18,961 certified. Lean is the Ministry's route to less waste and tighter operations. Certification is the result that can touch productivity. Registration is the application.

ZED: more than 93.61 lakh registered, and more than 6.68 lakh certified. ZED is a quality and environment certificate for Udyam-registered manufacturing MSMEs, with Bronze, Silver, and Gold levels. The gap between registration and certification is the skills and audit bottleneck, not a claim that 93 lakh factories have changed their process.

Technology Centres train around the same firms. As of November 2025, 25 extension centres had trained 53,963 young people and supported 1,357 MSMEs. Nine centres under the Technology Centre Systems Programme trained 59,357 people and assisted 1,520 MSMEs in January–November 2025. Those are training volumes at public centres. They are not a national skill-gap percentage. No official gap percentage is used here because these releases do not state one.

PM Vishwakarma is a separate artisan scheme, across 18 trades, with its own training and a toolkit incentive. It should stay in the artisan column. Merging it with ASUSE manufacturing employment double-counts a workforce the survey and the scheme do not define the same way.

Credit still decides whether a trained unit can hire. CGTMSE, with SIDBI, guarantees micro and small enterprise loans. A skill certificate does not create the post.
