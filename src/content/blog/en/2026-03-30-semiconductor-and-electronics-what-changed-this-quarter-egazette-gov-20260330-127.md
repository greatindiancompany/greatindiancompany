This quarter, for a page updated on 24 September 2026, is 1 July through 24 September 2026. Two official texts in that window change how the semiconductor programme should be described. Neither text approves a thirteenth factory.

## The decision this quarter

On 15 July 2026 the Union Cabinet approved Semicon 2.0. The [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) puts the outlay at ₹1,27,500 crore and lists six pillars: chip design, machines and materials, more fabs, a deeper ATMP and OSAT industry, research on more advanced technologies, and talent.

The same release is also a status report on the earlier programme. It says 12 manufacturing units had been approved, with cumulative investment of over ₹1.64 lakh crore: one silicon fab, one silicon-carbide fab, one integrated gallium-nitride micro-LED display fab, and nine packaging units. Micron, Kaynes, and CG Semi had started commercial production. One further unit was expected to start in 2026. The release does not name it. On design, 24 startup and MSME projects had financial support, and 105 startups and MSMEs had access to industry-standard design tools.

That is the new instrument. The factories named as already approved belong to the earlier mission. Semicon 2.0, in this text, is the decision to keep going, and to add equipment, materials, and a longer research and talent agenda, with a multi-year outlay.

## What Parliament said a week later

On 22 July 2026 the Minister of State for Electronics and Information Technology answered Lok Sabha unstarred question 483. The [reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) confirms the Cabinet date and the outlay, which it writes as ₹1.275 lakh crore. It places the 12 manufacturing projects in six states: Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Gujarat, Punjab, Odisha, and Uttar Pradesh. Investment commitments are about ₹1.64 lakh crore. Direct employment is put at around 32,000, and the reply says that number depends on how automated the factories are.

Commercial production, the reply says, had commenced at three plants: Micron Semiconductor Technology India, Kaynes Semicon, and CG Semi. Project details are pointed to [ism.gov.in](https://www.ism.gov.in/). The reply also records 24 design projects with financial support, 105 startups and MSMEs on design tools, and tool use above 87 lakh hours.

The question asked for progress under India Semiconductor Mission 2.0, including projects approved till May 2026. The answer treats Semicon 2.0 as approved on 15 July 2026, and the 12 factories as the manufacturing book already approved under the 2022 programme. A reader who wanted a list of plants sanctioned under the new outlay will not find one in this reply. The new outlay had been approved seven days earlier. The plants on the page are the earlier book.

## What this quarter did not change

The step from 10 approved projects to 12 happened on 5 May 2026, which is the previous quarter. That day's [Cabinet release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2258116) approved Crystal Matrix at Dholera, for gallium-nitride mini and micro-LED displays, and Suchi Semicon at Surat, for discrete-semiconductor OSAT. It said the count had reached 12 and the cumulative commitment was around ₹1.64 lakh crore. It also said two projects had already started commercial shipments and two more were expected soon.

By 15 July the commercial-production count in the Cabinet text was three named companies, plus one unnamed unit still expected in 2026. That is a movement in status across May to July. It is not a new approval inside July to September, and the July text still does not say which plant is the fourth to start.

No release used for this brief, dated inside July to September 2026, adds a project in a seventh state or revises the ₹1,27,500 crore outlay.

## How to describe the quarter in one paragraph

Say that the Cabinet extended the policy on 15 July 2026 with a ₹1,27,500 crore, six-pillar programme, and that Parliament on 22 July restated a 12-project manufacturing book of about ₹1.64 lakh crore, with three plants in commercial production. Say that the fourth production start expected in 2026 was still unnamed on those dates. Leave Crystal Matrix and Suchi Semicon in May, where the approval release puts them.
