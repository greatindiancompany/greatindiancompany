# The three rulebooks a retailer actually lives under

The regulatory outlook for Indian retail is not a single forthcoming bill. Three notified regimes already decide what an online or offline seller may do. A draft that was never notified is not part of that outlook.

## Foreign capital: DPIIT

The Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2020 still draws the capital line. Marketplace e-commerce may receive 100% FDI on the automatic route. Inventory-based e-commerce may not receive FDI. Crossing into ownership or control of inventory, including the 25% purchase test for a vendor, pulls a marketplace into the prohibited model. Multi-brand retail with FDI may not sell by e-commerce at all. Single-brand retail may sell online, with a two-year outer limit for opening stores if online sales start first. Later press notes, if any, are listed on DPIIT’s FDI policy page and override the consolidation where they say so.

## The consumer: Ministry of Consumer Affairs

The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, notified under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, apply to marketplace and inventory models. The Department of Consumer Affairs hosts the rules. A Press Information Bureau reply of 18 September 2020 records Rule 4: an e-commerce entity is a company under the Companies Act, or a foreign company, or an office, branch, or agency outside India owned or controlled by a person resident in India, and it must appoint a nodal contact who is resident in India.

The rules also put seller identity, grievance handling, and a ban on unfair trade practices on the record of a marketplace sale. They are consumer-protection rules. They do not grant FDI permission, and FDI permission does not satisfy them. Proposed amendments that remained proposals are not this rulebook. The text to follow is the notified 2020 rules and any amendment the department has actually published, including the 2021 amendment listed on its [acts and rules page](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/pages/consumer-protection-acts).

Packaged goods on a shelf also meet the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, administered by the same department: the declaration regime for net quantity, sale price, and consumer-care details. That is the counter rule, distinct from the platform rule.

## The tax trail: GST

Section 52 of the CGST Act requires an e-commerce operator that collects consideration to collect tax at source on the net value of taxable supplies made through it by other suppliers, at a notified rate not exceeding 1%, and to file a monthly statement. Suppliers claim that amount in their cash ledger. The 56th GST Council’s September 2025 rate recast changed many goods and services rates from 22 September 2025. It did not remove section 52.

A practical outlook, then, is a stack. Capital structure is a DPIIT question. The buyer’s remedy is a consumer-affairs question. The invoice trail on a marketplace is a GST question. Teams that fold all three into “e-commerce regulation” miss which file answers which objection.

The FDI text is the [2020 circular](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/6457fc2703ee6082366c4a958b6473a8.pdf). The consumer rules are on the [department page](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/theconsumerprotection/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020). Section 52 is in the [CBIC repository](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter10/section52_v1.00.html).
