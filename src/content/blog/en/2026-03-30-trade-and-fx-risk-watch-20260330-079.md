# What to watch in India's trade gap, reserves, and capital flows

The external risk worth watching is specific. The goods deficit is wider than a year ago. Portfolio investment left in the June quarter. Valuation moved reserves by more than the current account did. July's non-resident deposit inflow was large enough to dominate the monthly financing picture, and it is still marked preliminary. None of these is a prediction. Each one has a published level and a document that will update it.

## The goods deficit is wider on both clocks

On the RBI clock, the April–June 2026 merchandise deficit was US$86.1 billion, against US$68.9 billion a year earlier. The July merchandise deficit was US$31.7 billion, against US$28.2 billion. April–July was US$117.8 billion, against US$97.1 billion.

On the customs clock, Commerce puts the April–July merchandise deficit at US$118.60 billion, against US$96.66 billion. DGCIS provisional quick estimates put April–August exports at US$215,912.45 million and imports at US$363,000.93 million. The gap between those two lines is US$147,088.48 million. A year earlier the same two columns differed by US$123,879.93 million. August itself was a strong export month, up 26.12 per cent, with imports up 14.07 per cent. One strong month did not put the five-month customs gap below last year's.

What would ease this watch: an RBI month in which the merchandise deficit narrows on a year-ago basis, or a Commerce release in which the year-to-date merchandise deficit stops widening. What would sharpen it: another quarter in which goods imports outrun both goods exports and the services surplus.

The services offset is still large. Net services receipts were US$51.6 billion in April–June and US$69.3 billion in April–July. The current account deficit for April–June was 0.5 per cent of GDP. The risk is a goods gap that grows faster than that offset, not the existence of a goods gap.

Petroleum is the piece of the goods account with the clearest external price. The April–June POL deficit was US$37.6 billion, against US$32.2 billion. The next quarterly balance of payments is the document that updates it.

## Portfolio outflows and a deposit inflow are different risks

In April–June 2026, foreign portfolio investment was a net outflow of US$9.6 billion. A year earlier it was a net inflow of US$1.6 billion. FDI was a net inflow of US$6.1 billion, above the year-ago US$5.2 billion. The quarterly current account was small. The financing mix was the fragile part: a portfolio outflow larger than the current account deficit.

July changed the monthly mix. Portfolio investment was a net inflow of US$4.1 billion. For April–July, portfolio investment was still a net outflow of US$5.5 billion. Non-resident deposits were a net inflow of US$33.5 billion in July and US$36.2 billion for April–July, against US$4.7 billion in April–July 2025. The July overall balance was a surplus of US$20.8 billion, so reserves rose on a payments basis.

The deposit print is preliminary. A one-month inflow of that size can be a special window, a classification, or a flow that reverses. The confirmation document is the next quarterly balance of payments, where July is revised inside a full financial account. Until then, the prudent reading is that July's reserve build leaned on banking capital, and that April–July portfolio investment was still negative.

External commercial borrowings need the same care with labels. In the BPM6 quarter, ECBs to India were a net US$3.3 billion. In the older-format variation note for the same quarter, external commercial borrowings were US$1.0 billion. Those are different lines. Watching "ECBs" requires naming the table.

## Valuation can move the headline without a trade event

In April–June 2026, reserves fell US$22.5 billion in nominal terms. Of that, US$14.4 billion was valuation, which the RBI ties mainly to a lower gold price and a stronger dollar against major currencies. The payments-basis decline was US$8.1 billion.

The weekly stock shows the same split in miniature. As on 11 September 2026, total reserves were US$780,782 million, down US$4,924 million on the week. Gold was US$111,225 million, down US$2,591 million on the week. Foreign-currency assets were US$645,796 million, down US$2,372 million. In that week the gold component fell by US$2,591 million inside a US$4,924 million decline in the total. As on 4 September the stock had been US$785,706 million, up US$44,903 million on that week. Large weekly moves are now part of the series. They are a reason to read the components, not a reason to treat each Friday as a new regime.

Since end-March 2026 the stock is up US$89,674 million. The buffer is larger than it was at the close of the financial year. The watch is the composition of the next move: transactions, gold, or the dollar.

## Concentration is a market risk already in the tables

Software exports in 2025-26 were 54.1 per cent to the United States and 72.6 per cent invoiced in dollars, on a total of US$221.4 billion excluding overseas commercial presence. Merchandise exports in April–August 2026 sent US$42,790.23 million to the United States out of US$215,912.45 million. The UAE, the largest FTA merchandise market in 2025-26 at US$37,359.11 million, is down 10.61 per cent in the April–August quick estimates.

A demand or policy shock in one destination shows up first in these shares, not in the all-India total. The DGCIS country table and the next software survey are the updates. The July Commerce release already flagged destination growth rates, including the United States and China, on a "change in value" basis. Use the level table when you need rank, and the growth list when you need who accelerated.

## What this watchlist refuses to do

It does not turn the 0.5 per cent current-account ratio into a crisis call. It does not annualise July's deposit inflow. It does not read the weekly reserve decline of US$4,924 million as intervention. The RBI supplement does not identify intervention in that cell. Readers who want the intervention question need an RBI statement that speaks to operations, not the stock table alone.
