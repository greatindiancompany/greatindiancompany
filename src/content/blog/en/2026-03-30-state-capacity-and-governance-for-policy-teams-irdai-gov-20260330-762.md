# What Policy Teams Should Ask About State Finance and Delivery

Policy teams lose state capacity in the gaps between documents. Tax devolution, capital loans, local-body grants, portal dashboards and civil-service training are run by different offices and published on different days. A review that asks one question of each office will see the system. A review that asks for "the latest governance reform" will get a speech.

## Ask the finance department which award it is using

The Fifteenth Finance Commission, summarised by PIB on 1 February 2021, set vertical devolution at 41 percent of the divisible pool for 2021-26. The states' share was assessed at Rs 42.2 lakh crore. Horizontal distribution put 45 percent on income distance, 15 percent each on population and area, 12.5 percent on demographic performance, 10 percent on forest and ecology, and 2.5 percent on tax and fiscal effort. Revenue deficit grants of Rs 2,94,514 crore went to seventeen states. That award is the path states have just finished living through.

The question for 2026-27 is no longer only that percentage. The Department of Expenditure's capital-loan guidelines of 27 March 2026 split Rs 67,000 crore of untied assistance among states in proportion to Sixteenth Finance Commission shares of central taxes. The loan is Rs 2,00,000 crore, interest-free for 50 years, and above the net borrowing ceiling that the Public Finance (States) Division administers under Article 293(3). A policy note that quotes 41 percent and ignores the loan, or quotes the loan and ignores devolution, is describing half the transfer.

The follow-up questions are operational. Has the state filed the finance secretary's certificate on PFMS? Has it closed Centrally Sponsored Scheme nodal accounts, which the second installment requires? Which five sectors did it prioritise? Is own-resource capital expenditure, excluding SASCI releases, on course for the 10 percent growth that unlocks Part III? The Reserve Bank's 23 January 2026 release gives the macro check: a 3.3 percent consolidated deficit in 2024-25, explained mainly by these loans, and liabilities budgeted at 29.2 percent of GDP at end-March 2026. Ask which demographic window RBI would put this state in. The press note distinguishes youthful, ageing and intermediate states and does not hand you the list. The full study does the state work.

## Ask what reached the panchayat and the city

Local government grants under the Fifteenth Finance Commission were Rs 4,36,361 crore for 2021-26. Rural bodies were earmarked Rs 2,36,805 crore, urban bodies Rs 1,21,055 crore, and health grants through local governments Rs 70,051 crore. Cities under a million had basic grants. Million-plus cities were entirely on the performance-linked challenge fund. A policy team reviewing "state spending on health" should ask how much of the primary-care grant actually sat with the local body, because that is the route the Commission chose for urban health and wellness centres, sub-centres and primary health centres.

DARPG's December 2025 service count is the delivery twin of that question. Of 24,090 e-services, 8,656 were in local governance and utilities, and the 22 January 2026 release says municipal contact is where citizens show up. Ask for the city's portal, not only the state's. Ask whether the state is on the high mandatory-saturation list and, separately, whether its unified portal is one of the seven named at 100 percent. Those are different answers.

## Ask who is accountable at the district

NITI Aayog's Aspirational Districts Programme, launched in January 2018, covers 112 districts. The published method is convergence of schemes, collaboration that includes the collector, and a monthly delta ranking. States drive it. NITI anchors it. If a policy team is writing about district capacity, the question is whether this district is in the 112, who the state nodal officer is, and what the latest delta shows on Champions of Change. For every other district, that apparatus is not the monitoring system, and the note should say so.

On people, ask whether the state is among the 29 with a Mission Karmayogi memorandum, what the 6 March 2026 user stock was relative to DoPT's design aim of about two crore, and how the department trains district staff given that iGOT does not monitor learning at district level. The Cabinet Secretary's coordination unit and the Capacity Building Commission are the Union-side owners of that question. DoPT holds the programme. Karmayogi Bharat holds the platform.

## Ask how a failed service is seen

The June 2026 CPGRAMS report gives a central benchmark: 14-day average disposal in central ministries for 2026, 78,793 cases pending, a secretary review module with 377 meetings by 30 June 2026. Then ask the state question the 10 December 2025 reply leaves open: there was no API between CPGRAMS and the state's Right to Service system. A policy team that recommends "integrate grievance redressal" can point at that sentence as the specific missing link, and at the National Centre for Good Governance study as the review already commissioned on whether statutory timelines hold.
