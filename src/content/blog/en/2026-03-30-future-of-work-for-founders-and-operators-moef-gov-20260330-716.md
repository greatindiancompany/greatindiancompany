# What founders and operators need to operationalise under the labour codes

This is an operating note for someone who employs people or runs a platform in India. It follows the Ministry of Labour and Employment's account of the codes that took effect on 21 November 2025, the text of the Code on Social Security, and the Rajasthan and Karnataka Acts. It is not a substitute for reading the commencement notification that applies to your establishment. The Ministry said that during the transition, relevant provisions of the existing Acts and rules continue. Where this note and your current licence condition seem to conflict, the Gazette wins.

## If you employ people directly

The Ministry's 21 November 2025 note says every worker gets an appointment letter stating the terms, and that this is the written proof of the engagement. Build the letter so it can also carry wage, designation, and social-security entitlement. The note says digital and audio-visual workers, including journalists in electronic media, dubbing artists, and stunt persons, are within this expectation, not only factory staff.

Minimum wages, on the Ministry's description of the Code on Wages, are no longer limited to the old list of scheduled employments. Budget a wage at or above the applicable minimum, and at or above the national floor wage once that floor is the rate you are required to use. Do not assume a startup exemption that the note does not give. MSME workers are described as covered by the Social Security Code on the basis of employee count, with minimum wages, canteen and rest facilities where the thresholds say so, standard hours, overtime at double the rate, and paid leave.

For IT and IT-enabled services, the note says salary is to be released by the 7th of every month. Put that date in the payroll calendar rather than in a policy PDF nobody runs. Overtime, where the note discusses it for sectors including textiles, mines, and export work, is consent-based and at least double the ordinary rate. Working hours in the passages on beedi and mine workers are described as capped in a range of 8 to 12 hours a day and 48 hours a week. Use the cap that the rule for your sector actually states.

Workers above 40 are to receive a free annual health check. That is a vendor contract and a roster, not a poster. Women may work at night and in all types of work, including work that was previously closed to them, subject to their consent and to safety measures. The export-sector passage mentions written consent, safe transport, and security. If you cannot provide the safety measure, you do not have the night-shift opening.

Fixed-term employees, on the same note, receive benefits equal to permanent workers and become eligible for gratuity after one year of continuous service rather than five. If your model is a one-year or eighteen-month contract, gratuity is now part of the unit cost in the Ministry's description. Price it before you hire, and confirm the provision has commenced for your case.

## If you are an aggregator

Confirm you are in the Seventh Schedule of the Code on Social Security. The categories are ride sharing, food and grocery delivery, logistics, e-marketplaces (marketplace and inventory, wholesale and retail, business and consumer), professional services, healthcare, travel and hospitality, content and media, and any other goods and services platform.

If you are, section 114(4) is your formula. The Centre notifies a rate not below 1 percent and not above 2 percent of annual turnover. Turnover excludes tax, levy, and cess paid to the Centre. The amount cannot exceed 5 percent of what you pay or owe gig and platform workers. Section 114(5) says the Centre also notifies the date this contribution starts. Do not remit, and do not tell workers the levy is already funding a benefit, until that date is in a notification. You can build the calculation now. Accruing it as a present statutory debt before the date is a finance choice, not a reading of subsection (5).

A gig worker, in the code, earns outside a traditional employer-employee relationship. If your contracts are in fact employment contracts, the aggregator formula is the wrong instrument and the employer duties above are the right ones. Misclassification does not become legal because a worker uses an app.

The e-Shram Universal Account Number is the unorganised-worker identity, issued on self-declaration. It is not the EPFO Universal Account Number. Do not write them into the same field in your database.

## If you operate in Karnataka or Rajasthan

Karnataka's Act 72 of 2025 is deemed in force from 30 May 2025. Covered aggregators and platforms were required to register with the welfare board within 45 days of that commencement, so by September 2026 the question is whether the registration was done, not whether a fresh 45-day clock starts today. Workers onboarded on a platform are to be registered and given a unique ID that applies across platforms, regardless of how short the engagement is. Contracts must show piece or time rates, deductions, incentives, and the right to refuse a task, and a change needs at least 14 days' notice. You must explain automated monitoring and decision systems, in simple language, where they affect fares, earnings, and related conditions, and you must take measures so those systems do not discriminate on religion, race, caste, gender, place of birth, or disability.

The welfare fee is a state-notified percent of payout, not less than 1 percent and not more than 5 percent, which the Act said should be notified within six months of commencement. The Act says the fee counts as the section 114(4) contribution, with a yearly reconciliation of any difference. When you model Karnataka, model the reconciliation. Do not add the state percent and the central percent as if the Act had not mentioned section 114.

Rajasthan's Act 29 of 2023 starts on a date the state notifies. Its fee is a notified percent of transaction value, excluding tax. Once a fee is due and late, interest is 12 percent a year. Payments are to be mapped to a state transaction system. Grievances go to a designated officer, with an appeal within 90 days. Until you have the commencement notification, do not collect a fee you cannot show the worker is owed under a live Act. Once you do, the interest rate is already in the text.

These two states are examples, not a complete map. Bihar's Act 8 of 2025 also waits on a commencement notification, and different provisions may start on different dates. Telangana's Act 21 of 2026 was appointed to come into force on 2 June 2026. If you operate in either state, read that Act's fee base rather than pasting the Karnataka percent onto the payout.

## A short order of work

Issue appointment letters that match how people actually work. Align payroll dates, overtime, and the annual check for workers over 40. Decide whether each cohort is employed or gig, and do not staff one cohort with the other cohort's paperwork. Build the aggregator calculation, and wait for section 114(5) before you treat it as payable. If Karnataka is in your map, register, disclose the automated rules, and reconcile the state fee against the central contribution. If Rajasthan is in your map, find the commencement date before you change the payout flow.
