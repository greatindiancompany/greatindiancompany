# A Reader’s Map of Official Green Hydrogen Figures, Including the Ones That Disagree

Use this page before quoting a figure. Each number below is tied to the document that printed it. Where two documents disagree, both sentences stay.

## Mission design

The [MNRE mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) states the objective: a global hub for production, use, and export. It states an initial outlay of ₹19,744 crore, of which SIGHT is ₹17,490 crore, pilot projects are ₹1,466 crore, research is ₹400 crore, and other components are ₹388 crore. On the same page, separate lines list low-carbon steel pilots at ₹455 crore through 2029-30, mobility pilots at ₹496 crore through 2025-26, shipping pilots at ₹115 crore through 2025-26, and hubs at ₹400 crore through 2025-26, with at least two hubs in the initial phase. Demand tools on that page are export policy, a minimum share for designated consumers decided by the Empowered Group, and competitive bidding. The page says bidding is to use equipment approved by the Government of India.

## The product rule and the certificate

PIB, 19 August 2023: not more than 2 kg of CO2 equivalent per kg of hydrogen, well-to-gate, stated in the headline as a 12-month average. The boundary includes water treatment, electrolysis, purification, drying, and compression. Electrolysis and biomass are both in scope. The Bureau of Energy Efficiency accredits verifiers.

MNRE’s certification-scheme notice is dated 29 April 2025. The PIB note of 12 November 2025 says a final certificate is mandatory if the facility takes a central or state incentive, or sells or uses the hydrogen in India. Minister Pralhad Joshi launched the certification portal on 17 June 2026.

## Awards

Minister, 17 June 2026: SIGHT incentives awarded for 8,62,000 tonnes a year of green hydrogen; 15 companies awarded incentives for 3,000 MW a year of electrolyser manufacturing; contracts for 30,000 tonnes a year to IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, and NRL; agreements for 6.7 lakh tonnes a year of green ammonia to 11 fertiliser plants; ₹84 crore sanctioned for steel pilots on 100 percent hydrogen injection; nearly ₹208 crore for 37 hydrogen vehicles and 9 refuelling stations; ₹193.35 crore across seven testing facilities; a research roadmap of ₹113 crore for 21 projects; ₹100 crore allocated for startups, with a first list of nine startups at ₹22 crore; JSW’s 3,600 tonne-a-year plant commissioned in November 2025.

The November 2025 note says that, as of May 2025, 19 companies had been allocated 862,000 tonnes a year and 15 firms had been awarded 3,000 MW of electrolyser capacity. The tonne figure and the electrolyser megawatts match the later speech. The production-side company count of 19 is in the November note. The June speech does not repeat a company count for the 8,62,000 tonne award. Do not collapse 19 and 15 into one sentence.

Rajya Sabha reply, reported by PIB on 11 August 2026: Mode-2B refinery capacity of 30 kilo-tonnes a year, at Panipat 10, Bina 5, Vizag 5, and Numaligarh 10, on a build-own-operate basis.

## Ammonia, two sentences

November 2025 note: aggregate procurement of 7.24 lakh metric tonnes a year, at ₹55.75 per kg. June 2026 speech: agreements signed for 6.7 lakh tonnes a year to 11 plants. Both stand as written.

## Vehicles, two layers

June 2026 speech: nearly ₹208 crore, 37 vehicles, 9 stations. Lok Sabha, 29 July 2026: 12 projects, 70 vehicles (27 buses and 43 trucks), 16 stations, 21 routes, about ₹410.77 crore allocated, ₹41.582 crore released. The annex’s Phase I total is ₹207.91 crore for 10 buses and 27 trucks, which is the earlier 37-vehicle picture. Phase II totals ₹202.86 crore for 17 buses and 16 trucks.

## Hubs

PIB, 10 October 2025: Deendayal, V.O. Chidambaranar, and Paradip port areas recognised under Component B2, which allows recognition without direct financial assistance. Lok Sabha, 5 August 2026: those three ports, plus MNRE endorsement of NTPC’s Pudimadaka proposal. Use that annex for Kandla’s 3,400 acres and 30-year lease, the 1 MW plant at ₹14.1 crore, the 5 MW contract at ₹62.80 crore dated 31 December 2026, Paradip’s ₹797.17 crore jetty at 4.0 million tonnes a year of handling capacity, Tuticorin’s allotments, and Pudimadaka’s ₹85,000 crore commitment excluding renewable projects, with planned hydrogen output of 0.21 million tonnes a year by 2030.

The November 2025 note describes the Tuticorin pilot as a ₹25 crore, 10 Nm³ per hour facility commissioned in September 2025. The August annex describes a 10 Nm³ pilot commissioned in April 2025 with port funding of ₹3.88 crore. Quote the document you mean.

## Aims

Repeated in the November 2025 note and the June 2026 speech: 5 million tonnes a year, about 125 GW, investment expected above ₹8 lakh crore, over 6 lakh jobs, about 50 million tonnes of annual emissions avoided. The November note also says fossil-fuel imports are expected to fall by more than ₹1 lakh crore, and that more than 5,600 trainees had been certified in hydrogen-related qualifications. MNRE’s schemes list dates skill guidelines to 16 March 2024 and a skilling call to 23 March 2026, and dates the electrolyser-as-a-service model agreement to 24 July 2026.
