# What Changed in India's State Capacity Calendar This Year

The useful change in 2026 is a pair of operating rules, dated and in force, that tell a state how money moves and how online services will be judged. By late September the question is whether those rules are being met, because the announcements themselves landed in March and May.

## A redesigned capital loan, with gates

On 27 March 2026 the Department of Expenditure wrote to every Chief Secretary with the guidelines for Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment (SASCI), 2026-27. The letter, file F. No. 44(1)/PF-S/2026-27 (CAPEX), allocates Rs 2,00,000 crore. The instrument is the same shape the Centre has used since 2020-21: a 50-year interest-free loan, over and above the normal net borrowing ceiling, meant to be spent inside the financial year unless a clause says otherwise.

What changed is the design around that loan. States and Union Territories with legislatures file certificates and project lists only through the PFMS module. Manual submissions are not accepted. The senior-most finance secretary signs. States are told to prioritise a maximum of five sectors. The first slice of untied funds, 66 percent, follows those mandatory conditions. The second slice, 34 percent, waits on three further proofs: use of at least 75 percent of the first slice, closure of every Single Nodal Agency bank account for Centrally Sponsored Schemes with the unspent central share and interest returned to the Consolidated Fund of India, and upload of project details for SASCI years 2020-21 through 2024-25.

Part I of the untied window is Rs 75,000 crore, of which Rs 67,000 crore is shared among states in proportion to their share of central taxes under the Sixteenth Finance Commission award. Part II sets aside Rs 10,000 crore for the state share of central infrastructure projects and infrastructure-oriented schemes such as AMRUT, PMGSY and Jal Jeevan Mission. Part III sets aside Rs 25,000 crore for states whose own-resource capital expenditure grows by more than 10 percent, and it tells states to leave SASCI releases out of that growth sum. Part IV sets aside Rs 4,000 crore for public-finance IT, in blocks of Rs 50 crore a component, with reform reports due to the Comptroller and Auditor General by 15 December 2026.

That is the calendar a finance department is actually running in this quarter.

## A new assessment portal, beside the monthly count

On 25 May 2026 DARPG opened the NeSDA 2025 portal in a meeting of central, state and UT nodal officers. The Press Information Bureau note of 28 May 2026 describes NeSDA as a biennial assessment, built from the UN e-Government Survey's Online Service Index and adapted to India's federal structure. For this round, 59 mandatory services are proposed for every state and UT, and 43 services for central ministries and departments. City portals sit in the coverage, alongside state, UT and central portals. Data are collected from the governments themselves. DARPG said it would hold consultations, capacity-building sessions and review meetings through the cycle.

The monthly NeSDA Way Forward series has not stopped while that biennial round opens. The thirty-second edition, for December 2025, was released through PIB on 22 January 2026. It recorded 24,090 e-services across states and UTs as of 31 December 2025, of which 8,656 were in local governance and utility services, and 156 services added in November 2025. Those figures are state-uploaded dashboard numbers. They are a stock. The May portal is the next exam of depth: accessibility, content, ease of use, security and privacy, end-to-end delivery, integration, tracking, open data, e-participation and use of emerging technologies.

## Grievance reporting is now a monthly institution

DARPG's CPGRAMS report for June 2026, the fiftieth such report on central ministries and departments, was issued on 29 July 2026. Central ministries redressed 1,99,968 grievances that month. PIB states that the average disposal time in central ministries and departments for 2026 is 14 days, and that monthly disposal has crossed one lakh cases for the forty-eighth month in a row. The report is part of the ten-step CPGRAMS reform. A secretary-level review module, live since 14 June 2025, had logged 377 review meetings by 30 June 2026.

Read together, the quarter does not turn on a single new slogan. Capital loans now clear utilization and scheme-account gates. Online services are about to be scored again on a citizen-facing framework. Grievance disposal is being published every month against a 14-day central average.

## How to use this

A founder timing a state payment, a portal integration or a works contract should ask which SASCI part the counterpart's project sits in, and whether the second installment's conditions are still open. A policy reader should treat the December 2025 service count and the May 2026 assessment portal as two different objects: one is a saturation dashboard, the other is a maturity assessment that had only just begun collecting data when this brief was updated.
