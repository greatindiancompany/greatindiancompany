# Which desk owns an Indian capital-markets rule

Policy arguments about "the IPO market" often ask SEBI to do work that sits in the Finance Ministry, or ask the Reserve Bank to loosen an equity rule it does not write. The split below is the one in the departments' own descriptions and in the instruments they issued.

## Finance Ministry: the Acts, the Board, and the foreign-exchange frame

The Department of Economic Affairs, Financial Markets Division, is the finance-ministry desk for securities-market policy, external commercial borrowings, and foreign portfolio flows under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999. It handles the administrative relationship with SEBI and the Securities Appellate Tribunal. It administers the SEBI Act, 1992, the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the Depositories Act, 1996, and section 20 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. Commodity derivatives have sat with the division since 2013.

A policy team that wants a change to who may regulate, what a securities contract is, or how a depository is constituted is in this division's statutes. A team that wants a different disclosure line in a prospectus is not. That line is a SEBI regulation made under those Acts. The division also sits on the government side of foreign portfolio policy. The day-to-day debt ceiling is then notified by RBI. Treating a DEA discussion and an RBI circular as the same document produces two answers to one question.

Public-sector delisting is the clearest recent case of a government interest inside a SEBI rule. Chapter 3 of SEBI's Annual Report 2025-26 describes a carve-out for PSUs, other than banks, NBFCs, and insurers, where the government and other PSUs hold at least 90 per cent. The public-shareholder two-thirds vote is waived. The route is a fixed-price delisting at least 15 per cent above a floor. The floor is the highest of a 52-week acquisition price, a 26-week acquisition price, and a joint valuation by two independent valuers, replacing a 60-day market average that the chapter says had inflated the price of thinly traded PSU stocks. Amounts unclaimed a year after delisting go to the exchange for seven years and then toward the Investor Education and Protection Fund or SEBI's investor protection fund. This is a fiscal and ownership policy delivered through SEBI's delisting regulations. A disinvestment desk that only reads ICDR will miss it.

## SEBI: issue, list, funds, and the conduct of intermediaries

ICDR, last amended 21 March 2026, is the public-issue, rights, preferential, and placement rule. LODR, last amended 14 July 2026, is the rule for staying listed. The Mutual Funds Regulations, 2026, last amended 7 July 2026, govern the domestic buyer that now anchors issues. The Foreign Portfolio Investors Regulations, 2019, govern registration and the securities an FPI may buy. Exchanges and depositories implement the circulars. They do not replace them.

The 2025-26 record shows what that perimeter produced, which is the evidence a policy review should start from rather than a talking point. Primary mobilisation was ₹13.6 lakh crore. Equity public and rights issues were ₹2.34 lakh crore. SME listings were 257 and raised ₹11,587 crore. Main-board IPOs raised ₹1.8 lakh crore across 109 issues. Domestic institutions held 17 per cent and foreign portfolio owners 15.8 per cent by March 2026. Demat accounts were 22.5 crore. Those are outcomes of the SEBI perimeter plus household behaviour. They are not, by themselves, a reason to amend the SEBI Act.

Where SEBI's chapter explicitly aimed at government balance sheets, the instruments were municipal bonds and REITs and InvITs. Municipal issues raised ₹1,756 crore across 14 deals. Outreach named Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Tamil Nadu for municipal bonds, and Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, and Gujarat for state-asset monetisation through the trusts. A state finance department looking for an equity-IPO incentive will not find it there. It will find a bond and a yield vehicle, at a scale far below the equity table.

Minimum public shareholding remains a 25 per cent destination. The six-tier path, which lets a large issuer start with a smaller offer, is the policy choice that most affects how quickly a newly listed firm meets that destination. The tier text should be read in the regulation and in the contractual rules under the SCRA framework, not paraphrased into a single percentage for every issuer.

## RBI: the debt cap, not the equity book

Circular RBI/2026-27/07 of 6 April 2026, on the [Reserve Bank circular index](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=13366), sets FY 2026-27 limits for foreign portfolio investment in debt. General Route ratios are unchanged at 6 per cent of eligible central government securities, 2 per cent of state government securities, and 15 per cent of corporate bonds. Fully Accessible Route securities sit outside that ration. From 1 April 2026, Voluntary Retention Route holdings count inside the General Route limits. Absolute ceilings step from ₹15,51,646 crore in April-September 2026 to ₹16,32,640 crore in October-March 2027. Credit-default swaps sold by these investors are capped at 5 per cent of the corporate-bond stock, ₹3,30,464 crore of additional notional in the circular.

A policy note that proposes "easing FPI limits to support the IPO market" has to say which limit. The equity outflow SEBI reports for 2025-26, USD 19.7 billion net, is visible in SEBI's FPI statistics. It is not a breach of the RBI debt percentage. Changing the 6 per cent G-sec line does not change ICDR eligibility. Changing ICDR does not change the debt line.

## A workable assignment

Statute and SEBI's own governance: DEA Financial Markets Division. Prospectus, listing, mutual funds, FPI registration, exchange procedure: SEBI, with NSE and BSE circulars as the implementation layer for a live issue. Rupee debt quotas and the FEMA debt regulations: RBI. PSU delisting price and unclaimed proceeds: SEBI rules built for a government shareholding pattern, with the fiscal interest upstream at the shareholder ministry. Municipal and state-asset monetisation: SEBI's bond and trust framework, aimed at the states the outreach already named.
