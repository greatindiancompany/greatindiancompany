# Where Rail Freight Grew, and Where Metro Now Runs

India does not publish rail freight as a state league table. Indian Railways reports loading by zone. Metro rail is a city network under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs. A “state-wise rail” page that forces both into one ranking is misreading the map.

## Freight growth in FY 2025-26 was broad, and uneven

The 1 April 2026 ministry release lists zones that recorded positive freight-loading growth in 2025-26. Four zones were in double digits:

- South Western Railway, 14.89 percent
- North Central Railway, 12.62 percent
- East Coast Railway, 10.42 percent
- West Central Railway, 10.06 percent

Other zones named with positive growth were Northeast Frontier Railway at 6.75 percent, North Western Railway at 5.17 percent, Western Railway at 3.57 percent, South East Central Railway at 3.18 percent, South Central Railway at 2.59 percent, Southern Railway at 1.10 percent, Eastern Railway at 0.78 percent, East Central Railway at 0.39 percent, and North Eastern Railway at 0.25 percent.

The national loading increase was 3.25 percent, to 1,670 million tonnes. The release does not give each zone’s tonnes, and it does not state that the list above is every zone. Zones absent from that positive list are not assigned a decline here. South Western, North Central, East Coast, and West Central are the growth leaders the ministry named. Eastern and East Central grew, but by less than one percent. That is a corridor-and-commodity pattern — ore, coal-bearing and steel belts, and the western and southern construction markets — not a state GDP ranking.

## The freight corridors cut across zones

The commissioned Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor runs from Ludhiana to Sonnagar, 1,337 km. The Western corridor runs from Dadri to Jawaharlal Nehru Port, 1,506 km. About 443 trains a day were reported on the two corridors on 7 August 2026. The next corridor named in Union Budget 2026, Dankuni to Surat, would tie the east to the west if the detailed project report becomes a sanctioned work. It is not an operating route in that reply.

Gati Shakti terminals are sited by demand and railway infrastructure, not by a state quota in the releases used here. National handling at commissioned terminals was 146 million tonnes in 2025-26.

## Metro is 26 cities, not a state average

On 15 March 2026 PIB reported about 1,095 km of metro and regional rapid transit in operation across 26 cities, including 55 km of the Delhi–Meerut RRTS. Cities named in that note include Delhi and the National Capital Region, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Chennai, Lucknow, Pune, and Ahmedabad. The note says “and many others.” It does not print all 26. An earlier PIB note, dated 9 August 2025, had 1,013 km across 23 cities by May 2025. The later count is the one to use.

Namo Bharat service on the Delhi–Meerut corridor, operational since October 2023, runs at 160 km/h with a design speed of 180 km/h. That is regional rapid transit inside the metro length above, and it is not an Indian Railways Vande Bharat service. Kolkata’s underwater section and Kochi’s water metro, also described in the March 2026 note, are city systems under the urban programme.

Zone growth and city metros answer different location questions. The commodity reason for the zone gaps is in the [market-signal brief](/blog/rail-and-transit-market-signal-brief-commerce-gov-20260330-188). Why sanctioned kilometres lag open track is in the [bottleneck review](/blog/rail-and-transit-execution-bottleneck-review-commerce-gov-20260330-588).

Great Indian Company keeps zones, corridors, and metro cities on one map without pretending they are the same statistic.
