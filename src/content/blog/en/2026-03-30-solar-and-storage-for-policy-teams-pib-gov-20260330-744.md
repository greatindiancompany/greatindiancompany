# What Policy Teams Should Separate in Solar and Storage Design

Policy teams inherit four instruments that all get called "solar support." They do not pay the same person, and they do not pay at the same milestone. Collapsing them into one budget line hides which rule is working.

## An obligation is not a grant

MNRE's storage page, updated 21 November 2023, describes the Ministry of Power's energy storage obligation. It rises from 1 percent in FY 2023-24 to 4 percent in FY 2029-30, by 0.5 percent each year. It counts as met only when at least 85 percent of the energy stored in the year comes from renewable sources. The payer is the obligated entity, buying or building storage and filling it mostly with renewable power. The page also quotes CEA's 2023 plan: 82.37 GWh of storage required in 2026-27 and 411.4 GWh in 2031-32, with batteries at 34.72 GWh and then 236.22 GWh.

That obligation does not, by itself, write a cheque to a battery developer. It creates a compliance demand. Whether the demand is met with a battery, pumped storage, or a contract is a portfolio choice inside the 85 percent renewable-charging condition.

## A grant has milestones, and this one missed the first

PIB release 2118325 of 3 April 2025 is the grant. Cabinet approval was 6 September 2023. Viability-gap funding for batteries approved during 2023-26 is paid 10 percent at financial closure, 45 percent at commercial operation, and 15 percent a year for three years after that. CEA monitors. The Ministry of Power oversees.

The design was then revised inside a fixed allocation of Rs 3,760 crore. Capacity covered rose from 4,000 MWh to 13,200 MWh. The rate fell from Rs 96 lakh per MWh to Rs 46 lakh per MWh or 30 percent of capital cost, whichever is lower, because battery prices fell. In 2024-25 the first slice was not paid, because no project reached financial closure.

A policy review that celebrates the larger megawatt-hour figure and skips the nil expenditure is reviewing the revision, not the delivery. The design lesson already on the page is that a per-unit grant inside a capped budget will shrink when capital costs fall, and that a closure-linked first tranche will show a zero if closure slips.

## A production incentive is not a construction grant

MNRE's module PLI, outlay Rs 24,000 crore, pays selected manufacturers for five years after commissioning, on modules made and sold. Tranche I is Rs 4,500 crore through IREDA for 8,737 MW of fully integrated capacity. Tranche II is Rs 19,500 crore through SECI for 39,600 MW of fully or partially integrated capacity. Better module performance is incentivised. The policy intent on the page is an integrated domestic industry and less import dependence.

The test of that instrument is commissioned factories and subsequent sales, not the date of the letter of award. SECI's allocation of about Rs 13,937.535 crore to the eleven tranche-II firms is still a post-commissioning schedule. Reporting the outlay as support already received would mis-state the instrument.

## A farm tariff incentive can be passed through

PM-KUSUM, in PIB release 2042069 of 6 August 2024, uses yet another design. For decentralised plants up to 2 MW, the distribution company buys at a pre-fixed levelised tariff. The centre pays the company Rs 0.40 per unit or Rs 6.6 lakh per MW, whichever is less, for five years, and the company may pass that incentive to the plant owner to obtain a lower tariff. Pump solarisation uses a percentage of cost, 30 or 50 percent, not this per-unit incentive.

A team designing the next farm solar rule can see the choice already made: a capped per-unit or per-megawatt incentive to the utility, optional pass-through, and a higher capital share in specified regions. The reply's outcome count is 4,11,222 farmers as on 30 June 2024. That is the delivery number to put beside the rule, not a substitute for it.

## A usable policy memo

Four rows are enough. Obligation and renewable-charging condition. Grant rate, budget cap, and closure status. Production incentive and the five-year sales window. Farm tariff incentive and regional capital share. Each row names the institution and the date of the note.
