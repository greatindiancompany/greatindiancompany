A scenario map that invents probabilities is fan fiction. The risks below are the ones the government’s own papers already force on a reader. Each one can go well or badly without a new crisis.

## The measurement risk

India is carrying two cost sentences. On [17 September 2022](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pm-launches-national-logistics-policy/) the Prime Minister said the aim was to move from 13–14 percent logistics cost to single digits. On 11 August 2026 the [starred reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS327_tYLfn2.pdf) said a 2025 DPIIT–NCAER assessment estimates the cost at about 7.9 percent of GDP, comparable to advanced economies.

Scenario one: the methods match, and the country has a single-digit ratio to defend. Firms that still price “India logistics is structurally 14 percent” into export quotes are using a retired speech. Scenario two: the methods do not match, and 7.9 percent becomes a controversy the next time a shipper’s own cost sheet looks nothing like it. Either scenario is a communications and contracting risk. Neither is resolved by repeating the more convenient number. The reply did not publish the worksheet. Until it does, a board paper should show both sentences and the dates.

The rank has a quieter version of the same risk. The reply cites 38th on the Logistics Performance Index, six places higher. The 2022 cabinet aim was the top 25 by 2030. A firm that tells an overseas buyer “India is already at global benchmark” is ahead of the rank the ministry cited.

## The execution risk

The [written reply of 11 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297818) is blunt: Gati Shakti sets no spending target of its own. Projects are approved under ministry and state budgets. Inside the planning group’s book, 396 projects had been evaluated, 256 sanctioned, and 198 put under implementation. Fifty-eight sanctioned projects were not yet under implementation.

The roads ministry row is the concentrated version: 108 sanctioned, 53 under implementation, against 171 evaluated and an estimated cost of ₹9,01,668 crore. Scenario one: those sanctioned-but-idle roads projects move into implementation and the highway book [Bharatmala](https://morth.nic.in/en/bharatmala) is meant to deliver actually thickens. Scenario two: they stay sanctioned, and every park, warehouse, and factory that assumed the link keeps its old route. Rail, in the same annex, shows 102 sanctioned and 102 under implementation. A scenario that treats “infrastructure” as one risk is averaging away the mode that is stuck.

Port risk sits in a different book. Sagarmala is the [ports ministry programme](https://shipmin.gov.in/en/division/sagarmala). The August replies praise it in the same breath as highways and dedicated freight corridors. They do not give it a clean project count in the annex. A port-dependent investment that cannot name its sanctioning order is running a documentation risk, which often arrives later as a time risk.

## The visibility risk

The Logistics Data Bank, as described in the starred reply, tracks export-import containers across ports, container freight stations, and inland container depots. That is a strong trail for a box that has touched the trade system. It is silent on the domestic truck that never enters it.

Scenario one: ULIP’s APIs, which the reply says are operational, extend usable visibility to the domestic leg for firms that complete the access process, and the export trail and the domestic trail meet. Scenario two: export boxes are transparent and the factory-to-port truck remains a phone call. Most Indian freight, by the structure of that design, is closer to scenario two until a shipper proves otherwise for a lane.

State paper is a related visibility gap. Twenty-nine states and Union Territories have logistics policies; 32 have granted industry status. A policy can exist and the approach road can still be absent. LEADS is the assessment frame the reply names. An old rank used as a current one is its own operational risk.

None of these scenarios needs a shock. They need a reader who keeps the August columns separate: cost speech versus cost assessment, sanctioned versus under implementation, export container versus domestic truck.
