# Market signals that show whether India's manufacturing trade is diversifying

A diversification signal is a number that can change a sourcing decision. An outlay, a vision statement, and a consultant chart usually cannot. India does publish a few figures that qualify, if you keep the unit and the date attached.

## A signal you can put next to a buyer's scorecard

On 13 May 2026 the Commerce and Industry Minister said exports reached USD 863 billion in the financial year ending 31 March 2026, with services exports growing about 8.5 to 9 percent and overall exports growing nearly 5 percent over the previous year. He called USD 1 trillion an aspirational target for the then-current year. That is a scale signal: India is a large export platform, and the government is aiming higher. It is not a signal that any one component moved from one country to an Indian plant.

The same remarks are a preference signal only at the portfolio level. The minister said nine agreements over three and a half years covered 38 economies, four of them already operational and five expected within 12 months, and that together with earlier agreements with Japan, Korea, and ASEAN the set covered more than two-thirds of global trade and economies. Before a buyer treats a line as duty-preferred, the operational list has to be checked agreement by agreement. The India-UAE CEPA is one that Commerce has already dated: in force from 1 May 2022, with the UAE removing duties on 97.4 percent of tariff lines corresponding to 99 percent of imports from India.

Merchandise detail lives on the Department of Commerce databank, tradestat.commerce.gov.in. A rising bilateral total can be oil, gold, or a single commodity. Diversification of a supply chain shows up as new tariff lines and a certificate-of-origin trail, not as the headline total alone.

## A cost signal, with a warning on the old number

The signal buyers actually feel is landed cost. On 27 November 2025, PIB reported the DPIIT-NCAER assessment for 2023-24: logistics cost at 7.97 percent of GDP, 9.09 percent of non-services output, and Rs 24.01 lakh crore. The note says the familiar 13 to 14 percent of GDP was an overestimate from partial data. Use 7.97 percent as the official level for that year. Do not describe it as a proven drop from 14 percent, because the older figure was not this study's prior reading.

Two details in that note matter more than the headline for a plant. Smaller firms face higher logistics costs than the aggregate. And for journeys around 600 kilometres, the first and last 50 kilometres move the total. A supplier who is cheap at the factory gate and expensive on the last mile is not a diversified source yet.

## What looks like a signal and is not

The PLI outlay of Rs 1.97 lakh crore, stated on 2 August 2023, is an incentive envelope across 14 sectors. It does not record machines installed or units shipped. The count of 733 selected applications, including 176 MSMEs, is a selection stock at the date of that Lok Sabha reply. A later PIB note may refresh it. Until you have that later note, do not annualize the 2023 count into a 2026 run-rate.

FDI policy is a rule, not a flow. Manufacturing on the automatic route tells you the door is open for eligible investors. It does not tell you how much manufacturing FDI arrived this month. DPIIT is the nodal department for the policy and for the data it compiles from bank remittances. Read the policy circular for permission, and the department's data release for the flow. Do not swap them.

## A small dashboard

For a quarterly operating review, four lines are enough: the latest Commerce export print with its year, one FTA you actually use and its entry-into-force date, the logistics-cost level for the year the study covers, and whether your product is inside the notified PLI list.
