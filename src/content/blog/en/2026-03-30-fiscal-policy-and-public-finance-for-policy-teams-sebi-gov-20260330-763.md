Policy work on India’s public finances goes bad in a familiar order. A speech line is treated as an outcome. A monthly deficit is treated as the year. The Centre and the states are added into a number neither document publishes. The remedy is a reading order, and a refusal to compute what the papers refuse to compute.

## Read in this order

**The FRBM statement before the speech.** The statement laid before Parliament with the 2026-27 Budget contains the definitions. Fiscal deficit, revenue deficit, primary deficit, and Central debt are specified. Debt includes public-account liabilities and NSSF special securities of the states. Those securities are 0.5 percent of GDP in the 2026-27 estimates. Debt excluding them is 55.1 percent, not the headline 55.6 percent. A note that quotes one and then the other without the exclusion is not a revision. It is two definitions.

The statement also contains the deviation. The Act’s 3 percent deficit rule and the 40 percent Central debt endeavour are missed, and section 7(3)(b) requires the Minister to say why. The 2026-27 explanation cites the global setting and restates a declining debt path toward 50±1 percent by 2030-31, with the year’s deficit at 4.3 percent of GDP after 4.4 percent. Rolling targets for the next two years are withheld. Record the withhold. Do not fill it.

**The speech for the sentences Parliament was asked to hear.** The commitment to be under 4.5 percent by 2025-26 is reported as met. Net market borrowings of dated securities are given as ₹11.7 lakh crore and gross as ₹17.2 lakh crore, matching the statement’s ₹11.73 lakh crore and ₹17.20 lakh crore. Use the statement when you need a decimal. Use the speech when you need the political commitment in the Minister’s words.

**The Key Features booklet for composition, not for new policy.** Its rupee graphic is the cleanest picture of incidence: borrowings 24 percent of receipts, income tax 21, corporation tax 18, GST and other taxes 15, and states’ tax share 22 percent of expenditure, interest 20 percent. Composition is not a marginal change. A policy note about “where the money goes” should start there and then open the relevant demand for grants.

**The Controller General of Accounts for the year in progress.** The June 2026 print is unaudited and provisional. It says the fiscal deficit to date is not the annual deficit. June’s fiscal deficit was 18.2 percent of the estimate and the revenue deficit 0.4 percent. Quote both, plus the warning, or do not quote the month.

**The GST monthly report for indirect taxes.** Gross, refunds, and net are different facts. August 2026 gross GST was ₹1,99,853 crore and net was ₹1,68,057 crore, provisional. The Budget’s CGST estimate of ₹10.19 lakh crore is only the Centre’s GST. Do not compare a gross all-India month with that estimate as if they were the same series.

**The Reserve Bank’s state study for the other half of the federation.** The January 2026 edition puts the consolidated state gross fiscal deficit at 3.3 percent of GDP in 2024-25 and in 2025-26 budgets. Sixteen states budgeted above 3 percent of their own product. The study’s theme is demographic transition. It is not a substitute for a state’s finance accounts.

## The calculation to refuse

Do not add 4.3 and 3.3 and call the sum general government. The Centre’s 4.3 percent is for 2026-27. The states’ 3.3 percent in that study is for 2024-25 and for 2025-26 budgets. The years do not match. Even in a matched year, tax devolution of ₹15.26 lakh crore and Finance Commission grants of ₹1.29 lakh crore are recorded on both sides. Grants for capital assets are revenue at the Centre and may be capital at the state. Adding deficits double-counts the transfer and still misses local bodies.

Do not treat 41 percent devolution as a new decision. The statement says the Sixteenth Finance Commission recommended retaining 41 percent, and the government accepted it. A brief that calls this a policy shift is wrong about the direction. The shift worth writing is the debt anchor, the withheld rolling targets, and the still-unchanged revenue deficit of 1.5 percent.

Do not import a securities-market circular into this file. Borrowing strategy, yields, and switches are in the fiscal statement: weighted average yield about 6.65 percent on 2025-26 primary issuance through 27 January 2026, switches of about ₹1.64 lakh crore, buybacks of ₹86,775 crore. That is debt management. It is already in the Budget division’s document.

## A short internal check before a note goes out

Name the document, the vintage (Budget estimate, revised estimate, provisional actual), and the unit (percent of GDP or ₹ crore). Say whether the figure is the Centre, a state, or gross GST. If the sentence needs a peer-country deficit, stop: this Budget set does not contain one. If the sentence needs a job count from the capital line, stop: the capital line is ₹12.22 lakh crore, and no employment coefficient is printed next to it.
