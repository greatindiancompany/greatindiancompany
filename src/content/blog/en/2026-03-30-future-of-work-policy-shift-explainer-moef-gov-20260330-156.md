# What changed when India's four labour codes took effect

On 21 November 2025 the Ministry of Labour and Employment said the four Labour Codes were being made effective that day. The Prime Minister welcomed the step the same day and described it as a reform that empowers workers, simplifies compliance, and supports ease of doing business. The Economic Survey 2025–26 later recorded the notification in its opening narrative and said rules were expected over the following months.

The shift is real. It is also easy to overstate. The codes change the legal frame. They do not, by themselves, deposit a benefit into every worker's account on the commencement date.

## The frame that was replaced

The Ministry's account is that much of the old law dated from the 1930s to the 1950s and sat in 29 central Acts. Minimum wages applied to scheduled employments, so large groups of workers were outside that statutory right. Appointment letters were not mandatory. Social-security coverage was limited. Women's employment at night and in some occupations was restricted. ESIC coverage was tied to notified areas and, in general, to establishments with 10 or more employees. An employer dealt with multiple registrations, licences, and returns.

That description is the Ministry's before-and-after note of 21 November 2025. It is the right baseline for reading the codes. It is not a claim that every establishment in India was outside every old Act.

## What the new frame puts in the statute

Four codes now carry the field.

The Code on Wages, 2019 extends the statutory right to a minimum wage beyond the old schedule of employments. The Ministry also describes a national floor wage, so that no worker is paid below the minimum living standard the Centre sets, and it describes timely payment of wages as mandatory. For IT and IT-enabled services, the same note says salary must be released by the 7th of every month.

The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 and the social-security provisions, as the Ministry summarises them, make fixed-term employees eligible for the same leave, medical, and social-security benefits as permanent workers, at equal wages, with gratuity after one year of continuous service rather than five. The stated aim is direct hiring with less dependence on layered contracting.

The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020, again in the Ministry's account, requires appointment letters for all workers, allows women to work at night and in all types of work — including underground mining and heavy machinery — subject to consent and safety measures, and requires a free annual health check-up for workers above 40. ESIC coverage is described as pan-India: voluntary for establishments with fewer than 10 employees, and mandatory where even one employee is engaged in a hazardous process.

Compliance is supposed to collapse to a single registration, a single pan-India licence, and a single return, with an inspector-cum-facilitator role oriented to guidance as well as enforcement.

## The part that is specifically about future forms of work

The Code on Social Security, 2020 is the first central statute to define this work.

A gig worker is a person who performs work, or participates in a work arrangement, and earns from that activity outside a traditional employer-employee relationship. An aggregator is a digital intermediary or a marketplace that connects a buyer or user with a seller or service provider. Platform work is work organised through such an online platform.

Section 114 allows the Centre to frame schemes for gig and platform workers covering life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity benefits, old-age protection, creche, and other benefits the Centre determines. Funding can come from the Centre, the states, aggregators, beneficiaries, corporate social responsibility funds, or a combination.

For aggregators in the categories listed in the Seventh Schedule, section 114(4) sets the contribution that will fund part of this. The rate is notified by the Centre. It cannot be below 1 percent or above 2 percent of annual turnover. Turnover for this purpose excludes tax, levy, and cess paid to the Centre. The contribution also cannot exceed 5 percent of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers. The Seventh Schedule lists ride sharing, food and grocery delivery, logistics, e-marketplaces, professional services, healthcare, travel and hospitality, content and media, and any other goods and services platform.

Section 114(5) is the sentence that keeps this from being an automatic levy. The date on which aggregator contributions commence is itself notified by the Centre. Section 114(6) makes the National Social Security Board the board for gig and platform workers, with a modified composition while it does that work.

The Ministry's 21 November note adds that an Aadhaar-linked Universal Account Number is meant to make these benefits portable across states.

## What did not flip on with the commencement date

The same press note says the government will consult while it frames rules, regulations, and schemes, and that during the transition the relevant provisions of the existing Acts and their rules, notifications, and schemes continue. A reader who treats 21 November 2025 as the day every old form, threshold, and scheme died will get the operating position wrong.

Two further limits matter. The Ministry's comparison table says all workers, including gig and platform workers, will get social security, and in one line it says all workers will get provident fund, ESIC, insurance, and other benefits. The more precise ESIC rule in the same note is the one to use when designing a policy: pan-India scope, voluntary under 10 employees, mandatory for hazardous processes. Provident-fund membership still depends on the establishment being covered and on contributions actually being deposited. And the "more than 64 percent" social-security coverage the note cites for 2025, up from about 19 percent in 2015, is the Ministry's coverage statement. It is not a finding of the Periodic Labour Force Survey, and it is not the same thing as a gig-worker scheme that has started collecting the 1–2 percent.

The policy shift, stated tightly, is this. India now has one wage code, one industrial-relations code, one social-security code, and one safety code, in force from 21 November 2025, with gig and platform work named in statute. The wage right, the appointment letter, and the fixed-term gratuity rule are the changes the Ministry presents as immediate design. The gig contribution is a band and a start date still waiting on notification, and the old rules remain the fallback until the new ones replace them.
