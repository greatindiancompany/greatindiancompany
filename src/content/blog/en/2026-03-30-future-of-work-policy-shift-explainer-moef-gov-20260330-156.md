# Future of Work in India: The Four Labour Codes Explained

## Four statutes in place of twenty-nine

On 21 November 2025 the Ministry of Labour and Employment brought four codes into force and described them as a rationalisation of 29 central labour laws. The future-of-work rules that follow are these four texts, plus the rules of the appropriate government.

## Wages

The Code on Wages, 2019 is the wage statute. The ministry's implementation note says minimum wages, earlier limited to scheduled employments, are a statutory right for workers generally, with a national floor wage so that pay does not fall below the floor the Centre sets. The ministry's compliance handbook for establishments in the central sphere says a monthly wage is due before the seventh day of the following month. For a worker whose minimum wage is fixed under the code, overtime beyond normal hours is at least twice the ordinary rate. The handbook also bars wage discrimination by gender for the same or similar work.

## Industrial relations

The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 consolidates the law on trade unions, conditions of employment, and industrial disputes. The ministry's note describes two-member industrial tribunals and a route to the tribunal after conciliation. Dispute timing is part of how a workplace runs. It is separate from how many people the workplace employs.

## Social security, including platform work

The Code on Social Security, 2020 is the formalisation and gig-work statute. The ministry's 21 November 2025 note says gig work, platform work, and aggregators are defined here for the first time, and that aggregators contribute 1 to 2 percent of annual turnover, capped at 5 percent of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers. Fixed-term employees, the note says, receive benefits on par with permanent workers, and gratuity after one year of service rather than five.

A Lok Sabha reply on 20 July 2026 describes those gig and platform definitions as the basis for schemes on life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity benefits, and old-age protection. A definition lets a scheme be framed. The scheme notification is what makes a benefit operational.

## Safety and conditions

The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 carries workplace safety. The ministry's note provides for a National OSH Board, an inspector-cum-facilitator role aimed at guidance and compliance support, and safety committees in establishments with 500 or more workers. Women may work at night and across occupations with their consent and with required safety measures.

## The written job

The ministry's before-and-after note says appointment letters are required for workers. Its note on audio-visual and digital-media workers says the letter should state designation, wages, and social-security entitlement. That letter is the formal record of a job. It is the document a founder, a worker, and an inspector can hold. Headcount is a different document, and it belongs to the Periodic Labour Force Survey when a national picture is required.

## Rules still sit with the appropriate government

On the day the codes took effect, the ministry said existing Act provisions and rules would continue during the transition. The 20 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply then records Central Rules notified on 8 May 2026, and states that states and Union Territories frame their own rules because labour is concurrent. Reading one code without the rules of the appropriate government leaves the shift half finished.
