# Where a public rupee attaches in the agri chain

An investment in an Indian agri value chain meets public money in five different legal forms. Mixing them produces a project model that no guideline will pay. The forms are a subsidised loan, a capital grant, a production incentive, a matching equity grant to a farmer company, and a cash transfer to a landholding family.

## A subsidised loan, with a hard edge on what it will fund

The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund is the loan. The ministry's note of 9 August 2025 states a facility of Rs 1 lakh crore through lending institutions, an interest cap of 9 per cent, and a 3 per cent a year subvention for a maximum of seven years. The subvention applies up to a loan of Rs 2 crore. Above that, it is limited to the first Rs 2 crore. Credit guarantee for loans up to Rs 2 crore runs through CGTMSE, with the fee borne by the government, and, for FPOs, through NABSanrakshan Trustee Company Pvt. Ltd. as well. The scheme is operational from 2020-21 to 2032-33. The same note says loan disbursement was to finish by the end of 2025-26.

What the loan may build changed on 28 August 2024. Community farming assets became eligible. Primary and secondary processing became eligible when they are one integrated unit. Standalone secondary processing stayed ineligible. PM-KUSUM Component-A solar projects became eligible for FPOs, cooperatives, and panchayats.

The stock of lending, from a Lok Sabha reply, was Rs 80,224.15 crore sanctioned for 1,50,431 projects as on 26 January 2026, mobilising investment of Rs 1,27,508 crore. A later reply, as on 15 July 2026, describes cumulative capital investment leveraged of Rs 1,61,537.22 crore, including Rs 50,332.66 crore for farm-gate storage and logistics. Use the label the reply uses. Sanctioned loan and leveraged project cost are not the same cell.

As of 30 June 2025 the ministry counted 2,454 cold-storage projects under AIF, with Rs 8,258 crore sanctioned, inside a wider mix that also included 30,202 custom hiring centres, 22,827 processing units, 15,982 warehouses, and 3,703 sorting and grading units. The cold store is one asset class inside the loan book, and on that date it was not the largest by count.

## A capital grant from the food-processing ministry

The integrated cold chain component of Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana is a grant-in-aid, not the AIF loan. A Lok Sabha reply of 6 August 2026 states the rate: 35 per cent of eligible project cost in general areas, and 50 per cent in difficult areas, including the North-Eastern states, and for projects promoted by Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe entrepreneurs, FPOs, and self-help groups. The cap is Rs 10 crore for cold chain, for agro-processing clusters, and for standalone Operation Greens projects. Integrated Operation Greens projects can go to Rs 15 crore. The August 2025 agriculture note adds an older design point: the integrated cold chain scheme supports integrated projects, including irradiation, and excludes standalone cold stores.

As of 30 June 2026, 409 cold-chain projects had been approved, with grant-in-aid of Rs 3,089.08 crore sanctioned and Rs 2,540.89 crore released. Of those, 311 were complete and had created 9.236 lakh metric tonnes of cold storage. The national cold-storage stock cited in that reply, from the Department of Agriculture as on 31 January 2026, was 402.87 lakh metric tonnes. The grant programme's completed tonnes are a thin slice of the national stock.

PMKSY as a whole had an outlay of Rs 6,520 crore for the period up to 2025-26, with 1,607 projects approved till 31 December 2025.

## An incentive on output, after private investment

The production-linked incentive for food processing runs from 2021-22 to 2026-27 with an outlay of Rs 10,900 crore. A Rajya Sabha reply on 27 March 2026 said 168 applicants had been approved as on 31 December 2025. They had invested about Rs 9,207 crore. The scheme had created around 35 lakh metric tonnes a year of processing and preservation capacity. Incentive paid out was Rs 2,714.79 crore. Sixty-nine applicants were MSMEs, and 40 contract units of the main applicants were also MSMEs.

The incentive is admissible when the whole chain of manufacturing, including primary processing, happens in India. Branding and marketing abroad are a separate incentive use. The Rs 9,207 crore is the applicants' investment. The Rs 2,714.79 crore is the public cheque against that investment. Quoting either figure as the size of the scheme confuses the outlay, the capex, and the payout.

## Equity for a farmer company, and cash for a family

An FPO is not a startup round. The 2020 scheme, outlay Rs 6,865 crore, offers a matching equity grant of up to Rs 2,000 per member and Rs 15 lakh per FPO, plus a guarantee of up to Rs 2 crore of project loan, plus up to Rs 18 lakh over three years for management. As on 31 December 2025, Rs 430.77 crore of equity grant had gone to 6,557 FPOs and Rs 662.71 crore of guarantee cover to 2,671 FPOs. That is public equity and a guarantee. It is not a valuation.

PM-KISAN is further away from a project. Rs 6,000 a year to a landholding family, continued to 2030-31 with an outlay of Rs 3.15 lakh crore, can fund seed and fertiliser. The Cabinet note says so. It does not capitalise a packhouse.
