# Where India's manufacturing push can stall

This is not a set of probabilities. The official papers do not assign a chance to any scenario. They do show where a plan can stall, because the design and the reported stocks already contain the friction.

## Most of the incentive outlay was still unpaid

The 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf) states an approved outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore and cumulative disbursement of ₹35,354 crore as on 31 March 2026. A company that models the outlay as cash it will receive is ahead of the record. Claims follow the scheme's production rules. The reply does not say the undisbursed balance has been cancelled. It also does not say the balance will be paid on a timetable. Both a "the money is gone" story and a "the money is booked" story go past the text.

DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 stated the outlay as Rs. 1.97 lakh crore. Two official outlays for the same family of schemes are themselves an operating risk for anyone who hard-codes a single ceiling. Use the 28 July figure when you mean the later parliamentary statement, and cite the annual report figure only as the report's figure.

## The jobs series moved down

As on 31 December 2025, a Rajya Sabha reply reported employment of more than 14.39 lakh, direct and indirect ([PIB PRID 2246089](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246089)). As on 31 March 2026, the Lok Sabha reply reported over 14.15 lakh. Investment and exports in the same pair of replies moved up. Employment did not. A scenario in which every PLI indicator rises together is already inconsistent with these two dates. The sector annexure sums to 8,49,069 and carries a footnote for 5.66 lakh indirect jobs in only three sectors ([PIB PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008)). Misreading the column as the 14.15 lakh headline will overstate sector hiring, especially in food products, which dominates that column.

## Delivery sits in many ministries, and on state land

DPIIT coordinates and monitors. Fourteen administrative departments implement. A stall in solar-module claims is an MNRE scheme problem. A stall in white goods is a DPIIT scheme problem. A stall in automobiles or ACC batteries is a Heavy Industries problem. The Empowered Group of Secretaries can modify guidelines. That is a way through a blockage, and it is also a way for eligibility to change after an investment memo was written.

Corridor risk is physical. The 30 August 2024 note, [PIB PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136), is an approval of nodes at an estimated ₹28,602 crore, with state joint ventures. The note's own list of places is shorter than its count of twelve. Plug-and-play language describes the aim. It does not describe a surveyed, allotted plot. A factory schedule that starts on Cabinet approval, rather than on possession and utilities, is the stall.

## Concentration is a scenario by itself

Three annexure lines — solar modules ₹64,873 crore, pharmaceutical drugs ₹45,158 crore, and automobiles and auto components ₹44,326 crore — are this page's addition of ₹1,54,357 crore inside a ₹2,40,138 crore total. If those three schemes slow, the national PLI investment stock slows with them, even if drones, IT hardware, or medical devices do well. The product claims in the July reply (mobile phones, 191 bulk drugs made for the first time, compressor capacity) are the places a later reply could revise. Until it does, they remain claims in a ministerial answer, not an independent census.

FDI from land-border countries remains on the government route, with a 2026 description of faster handling for a short list of manufacturing activities and a requirement of Indian control. A joint venture that ignores that paragraph can stall in approval even when the PLI sector is open on paper. Press Note 3 of 2026 changes the e-commerce inventory rule for exports of domestic goods. It does not insure export demand.
