# Green Hydrogen in India: What Parliament and MNRE Put on Record in July and August 2026

The quarter that includes 24 September 2026 runs from July. The texts that actually add facts, among the pages read for this brief, land in late July and the first half of August. Older Mission aims, including 5 million tonnes a year by 2030, are repeated often. They are not a quarterly result.

## A contract model for the electrolyser

On 24 July 2026, the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy listed a new item on its [hydrogen schemes and guidelines](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/) page: publication of the model service agreement for “Electrolyser as a Service.” The page dates the document. It does not, by itself, name a company that has signed under that model. The change that matters is procedural. A standard service contract now sits beside the older SIGHT incentive guidelines, so a buyer can look at paying for electrolyser performance as a service as well as looking at a manufacturing award.

## Twelve vehicle pilots, with a phase split

On 29 July 2026, Minister of State Shripad Yesso Naik answered Lok Sabha question 1648 on hydrogen-based vehicles. The answer says 12 pilot projects have been awarded for 70 hydrogen-powered vehicles: 27 buses and 43 trucks, including fuel-cell and hydrogen internal-combustion vehicles, with 16 hydrogen refuelling stations across 21 routes. Financial assistance allocated is about ₹410.77 crore, of which ₹41.582 crore had been released. The [annex](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1648_XD1qA0.pdf) marks the sums as sanctioned viability-gap funding at 90 percent.

The annex splits the money. Phase I totals ₹207.91 crore for 10 buses and 27 trucks. Phase II totals ₹202.86 crore for 17 buses and 16 trucks. That split explains an older sentence. On 17 June 2026, Union Minister Pralhad Joshi was still describing mobility support as nearly ₹208 crore for 37 vehicles and 9 refuelling stations. Thirty-seven vehicles is the Phase I fleet in the July annex. The 29 July answer is the later text, and it is the one that puts Phase II on the parliamentary record. The answer also says executing agencies must file performance reports on efficiency, fuel consumption, safety, and utilisation, so the pilots can be judged on techno-economic viability.

## Hubs, with project facts attached

On 5 August 2026, the same minister answered Lok Sabha question 2987. The named hubs are Deendayal Port at Kandla, V.O. Chidambaranar Port at Tuticorin, Paradip Port, and an endorsed NTPC proposal at Pudimadaka in Andhra Pradesh. The [annex](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2987_27NWXF.pdf) is more specific than the October 2025 recognition note. It records a 4.0 million-tonne-a-year hydrogen and ammonia handling jetty at Paradip, estimated at ₹797.17 crore, approved for public-private partnership. At Kandla it records 3,400 acres on a 30-year lease, a completed 1 MW demonstration plant at ₹14.1 crore, and a 5 MW plant, scalable to 10 MW, contracted at ₹62.80 crore with completion dated 31 December 2026.

## Refinery locations under Mode-2B

On 11 August 2026, the Press Information Bureau reported a Rajya Sabha reply on SIGHT Mode-2B. Awarded green hydrogen capacity for refineries is 30 kilo-tonnes a year: Indian Oil at Panipat 10, Bharat Petroleum at Bina 5, Hindustan Petroleum at Vizag 5, and Numaligarh 10. The projects are build-own-operate arrangements with private developers. On 17 June the minister had already said contracts covered 30,000 tonnes a year for those four companies. The August reply is the document that names the refinery locations. The [release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297571) is the public copy of that reply.

## What this quarter does not settle

This brief does not summarise September notices whose text was not read. It also does not treat the Mission outlay of ₹19,744 crore, stated on the [MNRE mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/), as money spent in July. Programme design and a quarterly parliamentary answer are different layers.
