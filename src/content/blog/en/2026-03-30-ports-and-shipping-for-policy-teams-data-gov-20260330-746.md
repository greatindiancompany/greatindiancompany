# Ports and shipping for policy teams: who decides what

Policy notes on Indian ports go wrong when they assign a decision to "the ministry" and stop. The Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways is several desks, two companies, an association, an attached office, a university, and a new tribunal. State governments hold the non-major ports. A note that names the desk can be checked. A note that says "Sagarmala will do it" cannot.

## The ministry's own split

The Ports Wing develops, regulates, and modernises major ports. It holds the Major Port Authorities Act, 2021, in force since 3 November 2021, and the rules under it. It is the page that says 14 major ports exist, 12 operate, and about 200 non-major ports sit with state maritime boards, of which around 65 handle cargo. It monitors turnaround, berth output, detention, and dwell, and it owns the Sagar Aankalan ranking frame and the Harit Sagar green-port guidelines of May 2023. Green tugs, half by 2030 and all by 2047, are a Ports Wing target. If your note is about a major-port tariff or a concession, this is the wing, and the Adjudicatory Board in Mumbai is the dispute forum since 13 August 2025.

The shipping division writes policy for shipping, shipbuilding, ship repair, and ship recycling. The Directorate General of Shipping, attached since September 1949 and based in Mumbai, handles merchant-ship executive work. Indian Maritime University in Chennai is the central university for maritime education. The Shipping Corporation group, including its inland subsidiary, its GIFT City company, and the land-and-assets company, is supervised from this side of the ministry. Demand aggregation for public-sector ships runs through a Shipping Technical Advisory Committee. A note about seafarer certification, a yard subsidy, or Alang belongs here, not on the Ports Wing page.

The Sagarmala division is the programme: five pillars since the Cabinet approval of 25 March 2015, a perspective plan from 14 April 2016, and two companies. Sagarmala Finance Corporation, registered with the Reserve Bank as a maritime non-banking financial company on 19 June 2025, finances. Indian Port Rail and Ropeway Corporation, from 2015, builds last-mile rail and related links. A note that gives either company the powers of a port authority is wrong. They do not set tariffs and they do not license state jetties.

## The association, the bureau, and the states

The Indian Ports Association is not the ministry. It is a society from 1966, the apex body of the major ports, headquartered in New Delhi. It publishes traffic notes and daily vessel profiles, and it carries shared systems: the Port Community System, the National Logistics Portal Marine, the enterprise business system, cruise promotion, and the green tug programme. Quote it for major-port operations and shared systems. Do not quote it as the regulator. Regulation of the major ports sits in the Act and the port boards. Regulation of non-major ports sits with the state.

The Press Information Bureau is how a Lok Sabha reply becomes a public page. The 24 July 2026 reply is the current public account of the ₹69,725 crore shipbuilding package, the fund manager appointed on 26 May 2026, and in-principle clusters in Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, and Tamil Nadu. Cite the reply for those facts. Cite the December 2025 scheme note for the 15 to 25 percent rate, the scrap credit, and the 31 March 2036 end date. Do not merge them into an undated "government announced."

State maritime boards are the missing institution in most central notes. A coastal policy that only instructs major ports leaves the cargo that moves through state jetties untouched. The Ports Wing lists an Indian Ports Act, 2025. Until a note is based on that text, it should say the ministry page still places non-major ports under the states.

Skill policy has its own small map. The July 2026 reply records shipyard tie-ups with industrial training institutes, a section 8 technology centre, and a cooperation plan with the Korea International Cooperation Agency. That is yard-trade training. It is not a substitute for the maritime university's seafaring mandate.
