# Where Coverage Still Stalls Before a Social Outcome

India’s social programmes are no longer mainly a story of empty rolls. The stall is between a registered beneficiary, or a school that has a teacher, and the outcome the programme exists to change. The official numbers below are the stall points. They are not a ranking of state capacity.

## Nutrition: monitored children, unchanged weight

A Press Information Bureau note recording Poshan Tracker status as of May 2026 says the platform held a live nutrition record for over 7.7 crore children and growth monitoring for over 6.3 crore children aged 0-5, about 94% of registered beneficiaries in that age band. By 31 July 2026 the same ministry’s Poshan Maah note put the wider universe at almost 8.94 crore beneficiaries, nearly 13.99 lakh centres, and 13.30 lakh workers.

NFHS-6, the household outcome survey for 2023-24, still finds stunting at 29.3% (from 35.5%), wasting at 19.0% (from 19.3%), underweight at 31.8% (from 32.1%), and an adequate diet at 6-23 months at 15.3% (from 11.0%). Growth monitoring can be nearly universal among registered children and the weight-for-age rate can barely move. The bottleneck is not “we do not know who the child is.” It is what happens after the measurement: the meal, the take-home ration, the illness, the household diet.

Two design facts keep that bottleneck in place. The Ministry of Women and Child Development says three of the six Anganwadi services — immunisation, health check-up, and referral — are delivered through the National Health Mission, not by the Anganwadi department alone. A nutrition review that never sits with the health session will miss half the package. And the Saksham upgrade is 2 lakh centres out of a stock near 13.99 lakh. Most centres are not in the capital-upgrade slice.

Facial recognition for take-home ration and a master-trainer cascade (3,901 state-level master trainers as of 18 March 2026 under Poshan Bhi Padhai Bhi) are execution tools. A trainer count is not proof that every worker can complete a facial-recognition issue on a given day.

## Maternal courses that start and do not finish

Institutional births are 90.6%. Any antenatal care is 95.9%. Four or more visits are 65.2%. Iron and folic acid for 100 days or more is 54.9%, and for 180 days or more is 37.8%, up from 26.0%. The stall is the long course, after the woman is already in contact with the system. Births in a public facility fell from 61.9% to 58.6% while institutional births rose, so the incremental delivery is not an automatic public-facility story. Full vaccination on cards at 12-23 months is 87.1%, not the “any vaccine” rate which the ministry says stays above 96%. The second dose of measles-containing vaccine is 71.8%, from 58.6%. The bottleneck has moved from first contact to completion.

## Schools: the ratio is not the remaining constraint

UDISE+ 2025-26 reports pupil-teacher ratios of 10, 12, 17, and 21, inside the National Education Policy reference of 30:1, and 1,02,73,020 teachers. Secondary retention is 51.9%. Secondary dropout is 7.0%. The middle dropout series moves from 3.5% to 3.6%. Single-teacher schools still number 1,00,843. Computers are in 69.9% of schools. The average school is not short of teachers on the published ratio. The child who does not reach or stay at secondary, and the school that still has one teacher, are the execution residuals. This census does not say whether those children can read.

## Wages: the rule is ahead of the proof

The new rural wage Act writes a fifteen-day payment duty, delay compensation, and an exception when face authentication fails. The first-month person-day figure told to the Lok Sabha is an output. It does not show how often the exception was used, or how often compensation was paid. That gap is the bottleneck to watch, because the statute has already defined the failure.
