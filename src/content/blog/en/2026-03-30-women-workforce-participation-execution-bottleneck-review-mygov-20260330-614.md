# Care time, creche approvals and the step from training into work

The binding constraint on women’s paid work, in the surveys that measure it, is time spent on unpaid domestic and care work, followed by a thin public creche build-out and a weak step from vocational training into a job. It is not a shortage of a participation headline. The headline already exists. Usual-status participation for women aged 15 and above was 40.0 percent in calendar 2025.

## The time account

MoSPI’s Time Use Survey for January–December 2024 is the second all-India round, after 2019. Among persons aged 6 and above, women who participated in unpaid domestic services for household members spent 289 minutes a day on them. Men who participated spent 88 minutes. The participation rates were 81.5 percent for women and 27.1 percent for men. On unpaid caregiving for household members, women participants spent 137 minutes and men 75, with participation of 34.0 percent and 17.9 percent.

For women aged 15–59, time in unpaid domestic services among participants was 305 minutes in 2024, against 315 minutes in 2019. PIB describes that 10-minute decline as a shift from unpaid toward paid activity. The level remains about five hours a day for the women who do this work. Participation in employment and related activities, at age 6 and above, was 20.7 percent for women and 60.8 percent for men. That employment participation rate is not the PLFS labour force rate. The age base includes children. It still shows how unevenly paid work appears in the day.

PLFS asks a different question of people outside the labour force. In 2025, 44.4 percent of women in that group named child care or personal commitments in home-making as the main reason. Among men outside the labour force, 69.8 percent named continuing studies. The two surveys agree on the shape of the constraint. They do not agree on a single minute count, because one is a time diary and the other is a reason code.

Hours in paid work are shorter for women even after they are employed. Urban self-employed men worked about 17.5 hours more per week than urban self-employed women. The rural gap was about 12.3 hours. Regular wage work showed a gap of about 7.9 hours a week. The bottleneck is not only entry. It is the length of the paid day once someone is counted as a worker.

## The creche system that is supposed to carry part of this time

The establishment rule covers creches at 50 or more employees. Most women workers are outside that rule: 64.2 percent of women workers were self-employed in 2025, and only 9.3 percent of rural women workers were in regular wage jobs. Mission Shakti’s Palna component is the public creche for that wider group. PIB says it serves children aged 6 months to 6 years and is open to mothers irrespective of employment status. The funding pattern is 60:40, 90:10 for the North East and special category States, and full central funding for Union Territories without a legislature.

The same note says the Ministry was targeting 17,000 new Anganwadi-cum-creches in 2024–25. As of March 2025, 11,395 had been approved across 34 States and Union Territories, on proposals received from them. Approval is not the same as a centre operating every working day. The gap between 11,395 and 17,000, dated March 2025, is the execution number a later MoWCD statement has to update. Until that update, the bottleneck is visible as an approval shortfall against the Ministry’s own target, on top of a time-use gap the creche is meant to relieve.

Sakhi Niwas, also under Samarthya, provides accommodation for women in the workforce or seeking to join it, with day care for residents’ children. It is a hostel instrument, not a substitute for a neighbourhood creche.

## Training does not clear the same bottleneck

Among persons aged 15–59 who had received or were receiving formal vocational or technical training, 51.4 percent of women were in the workforce, against 83.3 percent of men. Only 4.2 percent of persons in that age group reported such training at all. A woman can complete a course and still be in the 44.4 percent who cite home and child care, or in the half of trained women who are not in the workforce. National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme data show women’s share of apprentices rising to 22.84 percent in 2024–25. That is progress on intake. Intake is upstream of the 51.4 percent.

Urban young women face a further labour-market bottleneck once they are in the force. Usual-status unemployment for urban women aged 15–29 was 18.9 percent in 2025, against 8.6 percent for rural women of the same age. The all-ages female unemployment rate of 3.1 percent hides that cell.

## What “unblocking” means in the published units

Three units can move independently. Minutes in the next Time Use Survey, against 289 and 305. Palna approvals and, when MoWCD publishes them, operational creches, against 11,395 and the 17,000 target. The share of trained women who are in the workforce, against 51.4 percent. A rise in the usual-status participation rate without movement in those three is a different event, and on the 2025 report that rate was described as stable.
