## Three facts that get collapsed into one sentence

Public pages on India’s healthcare access publish large numbers. They answer different questions. An entitlement is who may receive a benefit. A facility count is how many centres states have reported. A use figure is a test, a session, or a paid admission. This snapshot keeps those apart and cites the page each figure comes from. It does not add a live PM-JAY claims total, because copying a dashboard without a timestamp would be stale the day it is typed.

## Entitlement, and the two household counts

The National Health Authority’s current PM-JAY about-page says the scheme aims to cover over 12 crore poor and vulnerable families, about 55 crore people, the bottom 40 percent. Inclusion is SECC 2011 deprivation and occupational criteria, plus families covered by the Rashtriya Swasthya Bima Yojana who are missing from SECC 2011. The benefit is ₹5 lakh per family per year for listed secondary and tertiary hospitalization.

The Authority’s revised empanelment guideline of November 2021 says PM-JAY covers the bottom 40 percent, “about 10.74 crore households,” up to ₹5 lakh a year. A December 2021 grievance guideline uses “over 10.74 crore” entitled families. The about-page and those 2021 documents are both official. They do not state the same household count, and they have not been edited into one figure. Quote the document you opened. Do not blend 10.74 and 12 into a third figure.

A further entitlement sits beside that floor. The Cabinet decision of 12 September 2024, on the Authority’s homepage, covers every senior citizen aged 70 and above irrespective of income. The FAQ says the ₹5 lakh for that group is a family amount, shared by the members who are 70 or older. In a family that already has cover, the additional ₹5 lakh is for those older members only.

## Facility counts are state reports

The February 2018 announcement, repeated on the mandir site, was 1,50,000 Health and Wellness Centres. The first centre opened in Bijapur, Chhattisgarh, on 18 April 2018. The site says more than 17,000 were operationalized in the first year, against a 2018–19 target of 15,000.

The ministry’s written reply of 25 March 2025 says states had reported 1,76,573 Ayushman Arogya Mandirs operationalised till February 2025. Press Information Bureau releases then state 1,81,873 as on 30 November 2025 and 1,82,944 as on 31 December 2025. The series rises. Each point is a state-reported operational total on a date. It is not an audit that every centre had a mid-level provider, a drug stock, and a referral path on that date. PM-ABHIM’s separate approval of 10,609 building-less mandirs is a reminder that “operational” and “housed” can diverge.

## Activity counts are events

The same 25 March 2025 reply states 107.10 crore screenings for hypertension and 94.56 crore screenings for diabetes at the centres, and 5.06 crore wellness sessions including yoga as on 28 February 2025. A person screened twice is two screenings. A wellness session is a session. Neither number is a patient census, a control rate, or a hospitalization.

Paid use of the ₹5 lakh cover is claims and admissions in the Authority’s transaction system. That series is not reproduced here. Eligibility of 12 crore families does not imply 12 crore admissions.

## Background figures that carry a year

The PM-JAY about-page cites National Health Accounts 2015 for government health spending of about 1.2 percent of GDP, 21 percent of health spending from government revenue, and 62 percent out of pocket. Use them as 2015 figures. The page’s line that medical spending pushes nearly 6 crore Indians into poverty each year is the scheme’s stated rationale in that narrative. It is not labelled on the page as a 2015 accounts cell in the way the 62 percent figure is. Treat it as the programme’s own justification, not as a statistic this brief has remeasured.

The National Health Policy’s target of 2.5 percent of GDP by 2025 is a target. It is not a result sitting in this snapshot.
