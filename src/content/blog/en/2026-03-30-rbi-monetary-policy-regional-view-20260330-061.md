India has one policy repo rate. The Monetary Policy Committee set it at 5.25 per cent on 5 August 2026 for the whole banking system. There is no state repo rate, and the August resolution does not publish a state inflation target. The regional content of this policy is in where the shock lands: rainfall, rural prices, and rural demand.

## The price gap the Bulletin actually prints

The August 2026 Bulletin says headline CPI edged up in both urban and rural areas in July, and that most states and Union territories recorded inflation between 2 and 6 per cent. The footnote gives the aggregates. Urban inflation was 4.0 per cent in July and rural inflation was 4.8 per cent. In June those figures were 3.9 and 4.7 per cent. Across states and Union territories, inflation ranged from 1.8 per cent to 6.3 per cent.

That range matters because the tolerance band around the 4 per cent target runs from 2 to 6 per cent. The state span in July ran from just under the floor of the band to just over its ceiling, while the all-India print, 4.45 per cent in the Bulletin’s July reading, sat near the middle. A single national instrument is aimed at the national index. It cannot match a state at 1.8 per cent and a state at 6.3 per cent at the same time. The Bulletin does not name those states in the text that accompanies the chart, so this brief does not assign the extremes to a geography the table does not name.

Rural inflation above urban inflation lines up with the composition the resolution emphasises. The June rise in headline CPI, to 4.4 per cent in the resolution’s rounding, was led by food and fuel. Food has a heavier weight in the consumption of rural households. Fuel inflation in the CPI rose from 4.5 per cent in June to 4.6 per cent in July. Retail petrol and diesel prices in four metros were unchanged between July and 1–21 August, at ₹108.7 and ₹98.1 a litre, after the May increases the June resolution had already counted.

## Rainfall is the regional transmission channel

The Governor’s 5 August statement treats the south-west monsoon as a risk to agriculture and to rural demand, not as a national average that can be ignored once it is printed. Footnotes to that statement, both as on 3 August, put the cumulative rainfall shortfall at 11.9 per cent versus normal and at 12.0 per cent versus the long-period average. Fifteen of 36 meteorological subdivisions had deficient rainfall. The India Meteorological Department’s assessment, as cited there, was that El Niño conditions were likely to persist and strengthen, with a risk of below-normal rain in August and September.

Water storage tells the same uneven story. In 166 major reservoirs, all-India storage was 44.4 per cent of capacity on 30 July 2026, against 69.3 per cent a year earlier and a decadal average of 47.7 per cent. Stocks of grain are the buffer on the other side of that risk. On 16 July, rice stocks were 654.7 lakh tonnes, 4.8 times the buffer norm, and wheat stocks were 516.3 lakh tonnes, 1.9 times the norm. The resolution relies on those stocks, and on supply management, as the reason a weak monsoon need not become a lasting food-price spiral. It does not claim the rainfall itself has evened out.

## Demand has been split the same way

The high-frequency record in the Governor’s statement still shows rural goods demand holding up in the first quarter of 2026-27. Two-wheeler retail sales grew 15.1 per cent and tractor sales 21.4 per cent. The August Bulletin says July retail vehicle sales accelerated further, led by tractors and two-wheelers. That is the recent past.

The forward sentence in the resolution is more careful. Deficient and uneven rain under El Niño is a risk to the agricultural outlook and to rural demand. Urban demand, in the same paragraph, is supported by services momentum, the continuing effect of GST rationalisation, and broadly stable employment. Services PMI in the first quarter was 58.7, up from 58.0 in the previous quarter. Passenger-vehicle retail sales grew 21.0 per cent in the quarter. The national growth projection, 6.7 per cent for 2026-27, averages those two stories.

Credit does not repair the gap. Bank credit is a national aggregate in these tables. The Bulletin says agricultural credit accelerated and personal loans picked up, with housing and gold-jewellery loans supporting the personal-loan line. It does not publish a state-wise transmission of the 5.25 per cent repo rate. A district bank and a metro bank draw on the same policy corridor. What differs is the income shock if the local monsoon fails.

The October meeting, on 5 to 7 October 2026, will have August and September rainfall and another CPI print. Until then the regional reading of a neutral stance is simple. The committee has one rate, because the target is the all-India headline index. The risk it named is that food prices and rural incomes will not move with that average.
