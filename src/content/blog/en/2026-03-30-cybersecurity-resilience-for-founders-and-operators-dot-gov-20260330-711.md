Founders and operators in India run cybersecurity resilience as a weekly operating review. The review has assets, a clock, and open findings. A paragraph about the country's digital future does not belong in it. This page is the cadence. It is not an audit of your company.

## The week

Hold one meeting. Bring the documents, not a theme.

- **Assets in production.** Ten systems, each with an owner. If you are connecting telecom equipment, mark the ones that still need a trusted-product determination or a security certificate. Those gates are Department of Telecommunications business. Open them from [dot.gov.in](https://www.dot.gov.in/), then write the status of your product, not the status of the homepage.
- **The incident clock.** Who is on shift tonight, which direction sets the clock, and whether the last report met it. CERT-In's direction and a telecom incident rule are separate lines. If you have not opened the direction, the line stays blank.
- **Open findings.** Every licence or regulator audit finding that has no closure date. One name per finding. A finding with no name is the work of the week.
- **One retrieval.** Pull one log the direction would ask for. If the platform cannot produce it, that is the stall. Do not buy a second platform in the same week.

Nomura research pages were on an earlier draft. They are not an input to this meeting. The link is omitted.

## What you tell the board

Say which control ran, which one failed, and which name is missing. Do not brief a country rank, a market size, or a policy rumour. If a duty might have changed, send one person to apply the [quarter test](/blog/cybersecurity-resilience-what-changed-this-quarter-dot-gov-20260330-111) and come back with the four lines, or with nothing.
