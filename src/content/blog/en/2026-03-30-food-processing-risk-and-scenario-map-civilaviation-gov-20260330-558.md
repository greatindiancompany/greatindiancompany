# Risks that official documents already describe

A risk map for Indian food processing does not need a made-up downside case. The ministry's annual report, its expenditure statement, and its year-end review already describe where projects stall, where budgets shrink, and where a large number is an expectation rather than an asset.

## Perishables still meet an old capacity study

The cold-chain chapter of the Annual Report for 2024-25 says high losses in perishables come from the lack of an unbroken cold chain from farm to consumer. It then cites two official estimates that are old, and it says so. A committee chaired by Saumitra Chaudhuri, set up by the then Planning Commission, estimated in 2012 that the country needed 61 million tonnes of cold storage. The National Centre for Cold-chain Development's all-India assessment of August 2015 put existing pack-houses at about 250 against an approximate requirement of 70,000, reefer trucks under 10,000 against about 62,000, bulk and hub cold stores at 32 million tonnes against 35 million tonnes, and ripening chambers at about 800 against about 9,000. The ministry notes that this gap study left out milk, meat, marine products, and processed foods.

Those ratios are not a 2026 census. Using them as if they were current overstates ignorance of capacity built since 2015, including the ministry's own 394 cold-chain projects. Ignoring them hides the fact that the ministry still explains losses with a farm-to-consumer break. The honest use is: the official gap picture on pack-houses and reefers is a decade old and incomplete, while the ministry's own completed cold stores, as on 31 December 2024, were 8.46 lakh metric tonnes plus 1,477 reefer vehicles. Both sentences can be true.

NABCON's evaluation, as the Year End Review summarises it, found that where 70 percent of approved cold-chain projects were completed, waste fell by up to 70 percent in fisheries and up to 85 percent in dairy. That is an evaluation claim about completed projects. It is also a reminder that the projects not completed do not produce that result.

## Cancellation and delay are written into the scheme history

Cold chain has a long cancellation record in the same chapter. In the 11th Plan, 19 of the projects the ministry had sanctioned were cancelled, mainly for slow progress. Of a later batch of 66, 16 were cancelled or withdrawn. As on 31 December 2024, 108 of 394 projects were still under implementation. The rule for a late project is financial, not rhetorical: beyond 24 months in general areas or 30 months in difficult areas, the grant can be reduced by up to 5 percent of the instalment for each month.

A scenario in which a promoter has land and a term-loan sanction, and still loses part of the grant, is the scenario this clause describes. Equity and term-loan floors (20 percent in general areas, 10 percent for SC or ST promoters and the North East) mean the ministry's exit does not remove the bank's claim.

## The revised estimate is a recurring risk to cash

Multi-year outlays are poor predictors of a single year's release. In 2024-25, on the ministry's expenditure statement for that year, the food-processing PLI moved from a budget estimate of Rs 1,444.02 crore to a revised estimate of Rs 700 crore and actual expenditure of Rs 450.49 crore. In 2025-26, on the next year's statement, the same scheme held: budget, revised, and actual were all about Rs 1,200 crore. PMFME in 2025-26 went from Rs 2,000 crore budgeted to Rs 1,500 crore revised and Rs 1,296.53 crore spent. Inside PMKSY that year, Operation Greens went from Rs 177.36 crore budgeted to Rs 73.02 crore revised.

Two scenarios follow from that pattern, and they should be labelled as patterns, not forecasts. In one, 2026-27 releases stay near Demand No. 45's opening estimates: Rs 915 crore for PMKSY, Rs 1,200 crore for PLI, Rs 1,700 crore for PMFME. In the other, a revised estimate cuts Operation Greens or PMFME the way 2025-26 did, and a sanctioned project waits on an instalment. The second scenario has happened. The documents do not say it will happen again.

## Big event numbers are intention risk

World Food India 2025 produced MoUs the ministry valued at more than Rs 1,02,000 crore. PMKSY's wider claim, that approved projects are expected to leverage Rs 21,917 crore and more than 7.22 lakh jobs, uses the word expected. PLI's Rs 9,702 crore is investment reported by beneficiaries. A risk note that books any of these as committed capital will be wrong if the underlying projects slip, and the cold-chain cancellation history says some projects do slip.

Credit risk sits one step outside the ministry. PMFME's subsidy arrives only with a bank loan, the applicant's 10 percent, and a project report. A micro enterprise can be eligible on the FAQ and still be declined by the bank. That is a designed feature of a credit-linked scheme, and it is why sanctioned-loan counts and released-subsidy amounts in the year-end review are different sentences.
