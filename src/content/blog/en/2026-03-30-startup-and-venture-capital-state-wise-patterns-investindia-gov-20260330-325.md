State startup rankings in the press often follow funding announcements. The official geographic series is duller and more useful: how many entities DPIIT had recognised in each state and Union Territory on a stated date.

Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, and Gujarat account for 1,32,499 of the 2,40,092 entities. That is 55.2 percent of the registry. The arithmetic uses only the annex. It is not a funding share. Delhi is a Union Territory in this table, not a proxy for the National Capital Region. Haryana is a separate row, at 12,482.

The thin end is also in the annex, not an omission. Lakshadweep has 3 recognised startups, Sikkim 21, Ladakh 25, Mizoram 78. PIB’s 15 January 2026 note said around half of startups were emerging from Tier-II and Tier-III cities. This annex cannot confirm that sentence, because it is cut by state, not by city.

The March and June Karnataka counts move in the same direction. They are not a city-level Bengaluru series. Hyderabad is inside Telangana’s 13,622, not a separate official row.

## State policy still points back to DPIIT

Invest India hosts the Chandigarh Administration startup policy PDF dated to an April 2025 gazette. That policy recognises a startup for local benefits when DPIIT has recognised it and the entity is incorporated or registered in Chandigarh, or registered with the Chandigarh GST commissionerate. It also says later changes in the Government of India’s startup definition apply. A Union Territory incentive can therefore move when G.S.R. 108(E) moves, without a new local definition.

DPIIT’s States’ Startup Ranking Framework, described in the 15 January 2026 PIB note, grades states and Union Territories on policy and implementation into bands such as Best Performers, Top Performers, Leaders, Aspiring Leaders, and Emerging Startup Ecosystems. The note does not publish the latest band for each state. This brief does not fill that list from memory or from a sponsor ranking.
