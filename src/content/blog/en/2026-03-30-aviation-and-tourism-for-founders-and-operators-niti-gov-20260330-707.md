# What an airline or airport operator can actually bid for

A founder entering Indian regional aviation in this scheme window is bidding into a tighter contract than the one that produced 679 operationalised routes and left 348 still flying. The contract is public. It is the Modified UDAN comparison published by the Press Information Bureau on 16 July 2026, and the Lok Sabha statement of 6 August.

## The bid the ministry will accept

The airline needs an air operator permit. Award is supposed to rest on the business plan and on aircraft the airline has, not on a network drawn for a permit that is still pending. If the flight does not start in the stipulated time, the route is re-bid and the original bidder is barred. That is the rule written to stop the pattern the minister described: carriers bidding beyond their capacity because a permit was not required.

Fare support still covers 50 percent of seats. On aircraft with more than 80 seats, the old ceiling of 40 reduced-fare seats is gone. Viability-gap funding can last five years. The taper is 75 percent of the awarded amount in year three, 50 percent in year four, and 25 percent in year five. A plan that needs the year-one subsidy in year five is outside the published taper.

The ministry has also said, on 30 July 2026, that it does not pick aircraft type, market, or network. International routes remain a commercial choice inside bilateral caps. A pitch that asks the ministry to “allocate” a trunk route misunderstands both instruments. UDAN is the regional support. Bilaterals are the international capacity.

## What an airport-side operator is actually offered

Development money is not a cheque for every quiet airstrip. States nominate, and the challenge scores clear land, readiness, feasibility, traffic potential, clearances, and state support. The unit allowances in the scheme note are about ₹100 crore per airport across 100 airports (₹12,159 crore in the Lok Sabha statement) and about ₹15 crore per helipad across 200 helipads (₹3,661 crore). Manpower support, for three years, is capped at ₹3.06 crore a year per RCS airport and ₹0.90 crore for a heliport or water aerodrome.

The only aircraft the Centre has approved for purchase inside this phase are two HAL Dhruv helicopters for Pawan Hans and two HAL Dornier aircraft for Alliance Air. A private operator’s fleet is not that purchase. At the 8 May 2026 leasing summit, the aircraft-side announcements that were company statements, not ministry orders, were smaller and specific: Air India’s GIFT City leasing unit talking about growing a portfolio from over 50 aircraft to 76 by March 2027, IndiGo’s from around 75 to nearly 150 over the same period, Akasa leasing about 60 aircraft over five years through a new IFSC entity, and Star Air leasing Embraer E190s with an aim of 6 to 8 more regional aircraft from GIFT City in 2027. Those are summit announcements. They become a fleet when the aircraft are delivered and endorsed.

## The operating standard DGCA already publishes

Before a new route, read the monthly traffic note as an operating report. In June 2026 the cancellation rate on scheduled domestic airlines was 0.63 percent. Airlines paid ₹61.98 lakh in compensation and facilities for 43,968 passengers affected by cancellations, and ₹2.85 crore for facilities to 1,10,273 passengers affected by delays. On-time performance at the ten metro airports DGCA uses — Bengaluru, Delhi, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Kochi, Guwahati, and Lucknow — was 89.4 percent for IndiGo, 85.9 percent for the Air India group, 82.7 percent for Akasa, 74.7 percent for Alliance Air, and 33.5 percent for SpiceJet. A regional founder will be scored on this kind of table, not on the launch photograph.

Udan Yatri Cafe prices mentioned by the minister — tea at ₹10, snacks at ₹20 — are a passenger amenity at airports. They are not a concession model.
