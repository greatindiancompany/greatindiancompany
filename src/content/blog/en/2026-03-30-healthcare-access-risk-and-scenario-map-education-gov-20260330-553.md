## Risks named as mechanisms

A risk map that assigns percentages to these outcomes would be fiction. The National Health Authority has published the rules. It has not, on the pages used here, published a probability that private hospitals will exit a package or that a state will miss its premium share. What follows is the mechanism by which access fails if the rule bites, and the official hook for each mechanism.

## Eligibility that ages badly

PM-JAY’s floor is SECC 2011 deprivation and occupational criteria, plus RSBY families absent from that database. The about-page says states must cover every SECC-eligible family and may add others at their own cost. A household that became poor after 2011, and that the state has not added, can be outside the ₹5 lakh cover. The September 2024 Cabinet decision removes the income test for people aged 70 and above. It does not rebuild the list for younger families. Scenario: the card a field worker expects is missing, the clinic still sees the person because mandir registration is for residents, and the hospital admission is paid in cash. That split is allowed by the two designs.

## Rates, reserved packages, and exit from a procedure

Prices are fixed by the Authority, with limited state revision. Some procedures are reserved for public hospitals. If a private hospital’s cost sits above the rate, the rational response is to stop offering that package, or never to empanel for it. This page does not claim that response has been measured at a national rate. It claims the contract allows it. Public hospitals paid at par face the same rate and a different cost structure, and states may deduct a share of public-hospital claims. Scenario: the procedure exists on the national list and is unavailable in the district’s private hospitals, while the district hospital is not equipped for it either. The patient then travels under portability, or goes without.

## State money and insurer math

Extra families and extra rupees above ₹5 lakh are a state bill. In insurance mode the Authority’s page caps insurer administration by claim-ratio bands and requires surplus to return to the state agency, with a further sharing rule when claims exceed stated thresholds. Those bands differ for the page’s Category A and Category B. Scenario: a state that widened cover meets a high claim year, the insurer’s capped margin and the Centre’s ceiling premium do not absorb it, and hospitals wait on payment. Waiting hospitals tighten admission rules at the desk. Access fails in the queue, not in the gazette.

Trust mode moves the same shock onto the state agency directly. The payer changes. The hospital’s cash-flow risk remains.

## Counts that look like care

The ministry can report more operational mandirs and crores of screenings while a building-less centre, a missing drug, or an absent mid-level provider blocks the visit. PM-ABHIM’s approval list includes 10,609 building-less mandirs precisely because a reported centre and a building are different objects. Scenario: a dashboard improves, and the Monday clinic does not. The [evidence snapshot](/blog/healthcare-access-evidence-snapshot-education-gov-20260330-673) is the antidote to reading those series as one fact.

## Portability and the district specialist

Portability is an entitlement to any empanelled hospital in India. It depends on systems that recognize the beneficiary and on two agencies settling. Scenario: the beneficiary is genuine, the hospital is empanelled, and the interstate payment is slow enough that the hospital asks for a deposit. That deposit undoes cashless care for the person least able to pay it.

A parallel scenario sits inside the district. The 2017 policy wants secondary care at the district hospital. Standards revised in 2022 say clinical staff should be hired in-house. If the specialist post is empty, PM-JAY volume flows to empanelled private hospitals in larger towns. Families who cannot travel lose the admission the cover promised.

## Identity mix-ups

An Ayushman Bharat Health Account links records in the digital mission launched in September 2021. PM-JAY eligibility is a beneficiary-database check. Scenario: a desk accepts the 14-digit number as proof of cover and discovers the gap after admission. The [regulatory note](/blog/healthcare-access-regulatory-outlook-education-gov-20260330-433) keeps those instruments apart. Investors pricing these scenarios should start from the [investment note](/blog/healthcare-access-investment-implications-education-gov-20260330-233).
