## Which number is the market

A trade-corridor market signal is a count of freight that official agencies publish on a named network. Three series matter, and they are not substitutes.

The dedicated freight corridors are the cleanest corridor series. On 19 March 2025 the Railways Minister told the Lok Sabha that traffic on the corridors had risen from 247 average trains a day in 2023-24 to 352 average trains a day in 2024-25 up to February 2025, and that February 2025 itself ran 371 average trains a day. On 1 February 2026 a ministerial briefing put operations near 400 freight trains a day and described the corridors as close to saturation. On 7 August 2026 the Rajya Sabha was told that about 443 trains a day were being operated on the corridors, after both the 1,337 km Eastern corridor and the 1,506 km Western corridor had been commissioned.

Read that sequence as rising use of a network that was still closing its last western section in early 2025 and was described as commissioned by August 2026. The jump from about 400 to about 443 is two official statements five months apart. It is not a published monthly index.

## System loading is a second signal

Indian Railways' freight loading, in the same 7 August 2026 reply, runs from 1,098 million tonnes in 2014-15 to 1,670 million tonnes in 2025-26. The minister described Indian Railways as the second largest freight-carrying railway. Most of those tonnes still move on the mixed network. Corridor train counts and system tonnes answer different questions: how hard the dedicated tracks are being worked, and how large the railway freight market is.

The reply also says diverting freight onto the dedicated corridors created paths on the conventional network for additional goods and coaching trains. That is a capacity signal for passenger and non-corridor freight operators, stated as an effect, without a published count of paths freed.

## Containers are a third signal

DPIIT's 16 September 2025 note says the Logistics Data Bank had tracked more than 75 million export-import containers across 101 inland container depots, and that the Unified Logistics Interface Platform had passed 160 crore transactions across more than 30 systems as of August 2025. Those figures measure visibility and digital use. They do not measure corridor throughput.

Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals add a private-terminal signal. By 7 August 2026, 142 terminals were commissioned, with estimated capacity of 224 million tonnes a year and 146 million tonnes handled in 2025-26. A shipper comparing a terminal with the corridor should ask whether the terminal is on the dedicated alignment or on the conventional railway. The policy allows both, because location follows cargo demand, railway infrastructure, and the logistics potential of the area.

## What these signals do not price

The March 2025 reply says the Eastern corridor will mostly cater to mineral traffic from eastern India, and that the project enables double-stack container trains and faster access from western ports into the northern hinterland. That is a commodity and hinterland clue. It is not a rate card. No figure in these releases is a market price for a container from JNPT to Dadri, or a measured saving already realised by shippers.

The India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor is a different class of signal. On 16 April 2025 the Commerce and Industry Minister said it would bring logistics costs down by up to 30 percent and transportation time down by 40 percent. That is an expectation for a corridor that the September 2023 memorandum of understanding still describes as a commitment to build a ship-to-rail route. It is not an observed rate on the dedicated freight corridors.

Use the train counts to judge how full the operating rail corridors are. Use Railway loading to judge the wider freight market. Use container-tracking totals to judge visibility. Hold ministerial percentages for IMEC until an operating service publishes a before-and-after.
