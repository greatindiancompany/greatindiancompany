# Future of Work in India: Jobs, Skills, and Formal Records

## Three questions

Jobs and skills are often written as one statistic. In the Indian record they are three questions, answered by different institutions.

## Who trains, and to which standard

The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship launched Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana in 2015 for short-duration training and certification. Its PMKVY 4.0 guidelines place that phase under the Skill India Programme for FY 2022-2026. The National Council for Vocational Education and Training recognises awarding bodies and assessment agencies. Courses align to the National Skills Qualification Framework. The National Skill Development Corporation supports implementation. The guidelines describe Skill India Digital as the system that carries a trainee from registration through post-certification tracking.

A certificate answers a training question. Employment is a later fact. The guideline window runs through March 2026. After that, the live scheme text is whatever the ministry has published. This brief reports no trainee count.

## Who is at work

The Periodic Labour Force Survey, from the National Statistics Office, is the household source for labour force participation, the worker population ratio, and unemployment, including among people who have received formal vocational or technical training. The press note for the annual report covering January to December 2025 is dated 27 March 2026. Rates stay in that note. PLFS and a training dashboard will differ, because one samples households and the other runs a scheme.

## What makes a job formal on paper

The Ministry of Labour and Employment, bringing the four labour codes into force on 21 November 2025, said appointment letters are mandatory. The letter is the document the codes use to make a job readable: a named worker, a wage, and a social-security entitlement. Fixed-term employees, the ministry says, receive benefits equal to permanent workers and gratuity after one year. Gig and platform workers are defined in the Code on Social Security so that schemes can be framed. The Lok Sabha reply of 20 July 2026 lists life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity benefits, and old-age protection as the subject of those schemes. A scheme category is a legal slot.

IT and business-process employers hire from skill certificates and from degree pipelines. The ministry's IT and ITES note regulates wage timing, equal pay, night work with consent and safety, and appointment letters. Mapping a particular NSQF course to a BPM role remains the employer's work. The codes do not do that mapping.
