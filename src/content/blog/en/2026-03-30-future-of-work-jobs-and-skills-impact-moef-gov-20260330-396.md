# What the jobs data and the gig-skill estimates actually say

The work most Indians do is still described by the household survey, not by the platform economy. The platform economy matters, and the only official India-wide estimate of its skill mix is a 2022 NITI Aayog report that is explicit about being an estimate. Public skilling policy is a set of named programmes linked, in one important case, to the unorganised-worker register. Those three layers should not be collapsed into a single "skills gap" sentence.

## The jobs layer is a participation story, led by women

For persons aged 15 and above, usual status, July 2023–June 2024, the Periodic Labour Force Survey press note reported labour-force participation of 60.1 percent, a worker population ratio of 58.2 percent, and unemployment of 3.2 percent.

The movement over six years is concentrated among women. The press note records women's participation at 23.3 percent in 2017–18 and 41.7 percent in 2023–24. Men's participation over the same years went from 75.8 percent to 78.8 percent. The Directorate General of Employment's summary puts women's worker population ratio at 22 percent in 2017–18 and 40.3 percent in 2023–24, and the all-India worker ratio at 46.8 percent rising to 58.2 percent.

In the single year from July 2022–June 2023 to July 2023–June 2024, women's participation rose from 37.0 percent to 41.7 percent, while men's rose from 78.5 percent to 78.8 percent. Women's unemployment on the usual-status measure rose from 2.9 percent to 3.2 percent in that year, even as the worker ratio rose. More women were in the labour force, more were working, and the share of labour-force women without work edged up. That combination is what a rising-participation labour market looks like. It is not a story in which unemployment and participation always move as opposites.

Urban weekly figures show how much of this is still rural and annual rather than urban and current. In July–September 2024, urban current-weekly-status participation was 50.4 percent overall and 25.5 percent for women. Calendar 2024 urban weekly participation for women was 25.8 percent. A skills programme aimed only at city salaried work is aimed at a much smaller women's labour force than the usual-status headline suggests.

This brief does not quote a youth unemployment rate. The full PLFS report carries age tables. The press notes used here do not give a single youth rate that this page should lift out of context.

## The gig layer is an estimate with a skill shape

NITI Aayog's report, *India's Booming Gig and Platform Economy* (June 2022), estimated 68 lakh gig workers in 2019–20 and 77 lakh in 2020–21. The 2020–21 figure was about 2.6 percent of the non-agricultural workforce and 1.5 percent of the total workforce. The report's extension to 2029–30 is 2.35 crore workers, about 6.7 percent of the non-agricultural workforce and 4.1 percent of total livelihood. It also reported employment elasticity for gig workers above one from 2011–12 to 2019–20, and above the elasticity for workers as a whole. That is a statement about the survey years the model used. It is not a measured elasticity for 2024 or 2025.

On skills, the report's estimate put about 47 percent of gig work in medium-skilled jobs, 22 percent in high-skilled jobs, and 31 percent in low-skilled jobs. It said the medium-skilled share was gradually declining and the low-skilled and high-skilled shares were rising, and that medium-skilled work could still dominate through 2030. On industries, it placed about 26.6 lakh gig workers in retail trade and sales and about 13 lakh in transportation, with about 6.2 lakh in manufacturing and 6.3 lakh in finance and insurance. These are classifications of an estimate, not a census of app-based workers. The report exists because the labour-force survey does not publish a "gig worker" category. Anyone training people for "gig jobs" as if they were one occupation is ignoring a split that runs from delivery and retail to professional services.

## The public skill rails that are named, and nothing invented beyond the names

The Economic Survey 2025–26 lists, among the government's skill instruments, the Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana, the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme, the National Apprenticeship Training Scheme, and the Skill India Digital Hub. This page does not attach a trainee count to any of them. A count that is not taken from a current ministry release would be decoration.

What can be said from a primary reply is how those rails meet the unorganised register. The Lok Sabha reply of 20 July 2026 says e-Shram is linked to the Skill India Digital Hub for skill development and to the National Career Service for job opportunities, alongside Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maandhan for pensions. The register, at 31.78 crore people on 14 July 2026, is the distribution list those linkages can see. Whether a person on that list is offered a course, completes it, or is hired is not in the registration total.

The Ministry of Labour's 21 November 2025 note treats skilling as one of the outcomes the labour codes are meant to support, alongside employment, by reducing compliance burden and allowing more flexible work arrangements. That is a statement of intent. The codes' concrete skill-adjacent rules are elsewhere: appointment letters that create an employment record, fixed-term contracts that carry benefits, and recognition of gig and platform workers so that a scheme can be addressed to them. None of those is a training curriculum.

The jobs-and-skills picture, kept in proportion, is this. The measured change in Indian work since 2017–18 is a large rise in women's participation and in the worker ratio, with usual-status unemployment lower than it was at the start of that window. The estimated gig workforce is a small share of total workers, concentrated in the NITI estimate in medium-skilled retail, sales, and transport roles, with a modelled tilt toward both higher and lower skills. The state's skilling architecture is real and named, and its new front door for unorganised workers is the link from e-Shram to the Skill India Digital Hub and the National Career Service. Report those as three facts. Do not average them into one.
