# What the agri schemes pay for in people

Official agri-chain documents are generous with projects and careful with jobs. Where they name a person, the person is usually a member of a farmer company, a beneficiary of a cash transfer, or a professional the scheme will pay for a fixed number of years. A national employment total for warehouses, mandis, and processing plants is not in the releases used here. The honest reading is the line that is.

## The management grant is a job, for three years

The scheme to form 10,000 farmer producer organisations, launched in 2020 with an outlay of Rs 6,865 crore, pays "financial assistance up to Rs 18 lakh per FPO for a period of three years for FPO management." That sentence, from the Lok Sabha reply of 3 February 2026, is the scheme's labour line. It is a budget for a chief executive and a small office, not a wage subsidy for members.

The members are the other people in the design. The matching equity grant is up to Rs 2,000 per farmer member, capped at Rs 15 lakh per FPO. The credit guarantee covers up to Rs 2 crore of project loan. As on 31 December 2025, 10,000 FPOs were registered, the equity grant had reached 6,557 of them (Rs 430.77 crore), and the guarantee had reached 2,671 (Rs 662.71 crore). As on 1 March 2026, 1,175 of the FPOs had an entirely women membership, and 23.55 lakh women farmers were registered under the scheme. The reply does not give the total of all farmer members, so a women's share of membership cannot be computed from it. It does show that the scheme counts women both as members and as whole organisations.

An FPO on the mandi platform is a smaller set. e-NAM had onboarded 4,724 FPOs as on 28 February 2026, against 10,000 registered under the formation scheme. Some of the 4,724 may pre-date that scheme. The two registers should not be forced into one headcount of "FPO professionals in the market."

## The processing schemes name off-farm work, and then count something else

The food-processing production-linked incentive lists "increase employment opportunities for off-farm jobs" among its objectives, in the Rajya Sabha reply of 27 March 2026. The same reply's progress numbers are applicants, investment, capacity, and incentive: 168 approved applicants as on 31 December 2025, about Rs 9,207 crore invested, around 35 lakh metric tonnes a year of processing and preservation capacity, and Rs 2,714.79 crore of incentive disbursed. Sixty-nine applicants were MSMEs. The reply does not give the jobs those units employ. The objective stands. The headcount does not.

The cold-chain programme is similarly built around assets. As of 30 June 2026, 311 completed projects had created 9.236 lakh metric tonnes of cold storage, out of 409 approved. A ministry-commissioned evaluation, cited in the 23 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply, reported that commissioning these projects had reduced post-harvest losses and raised processing and preservation capacity. The reply as fetched does not print the loss percentage or a jobs figure from that study. Capacity is the published result.

One unit is described in full because the ministry chose it as an illustration, and it should stay an illustration. The August 2025 note on the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund describes a cashew unit at Kidigam in Gajapati district of Odisha: project cost Rs 57 lakh, loan Rs 42.75 lakh from UCO Bank, a stated direct employment of 20 people, and 40 local farmers with assured procurement. That is one promoted story inside a book of 1,50,431 sanctioned projects as on 26 January 2026. Multiplying 20 by 1,50,431 would invent a workforce the ministry has not published.

## The cash transfer is not a wage

PM-KISAN pays a landholding family Rs 6,000 a year. The Cabinet note of 31 July 2026 says the assistance has been used for seeds, fertiliser, irrigation, and machinery, and it reports the DMEO finding that more than 92 per cent of beneficiaries said they used it for agricultural activities. More than 9.49 crore farmers were in the 23rd instalment. Nearly one in four beneficiaries is a woman farmer, and women have received more than Rs 1.06 lakh crore.

Those are cultivators receiving a transfer. They are not employees of the value chain. The March 2026 reply adds a practical skill of a different kind: in 19 states where the farmer registry has started, a Farmer ID is mandatory for new PM-KISAN registrations, and farmers without a phone can use a common service centre or a block office. That is an inclusion rule for the register, not a training scheme.

## What a jobs reading can still conclude

The schemes create three kinds of paid or supported roles that a reader can point to. A professional team inside an FPO, funded up to Rs 18 lakh over three years. A borrower whose project the AIF or a food-processing grant has financed, with employment left to the project report rather than to a national total. And a member-farmer whose equity, guarantee, mandi registration, or PM-KISAN instalment is counted. Beyond those, the documents record assets and transfers. A skills-gap percentage, a wage, or a national off-farm job count would have to come from a later official table that states it.
