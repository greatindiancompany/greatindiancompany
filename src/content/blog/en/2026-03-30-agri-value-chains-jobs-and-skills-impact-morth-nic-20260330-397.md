# Where work sits in India’s agri chain

There is no single official headcount for “agri value-chain jobs.” The published numbers sit in three different units: scheme employment, scheme membership, and household income. They should stay in those units.

The clearest jobs print is from the Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Food Processing Industry. The Ministry of Food Processing Industries told the Lok Sabha that approved applicants had generated about 3.35 lakh direct and indirect jobs, against a scheme target of 2.50 lakh. The same reply says the investments created 34 lakh tonnes a year of processing capacity, and that applicants had invested ₹9,207 crore against a committed ₹7,722 crore. Incentives disbursed up to June 2026 were ₹3,271.44 crore. The outlay for the scheme, from FY 2021-22 to FY 2026-27, is ₹10,900 crore. The 3.35 lakh figure is the applicants’ employment claim as reported by the Ministry. It is direct and indirect together, so it is not a payroll census of factories.

An older processing geography still carries an employment expectation rather than a fresh survey. The Mega Food Park scheme stopped taking new projects from 1 April 2021. Forty-one parks in 25 states had been approved, 25 of them operational. Those projects are expected to create preservation capacity of 28.57 lakh tonnes a year and processing capacity of 15.41 lakh tonnes a year, to benefit about 86,881 farmers, and to generate 1,02,440 direct and indirect jobs. “Expected” is the Ministry’s word. It is not a substitute for the PLI jobs figure, and the two should not be added.

## Membership is not a job

The 10,000 Farmer Producer Organisation scheme is a membership scheme. As on 1 March 2026, 10,000 FPOs were registered, 1,175 of them with all-women membership, and 23.55 lakh women farmers were on the rolls. That is a count of women inside the organisations. It does not say how many draw a salary, how many are directors, or how many sold produce through the FPO in the last season.

e-NAM is a registration scheme of a different kind. As on 28 February 2026 it had 1.80 crore farmers, 2.72 lakh traders, and 4,724 FPOs. The skill the Centre has chosen to highlight is assaying. The same reply says 134 e-NAM mandis in Rajasthan were using AI and machine-learning machines for quality tests, with central assistance, to cut testing time. Quality assaying, electronic weighing, and grading are also what the per-mandi grant of up to ₹75 lakh is meant to buy. That is a works-and-skills fact about mandi staff and machinery. It is not a national employment total.

Household income is a third series, and it is old. The latest Situation Assessment Survey the Ministry cites is the National Statistical Office’s 77th round, for agricultural year 2018-19. Average monthly income per agricultural household was ₹10,218, up from ₹6,426 in 2012-13. The reply does not break that average into wages, crops, and livestock. Anyone using ₹10,218 as a 2026 wage for a packhouse worker, or as proof that MSP procurement is the household’s main cash flow, is using the survey for a question it was not asked in that reply.

A founder hiring graders, lab technicians, or FPO managers can take three official clues and no more. Processing incentives have a reported jobs number of about 3.35 lakh, direct and indirect. Women’s participation in the flagship FPO scheme is 23.55 lakh members, which is a talent pool only if the FPO is actually trading. Mandi assaying is being equipped, at least in 134 Rajasthan markets, and that is a skill the trade now has to staff.
