# Which Official Series Measures India’s Social Outcomes

A social outcome in India is only as good as the series that measured it. Six public series are in use right now. They answer different questions, on different calendars, and a sentence that mixes them will sound precise and be wrong.

## NFHS-6: a household survey, with a published boundary

The National Family Health Survey is a household sample run for the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, with the International Institute for Population Sciences as the nodal agency. NFHS-6 fieldwork was May 2023 to December 2024, about 6.79 lakh households in 715 districts. India and state fact sheets were released on 29 May 2026. District compendiums followed on 20 August 2026 for six states and Union territories and on 21 August 2026 for the rest of the covered set. Manipur is outside that compendium. The Director General of Statistics’ note in the fact sheets says they carry 101 indicators, with a subset at district level.

Use it for stunting (29.3%), wasting (19.0%), underweight (31.8%), exclusive breastfeeding (55.8%), institutional births (90.6%), and household health-insurance or financing coverage (60.2%). Do not use the 29 May press note’s “17%” stunting line as a 17-point drop. That headline is a relative change on the NFHS-5 base of 35.5%. Do not quote an NFHS-6 anaemia prevalence from these fact sheets. The press note and the nutrition lines in the compendium do not publish one. Do not treat “currently breastfeeding” (95.6% under six months) as exclusive breastfeeding.

## Poshan Tracker: an administrative register

The Tracker, launched on 1 March 2021, is the Ministry of Women and Child Development’s operating system for centres, attendance, and growth monitoring. As of 31 July 2026 it covered nearly 13.99 lakh centres, 13.30 lakh workers, and almost 8.94 crore beneficiaries. A May 2026 note said growth monitoring covered over 6.3 crore children aged 0-5, about 94% of those registered. These are registered people and recorded services. They are not a re-estimate of the NFHS stunting rate, and they update on a different clock. A centre can record a weight and the household survey can still find underweight unchanged.

## UDISE+: a school census, not a learning exam

Released on 7 July 2026 for academic year 2025-26, UDISE+ counts schools, teachers, enrolment, dropout, retention, and facilities. Teachers: 1,02,73,020. Secondary retention: 51.9%. Secondary gross enrolment: 71.7%. Computers: 69.9% of schools. It does not report what children learned. Pupil-teacher ratios are staffing facts. Dropout is a participation fact.

## PLFS: the labour market, current weekly status

The National Statistics Office monthly bulletin for August 2026, released 15 September 2026, covers persons aged 15 and above on the current weekly status, from 3,70,160 persons surveyed. Labour force participation 55.6%, worker population ratio 52.8%, unemployment 5.0% (rural 4.1%, urban 6.8%). It is not a count of Anganwadi workers, teachers, or VB-G RAM G person-days. Those sit in the scheme systems. The method changed from January 2025 to produce monthly and quarterly rural and urban estimates. Compare months inside this series, and say that you are on the current weekly status.

## NSAP and the rural wage Act: rules plus early outputs

The National Social Assistance Programme is a beneficiary pension system. On 21 July 2026 the ministry said the central rate was last revised in 2012, that more than 3 crore people are on the central below-poverty-line rolls, and that about 5.9 crore further pensioners are on state schemes. That is coverage and a price, not a poverty outcome.

VB-G RAM G, in force from 1 July 2026, is a statutory guarantee of 125 days. Person-days reported to the Lok Sabha on 11 August 2026 (more than 9.69 crore in about the first month) are an early output under a new Act. They do not continue the old MGNREGS time series unless a ministry note explicitly joins them.
