The National Statistics Office released the August 2026 monthly bulletin of the Periodic Labour Force Survey on 15 September 2026. It is the seventeenth bulletin in the monthly series. The survey design that produces these bulletins was modified from January 2025 so that labour-force indicators could be published every month for the country as a whole.

Every figure below is for persons aged 15 years and above, on the current weekly status. That status is the activity in the seven days before the interview. It is the only status this bulletin reports.

## The month, in the bulletin's own rates

The overall labour force participation rate rose from 55.4% in July 2026 to 55.6% in August 2026. A year earlier, in August 2025, it was 55.0%.

Rural participation rose from 58.0% to 58.2%, and was 1.2 percentage points above August 2025 (57.0%). Urban participation stayed at 50.4%, the same as July, and was 0.5 percentage point below August 2025 (50.9%).

The worker population ratio, the share of the population that is employed on this weekly status, rose from 52.5% to 52.8%. The bulletin calls 52.8% the highest level since March 2026. Rural employment rose from 55.4% to 55.8%. Urban employment stayed at 47.0%.

The unemployment rate eased from 5.1% in July to 5.0% in August. The snapshot line in the same note describes the 5.0% rate as stable. Both sentences are in the press note: a one-tenth move, at a level the office treats as steady. Rural unemployment fell from 4.5% to 4.1%, which the note calls the lowest rural rate since January 2026. Urban unemployment moved from 6.7% to 6.8%.

## Women, and men, inside that same week

Overall female participation rose from 34.4% in July to 34.8% in August, and from 33.7% in August 2025. Rural female participation rose from 38.8% to 39.4%, and was 2.0 percentage points above August 2025 (37.4%). Urban female participation was 25.4%, against 25.3% in July and 26.1% in August 2025.

Overall female employment on this weekly status was 33.0% in August 2026, up from 32.0% in August 2025.

Male unemployment fell for a third straight month, to 4.9% in August from 5.4% in May 2026. Rural male unemployment was 4.3%, down from 4.6% in July. Urban male unemployment was 6.1%, up from 5.9%. Female unemployment was 5.1%, down from 5.9% in June, with rural female unemployment at 3.9% against 4.3% in July. The note says urban female unemployment was broadly stable over the month and does not print a separate August figure for it.

## What a monthly move is allowed to mean

The August estimates rest on 3,70,160 persons: 2,11,353 in rural areas and 1,58,807 in urban areas.

On 1 December 2025, the Ministry of Labour and Employment told the Lok Sabha that monthly PLFS ratios can move because the survey is more frequent and because of the season, and that those moves do not necessarily mark a lasting trend. That reply was about the August and September 2025 prints (overall unemployment 5.1% and 5.2%). The August 2026 bulletin is the same weekly-status monthly design, seventeen releases on. A rural dip from 4.5% to 4.1% in one month is a published fact. It is not, by the ministry's own caution, a new trend line by itself.

The annual usual-status unemployment rate of 3.2% for July 2023–June 2024 answers a different question: work or search over a year, including subsidiary work. How those rates fit together is in the [LFPR, WPR, and unemployment brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-key-numbers-20260330-020).

## The policy decision published the next day

On 16 September 2026 the Union Cabinet approved a higher wage ceiling for mandatory Employees' Provident Fund coverage, from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month. The Press Information Bureau note says this is expected to bring more than 51 lakh additional employees into mandatory coverage, and that the Ministry of Labour and Employment and the EPFO still have to complete the statutory steps. That expectation is not a count of people already enrolled in August. The employer reading of the decision is in the [EPFO wage-ceiling brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-impact-on-smes-20260330-030).
