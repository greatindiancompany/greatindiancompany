# India’s Green Hydrogen Label, and What an Export Buyer Still Decides

Comparisons of “who is ahead in green hydrogen” usually mix a target, a subsidy, and a definition. The only comparison this brief will make from documents it has actually read is between India’s notified label and the fact that an export sale can still be governed by the buyer’s rule.

## What India notified

On 19 August 2023, the Press Information Bureau reported that the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy had notified the Green Hydrogen Standard for India. The [release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1950421) says hydrogen is green when well-to-gate emissions are not more than 2 kg of CO2 equivalent per kg of hydrogen. The headline states that threshold as a 12-month average. The boundary named in the release includes water treatment, electrolysis, gas purification, drying, and compression of hydrogen. The scope covers electrolysis and biomass. The release says MNRE would specify the methodology for measurement, reporting, monitoring, on-site verification, and certification, and that the Bureau of Energy Efficiency, in the Ministry of Power, would be the nodal authority for accrediting the agencies that do that work.

PIB also says that with this notification India became one of the first few countries to announce a definition of green hydrogen. That is the government’s claim about timing. This brief does not extend it into a ranking against the European Union, the United Kingdom, the United States, or any other threshold. Those comparisons need the other rule’s boundary, the gases counted, and the averaging period. Setting a well-to-gate Indian kilogram next to another country’s tax-credit kilogram produces a false league table.

## A unit conversion is not an audit

The International Energy Agency, in its work on common criteria for sustainable fuels, restates India’s green label as a threshold of 17 grams of CO2 equivalent per megajoule, equal to 2 kg per kg of hydrogen. That restatement is useful when a contract is written in energy units. It is the same Indian number in another unit. It is not an independent count of emissions from Indian plants. The [IEA](https://www.iea.org/) is the right shelf for that methods work. The operative Indian text remains the August 2023 notification and the certification scheme that followed it.

## Domestic certificate and foreign buyer

The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) puts export inside the objective, alongside domestic use. Certification is how the domestic word “green” is policed. MNRE’s notice of the Green Hydrogen Certification Scheme of India is dated 29 April 2025. The PIB note of 12 November 2025 says the scheme certifies hydrogen by assessing greenhouse-gas emissions across the production cycle, and that a final certificate is mandatory for a facility in India that receives a central or state subsidy or incentive, or that sells or uses the hydrogen domestically. On 17 June 2026 the minister launched the certification portal that administers this.

Read that mandatory list closely. The November 2025 note states two cases: incentives, and domestic sale or use. A producer who takes Indian support, or who sells in India, is inside those cases. A buyer in another country can still require that country’s certificate, guarantee of origin, or shipping document. MNRE’s 2 kg rule tells an Indian producer when the Indian government will use the word. It does not, on these pages, bind a foreign offtaker. Ammonia and methanol standards, which the minister said in June 2026 had been finalised in recent months, matter because much of the export conversation is about those derivatives, not about hydrogen gas in a container.

The practical benchmark for a founder is therefore a pair of documents: the Indian standard that governs the domestic label and any incentive, and the specification attached to the export contract. Great Indian Company leaves the country-versus-country ranking out until both documents are on the table.
