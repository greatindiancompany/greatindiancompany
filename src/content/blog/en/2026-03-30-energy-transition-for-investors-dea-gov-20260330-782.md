# Reading India's Energy Transition as an Investor

Investors in this transition are offered four kinds of claim, often in one paragraph. A national target. A contract with a buyer. An incentive that pays after a factory sells. A merchant or open-access position that depends on the tariff and the grid. They do not have the same cash flows. The public record is good enough to tell them apart. It is not a substitute for a term sheet, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell a security.

## Start with what is already built, and what it earned in energy

On 31 July 2026 non-fossil capacity was 300.50 GW, which PIB called over 54 percent of a system around 552 GW, and over 60 percent of the 500 GW planning mark. Solar was 164.59 GW and wind 58.14 GW. FY 2025-26 added 55.29 GW of non-fossil capacity, including 44.61 GW of solar and 6.05 GW of wind. Those are reasons to believe the build-out is funded and executed at scale.

The energy account is the reason not to stop there. Non-fossil sources were 29.2 percent of generation in FY 2025-26, 538.97 billion units out of 1,845.921. The National Electricity Plan still carries a coal fleet of about 235 GW at the end of 2026-27 and about 260 GW at the end of 2031-32, run at roughly a 58 percent load factor, with domestic coal use still around 866 million tonnes and then 1,026 million tonnes. An investment case that needs coal plants to shut on the capacity-share headline is arguing with the notified plan. An investment case that needs solar plants to be paid for energy they can actually evacuate is arguing with the transmission report, which says plants arrive faster than lines.

## Sort the revenue

Power-purchase-agreement projects were about 15 GW of FY 2025-26 solar addition, on MNRE's split. Commercial, industrial, and captive projects were also about 15 GW. The first depends on a power-purchase agreement, a payment security practice, and the line. The second depends on a private buyer, open-access charges, and the 100 kW threshold in the Green Energy Open Access Rules. Captive projects have no load floor. Both showed up in volume last year. They are different credit risks.

Household rooftop is a third revenue. The buyer is a family, the subsidy is central assistance under a ₹75,021 crore scheme, and Budget 2026-27 sets aside ₹22,000 crore. As of 12 August 2026, 51.58 lakh households and 14.8 GW were in, against one crore households by March 2027. The cash-flow questions are vendor working capital, the inspection queue, and the end of the scheme window. They are not the questions you ask of a 25-year utility PPA.

Factory incentives are a fourth revenue. Solar PLI is ₹24,000 crore and pays for five years after commissioning on modules made and sold. Nameplate module capacity is already about 172 GW. The incentive does not pay on a press release about capacity installed in the factory. Upstream, the ingot-and-wafer list is a June 2028 issue, and the advanced-chemistry-cell pool is ₹18,100 crore for 50 GWh, with 10 GWh of that still the grid-scale slice. Hydrogen's SIGHT programme sits inside a ₹19,744 crore mission, while Budget 2026-27 provides ₹600 crore net and commissioned output cited for February 2026 was about 8,000 tonnes a year. A mission envelope is not revenue.

## Sort the balance sheet that is not yours

IREDA's internal and extra-budgetary resources in Budget 2026-27 are ₹40,064.74 crore. SECI's are ₹2,824.86 crore. IREDA is a lender. A large lending plan says the public renewable financier expects volume. It does not disclose asset quality in the Budget paper, and it is not equity in a project you might own. SECI as a PLI implementing agency and SECI as a company with its own investment plan are two roles.

The Electricity Plan's ₹14.54 lakh crore for 2022-27 and ₹19.06 lakh crore for 2027-32, about ₹33.60 lakh crore together, is a system capital estimate. The inter-state renewable transmission estimate is a further ₹2.44 lakh crore. These numbers explain why the sector can absorb large pools of capital. They do not tell you the return on a particular special-purpose vehicle.

## Questions that change the price

Where is the offtake, and is it a discom, a commercial buyer, a household subsidy, or a hope that the 2030 target creates a buyer? Is the corridor dated, or is the project relying on the general statement that transmission is being planned for 537 GW? If the asset is a factory, what is sold, and which list or duty window expires in 2028? If the asset is storage, is there a contracted use, given that batteries are still a plan requirement of 8.7 GW by 2026-27 and 47 GW by 2031-32 without a national stock on the MNRE sheet? If the asset is hydrogen, what is the offtake in tonnes, next to a commissioned base of about 8,000 tonnes a year?

The capacity story is strong enough that "will India build renewables" is no longer the investor's main question. The main question is which of the four revenues is in the instrument, and which official constraint, coal in the plan, wires before watts, or a scheme end-date, sits on top of it.
