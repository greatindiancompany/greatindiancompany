# The market signal in India's water programmes is unfinished service

A supplier who reads only the rural tap-connection rate will think the build-out is mostly done. The official record says something narrower. A large majority of rural households are reported connected. The programmes now in force still have open work in maintenance, village waste, urban sewage, and treatment plants that are sanctioned and not yet complete.

There is no private market-size estimate on this page. The signal is the shape of the public programmes.

## Rural taps: the residual is real, and it is no longer the whole job

On 10 March 2026 the Cabinet note on Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 said that, of 19.36 crore rural households, around 15.80 crore (81.61 percent) were reported to have tap connections. The mission's own next requirement is not another national percentage. A Gram Panchayat declares itself Har Ghar Jal only once the state has established in-village operation and maintenance. The digital layer that is supposed to make a scheme auditable, Sujalam Bharat, assigns every village a service-area identity and maps the system from source to tap.

For an operator, the buyer after handover is no longer only the state engineering department. It is also the Gram Panchayat and the Village Water and Sanitation Committee that receive the scheme under Jal Arpan. A product that assumes a centralised utility will be selling into the wrong institution in a large share of villages.

## Village sanitation: the open gap is the step up to Model

Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) Phase II, which the ministry says runs from 2020-21 to 2026-27, sorts villages into Aspiring, Rising, and Model. Aspiring villages manage either solid or liquid waste. Rising villages manage both. Model villages also show visual cleanliness and ODF Plus messaging.

The [SBM-G dashboard](https://sbm.gov.in/sbmgdashboard/), as on 11 June 2026, counted 5,86,944 SBM villages, of which 5,69,020 were ODF Plus: 5,07,634 Model, 3,463 Rising, and 57,923 Aspiring. The Aspiring stock is the clearest operating gap in that table. Those villages still lack one of the two waste arrangements. A later PIB feature, dated 17 September 2026, reports the position in lakhs: 5.69 lakh ODF Plus villages, of which 5.25 lakh are Model. The June integers and the September lakhs are separate as-on statements. They should not be subtracted from each other.

## Urban water and sewage: awarded is not completed

In a Lok Sabha answer on 6 August 2026, the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs described the AMRUT 2.0 State Water Action Plans then approved: 8,777 projects with a total cost of ₹1,97,191.85 crore, including operation and maintenance, and a central share of ₹66,638.59 crore. Water-supply projects are the largest count, 3,572, at ₹1,18,968.38 crore. Sewerage and septage projects are fewer, 605, but they account for ₹71,133.01 crore. As on 9 July 2026, states had reported 7,687 projects awarded, with capex of ₹1,37,130.13 crore, and physically completed works of ₹66,472 crore.

The signal is the distance between award and physical completion, and the fact that sewerage is a small number of large projects. A firm that can only do scattered rural pipe contracts is not looking at the same job as a firm that can build and run a city sewage works.

## Ganga basin treatment: capacity created versus capacity taken up

Namami Gange sits with the National Mission for Clean Ganga, under the Department of Water Resources, River Development and Ganga Rejuvenation ([jalshakti-dowr.gov.in](https://jalshakti-dowr.gov.in/)). A ministry statement placed before the Lok Sabha, giving the position till June 2026, says 219 sewerage projects costing ₹35,970 crore have been taken up, for 6,610 million litres a day, and that 146 projects with 4,263 million litres a day have been completed.

That is a completion gap inside one named basin programme. It is not a national wastewater market.
