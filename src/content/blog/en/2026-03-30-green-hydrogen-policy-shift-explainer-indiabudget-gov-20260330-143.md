# How India’s National Green Hydrogen Mission Is Built

The [National Green Hydrogen Mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) at the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy states one objective: make India the global hub for production, use, and export of green hydrogen and its derivatives. The same page shows that the objective is not one subsidy. It is a set of tools with different cheque-writers, and one of those tools was later narrowed in public.

## Demand is assigned, not assumed

The page lists three demand routes. Exports are to be helped by policy and partnerships. Domestic demand is a minimum share of green hydrogen, or of derivatives such as green ammonia and green methanol, for designated consumers. The year-wise path of that share is for the Empowered Group to decide. The third route is competitive bidding to aggregate and procure hydrogen and ammonia. A state note that says the Centre has already imposed a binding consumption quota is ahead of this page, unless it can cite the Empowered Group’s trajectory itself.

## SIGHT is the production incentive

Strategic Interventions for Green Hydrogen Transition, SIGHT, is the financial core. The page proposes two mechanisms through 2029-30, with an outlay of ₹17,490 crore: an incentive for electrolyser manufacturing, and an incentive for green hydrogen production. It also says that competitive bidding for hydrogen and its derivatives is to require equipment approved by the Government of India on stated quality and performance criteria. MNRE’s [schemes list](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/) later splits that idea into dated guidelines, including electrolyser manufacturing tranches and Mode-2A and Mode-2B production guidelines in January 2024.

## Pilots, research, and skills are other lines

The mission page separates pilot ceilings from the SIGHT outlay. It lists ₹455 crore through 2029-30 for low-carbon steel, ₹496 crore through 2025-26 for mobility, and ₹115 crore through 2025-26 for shipping, and it names biomass, storage, and decentralised uses as further areas. Research is the Strategic Hydrogen Innovation Partnership, a public-private framework with a dedicated fund. Skills are a programme to be run with the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship. Standards are a coordination task so that Indian rules can sit next to international ones.

## Who decides

An Empowered Group chaired by the Cabinet Secretary guides the Mission and includes secretaries and industry experts. An Advisory Group chaired by the Principal Scientific Adviser advises on science and technology. A Mission Secretariat at MNRE implements. The initial outlay on the page is ₹19,744 crore: ₹17,490 crore for SIGHT, ₹1,466 crore for pilot projects, ₹400 crore for research and development, and ₹388 crore for other components.

## Where the hub rule changed in public

The 2023 page says the Mission will identify regions for large-scale production or use, support hub infrastructure, and set up at least two hubs in the initial phase, with ₹400 crore through 2025-26 for hubs and other projects. On 10 October 2025, the Press Information Bureau reported a different operating rule. Revised guidelines for Hydrogen Valley Innovation Clusters and Green Hydrogen Hubs, issued on 27 June 2025, appear on the schemes list. Under Component B2, MNRE may recognise a location as a hub without direct financial assistance, so that projects in the zone can seek other central or state benefits. Using that route, the competent authority recognised the Deendayal, V.O. Chidambaranar, and Paradip port areas. The [release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2177591) also says the Mission uses clusters because long-distance hydrogen transport is difficult.

That is the shift a policy reader can cite. The hub idea stayed. Recognition of those three ports, on the October 2025 text, does not come with a direct Mission grant attached to the recognition itself. Later parliamentary answers describe separate port projects, including a Paradip jetty approved for public-private partnership. Those project approvals are not the same instrument as Component B2 recognition.

The enabling framework on the mission page is about power delivery: a waiver of interstate transmission charges for renewable energy used to make green hydrogen, renewable-energy banking, and time-bound open access and connectivity. Those are conditions for cheap electrons. They are not a hydrogen price.

Great Indian Company keeps the 2023 architecture and the 2025 recognition rule in one place so a reader does not treat a hub announcement as a cheque already issued.
