# MSME bank credit is outgrowing the overall loan book

Outstanding credit from scheduled commercial banks to micro, small, and medium enterprises reached **₹36.8 lakh crore** at end-December 2025. That was **23.5%** higher than a year earlier, when the stock was **₹29.8 lakh crore** and growth was **18.3%**. The Reserve Bank’s Annual Report for 2025-26 publishes the figures from priority-sector returns. That 23.5% sits above the system-wide rates printed around it: bank credit growth of 12.5% in 2024-25 once a merger is excluded, and non-food credit growth of 15.9% at end-March 2026. The MSME series is the faster one on those published comparisons. The December and March dates are not the same fortnight.

The longer stock series in the same table:

| Position | MSME accounts (lakh) | Amount outstanding (₹ lakh crore) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2023-24 | 257.0 | 27.3 |
| 2024-25 | 243.1 | 31.1 |
| End-December 2024 | 235.1 | 29.8 |
| End-December 2025 | 234.7 | 36.8 |

The amount rose. The number of accounts did not. End-December 2025 had 234.7 lakh accounts against 235.1 lakh a year earlier. The expansion is larger balances, not a rush of new loan accounts.

## Micro, small, and medium are not one borrower

At end-December 2025 the stock split this way:

- Micro enterprises: **217.4 lakh** accounts and **₹18.3 lakh crore**, from 216.5 lakh accounts and ₹14.4 lakh crore a year earlier.
- Small enterprises: **13.9 lakh** accounts and **₹11.5 lakh crore**, from 14.8 lakh accounts and ₹9.4 lakh crore.
- Medium enterprises: **3.4 lakh** accounts and **₹6.9 lakh crore**, from 3.8 lakh accounts and ₹5.9 lakh crore.

Micro enterprises are almost the entire account count and about half the rupees. Small and medium enterprises are fewer accounts and a rising share of the amount. Small and medium account counts fell even as outstanding credit rose. For an operator, that pattern means the average reported relationship got larger. It does not mean more enterprises entered the counted book.

## A rule change at the small end

From **1 April 2026**, scheduled commercial banks are not to take collateral on loans to micro and small enterprises up to **₹20 lakh**. The previous ceiling was **₹10 lakh**. The Annual Report states the change as a way to improve formal credit for enterprises with limited collateral. It is a supply rule. The December 2025 stock above was compiled before the higher limit took effect, so the ₹36.8 lakh crore figure does not yet show its result.

The Bank’s agenda for 2026-27 includes a review of the framework for revival and rehabilitation of MSMEs, aimed at earlier resolution of stress. That review sits next to the collateral change: easier entry at the small-ticket end, and a closer look at how stress is handled once a loan is on the book.

## Do not mix this table with “industry” credit

The monthly sectoral deployment release talks about micro, small, medium, and large **industries**. That is a different cut from the priority-sector MSME table, which covers industry and services.

On 31 March 2026 the sectoral release said credit to micro and small industries, and to medium industries, continued a robust expansion, and large industry strengthened. On 31 July 2026 the wording shifted: large and medium industries accelerated, while micro and small industries showed steady growth. Industry as a whole grew 20.0% year on year by 31 July, against 6.5% a year earlier. The release does not publish a separate percentage for micro and small industry in that paragraph. “Steady” is the Bank’s adjective, not a rate a reader should invent.

Priority-sector lending overall was **45.0%** of adjusted net bank credit, or the credit equivalent of off-balance-sheet exposure, as on 31 March 2026. The amount was **₹75.7 lakh crore**, against ₹67.3 lakh crore and 43.6% in 2024-25. Every bank group met the 40% overall target. MSME credit is only one part of that priority-sector total.

## What an operator can take from it

A small firm comparing its own limit with “credit growth” should use the MSME stock, not the 19.1% non-food headline. The official MSME series was growing faster than the system through December 2025, concentrated in larger outstanding amounts per account, and the collateral-free threshold has since doubled to ₹20 lakh. By July 2026 the industrial-credit commentary had tilted toward medium and large borrowers. Those two facts can both be true, because they describe different returns.
