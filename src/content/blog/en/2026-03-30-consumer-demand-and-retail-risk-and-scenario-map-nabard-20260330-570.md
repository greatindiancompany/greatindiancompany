# Food, fuel, and the inflation risks around household demand

A scenario for Indian consumer demand is useful when each branch names the official number that would confirm it. The 5 August 2026 Monetary Policy Committee resolution already named the branches. This brief does not add probabilities the committee did not publish.

The resolution kept the repo rate at 5.25% and the stance neutral. It described private consumption in the first quarter as robust on the high-frequency indicators then available, and it projected real GDP growth for 2026-27 at 6.7%. It then listed the risks that could bend household demand.

## Three branches, and the print that belongs to each

**Food and the monsoon.** The committee said a deficient and uneven south-west monsoon, with El Niño conditions, posed a risk to agriculture and to rural demand, even with supply measures and government crop programmes in place. The confirming prints are rural CPI and the Consumer Food Price Index, then the agricultural GVA line in the next national-accounts release. August 2026 already showed provisional rural inflation at 5.23% and food inflation at 5.95%, against combined CPI of 4.82%. The MPC, writing in early August, had projected the year’s inflation high in Q3, at 5.9%.

**Fuel and pass-through.** The resolution said the June rise in headline inflation, to 4.4%, came primarily from food and fuel, including retail-price revisions after the spike in international energy prices, with some pass-through into categories such as restaurant charges. Core inflation stayed at 3.9% in May and June. A fuel-led branch would show up as a higher fuel component inside CPI while core, and especially core excluding precious metals (2.3–2.5% in that window), stays subdued. The committee’s full-year core projection was 4.3%.

**Contained demand pressure.** The resolution said core excluding precious metals suggested demand pressures remained contained, and that the continuing effect of GST rationalisation, plus stable employment conditions, should support urban demand. The confirming prints on this branch are real PFCE holding up in the 30 November national-accounts release, and core inflation that does not broaden. April–June real PFCE had already been estimated, on 31 August, at 7.1% growth. That print arrived after the resolution and is the baseline the October meeting inherited.

What would cut across all three is a revision. MoSPI marks the August CPI provisional and flags quarterly GDP as open to later source-data revisions. A scenario note dated off the 5 August text should say so.
