# The medium-term path of India's food processing schemes

The medium term in this sector is the life of three schemes, not a five-year guess about consumption. PMKSY has already been restructured once. PMFME is near its coverage target and past the financial year written into its original five-year design. The production-linked incentive is in its last published year. The 2026-27 Budget keeps all three on the appropriation. A successor scheme, with a new name and a new outlay, had not been set out in those documents.

## 2016 to 2021: one umbrella, then a sales incentive

PMKSY was launched on 3 May 2017 as the umbrella for infrastructure schemes the ministry was already running. The Annual Report for 2024-25 says it was approved with Rs 6,000 crore for 2016-20, aligned to the 14th Finance Commission, and then extended through 2020-21. Mega food parks and cold chain are older than the umbrella. Both date from 2008 in the ministry's account.

In March 2021 the Cabinet added a different instrument. PLISFPI, with Rs 10,900 crore, runs from 2021-22 to 2026-27. It pays for manufacturing scale and for Indian brands in foreign retail, not for a cold store at the farm gate. PMFME had started in June 2020, aimed at 2,00,000 micro units, with Rs 10,000 crore.

By the start of the 15th Finance Commission cycle the ministry had decided that not every old component deserved new sanctions. Mega food parks, backward and forward linkages, and skill and HACCP work stayed only for commitments. Cold chain, processing capacity, clusters, testing labs, research, and Operation Greens stayed open.

## 2025: a larger PMKSY envelope, still inside the same cycle

On 31 July 2025 the Cabinet set the PMKSY outlay for 2021-22 to 2025-26 at Rs 6,520 crore, including an additional Rs 1,920 crore. Part of that addition, Rs 1,000 crore, is tied to 50 irradiation units and 100 NABL-accredited food testing labs. The annual report's earlier figure of Rs 5,520 crore (Rs 4,600 crore plus Rs 920 crore) is the account published before that Cabinet decision. The trajectory is an expansion of the same scheme, not a replacement of it.

Physical progress reported on 9 January 2026 shows a stock that is mostly built. Of 1,618 PMKSY projects approved since the components began, 1,185 were operational, with processing and preservation capacity of 270.51 lakh metric tonnes. The cold-chain chapter, dated 31 December 2024, is consistent with a mature book: 286 of 394 projects completed, 108 still being implemented. The medium-term question for PMKSY is completion and use of assets, plus the new irradiation and lab slice, more than a fresh wave of parks.

## Micro units are close to the numerical target

PMFME's target is 2,00,000 enterprises. The January 2026 review counts 1,72,707 credit-linked loans sanctioned since inception, of which 56,543 came since January 2025. That is progress against a coverage target. It is not a statement that each loan is a surviving business. The scheme was written through 2024-25 and then extended through 2025-26. Seed capital released since inception, Rs 1,282.98 crore for 3.76 lakh self-help group members, is the parallel track for the smallest producers.

Demand No. 45 still provides Rs 1,700 crore for PMFME in 2026-27. That appropriation continues the money one year beyond the extension the review describes. It does not, in the budget note, announce a second five-year mission.

## PLI is in its payout years

Reported investment under PLI is Rs 9,702 crore, with incentives of Rs 2,162.553 crore disbursed in 161 of 170 approved cases, and reported employment of 3.4 lakh. The 2026-27 budget estimate for the scheme is Rs 1,200 crore, the same as the 2025-26 budget and revised estimates. The scheme page's objective remains the one set in 2021: manufacturing champions in ready-to-cook and ready-to-eat foods, processed fruits and vegetables, marine products, and mozzarella, plus innovative and organic products and brands abroad. The millet component was added from savings inside that outlay.

After 2026-27, the published PLI calendar ends. Nothing in Demand No. 45 states a second PLI series for food processing.

## How to talk about the next few years

The path that is documented runs like this. Infrastructure grants are mostly in the ground, with a defined unfinished set and a new lab-and-irradiation tranche. Micro-enterprise formalisation is within sight of 2,00,000 sanctioned loans and still depends on banks. Large-firm incentives have a last budgeted year. Value added and the processed share of agri-food exports have risen over the decade the ministry cites, which is the economic backdrop, not a scheme target. A claim about what replaces these three after their published windows is a claim the budget and the year-end review have not yet made.
