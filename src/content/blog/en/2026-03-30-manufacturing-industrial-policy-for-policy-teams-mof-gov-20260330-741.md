# How DPIIT, ministries, and states divide industrial policy

A policy team that writes one "industrial policy note" for all of India will blur three mandates that the official record keeps apart. The practical question is which office can change the rule you are worried about.

## What DPIIT is actually assigned

DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 says the department, renamed in January 2019, is responsible for industrial policy at the Centre. The allocation includes the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951, industrial licences and Industrial Entrepreneur Memoranda, intellectual property, FDI promotion, internal trade, startups, and, from November 2021, integrated logistics. Make in India, launched on 25 September 2014, sits here as a cross-government mandate: the report says central ministries and state governments carry the activities, with 24 sub-sectors chosen for a closer push. The report is the [DPIIT Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/3d9c9c2daeefb97bb9ce964370938b71.pdf).

DPIIT also writes instruments that look like industrial policy and bind only its own domain. Quality control orders for light engineering, consumer industry, cement, paper, rubber, leather and footwear, and explosives are DPIIT orders. The public procurement preference order is a DPIIT-issued order under the General Financial Rules, last amended on 19 July 2024, for central procuring entities. A state tender is not automatically inside that order. A drug or a telecom product is not inside a DPIIT quality order just because DPIIT coordinates PLI.

## What DPIIT does not implement

The 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf) is explicit. Administrative ministries implement PLI. DPIIT coordinates and monitors. The sector table of 21 July 2026 shows white goods as the DPIIT-marked scheme and assigns the other thirteen lines elsewhere ([PIB PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008)). A review that asks DPIIT to "fix" a solar-module claim or a steel claim is addressed to the wrong implementing officer. The Empowered Group of Secretaries, chaired by the Cabinet Secretary, is the cross-cutting forum the July note describes. It can take a blockage that no single ministry will own. It is not a substitute for the scheme guideline.

MSME cluster programmes are a third centre of gravity. The same Lok Sabha reply lists the Micro and Small Enterprises Cluster Development Programme, the procurement policy for MSEs, and tool rooms as Ministry of MSME instruments. A DPIIT monitoring note on PLI will not tell you whether a cluster programme is funded.

## What only a state can do

Corridor nodes are joint ventures. The 30 August 2024 note describes Gujarat's majority in the Dholera SPV, Maharashtra's corporation at AURIC, and Greater Noida's authority as an equal partner ([PIB PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136)). Land, local clearances, and the state support agreement are state instruments. The Centre's equity or debt through the corridor trust does not municipalise the park. The India Industrial Land Bank is a DPIIT database for seeing areas. Rating parks, through the Industrial Park Rating System, gives states a gap list. Neither tool changes a state's power tariff or stamp duty.

FDI policy is the piece policy teams most often file under "industry" and then find in a press note. The 28 July reply walks through liberalisation since 2019 and the 2026 changes for land-border investment in a short list of manufacturing activities, plus Press Note 3 of 2026 on export e-commerce. Those are DPIIT press notes. They are not PLI guidelines. A cabinet note that mixes them will be implemented by different officers on different clocks.

The clean division of labour is: DPIIT for the frame, the procurement order, its own quality orders, white goods PLI, corridor coordination, and FDI press notes; the line ministry for every other PLI claim; the state for land and the park; the Ministry of MSME for the cluster programmes named in the reply.
