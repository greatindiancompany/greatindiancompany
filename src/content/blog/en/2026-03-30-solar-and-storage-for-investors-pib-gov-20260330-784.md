# How an Investor Should Underwrite Indian Solar and Storage

An Indian solar pitch often adds a factory, a power plant, and a battery into one story about returns. The official schemes pay those three assets on three schedules. Underwriting starts by refusing the blend.

## Factories: cash follows sales, after commissioning

MNRE's PLI page, updated 8 August 2023, sets an outlay of Rs 24,000 crore. The incentive runs for five years after the plant is commissioned, and it is paid on modules manufactured and sold. Technologies that perform better are incentivised more. Integrated plants are an explicit objective.

Tranche I, through IREDA, awarded 8,737 MW of fully integrated capacity to three bidders in November and December 2021, inside Rs 4,500 crore. Tranche II, through SECI, awarded 39,600 MW of fully or partially integrated capacity to 11 bidders in April 2023, inside an outlay of Rs 19,500 crore. SECI's solar page says about Rs 13,937.535 crore of PLI was allocated to those 11 firms, still over five years after commissioning.

Questions that belong in the model: which tranche, fully or partially integrated, and whether commissioning has been reported by the implementing agency. The August 2023 ministry page does not report incentive paid. Do not insert a disbursement the page does not contain. Also check ALMM. MNRE's list page says government schemes, open access, and net-metering must use List-I modules, and that a cell list has existed since 31 July 2025. A factory's offtake into those channels depends on enlistment as well as on the PLI award.

## Power plants: the contract is the award, the revenue is the commissioned plant

SECI, as of 1 May 2025, had awarded over 73.8 GW, including 45.9 GW of solar. As of March 2025 it reported 21.67 GW of solar commissioned. The investment is in the gap and in the product. A round-the-clock or firm-and-dispatchable contract is not a plain solar energy contract. SECI says it signs the power purchase agreement with the developer and the power sale agreement with the distribution company, and that this intermediary role is where payment security sits.

Own-and-operate is a smaller, separate exposure. As of 1 May 2025 SECI reported 122.7 MW operational on its own books, 425 MW under construction, and 900 MW under tendering, with a target of 10 GW of its own projects awarded by 2030. That target is SECI's statement about itself, not a developer pipeline you can book.

The solar-park line is infrastructure around the plant: 53 parks and 39,323 MW allocated, in a scheme aimed at land and transmission being ready before the developer builds. Allocated park capacity is not equity value.

## Batteries: the grant rate changed, and the first milestone did not clear

PIB release 2118325 of 3 April 2025 is the underwriting note. Support is 10 percent at financial closure, 45 percent at commercial operation, and 15 percent a year for three years. The rate used now in that note is Rs 46 lakh per MWh or 30 percent of capital cost, whichever is lower, down from an earlier estimate of Rs 96 lakh per MWh. The scheme covers 13,200 MWh inside Rs 3,760 crore, up from 4,000 MWh, because prices fell. In 2024-25, financial closure did not happen, and expenditure was nil.

A battery model should use the lower rate, should haircut or exclude the grant until closure is documented, and should not treat 13,200 MWh as operating capacity. CEA's planning figure, quoted by MNRE as 236.22 GWh of batteries in 2031-32, is a system requirement, not a project pipeline with tariffs.

## One page an investment committee can use

Three columns. Asset. Milestone that starts cash. Figure that is an outlay or an award, with its date, kept out of the revenue line. Module incentive: commissioning, then sales, five years. SECI project: award, then commissioning, then a sale agreement. Battery grant: financial closure, and only at the revised rate.
