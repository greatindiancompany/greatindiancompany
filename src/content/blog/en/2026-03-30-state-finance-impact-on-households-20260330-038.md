# Capex, pensions, and the household side of a state budget

Households meet a state budget in three places: a tax or a fee at the counter, a subsidy or a service in the month, and a road, a hospital, or a water scheme that shows up years after the appropriation. The Reserve Bank's study of state budgets for 2025-26, released on 23 January 2026, separates those places into capital expenditure and revenue expenditure. Mixing them is how a "record capex" line gets read as a larger pension or a lower electricity bill.

## Two kinds of spending

Capital expenditure was 2.7 per cent of GDP in 2023-24 and 2.7 per cent in 2024-25. States have budgeted 3.2 per cent of GDP for 2025-26. The Bank describes that as a sustained capital thrust. Chart II.7 breaks capital outlay into major components. For a household, capital outlay is the public asset: a road that cuts a commute, a hospital wing, a water scheme. It arrives late, and only if the budget estimate becomes a completed work.

Revenue expenditure is the monthly machine. Chart II.5 shows its components. Inside it sit wages and salaries (Statement 29), interest payments (Statement 13), pensions, subsidies (Statement 36), and operations and maintenance (Statement 30). The press release calls salaries, pensions, and their kin committed expenditure when it discusses ageing states. Committed expenditure is paid because a past law, a past wage agreement, or a past bond says so. It is the part of the budget a household that holds a government pension, or pays the interest bill through taxes, feels first.

The gross fiscal deficit can widen because capital spending rose, while the revenue account is in surplus. It can also widen because interest and subsidies outran receipts. Statement 2 is the revenue balance. Statement 4 decomposes the gross fiscal deficit. Those two statements are the household-relevant split. The consolidated deficit was 3.3 per cent of GDP in 2024-25 and is budgeted at 3.3 per cent for 2025-26, and the Bank says the move through 3 per cent mainly reflects interest-free capital loans above the normal borrowing ceiling. That composition matters: a deficit that is a capital loan has a different path into daily life than a deficit that is a revenue gap.

## Taxes and subsidies at the kitchen table

Stamp duty and registration fees fall on a home purchase. Box II.1 of the study compares how efficiently states raise that head. State excise falls on alcoholic liquor. Electricity duty and power subsidies land in the bill from the distribution company; Statement 36 is the subsidy table, and it will not match a single family's monthly bill. Motor vehicle tax falls on a two-wheeler or a car at registration. State GST is inside the price of most goods and services. None of these rates is uniform, and this brief does not invent a household rupee figure. The state's finance act and the tariff order are the price list. The Reserve Bank statements show whether that price list is a large share of the state's receipts or a small one.

Social services are the other monthly contact. Statements 26 and 27 report education and health spending as shares of expenditure. Statements 31, 32, and 37 cover the wider social sector, including water supply and sanitation. A higher share is a budget priority. It becomes a working school or a stocked clinic only after the state appoints staff and pays them, which is revenue expenditure, and after it builds the building, which is capital outlay.

## Why the state's age structure is a household fact

The theme of the 2025-26 study is demographic transition. The Bank says ageing states face fiscal pressure from a shrinking tax base and from rising committed expenditure, and that they need revenue capacity plus reforms in healthcare, pensions, and workforce policy. Youthful states still have room to invest in human capital. Intermediate states are in between. A pension promise and a school-building programme are both household policies. They load different lines of the same budget, and they load different states differently. Chart III.3 and the tables on old-age dependency are the study's map of that split.

Outstanding liabilities, budgeted at 29.2 per cent of GDP at end-March 2026, are the stock that turns into next year's interest. Interest is paid before a new subsidy is designed. That sequence is the practical meaning of a debt ratio for a household that depends on a state service.
