# How PMAY-Urban 2.0 Replaced the Closed Credit Subsidy

## One ministry, two housing regimes

The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs has run Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana – Urban since 25 June 2015. The launch that day, recorded by the Press Information Bureau, sat beside the Smart Cities Mission and the Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation. The housing scheme’s aim, restated in a Lok Sabha reply published by PIB on 24 July 2025, is an all-weather pucca house with basic civic amenities for eligible urban beneficiaries.

The first regime had four verticals: Beneficiary Led Construction, Affordable Housing in Partnership, In-Situ Slum Redevelopment, and the Credit Linked Subsidy Scheme. CLSS is the piece that has closed. The same July 2025 reply says the interest subsidy was up to ₹2.67 lakh a house for economically weaker, low-income, and middle-income beneficiaries. CLSS for the middle-income group ended on 31 March 2021. CLSS for economically weaker and low-income beneficiaries ended on 31 March 2022. It was a central sector scheme, run through the National Housing Bank, the State Bank of India, and the Housing and Urban Development Corporation. Banks and housing finance companies took the application. PIB records that ₹58,885 crore of interest subsidy reached 25,04,220 beneficiaries.

## What the Cabinet put in its place

The Union Cabinet approved PMAY-Urban 2.0 on 9 August 2024. A PIB background note circulated in December 2024 states the design: financial assistance for 1 crore additional urban poor and middle-class families to construct, purchase, or rent a house; government assistance of ₹2.30 lakh crore; an investment figure of ₹10 lakh crore. The mission took effect on 1 September 2024, for five years, as the July 2025 reply describes it.

The four verticals are now Beneficiary Led Construction, Affordable Housing in Partnership, Affordable Rental Housing, and the Interest Subsidy Scheme. In-situ slum redevelopment and CLSS are not the 2.0 list. A household chooses one vertical.

The December 2024 note sets government assistance under the partnership and beneficiary-led verticals at ₹2.50 lakh a unit. Central assistance inside that amount is ₹2.25 lakh (90 percent) for the North-Eastern region, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir, Puducherry, and Delhi; ₹2.50 lakh (100 percent) for other Union Territories; and ₹1.50 lakh (60 percent) for other states. That unit assistance is not the interest subsidy. Mixing the two numbers produces a false product.

## The new subsidy is narrower than CLSS

Under the Interest Subsidy Scheme, the July 2025 reply states a maximum subsidy of ₹1.80 lakh for a beneficiary with income up to ₹9 lakh. The eligible home loan is up to ₹25 lakh, on a property up to ₹35 lakh, with carpet area up to 120 square metres. The subsidy is 4 percent on the first ₹8 lakh, for a tenure up to 12 years, released in five yearly instalments. The loan must still be active, and more than half the principal must still be outstanding, when an instalment is released. Applications go through the unified portal at pmay-urban.gov.in.

Affordable Rental Housing, in the December 2024 note, is aimed at working women, industrial workers, urban migrants, people who are homeless or destitute, students, and similar groups. The note also names groups for special attention: safai karamcharis, street vendors identified under PM SVANidhi, artisans under PM Vishwakarma, anganwadi workers, building and other construction workers, and residents of slums and chawls.

## Conditions the first regime did not write this way

States and Union Territories were told, in that December 2024 note, to prepare an Affordable Housing Policy by March 2025 and to finish a demand survey by 31 March 2025. The same note said in-principle approval then covered more than 6 lakh houses, from 29 States and Union Territories that had signed memoranda with the ministry. This brief does not update those two administrative facts past December 2024. A later sanction figure has to come from a later MoHUA or PIB table.

The policy change to underwrite is therefore specific. The open-ended CLSS window is shut. The successor subsidy is capped by income, loan, property value, carpet area, and a principal test. Partnership and beneficiary-led construction carry a published per-unit assistance with a state-wise central share. Rental housing is a vertical of its own.

The market reading of those caps is in the [market signal brief](/blog/urbanization-and-housing-market-signal-brief-railministry-gov-20260330-200). The capital reading is in the [investment implications](/blog/urbanization-and-housing-investment-implications-railministry-gov-20260330-240).
