# Who Sets the Rules for India's Digital Public Infrastructure

## The stack does not have one regulator

The Press Information Bureau research note of 6 March 2026 and the Digital India pages put different institutions on different layers. Treating digital public infrastructure as a single licence does not match how these systems are described. This brief does not predict a new statute. None is named in the sources used here.

## Identity, documents, and government APIs

Aadhaar is the biometric identity system in the note, with more than 144 crore numbers generated as of March 2026. The note's footnote for that count points to the UIDAI dashboard. In 2024-25 the note records over 2,707 crore authentication transactions.

DigiLocker, launched in 2015, is the document wallet: 67.63 crore users as of 5 March 2026, and over 950 crore documents issued by March 2026. The note describes access as consent-based. It does not restate the consent rules section by section.

API Setu was initiated by MeitY in March 2020 as the open API platform. The note's March 2026 catalogue is 8,036 APIs, with 6,592 consumers, 2,559 publishers, and 10,530 organisations. Digital India itself is a MeitY programme, launched on 1 July 2015, with three vision areas on the programme about page.

## Payments statistics and consented financial data

UPI figures in the note are footnoted to NPCI's UPI product statistics. The operating facts stated there are 691 banks live and, for January 2026, 21.70 billion transactions. This brief does not add a legal commentary the note does not contain.

A separate Department of Financial Services page covers the account-aggregator framework. It states that the Reserve Bank of India issued the Non-Banking Financial Company – Account Aggregator Master Direction on 2 September 2016. Account aggregators are NBFCs that retrieve or collect a customer's financial information. The page says no information is retrieved, shared, or transferred without the customer's explicit consent, and that registering is voluntary.

As of 31 March 2026 the page reports 17 companies holding a certificate of registration, 179 institutions live as financial information providers, and 989 live as financial information users. It also reports more than 2.88 billion financial accounts enabled to share and 284.6 million accounts linked by users. The page attributes those progress figures to Sahamati. They are not in the March PIB note, and they are not re-checked here against Sahamati's own file.

## Sector systems stay with sector ministries

In the note, eSanjeevani, eHospital, and the national non-communicable disease platform sit with the health ministry. Aarogya Setu is described as having become a national health app on the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission. DIKSHA sits with NCERT and the education ministry. e-Courts sits with the Department of Justice under the Ministry of Law and Justice. POSHAN Tracker sits with the Ministry of Women and Child Development and was built through the National e-Governance Division. ONDC and Government eMarketplace are described on the commerce and industry side of the note's references.

## The picture on record

The split on record is identity and government APIs with MeitY and UIDAI, UPI statistics published through NPCI, account aggregation under the Reserve Bank direction cited by the finance ministry page, and health, education, justice, and nutrition with their own ministries. A later outlook would require a named draft or a named effective date. These pages do not provide one.
