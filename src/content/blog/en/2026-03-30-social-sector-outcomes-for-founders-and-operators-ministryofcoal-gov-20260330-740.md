# How Founders and Operators Should Read India’s Social Outcome Data

A founder reading India’s social outcomes needs a filter, not a bigger slide. The filter is: which series, which geography, who the user is, and which number did not move. The figures below are from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, the Ministry of Women and Child Development, the Ministry of Education, and the Ministry of Rural Development. They are not a market model.

## Do not sell a nutrition turnaround off one line

Stunting in children under five fell from 35.5% in NFHS-5 to 29.3% in NFHS-6. That is a real change, and the ministry’s “17%” headline is the relative drop on the old base, about six points, not seventeen points. In the same fact sheet, wasting is 19.0% against 19.3%, underweight is 31.8% against 32.1%, and an adequate diet at 6-23 months is 15.3% against 11.0%. Exclusive breastfeeding under six months is 55.8%, down from 63.7%, while the share currently breastfeeding is 95.6%. A product story that says “malnutrition has turned” is using the best line and hiding three others.

Rural stunting is 30.9% and urban is 23.9%. Gujarat’s rural rate is 39.5% against an urban 28.2%. Jharkhand’s rural rate is 37.4% against an urban 20.3%. A national SKU and a single price will not match those households. Bihar’s stunting is 35.6%, Kerala’s is 20.1%, and Madhya Pradesh improved on stunting while its underweight rate rose from 33.0% to 39.7%. State selection is part of the product decision. The compendium leaves out Manipur.

## The user is the worker, and the ration is identified

As of 31 July 2026 the Poshan Tracker network is 13.30 lakh Anganwadi workers and almost 8.94 crore beneficiaries. Facial recognition is how take-home ration is tied to the registered person. If your distribution step cannot survive that check, you are not in the public channel. Hot cooked meals, menu boards, and committee oversight are the September 2026 operating push. Build for a centre kitchen and a home visit, including the 34-milestone scheduler the ministry has asked states to roll out, not only for a consumer brand.

## Schools: the average ratio is the wrong constraint

UDISE+ 2025-26 reports pupil-teacher ratios of 10, 12, 17, and 21. Secondary retention is 51.9%. Secondary gross enrolment is 71.7%. Single-teacher schools number 1,00,843. Computers are in 69.9% of schools, so about three in ten are without one. A learning product that assumes a device, a second teacher, and a child still enrolled at secondary is assuming away the census. This release is not a test-score release. Do not imply one.

## Health demand is coverage plus a private operative-birth rate

Household coverage by a health insurance or financing scheme is 60.2%, from 41.0%. Institutional births are 90.6%. The public-facility share of births is 58.6%, down from 61.9%. Caesarean births are 27.2% overall and 54.1% in private facilities. An operator in delivery care or insurance is looking at a wide insured base and a birth that is slightly less often in a public facility than in the previous survey. Completion still lags contact: iron and folic acid for 180 days is 37.8%, and full vaccination on cards is 87.1%.

## Local wages just reset

From 1 July 2026 no notified rural wage under the new guarantee Act is below ₹300, and the national average notified wage is ₹327.4, from ₹298.8. Field teams, kitchen staff, and worksite childcare (required when five or more children under five are present) are priced against that floor in rural areas. Last year’s MGNREGS wage card is not the card to use.
