Placed in the World Bank’s Global Findex, India’s financial inclusion story is an ownership story that has run ahead of use. The comparison below uses the published survey, not a recast of RBI’s index. The FI-Index at 70.0 for March 2026 is a domestic composite. Findex is a household questionnaire, fielded so that countries can be lined up. They should stay in separate sentences.

## Ownership

The executive summary of [Global Findex 2025](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/042c3272-9a73-43f3-b117-985482acd7f8/content) says 79 percent of adults worldwide had an account at a bank or similar institution, with a mobile-money provider, or both, up from 74 percent in 2021. Table B.1 reports 89 percent for India in 2024. The barriers chapter rounds India’s rate to 90 percent when it explains why India is reported on its own: the sample of adults without accounts is small, but India’s sample is large enough to publish. The [2021 India country brief](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/4c4fe6db0fd7a7521a70a39ac518d74b-0050062022/original/Findex2021-India-Country-Brief.pdf) had put ownership at 78 percent, up from 35 percent in 2011 and little changed from 2017. The 2021-to-2024 move is the recent step; the long jump was earlier, and the [trend page](/blog/financial-inclusion-long-term-trend-20260330-047) keeps those dates in order.

On gender, the 2025 report says women and men in India are equally likely to have an account. In South Asia it groups India with Nepal and Sri Lanka as economies whose ownership gaps are not statistically significant, and it contrasts that with Bangladesh at 20 percentage points and Pakistan at 30. The global ownership gap in the same report is 4 percentage points, and 5 points in low- and middle-income economies. India’s labour-force gap in account ownership is 6 percentage points, which the report flags as small next to the rest of South Asia.

## Use

Use is where India sits apart from its ownership rate.

- Table B.1: 48 percent of Indian adults made or received a digital payment, 27 percent saved formally, and 15 percent borrowed formally. In low- and middle-income economies the report puts formal saving at 40 percent of adults and formal borrowing at 24 percent, and digital payments at 62 percent of adults, or 82 percent of account owners.
- Among account owners, 54 percent in India made or received a digital payment. South Asia as a region is at 57 percent of account owners. Every other region in that comparison is above 80 percent.
- The gender gap in digital-payment use among account owners is about 13 percentage points in India, in favour of men. The 2021 country brief had a related but different figure: 35 percent of adults used digital payments, with a 13 point gap between men and women. The denominator changed; the gap did not close.
- Inactivity: 16 percent of Indian account owners had no active account, against 4 percent of account owners in other low- and middle-income economies. In the 2021 brief, 35 percent of accounts were inactive.
- The most common reason an unbanked adult in India gives is that a family member already has an account. In most other low- and middle-income economies the leading reason is lack of money.
- Phone ownership in Table B.1 is 66 percent in India. The same report says 86 percent of adults worldwide own a mobile phone. AePS can authenticate with a biometric at an agent, so a phone is not required for every withdrawal, but a large share of digital use still assumes one.

India’s large population also means a high ownership rate still leaves a large absolute number of adults without accounts. The report says the five countries with the most unbanked adults have been the same since 2011 — Bangladesh, China, India, Indonesia, and Pakistan — even after India’s ownership rate rose.

RBI’s index can keep rising on Usage, as it did into March 2026, while these survey gaps remain the right external check.
