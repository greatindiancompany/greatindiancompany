## The target and the latest official level

NEP 2020 says the gross enrolment ratio in higher education, including vocational education, should rise from 26.3% in 2018 to 50% by 2035. It also says there should be at least one large multidisciplinary higher-education institution in or near every district by 2030, and a 100% gross enrolment ratio from pre-school through secondary school by 2030.

The latest level this brief can cite for higher education is in a Lok Sabha reply on 10 August 2026. The Minister of State for Education said the gross enrolment ratio rose from 23.7 in 2014-15 to 30 in 2023-24. Female participation rose from 22.9 to 31.2 over the same years. Total enrolment rose from 3.42 crore to 4.50 crore, which the reply describes as more than 1.08 crore additional students. The reply does not publish a separate male ratio.

From 26.3% in 2018 to 30 in 2023-24 is movement toward the 50% aim. The remaining gap, on those two official numbers, is 20 percentage points, with 2035 still the policy date. This page does not convert that gap into a required annual growth rate. That would add an assumption about the 18–23 population which the reply does not supply.

## Dates that have already arrived

One NEP date is no longer in the future relative to September 2026. The policy says that by 2025 at least 50% of learners in school and higher education should have exposure to vocational education. The August 2026 replies used here do not publish a national percentage of learners who received that exposure. The aim can be restated. It cannot be marked achieved or missed from these texts.

The school trajectory still runs to 2030 for universal pre-school-to-secondary enrolment. The only grade-wise ratios in hand are the ones printed in NEP 2020 itself: 90.9% in Grades 6–8, 79.3% in Grades 9–10, and 56.5% in Grades 11–12, plus 3.22 crore out-of-school children aged 6–17 in the NSSO 75th round of 2017-18. They show why the policy treats secondary retention as unfinished. They are not a 2026 school census.

## The skilling timeline beside the enrolment timeline

Short-term skilling has its own clock. The Skill India Programme’s Cabinet outlay covers 2022-23 to 2025-26. On 3 August 2026 Parliament was told that PMKVY 4.0 is extended for two years beyond 31 March 2026. Credit mobility is the bridge the education ministry points to: the National Credit Framework, already adopted by more than 700 universities and over 7,500 colleges in AISHE 2023-24, is how vocational learning is supposed to count inside the 2035 enrolment aim. The policy says vocational students are to be included when the gross enrolment ratio is calculated. The 30 figure for 2023-24 is the published ratio. The reply does not split out how much of it is vocational.
