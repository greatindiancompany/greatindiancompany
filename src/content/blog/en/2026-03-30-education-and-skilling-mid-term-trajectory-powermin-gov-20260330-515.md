Mid-term, for this system, means looking at goals that have a year on them and asking what the latest official number says. Some of those years are still ahead. One important vocational year has already passed. The foundational-learning year is the academic year now underway.

## Goals whose dates have arrived or are this year

The [National Education Policy](https://www.education.gov.in/nep/nep2020_final_eng.pdf) says that by 2025 at least 50% of learners in the school and higher education system shall have exposure to vocational education. September 2026 is past that date. The policy required an action plan with targets and timelines. This brief has not found, in the releases used here, an official statistic that says the 50% exposure mark was met or missed. What does exist is a different indicator: the skill ministry's statement that the share of youth aged 15–29 who are vocationally trained rose from 7.1% in 2017-18 to 26.1% in 2023-24. That can be read as movement in vocational reach among young people. It cannot be read as a score against the exposure target, because the populations differ. The honest mid-term statement is that the target date has passed and the matching indicator has not been published in these sources.

Foundational literacy is on a different clock. The policy text says every student should attain it by Grade 3, and sets a primary-school horizon of 2025. NIPUN Bharat, launched on 5 July 2021, aims at universal foundational literacy and numeracy by the end of Grade 3 in 2026-27. The academic year 2026-27 is that mission year. UDISE+ can tell you that teachers now exceed one crore and that foundational pupil-teacher ratio is 10. It does not, in the 28 August 2025 note, report the share of Grade 3 children who can read and do the arithmetic the mission's Lakshyas describe. Enrolment infrastructure and learning are on separate trajectories. A claim that the mission has succeeded because dropout in the preparatory stage fell to 2.3% would be using a retention statistic as a learning statistic.

PM-USHA's stated multi-year outlay, ₹12,926.10 crore including earlier RUSA liabilities, covered 2023-24 to 2025-26. That window has closed. The Department of Higher Education's centrally sponsored provision for 2026-27 is ₹1,850 crore, after a 2025-26 revised estimate of ₹800 crore. Whether a successor scheme carries the same design is a question for the next notification, not something the closed window answers.

## Goals still a decade out

The policy wants 100% gross enrolment from preschool through secondary by 2030. UDISE+ 2024-25 puts middle-stage gross enrolment at 90.3% and secondary-stage gross enrolment at 68.5%. Secondary-stage retention is 47.2%. Five years is enough time for those ratios to move a long way, and not enough time to treat 68.5% enrolment or 47.2% retention as close to universal completion. The transition rate from middle to secondary, 86.6%, is the hinge. If that rate stalls, the 2030 goal fails in the last school years even if the early grades stay near universal. Preparatory dropout is already down to 2.3%. The remaining national problem the census highlights is later.

Higher education is the longer goal: 50% gross enrolment by 2035, including vocational education, from 26.3% in 2018. AISHE 2023-24 reports 30 for ages 18–23. From 30 to 50 in about a decade is a large capacity task. The policy says most of it should come from expanding institutions that exist, and that there should be at least one large multidisciplinary institution in or near every district by 2030. The July 2026 release does not score the district goal. It does show that enrolment reached 4.50 crore, from 3.42 crore in 2014-15, with female enrolment at 2.24 crore. The path is upward. The level is still twenty ratio points short of the aim, before definitional differences are even argued.

Scheduled Tribe gross enrolment at 22.8, against 30 overall, is the equity trajectory inside the same goal. A national move from 30 to 50 that leaves the tribal ratio behind would meet a number and miss the policy's inclusion language. Scheduled Caste gross enrolment at 27.8 is closer to the average and still below it.

## The spending aim has no fresh official ratio here

The policy reaffirms public expenditure of 6% of GDP and records about 4.43% in 2017-18. No later official ratio is cited on this page. The mid-term fiscal fact that can be stated is about scheme revisions, not about the GDP share. In 2025-26, Samagra Shiksha, PM SHRI, and the skill ministry's sponsored schemes were all revised below their budget estimates, and the 2026-27 estimates put large numbers back, including ₹6,140.50 crore for PM-SETU. A trajectory drawn only from budget speeches will look smoother than the revised-estimate record.

The useful picture for the next several years is therefore uneven on purpose. Vocational exposure has a lapsed date and no published score in these sources. Foundational learning has a mission year that is current, and a census that does not yet answer it. School enrolment can approach the 2030 goal in the early and middle stages and still fail it at secondary retention. Higher education can keep adding enrolment, as it has up to 4.50 crore, and remain far from 50% in 2035 if the annual gain stays near the recent pace from 29.5 to 30. Those are different distances. They should not be averaged into one "India is on track" sentence.
