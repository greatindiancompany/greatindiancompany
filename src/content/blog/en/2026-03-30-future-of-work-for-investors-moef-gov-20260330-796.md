# How investors should read India's three work ledgers

Investors in Indian staffing, platforms, manufacturing, IT services, and consumer businesses keep being offered one "jobs" number. There is no such number. There are three ledgers, a statutory cost formula, and a 2022 scenario that should stay out of the revenue build. Reading them separately is the work.

## Ledger one: covered members, revised every month

The Employees' Provident Fund Organisation reported a provisional net addition of 21.89 lakh members in June 2025, the highest since it began releasing payroll data in April 2018. New subscribers were about 10.62 lakh. The month was 9.14 percent higher than May 2025 and 13.46 percent higher than June 2024.

Underwrite the definition before the adjective. Net addition is new Aadhaar-validated members, minus exits, plus people who left and rejoined. It covers establishments inside the provident-fund system. EPFO says the series is provisional, that records are updated continuously, that temporary staff with uneven contributions can be included, and that adding the months does not produce the number of people on the rolls at year-end. A diligence question for a staffing or manufacturing company is not "did EPFO have a good June." It is "what does your own revised trail of joins, exits, and rejoins look like, and how much of the net is re-enrolment." A company that cites the national 21.89 lakh as if it were its demand pipeline is borrowing a macro print it does not earn.

This ledger is the right one for formal blue-collar and white-collar rolls. It is the wrong one for a delivery platform whose riders are not employees. Strength in EPFO can coincide with a platform that is adding no covered members at all.

## Ledger two: who is working, in the household survey

For July 2023–June 2024, usual status, the Periodic Labour Force Survey reported participation of 60.1 percent, a worker ratio of 58.2 percent, and unemployment of 3.2 percent, for persons aged 15 and above. Women's participation was 41.7 percent, up from 23.3 percent in 2017–18. Urban participation over that longer window rose only from 47.6 percent to 52.0 percent, while rural participation rose from 50.7 percent to 63.7 percent.

For an investor in urban consumer services or in city staffing, the weekly urban figures are the more relevant demand and labour-supply read. Calendar 2024 current weekly status showed all-India participation flat at 56.2 percent, all-India unemployment at 4.9 percent, and urban unemployment at 6.7 percent. Urban women's weekly participation was 25.8 percent. A pitch that says "female labour-force participation is 42 percent, so urban demand for X is unlocked" has moved a national usual-status rate into a city weekly market. Ask which rate the company's own footprint matches.

The participation rise is still the largest measured change in Indian work across the PLFS window. It matters for businesses that sell to or hire from households newly attached to paid or self-employed work, especially outside the largest cities. It does not, by itself, raise the take rate of a platform.

## Ledger three: the platform cost, written as a formula

Section 114(4) of the Code on Social Security is the margin item for an aggregator in the Seventh Schedule. The schedule includes ride sharing, food and grocery delivery, logistics, e-marketplaces, professional services, healthcare, travel and hospitality, content and media, and other goods and services platforms. The contribution, when notified, is 1 to 2 percent of annual turnover excluding central taxes, levies, and cess, and not more than 5 percent of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers. Section 114(5) requires a separate notification of the start date. A model that expenses the midpoint from 21 November 2025 is assuming a commencement the statute does not grant.

The cap changes who feels the charge. Where payouts to workers are a large fraction of turnover, 5 percent of payouts can bind before 2 percent of turnover does. Where the platform keeps a wide margin and payouts are thin, the turnover rate binds first. That is a structural split between a low-take-rate marketplace and a high-take-rate one. It can be applied to a company's own payout ratio. It does not require a fictional example.

Karnataka changes the arithmetic again for anyone with workers there. Act 72 of 2025, deemed in force from 30 May 2025, sets a welfare fee of 1 to 5 percent of payout per transaction, at a percentage the state notifies, and says that fee counts as the section 114(4) contribution, reconciled yearly if the amounts differ. An investor adding a full central levy on top of a full Karnataka fee has not read section 20(5) of the state Act. Rajasthan's fee, a notified percent of transaction value excluding tax, does not carry that sentence, and the Act starts only when the state notifies commencement. A national platform needs a state page in the model, not a single "India gig cess" cell.

Fixed-term gratuity is the other cost migration. The Ministry's 21 November 2025 note says fixed-term employees qualify for gratuity after one year rather than five, with benefits equal to permanent staff. Contractors and project businesses that used to renew sub-year or just-over-year engagements to stay clear of gratuity are looking at a different unit cost, subject to the transition rule that old provisions continue until replaced. Ask management which contracts flip at twelve months, and whether the price to the client has been reset.

## What to keep out of the valuation

NITI Aayog estimated 77 lakh gig workers in 2020–21, about 1.5 percent of the workforce, and drew a line to 2.35 crore by 2029–30. About 47 percent of that estimated work was medium-skilled. Use this, if at all, as a ceiling scenario from 2022 that says medium-skilled retail, sales, and transport work was the bulk, and that the authors' historical elasticity was above one. Do not use 2.35 crore as this year's users, and do not multiply it by an assumed spend. The Economic Survey's view that labour-input growth can settle above the pre-pandemic pace of about 2.3 percent a year is a macro judgment, not a company growth rate.

The questions that earn their place in a note are plainer. Which ledger is this business on? What is the payout-to-turnover ratio the 5 percent cap would bind against? Does Karnataka's reconciliation apply to a material share of tasks? When does gratuity start on the contracts you actually use? What did the last EPFO revision do to the month management quoted? Those questions are answerable from the statute, the state Act, and the payroll file. The single jobs number is not.
