An opportunity in the public accounts is a rupee the government has estimated it will spend or transfer, in a place a firm or a state can actually meet it. It is not a tender, and it is not a promise that private investment will follow. The 2026-27 Key Features booklet and the fiscal statement are enough to see the large openings. They are not enough to see who will win them.

## What a rupee of Union spending already does

The booklet’s “rupee goes to” graphic for this Budget splits expenditure, and the shares add to 100:

| Share of the rupee | Percent |
| --- | ---: |
| States’ share of taxes | 22 |
| Interest payments | 20 |
| Central sector schemes | 17 |
| Defence | 11 |
| Centrally sponsored schemes | 8 |
| Other expenditure | 7 |
| Finance Commission and other transfers | 7 |
| Major subsidies | 6 |
| Civil pensions | 2 |

Forty-two percent is states’ tax share plus interest, before a scheme or a defence contract is reached. Another 8 percent is subsidies and civil pensions. The discretionary-looking opening is what remains: central sector schemes, centrally sponsored schemes, defence, and the residual. Anyone selling into “the Budget” is selling into that remainder, and only into the portion their ministry actually controls.

## The major-item list

The same booklet lists expenditure of major items, in ₹ crore. These are the booklet’s headings, not a ranking Great Indian Company constructed:

| Item | ₹ crore |
| --- | ---: |
| Transport | 5,98,520 |
| Defence | 5,94,585 |
| Rural development | 2,73,108 |
| Home affairs | 2,55,234 |
| Agriculture and allied activities | 1,62,671 |
| Education | 1,39,289 |
| Energy | 1,09,029 |
| Health | 1,04,599 |
| Urban development | 85,522 |
| IT and telecom | 74,560 |
| Commerce and industry | 70,296 |
| Social welfare | 62,362 |
| Scientific departments | 55,756 |

Transport and defence are the two heads near ₹6 lakh crore. Rural development is under half of either. Health and education are smaller still on this list. A firm that equates “public capex” with hospitals or schools is reading a different document from this table. The demand for grants, not the booklet line, shows how much of transport or defence is capital, how much is pay, and how much is a transfer. Pensions, in particular, are discussed separately in the fiscal statement and should not be assumed to sit inside the defence line above.

## Openings that are transfers, not purchases

The fiscal statement puts tax devolution to states at ₹15.26 lakh crore in 2026-27, against ₹13.93 lakh crore in the 2025-26 revised estimates. The Sixteenth Finance Commission share stays at 41 percent of the divisible pool. Finance Commission grants are ₹1.29 lakh crore: rural local bodies ₹0.56 lakh crore, urban local bodies ₹0.45 lakh crore, and disaster management ₹0.28 lakh crore. Together, devolution and these grants are ₹16.56 lakh crore.

That is the largest opening that does not pass through a Union tender. It reaches state treasuries and local bodies. A contractor, a city finance officer, or a co-operative bank meets it in a state budget or a municipal work, not in the Union speech. The Centre’s capital loan to states for their own capital spending is a further ₹2.0 lakh crore, inside the Union capital line of ₹12.22 lakh crore. The asset, if built, is chosen by the state under the scheme’s conditions.

## The market that formal tax records describe

A [Press Information Bureau note of 30 June 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2279318&lang=1&reg=3) says GST registrants rose from 66.5 lakh in 2017 to 1.65 crore as of May 2026. That is a count of tax identities. It is the addressable set of firms already inside the invoice system, which is the set that can sell to other registered firms and claim credit. It is not a count of firms ready to supply the transport ministry. The Bureau also says the rate structure in force after 22 September 2025 is mainly 5 and 18 percent, with 40 percent on a demerit list. Pricing a public contract on a pre-2025 rate card is a commercial error, not a fiscal insight.

The honest landscape is three doors. One is a Union major head, especially transport and defence, opened only after the demand for grants splits pay from capital. One is a state or local body funded by devolution, Finance Commission grants, or the capital loan. One is the registered GST base, which tells you who is formal, not who will be paid. Interest at 20 percent of the rupee is the door that is not an opportunity for a supplier. It is the claim that gets paid before the supplier does.

Great Indian Company stops at those doors. Award letters are a later document. The public-finance notes around this one are at [greatindiancompany.com](https://greatindiancompany.com/).
