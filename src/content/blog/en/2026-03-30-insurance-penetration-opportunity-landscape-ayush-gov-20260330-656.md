# Health Is the Open Edge of India's Insurance Penetration

The life book already dominates Indian insurance. The [PIB note of 23 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3) says life is about 74 percent of premium and 91 percent of assets under management. The room that shows up in official comparisons is non-life, and inside non-life the line that has already taken the lead is health.

PIB says health contributes 41 percent of non-life gross domestic premium and has surpassed motor. The [IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620) keeps non-life penetration at 1 percent of GDP and non-life density at USD 25. World non-life penetration, on the Swiss Re figures IRDAI cites for 2024, is 4.3 percent. Health can be the largest Indian non-life line and the country can still be early in non-life depth. That is the opportunity the numbers support. It is not a market-size projection.

## Who already writes health cover

The Department of Financial Services, on its [insurance overview](https://financialservices.gov.in/beta/en/page/insurance-overview), notes that the 2015 amendment recognised health insurance as its own vertical, with capital requirements aligned to life and general insurers. That page stops at 31 March 2021 for the insurer census and records five stand-alone health insurers at that date. PIB's later industry count is 74 insurers in total, without a fresh stand-alone split. Use the dated census for the five, and the later total for the industry.

IRDAI's 2024-25 office and agency figures show where stand-alone health capacity was added. Offices rose from 1,657 to 1,788. Individual agents rose 18.66 percent to 15.49 lakh, faster than life agency growth of 7.87 percent. Eighty percent of those health offices, 1,433 of them, are in Tier I cities. One stand-alone health office is in a Tier V centre and one is in a Tier VI centre. The growth is real. The footprint is urban.

The incurred claims ratio for stand-alone health insurers was 68.06 percent in 2024-25, lower than the non-life industry's 82.88 percent and lower than public-sector general insurers at 97.30 percent. That is a published economics difference. It is not a licence to assume every new health policy will earn that ratio.

## The product rules a health offer has to meet

PIB summarises the health-contract changes a retail offer now lives inside. The moratorium, after which a claim cannot be denied for non-disclosure except for established fraud, was cut from 8 years to 60 months in 2024. Policies with a term of one year or more have a 30-day free-look period. Renewal cannot be refused because the customer claimed, except for fraud or misrepresentation. Waiting-period credit and no-claim bonus can move with the customer when they change product or insurer. Insurers must offer choice across ages, regions, occupations, medical conditions, disabilities, and systems of medicine, including allopathic and AYUSH treatment, and across hospital types. A grace period of 15 days applies to monthly premiums and 30 days to other modes, with benefits held during that period.

From 22 September 2025, individual health policies, including family floaters, are exempt from GST, along with individual life policies and the related reinsurance. PIB says that removes 18 percent GST.

Government health cover sits beside this retail book and should not be added into the 1 percent. Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY, as PIB describes it, offers up to ₹5 lakh per family per year. As of 28 February 2026, 43.52 crore Ayushman cards had been created. That is a coverage count. The penetration ratio remains premium over GDP.
