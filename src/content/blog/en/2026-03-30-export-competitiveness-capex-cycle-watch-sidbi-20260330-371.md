# Capex, export infrastructure, and the trade cycle

Export competitiveness has a capital cycle, but the cycle is not one number. Public logistics capex, a firm’s duty-free capital-goods import, and a trade-finance mission get added together in pitch decks. They should not be.

## The national-accounts signal is investment, not a customs line

The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation’s first-quarter estimates, reported in the [1 September 2026 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2305422), put gross fixed capital formation growth at 11.9 percent in April–June 2026–27, against 5.8 percent a year earlier. Real exports of goods and services grew 12.0 percent in the same quarter, against 6.0 percent. Real GDP grew 7.8 percent. That is a coincidence of two volume measures in one quarter. It does not say that export orders caused the investment, or the reverse.

NITI Aayog’s 16 September 2026 Trade Watch note, covering that same quarter, said imports rose in capital goods, electronic components, and copper, and treated that as a sign of domestic investment and industrial capacity. Capital-goods imports can be export capacity. They can also be domestic capacity. The trade release does not split them.

## The firm-level instrument is EPCG, not the mission outlay

Foreign Trade Policy 2023 kept the Export Promotion Capital Goods scheme: capital goods for export production at zero customs duty, against an export obligation. The [31 March 2023 launch](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1912572) added Prime Minister MITRA textile parks to the common-service-provider window, exempted dairy from the average export-obligation condition, and added battery electric vehicles, vertical farming equipment, wastewater treatment and recycling, rainwater systems, and green hydrogen to the green-technology list that qualifies for a reduced export obligation.

That is the export-linked capex rule a company can file. The rupee value of EPCG authorisations in 2026–27 is not in that launch note, so this brief does not invent one.

The Export Promotion Mission is a different pot. The Union Cabinet approved it on 12 November 2025 with an outlay of ₹25,060 crore from 2025–26 through 2030–31. Niryat Protsahan is trade finance: interest subvention and collateral support. Niryat Disha is market access, branding, compliance, logistics support, and trade intelligence. Calling that outlay “export capex” mislabels working-capital and market support as plant and machinery.

## Public logistics capex is the third pile

The 20 September 2025 logistics-cost release lists the programmes the government credits for a slower rise in logistics cost relative to non-services output: PM GatiShakti, dedicated freight corridors, Bharatmala, Sagarmala, integrated check posts, the Unified Logistics Interface Platform, and LEAP. Those are multi-year infrastructure programmes. Their budget lines live in the ministries that build them. A trade brief should name them as the corridor underneath an export, and stop before it adds their capital stock to EPCG imports.

## What to watch in the cycle

Watch three series on their own clocks. MoSPI’s next capital-formation print says whether the 11.9 percent quarter persisted. DGFT’s EPCG and green-technology notifications say whether the duty-free capital route changed. The logistics-cost assessment, last launched as a 7.97 percent of GDP estimate, is not a monthly capex indicator.
