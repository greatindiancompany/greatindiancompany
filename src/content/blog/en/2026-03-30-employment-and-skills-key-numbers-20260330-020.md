Three rates carry almost every official sentence about jobs in India. They are defined in the Periodic Labour Force Survey press note for July 2023–June 2024, and they are easy to swap by mistake.

The labour force participation rate is the share of people who are working, or seeking work, or available for work. The worker population ratio is the share of people who are employed. The unemployment rate is the share of the labour force that is unemployed. Unemployment is a share of people already in the labour force. It is not a share of the whole population.

## The annual print to memorise

For persons aged 15 and above, in usual status (principal activity plus subsidiary activity), July 2023–June 2024:

| | Male | Female | Person |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Labour force participation | 78.8% | 41.7% | 60.1% |
| Worker population ratio | 76.3% | 40.3% | 58.2% |
| Unemployment rate | 3.2% | 3.2% | 3.2% |

Usual status looks back 365 days. Principal status is the activity that took the major part of that year. Subsidiary status is additional economic activity of 30 days or more. The rates above count both.

The same press note's all-India person rates a year earlier, July 2022–June 2023, were 57.9% participation, 56.0% employment, and 3.2% unemployment. Participation and employment rose. The unemployment rate for all persons stayed at 3.2%. Male unemployment edged from 3.3% to 3.2%. Female unemployment rose from 2.9% to 3.2%.

Rural unemployment in this status was 2.5% (male 2.7%, female 2.1%). Urban unemployment was 5.1% (male 4.4%, female 7.1%). Rural participation was 63.7% and urban participation was 52.0%.

## A check you can do on the published rates

Employed people plus unemployed people are the labour force. So the gap between participation and the worker population ratio is unemployment as a share of the population, and that gap divided by the participation rate is the unemployment rate.

For July 2023–June 2024, 60.1 minus 58.2 is 1.9 percentage points of the population. 1.9 divided by 60.1 is about 3.2%, which is the published unemployment rate. When a commentary quotes 3.2% as if three people in a hundred adults were unemployed, it has changed the denominator. The survey's own definition puts those unemployed people inside the labour force.

## The weekly-status twin in the same year

The same annual note also publishes current weekly status, the activity in the seven days before the survey. For persons aged 15 and above, July 2023–June 2024, all-India participation was 56.4%, the worker population ratio was 53.7%, and unemployment was 4.9%. Male unemployment was 4.8% and female unemployment was 5.0%. Rural weekly-status unemployment was 4.2% and urban was 6.7%.

A person can be counted as employed over the year and still be without work in the reference week. The weekly rate sits higher. Both rates are in one official note.

## Where the August 2026 rate belongs

The August 2026 monthly bulletin, released on 15 September 2026, reports current weekly status only. Overall unemployment was 5.0%, participation 55.6%, and the worker population ratio 52.8%, for persons aged 15 and above. The monthly design itself dates from the methodology change of January 2025. Placing 5.0% next to the annual usual-status 3.2% and calling the gap a deterioration compares a reference week in 2026 with a reference year in 2023–24, under a survey that the office has since modified.

The seven-year path of the annual rates is in the [long-term PLFS brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-long-term-trend-20260330-050). The August move, month by month, is in the [August 2026 bulletin brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-what-changed-20260330-010).

The July 2023–June 2024 annual sample for the first visit was 12,743 first-stage units, 1,01,920 households, and 4,18,159 persons, of whom 3,19,773 were aged 15 and above.
