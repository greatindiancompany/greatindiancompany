# The bottleneck has moved from laying pipe to proving service

Rural India is no longer early in the tap-connection programme. On 4 August 2026 the Ministry of Jal Shakti told Parliament that more than 15.90 crore of 19.36 crore rural households, 82.19 percent, were reported to have tap connections. The execution problem that remains is the proof that a connection is a service, and the parallel books of village waste and urban sewage that were never the same project.

## Certification is now gated on maintenance

Under the dashboard's own stages, a village can be fully entered on the information system and still be short of a state "reported" confirmation, and short again of a Gram Sabha certificate. JJM 2.0, from 10 March 2026, puts a further gate on that certificate. The Gram Panchayat declares Har Ghar Jal only after the state has established in-village operation and maintenance. Jal Arpan is the handover to the panchayat and the Village Water and Sanitation Committee.

That gate is the bottleneck. Pipe-laying can be substantially done in a state while handovers are not. The mission's digital fix is a Sujal Gaon identity and a source-to-tap map, including geo-tagging on PM Gati Shakti. A scheme that cannot be named as one asset cannot be handed over as one asset. Identity work, which looks like administration, is now on the critical path of certification.

## Payment is gated on the same identity

SNA SPARSH pays individual schemes. As on 6 August 2026 only ₹865.87 crore of the year's reported central outflow of ₹6,154.96 crore had moved on that rail, across 13 states. The larger piece, ₹5,289.09 crore, reimbursed older approved spending by ten states. If the new rail is the way JJM 2.0 intends to release money, then states that have not broken their works into scheme-level records will wait. The bottleneck is not the size of the ₹8.69 lakh crore outlay. It is the share of that outlay that has a scheme identity clear enough to be paid.

## The sanitation bottleneck is a class, not a toilet

By 11 June 2026, 5,07,634 of 5,86,944 SBM villages were ODF Plus Model, and 57,923 were still Aspiring. Aspiring means one of solid or liquid waste is arranged. The execution task is the missing stream, plus the visual-cleanliness test that separates Model from Rising. Third-party verification within 90 days applies when a village is first declared Model. A state that declares faster than it can verify will build a queue the 8 December 2025 rule already anticipates. The dashboard, not a national slogan, shows whether that queue is growing.

## Urban sewage is a different bottleneck: few projects, large balances

AMRUT 2.0's approved sewerage and septage book is 605 projects and ₹71,133.01 crore, beside 3,572 water-supply projects and ₹1,18,968.38 crore. Across the whole mission, awards had reached ₹1,37,130.13 crore of capex by 9 July 2026, and physically completed works ₹66,472 crore. The water-supply count can move through many smaller contracts. The sewage count moves through fewer, heavier ones: land, networks, and plants. Treating "AMRUT progress" as one percentage will hide a sewage book that dominates cost and lags the tap book.

The practical review is therefore four queues: Gram Panchayats waiting on a maintenance arrangement, schemes waiting on an identity before SNA SPARSH will pay them, Aspiring villages waiting on the second waste stream, and awarded urban works waiting on physical completion. The pages that show those queues are the [JJM dashboard](https://ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport/jjmindia.aspx), the [6 August 2026 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295509), the [SBM-G dashboard](https://sbm.gov.in/sbmgdashboard/), and the [AMRUT answer](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3022_tT0QXq.pdf).
