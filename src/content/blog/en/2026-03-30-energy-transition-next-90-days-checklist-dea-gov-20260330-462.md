# A 90-Day Checklist for India's Energy Transition

From 24 September 2026, ninety days runs to about 23 December 2026. That window will not contain a new National Electricity Plan. The current generation plan was notified on 18 May 2023, and section 3(4) of the Electricity Act asks the Central Electricity Authority for a plan once in five years. It will also not close the 2030 targets. The useful work is to read the releases that are already scheduled, against a July baseline that is already published.

## Capacity sheets, on their own calendar

MNRE's advance-release calendar says the monthly physical-achievement note is due by the 15th of each month. The sheet in force while this note was written is dated 31 July 2026 and was marked updated on 11 August 2026. By late December a reader should have the August, September, and October position, assuming the Ministry holds that calendar.

Check three numbers against the July base, not a vague sense that "capacity is rising":

- Solar added after 31 July, on top of the 14,334.05 MW already added from 1 April to 31 July 2026. The cumulative solar stock that day was 164,594.75 MW.
- Wind added after 31 July, on top of 2,042.05 MW in those four months. The cumulative wind stock was 58,136.89 MW.
- The non-fossil total, which was 300,505.64 MW, including 8,780 MW of nuclear that did not move in April–July.

If a month shows a large solar number and a near-zero wind number, say that. FY 2025-26 wind addition of 6.05 GW was a break from a slower history. One strong year is not yet a new normal. The Government has also said about 28 GW of wind is under implementation. The monthly row is how you find out whether that pipeline is reaching commissioning.

Pull the state PDF when it is posted beside the national table. In July, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka dominated the location-based renewable total. A national addition that does not show up in those states, or that shows up only as rooftop in Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra, is a different industry event from a new Rajasthan solar park.

## Schemes that are inside their implementation window

PM Surya Ghar runs through 31 March 2027. The next ninety days are inside the scheme, not after it. The PIB factsheet of 13 August 2026 reported 51.58 lakh households benefiting, about ₹28,024 crore of subsidy transferred, and 14.8 GW commissioned under the scheme, against a target of one crore households and a scheme outlay of ₹75,021 crore. Budget 2026-27 provides ₹22,000 crore for the scheme. The check is whether household count, commissioned gigawatts, and subsidy released keep moving together. A jump in applications without a jump in meters is the pattern the 16 July 2026 Lok Sabha annexure already showed nationally: 76,00,788 applications and 39,03,615 installations.

PM-KUSUM has been extended to 31 March 2027. MNRE's year-end review said 25 lakh pumps had been installed or solarised, 13.93 lakh of them in FY 2025-26, and that KUSUM solar addition that year was 7.67 GW. There is no reason to expect a new scheme design in this quarter. There is reason to see whether the pump and feeder-solarisation numbers keep being published.

The National Green Hydrogen Mission's production aim is a 2030 figure. The dated stock is much smaller: around 8,000 tonnes a year commissioned as of February 2026. In this quarter, look for a newer commissioned-capacity figure, and for any status on the 12 vehicle pilots and the Jind–Sonipat hydrogen train announced in July 2026. Absence of a new production number is itself information.

## Rules a designated consumer should touch this quarter

The renewable consumption obligation for FY 2026-27 is already 35.95 percent. The filing extension that ran to 31 May 2026 covered FY 2024-25 returns. It is over. In this quarter an obligated distribution company, open-access consumer, or captive user should know three operational facts: which compliance route it will use (renewable electricity, certificates including a virtual power purchase agreement, or the CERC buyout price), whether its data can support the distributed-renewable category, and whether wind or hydro it wants to count was commissioned after 31 March 2024.

No new quality-control order has to appear for module buyers to stay on the January 2025 goods order. The June 2028 ingot-and-wafer list date will not fall inside these ninety days. What can fall inside them is a factory announcement or a list update. Treat those as list events, and keep them separate from megawatts commissioned.

## What not to put on the list

Do not schedule a surprise revision of the 500 GW aim, the 45 percent intensity goal, or the National Electricity Plan's 2031-32 capacity table unless a gazette actually appears. Do not treat a minister's review of last year as a change in this quarter's stock. Do not add scheme outlays approved in July and August 2026, such as floating solar under the Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana or the GOBARdhan bioenergy scheme, to installed capacity until MNRE's technology rows move.

The discipline is dull and sufficient. New sheet, three lines, state PDF, household scheme, obligation route. That is the quarter.
