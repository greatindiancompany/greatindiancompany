# The post-pandemic path of state fiscal deficits

The long trend in Indian state finances is easier to misread than to measure. One year's budget estimate, one revised figure, and one audited account are three different objects. The Reserve Bank's annual *State Finances* study is the place they are lined up. The issue dated 23 January 2026, *A Study of Budgets of 2025-26*, is the latest point on that line. Earlier issues sit on the same publications page, which is how a reader checks a ratio against the previous vintage.

## What the latest point says

States' consolidated gross fiscal deficit increased to 3.3 per cent of GDP in 2024-25. During the previous three years it had stayed below 3.0 per cent. For 2025-26, states have budgeted 3.3 per cent of GDP. Chart II.1 is that consolidated path. Chart II.2 breaks the 2025-26 budget estimates into state-wise ratios of gross fiscal deficit to GSDP, so the national GDP ratio is the average.

The Bank's account of the step through 3 per cent is narrow. It mainly reflects 50-year interest-free loans from the Centre for capital investment, provided over and above the normal net borrowing ceiling. Chart II.18 draws the deficit and those loans together. A trend line that treats every year of deficit as the same kind of borrowing will mix ordinary market loans with this interest-free window, which has been part of the financing story since 2020-21.

Capital expenditure is the matching trend on the spending side. It was steady at 2.7 per cent of GDP in 2023-24 and 2024-25, and the 2025-26 budgets take it to 3.2 per cent of GDP. A deficit trend that rises with capital expenditure is a different fiscal history from a deficit trend that rises with revenue expenditure. Statement 4, the decomposition of the gross fiscal deficit, is the table that keeps those histories separate. Statement 2, the revenue balance, shows whether the current account itself is in deficit.

## The stock behind the flow

Each year's deficit adds to liabilities, after repayments. The study says consolidated outstanding liabilities remained elevated in the post-pandemic period. The budget estimate is 29.2 per cent of GDP at end-March 2026. Statements 18 to 20 give the composition of that stock and the ratio to GSDP. Statement 23 gives the maturity profile of outstanding state government securities. A comfortable deficit ratio in a single year can still sit on a maturity wall if a large share of the old stock comes due together.

Interest on that stock is the channel from an old deficit into a future budget. Statement 13 is interest payments. Once interest, salaries, and pensions take a larger share of revenue receipts, the room for new capital spending shrinks even if the deficit ceiling is unchanged. The theme chapter of the 2025-26 study says this pressure is sharper in ageing states, where the tax base narrows as committed expenditure rises. That is a long-trend claim about composition, and it is the reason a single all-state deficit ratio ages badly.

## The rule that the next five years will be scored against

The Sixteenth Finance Commission's award period is 2026-27 to 2030-31. The government's explanatory memorandum accepts, in principle, a state fiscal-deficit cap of 3 per cent of GSDP excluding loans under the capital-investment scheme, enforced through Article 293(3). Other recommendations, including a clean-up of off-budget borrowing and amendments to state fiscal-responsibility laws, were left for separate examination. The Department of Expenditure's guidelines of 27 March 2026 keep the capital-loan window open for 2026-27, with an allocation of Rs 2,00,000 crore, again outside the normal ceiling.

The trend to follow is therefore two series. One is the ordinary deficit against the 3 per cent of GSDP cap. The other is the capital loan, which the deficit ratio includes and the ordinary cap excludes. Publishing them as one number, which the consolidated gross fiscal deficit does, is correct accounting. Reading them as one decision is the mistake.

Past issues of the study are the check on whether a new press release has changed a definition. The January 2026 issue is the current end of the published line, and its 2025-26 column is still a budget estimate.
