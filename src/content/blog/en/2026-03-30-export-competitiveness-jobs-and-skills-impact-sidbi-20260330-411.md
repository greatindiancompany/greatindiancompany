# Jobs and skills tied to India’s export trade

Export competitiveness is often described as a jobs story. The official trade releases used here mostly publish dollars, rupees, and scheme rules. They do not publish a count of people employed because a shipment left India. Treating a missing count as if it were zero, or filling it from memory, both mislead.

## Where employment is named, and where it is not

The [22 April 2026 textiles release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254367) says continued export growth and policy support underline a commitment to strengthening the sector, promoting employment, and moving toward higher value-added exports. The figures in that note are export values, not headcount. Total textile exports, including handicrafts, rose from ₹3,09,859.3 crore in 2024–25 to ₹3,16,334.9 crore in 2025–26, up 2.1 percent. Ready-made garments of all textiles, the largest slice, rose from ₹1,35,427.6 crore to ₹1,39,349.6 crore, up 2.9 percent. Cotton yarn, fabrics, made-ups, and handloom products were ₹1,02,399.7 crore, up 0.4 percent. Man-made yarn, fabrics, and made-ups were ₹42,687.8 crore, up 3.6 percent. Handicrafts excluding handmade carpets rose 6.1 percent to ₹15,855.1 crore.

Those are the labour-intensive lines the rebate architecture already singles out. RoSCTL rebates state and central taxes on apparel and garments under customs chapters 61 and 62, and on made-ups under chapter 63. The April 2026 note says both RoSCTL and RoDTEP were extended beyond 31 March 2026. A jobs discussion that skips the chapter split will mix a general remission with the garment-specific one.

The Export Promotion Mission, approved on 12 November 2025, says its focus includes MSMEs, first-time exporters, and labour-intensive sectors. That is a targeting sentence. The operational numbers published on 2 January 2026 are an interest subvention of 2.75 percent, a ₹50 lakh cap per IEC for 2025–26, and a collateral guarantee, not a jobs created figure.

## The skill rule inside FTP 2023 is a training expectation

Foreign Trade Policy 2023 said two-star and higher status holders would be encouraged, on a best-endeavour basis, to provide trade-related training from a model curriculum. The launch tied that idea to a skilled pool for a larger economy. It did not report enrolment, completion, or placement. Status recognition was also recalibrated so more firms could reach four- and five-star ratings. Branding and training were linked. Neither is a payroll statistic.

District export hubs are aimed at local manufacturers, MSMEs, farmers, and small-scale units. The March 2026 Lok Sabha reply counts committees and action plans. It does not count jobs per district.

## What a serious jobs read needs next

Periodic labour-force results from the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation are the employment survey. They are not an export-by-IEC ledger. Until a commerce or statistics release joins the two, the honest statement is: apparel and made-ups are the export lines the government treats as employment-sensitive, their 2025–26 values are the figures above, and a headcount should be quoted only when a survey or administrative jobs series names the sector and the date.
