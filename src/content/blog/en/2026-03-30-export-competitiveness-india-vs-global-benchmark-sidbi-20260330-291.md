# India’s trade beside the global goods and services benchmark

“India versus the world” is usually a ranking someone else invented. The comparison that can be checked was released in New Delhi on 16 September 2026, when NITI Aayog published the Trade Watch Quarterly for the first quarter of 2026–27, April–June 2026.

## What the edition actually compares

The [PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2310941) says global goods trade reached US$ 13.7 trillion in the first half of calendar 2026, up 12.5 percent year on year, and that global services trade expanded 10.5 percent. Against that backdrop it puts India’s merchandise and services trade at US$ 506.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026–27, up 15.5 percent. “Total trade” here is the edition’s own aggregate. It is not the Reserve Bank’s current-account credit line, and it is not the Commerce monthly export total. Use it as the benchmark the authors published, and open the [PDF](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/Trade-Watch-Quarterly-January-March-Q1-FY27.pdf) before quoting a sub-series.

The file name on that URL says “January–March.” The release text says April–June 2026. If those labels ever disagree inside the PDF, the table caption wins over the file name.

The same note says merchandise exports in that quarter were led by mineral fuels, electrical machinery, nuclear reactors, iron and steel, and vehicles, with petroleum products, steel, engineering goods, and automobiles in the supporting sentence. Imports grew in capital goods, electronic components, and copper. Northeast Asia, West Asia–GCC, and ASEAN together accounted for around half of India’s imports. Trade with FTA partners “gained further momentum,” with exports up 36.3 percent and imports up 10.0 percent. The note does not, in the summary, define the partner set or the base period beyond the edition. Cite the pair of rates as the edition’s claim, and read the partner list in the PDF before using it in a board paper.

## A services benchmark that is not the IT export headline

Digitally delivered services exports rose from US$ 277 billion in 2024 to US$ 317 billion in 2025, up 15 percent. The note says India moved from fifth to fourth among exporters of those services, behind the United States, the United Kingdom, and Ireland. That is a narrower concept than “services exports” in the RBI monthly release. Computer services can dominate the RBI category list while this ranking is about digitally delivered services as the edition defines them. Do not slide one rank onto the other.

## Logistics cost is a cost benchmark, not a World Bank slide from memory

On 20 September 2025 the Commerce and Industry Minister launched the DPIIT–NCAER Assessment of Logistics Cost in India. The [release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2168995) says logistics costs are estimated at about 7.97 percent of GDP. It also says figures of 13–14 percent of GDP, often repeated from external studies or partial data, had misrepresented the cost. The assessment is for a measured year under the National Logistics Policy mandate, not a monthly score. A founder who still uses 13–14 percent in a 2026 pitch is using the number the ministry said was the problem.

Metals are the edition’s special subject, not a footnote. Metals exports were US$ 34.8 billion in 2025, with iron and steel, articles of iron and steel, and aluminium together about 78 percent. Metals and ores imports rose from US$ 32.2 billion in 2015 to US$ 60.5 billion in 2025. The note flags the European Union’s carbon border adjustment as a competitiveness issue for steel and aluminium. That is the global benchmark that can change a price, as distinct from a rank on a poster.
