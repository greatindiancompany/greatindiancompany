# Green Hydrogen Decisions to Read Before the End of 2026

From 24 September 2026, ninety days runs to about 23 December 2026. The official calendar in the documents used here has one hard project date inside that window. Several other documents are already published, and reading them is a better use of the quarter than waiting for a fresh target.

## Ask whether Kandla’s 5 MW contract finished on time

The 5 August 2026 Lok Sabha annex says the contract for a 5 MW green hydrogen plant at Deendayal Port, Kandla, scalable to 10 MW, costs ₹62.80 crore, and the completion timeline in the contract is 31 December 2026. By the end of December a reader should be able to ask whether completion has been reported, or whether the date has moved. The [annex](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2987_27NWXF.pdf) does not promise a public ceremony. It gives a contractual end date. The same annex times a 5-tonne-a-day bio-methanol plant at Kandla, costing ₹59.71 crore, to 31 January 2027, just outside this window. That is the next date, not this one.

The annex also says a 1 MW demonstration plant at the port is already complete, at ₹14.1 crore. Keep that plant in a separate sentence from the 5 MW contract. One is reported finished. The other has an end date still ahead of 24 September 2026.

## Read the service agreement that is already on the site

On 24 July 2026, MNRE published the model service agreement for electrolyser-as-a-service and listed it on the [hydrogen schemes page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/). Ninety days is enough for a refinery developer, a port tenant, or a lender to compare that contract with an ownership bid under the SIGHT manufacturing awards. The page links the file. A commentary is optional. The allocation of uptime, power, and stack replacement is in the agreement, and this brief does not substitute for it.

## Match a mobility project to the July annex

The 29 July 2026 Lok Sabha answer says 12 pilot projects cover 70 vehicles, 16 refuelling stations, and 21 routes, with about ₹410.77 crore allocated and ₹41.582 crore released. The annex names consortia and routes and marks the sums as viability-gap funding at 90 percent. Anyone supplying a station, a bus, or a truck on those corridors should match the project to Phase I or Phase II. The answer also says executing agencies must submit performance reports on efficiency, fuel consumption, safety, and utilisation. If a consortium is in the annex, that reporting duty is already written down. The next ninety days are a reasonable time to ask whether the first of those reports will be public, without pretending the answer sets a publication date. It does not.

## Know the certificate rule before the next invoice

The certification portal was launched on 17 June 2026. The November 2025 official note says a final certificate is mandatory when a facility takes a central or state incentive, or sells or uses hydrogen in India. The Bureau of Energy Efficiency accredits the verifiers. A company that holds a SIGHT award and plans to invoice in 2027 should know, this quarter, which accredited agency it will appoint. The [mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/) states the 2 kg well-to-gate threshold that the certificate is there to police.

## What this window does not contain

The texts read for this brief do not add a new national production target inside these ninety days, and they do not name the states whose policies the minister counted on 17 June 2026. Chasing either gap is a worse use of the quarter than reading the Kandla date and the two July documents.

Great Indian Company will treat a December commissioning claim as established when a port or MNRE record says the 5 MW plant is complete.
