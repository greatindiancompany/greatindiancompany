# Urban India’s Mid-Term Path Runs Through Census 2027

## Four published horizons, not one story

From September 2026, the mid-term in urban housing is the period through the Census 2027 results and the remaining years of PMAY-Urban 2.0. Two other MoHUA clocks have already reached the end printed on their own pages. Treating all four as open programmes is the planning error.

## The information horizon: March 2027, then the tables

PIB’s note of 25 April 2026 fixes the Census moment at 00:00 hours on 1 March 2027 for the country, and at 00:00 hours on 1 October 2026 for Ladakh and the snow-bound areas of Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand. Population Enumeration is scheduled for February 2027, and for September 2026 in those snow-bound areas. Phase I, the housing census of condition, amenities, and assets, was scheduled between April and September 2026.

The trajectory that follows is documentary. First the frame, then enumeration, then tables the Registrar General chooses to release. MoHUA’s current urban page will stay a 2011 page until those tables exist. The 2011 urban share in the ministry’s table is 31.16 percent, 377 million people in urban areas, after a decade in which 2,532 of 2,774 new towns were census towns. The 2027 distribution can move because people moved, because statutory status changed, or because census-town classification changed. Those are different stories. The release has to say which one it is measuring. Until it does, a mid-term demand model that starts from a round “40 percent urban” has no official base.

The Cabinet outlay for conducting the census, ₹11,718.24 crore in that PIB note, is the cost of the operation. It is not a housing budget.

## The assistance horizon: five years from September 2024

PMAY-Urban 2.0 took effect on 1 September 2024 to support 1 crore additional eligible beneficiaries over five years, on the July 2025 PIB account. That horizon runs past 2027, toward 2029, unless a later government decision shortens or extends it. This brief has not seen such a decision in the sources used here.

The shape of those five years is already specified. Beneficiary-led construction and partnership housing carry ₹2.50 lakh of government assistance a unit, with the central share varying by state and Union Territory as the December 2024 note sets out. The interest subsidy is capped at ₹1.80 lakh on the July 2025 terms. Affordable rental housing is a vertical aimed at workers, migrants, students, and other groups the note names. The first scheme’s stock, 1.18 crore sanctioned and 88.32 lakh delivered in that December 2024 note, is the base the new crore is added to. It should not be added twice.

## The horizons that have reached their printed end

AMRUT 2.0’s operational guidelines, released on 27 October 2021, set a five-year period from 2021-22 to 2025-26 and an indicative outlay of ₹2,99,000 crore. Financial year 2025-26 ended on 31 March 2026. Completion of city water action plans can outlive the last sanction year. A new five-year outlay cannot be assumed from the old one. Reforms in that text, including non-revenue water below 20 percent and reuse of treated water for at least 20 percent of city demand and 40 percent of industrial demand at state level, remain the published standard for judging systems that were built.

The Smart Cities Mission’s central budget closed on 31 March 2025. MoHUA says no new city will be taken up. The mid-term object is the asset: command centres in all 100 cities, and special purpose vehicles whose future role is the subject of Advisory No. 27 of 10 June 2025. States, through the chief secretary’s committee, were asked to say where those vehicles still have a job. That is a governance trajectory, city by city, not a second mission.

## What would change the path

A published Census table with a stated urban definition. A MoHUA or PIB note that either continues AMRUT’s financial support or replaces it. A PMAY progress table later than December 2024 for sanctions under the 2.0 verticals. Each of those would update a different line in a mid-term plan. None of them is interchangeable.
