The opportunity in India's semiconductor policy is not a blank map. Twelve manufacturing projects are already approved, three of them in commercial production. What remains open is a set of scheme doors with written products, ceilings, and applicants. This brief matches a type of company to the door that names it. It is current to 24 September 2026. Windows move; the [regulatory outlook](/blog/semiconductor-and-electronics-regulatory-outlook-egazette-gov-20260330-447) is the place to check whether a door is still accepting applications.

## Fabless design, under a ceiling that is real

The Design Linked Incentive, notified on 21 December 2021, is the door for a domestic chip-design company. Product-design support is reimbursement of up to 50 percent of eligible expenditure, capped at ₹15 crore per application, and the [guidelines](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Guidelines-for-Design-Linked-Incentive-DLI-Scheme-30.12.2022.pdf) limit that category to startups and MSMEs. Deployment support is 6 percent down to 4 percent of net sales over five years, capped at ₹30 crore, and it is open to domestic companies as well. C-DAC runs the scheme. The [ISM page](https://ism.gov.in/design-linked-incentive) is the public front door.

Twenty-four projects already hold financial support. The products named in the [15 July 2026 Cabinet note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) are satellite communications, drones, surveillance cameras, IoT devices, LED drivers, AI systems, telecom equipment, and smart meters. That list is evidence of what has been approved, not a reservation of those markets. A new applicant is competing with a scheme that has a per-application cap in the low tens of crores, tool access that 105 startups and MSMEs already have, and a deployment incentive that arrives only after sales. The opportunity is a designed chip with an Indian owner and a milestone path. It is not a venture round sized like a fab.

Semicon 2.0's design pillar says the next aim is Indian intellectual property and full-stack designs, including strategic and commercial building blocks. The pillar is approved. The claim formula beyond the 2021 gazette is not in the Cabinet release.

## Components and sub-assemblies, with thresholds by product

The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme is the door for the bill of materials around the chip. The [26 April 2025 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620) sets the outlay at ₹22,919 crore and writes a different incentive for each segment. Display-module sub-assembly and camera-module sub-assembly are turnover-linked, at cumulative investment thresholds of ₹250 crore. Non-SMD passives, electro-mechanicals, and multilayer printed circuit boards start at ₹50 crore of cumulative investment. Lithium-ion cells for digital applications, excluding storage and mobility, and enclosures for mobile and IT hardware, start at ₹500 crore. High-density, flexible, and similar boards combine a turnover incentive with 25 percent capex support at a ₹1,000 crore threshold. Surface-mount passives combine both at ₹250 crore. Supply-chain parts and capital goods are capex-linked at 25 percent, with a minimum investment of ₹10 crore.

Those thresholds are the opportunity screen. A ₹10 crore capital-goods maker and a ₹1,000 crore board maker are both inside the scheme, on different terms. Part of the incentive is held back unless employment thresholds are met. The base year for the turnover incentive is 2024-25. SPECS, at 25 percent of capex, is the older components instrument; its original application window closed on 31 March 2023, and later amendments have to be read before a new SPECS filing is assumed possible. Investment that has taken SPECS cannot be reused as the incremental investment for PLI 2.0.

## IT hardware, for companies that sell the box

[PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/production-linked-incentive-scheme-pli-2-0-for-it-hardware-wM0MDOtQWa) pays an average of around 5 percent on incremental sales of laptops, tablets, all-in-one PCs, servers, and ultra-small-form-factor devices, for six years, against a ₹17,000 crore outlay. The scheme page says semiconductor design, integrated-circuit manufacturing, and packaging count as incentivised components. The sales number the incentive multiplies is still the sale of those target-segment goods. A server maker that localises a board or a packaged chip is closer to this door than a pure fabless firm with no hardware revenue.

The demand signal beside this door is the parliamentary production series: mobile phones at about ₹6.27 lakh crore of production in 2025-26, and electronics goods at about ₹13.11 lakh crore. The scheme is how a hardware company is paid for deepening that assembly. It is not a subsidy on the phone export figure itself.

## Packaging, compound, and the layer the new pillar names

OSAT, ATMP, compound semiconductors, and discrete devices are where approvals have already clustered: nine of the twelve units in the July 2026 composition are packaging units, plus a silicon-carbide fab and a gallium-nitride display fab. A new packaging proposal is entering a category the government has shown it will approve, in six states, with fiscal support described in the February 2026 budget note as up to 50 percent under the first mission. It is also entering a category where Micron, Kaynes, and CG Semi are already in commercial production. The open question for a late entrant is whether Semicon 2.0's packaging pillar, which says India will be encouraged as a location for more advanced ATMP, has a notified scheme yet.

The clearer white space in the Cabinet text is machines and materials: equipment, chemicals, and gases. No approved project under that pillar is named in the 15 July release. A company that builds deposition tools, speciality gases, or process chemicals is the reader that pillar addresses. The rate and the application form are the missing documents. Until they are notified, the live capital-goods door with a published 25 percent rate is the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme's capital-equipment segment, which is electronics manufacturing equipment generally, not a semiconductor-node tool programme.
