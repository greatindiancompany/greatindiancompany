# A Risk Map for State Finance, Delivery, and Grievance Closure

A risk map is useful when each branch is a fact already in a primary document, and when the scenario is a way of misreading that fact. What follows is that kind of map for Indian state capacity. It does not assign probabilities.

## Branch one: the deficit that looks like a crisis

On 23 January 2026 the Reserve Bank reported that states' consolidated gross fiscal deficit rose to 3.3 percent of GDP in 2024-25, after three years below 3.0 percent, and that 2025-26 budgets repeated 3.3 percent. The same paragraph says the move through 3 percent mainly reflects 50-year interest-free loans under Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment, which are outside the normal net borrowing ceiling.

Scenario A is to treat 3.3 percent as a revenue accident and reprice every state project as if taxes had failed. RBI's sentence does not support that. Scenario B is to treat the loan as costless because it is interest-free and outside the ceiling. The liabilities line argues against that comfort. The budget estimate of consolidated outstanding liabilities is 29.2 percent of GDP at end-March 2026. The previous edition of the study, covering budgets of 2024-25, had put debt at 28.5 percent of GDP at end-March 2024, down from 31.8 percent at end-March 2004, and still well above the 20 percent mark the FRBM Review Committee recommended in 2017. The annual deficit can be "explained" by a capital loan and the stock can still be the risk that binds a long contract.

A third reading sits in the March 2026 loan guidelines. The second installment depends on spending 75 percent of the first and on closing Centrally Sponsored Scheme bank accounts. A state that draws the loan and cannot certificate will show a capex plan in the budget and a stalled cash flow in the year. That is an execution scenario, and it is written into the release rules.

## Branch two: delivery that is wide and uneven

DARPG's December 2025 NeSDA Way Forward report, released 22 January 2026, records 24,090 e-services and puts 8,656 of them in local governance and utility services. It also says a group of states and UTs, described in the note as 21, had crossed 90 percent saturation of 59 mandatory services, and it names seven unified portals at 100 percent of services. An earlier reply, on 10 December 2025, had mandatory saturation at 80 percent in October 2025, up from 66 percent in April 2023.

Scenario A is to quote 24,090, or "over 80 percent," as if every state and every municipal counter were at that level. The release itself prints a partial list and a separate portal list, and it says the figures are uploads as of 31 December 2025. Scenario B is to ignore the local-governance block. That block is the largest sector count in the note, and the same note says citizen interaction is largely municipal. A scheme that works on a state single window and fails on a city portal has failed where DARPG says the citizen is.

The 10 December 2025 reply adds a structural gap: there is no API integration between CPGRAMS and state Right to Service systems. A time-bound state service law and the central grievance portal can both be "working" and still not see each other. The National Centre for Good Governance study mentioned in that reply is the place to watch for whether statutory timelines are met in districts. Until it reports, the gap is the risk, not a finding that timelines are missed everywhere.

## Branch three: a fast central clock with a large pending file

The June 2026 CPGRAMS report, out on 29 July 2026, is the fiftieth monthly report on central ministries. Average disposal time in those ministries for 2026 is 14 days. June redressed 1,99,968 cases against 1,96,133 received, and 78,793 remained pending. Appeals pending were 21,753. Disposal has crossed one lakh a month for 48 months. The comfortable scenario reads only the 14 days. The operational scenario also reads the pending stock and the fact that the June report is about central ministries, while the service a household chases is often a state or a city. Group A of the Grievance Redressal Index is ministries with 500 or more grievances. A top rank in the smaller group is a different risk signal from a top rank in the larger one.

## Branch four: demography pulls states apart

RBI's theme for the 2025-26 study is demographic transition. Youthful states are described as having a wider window for human-capital investment. Ageing states are described as facing shrinking tax bases and rising committed expenditure, with the pressure landing on healthcare, pensions and workforce policy. The risk is using one national fiscal story for both. A supplier or a lender who does not know which window a state is in will misread both a capex boom and a pension bill.
