# What India’s Social Programmes Signal About Service Demand

A social-sector “market signal” in India is not a third-party revenue forecast. The ministries do not publish a total addressable market for nutrition products, school software, or hospital chains. They do publish who is already inside a public channel, who is paying or being paid, and which service is still thin. That is the signal an operator can use without inventing a market size.

## Health cover widened; the place of birth shifted

NFHS-6, fieldwork 2023-24, released by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare on 29 May 2026, puts household coverage by a health insurance or financing scheme at 60.2%, up from 41.0% in NFHS-5. The ministry names Ayushman Bharat–PM-JAY as central to that expansion. The signal is a larger insured base for inpatient care, not a count of claims or a premium pool. Those are not in the fact-sheet release.

Institutional births rose from 88.6% to 90.6%. Births in a public facility moved the other way, from 61.9% to 58.6% of births in the India fact sheet (urban 48.2%, rural 62.1%). Caesarean births rose from 21.5% to 27.2% overall, to 54.1% of births in private facilities and 16.9% in public facilities. Demand for delivery care is nearly universal and more often private than the previous survey, with an operative-birth rate that is now a service-mix fact, not a footnote.

Antenatal contact is wide: 95.9% of pregnant women received antenatal care, 76.2% in the first trimester (from 70.0%), and 65.2% had at least four visits (from 58.5%). Iron and folic acid for 180 days or more rose from 26.0% to 37.8%. The remaining gap is completion of the long course, not first contact.

## Nutrition demand is a ration and a meal, at known scale

Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 is the Ministry of Women and Child Development’s integrated nutrition programme: supplementary nutrition, early childhood care, Anganwadi infrastructure, and Poshan Abhiyaan. As of 31 July 2026 the Poshan Tracker universe was almost 8.94 crore beneficiaries across nearly 13.99 lakh centres, with children aged 6 months to 6 years above 7.2 crore and pregnant women plus lactating mothers near 1.1 crore.

The ninth Poshan Maah, launched on 9 September 2026, tells suppliers and state kitchens what the ministry wants emphasised now: dietary diversity and protein, weekly rotation of fresh hot cooked meals, and take-home ration that reaches the registered person. Facial recognition on the Poshan Tracker is the ministry’s tool for last-mile take-home ration, so a product that cannot be issued against a registered beneficiary is outside the public channel.

Children aged 6-23 months receiving an adequate diet rose from 11.0% to 15.3% in the India fact sheet. That is an improvement and still a small share. The demand signal for complementary food is a large eligible cohort and a low adequate-diet rate, not a claim that the public kitchen has already closed the gap.

## Schools are buying access, and the secondary cliff remains

UDISE+ 2025-26, released on 7 July 2026, says 69.9% of schools have a computer and 67.4% have internet, up from 64.7% and 63.5%. Electricity is in 95.0% of schools, drinking water in 99.5%, and ramps with handrails in 58.2%. The unmet share — about three in ten schools without a computer — is an infrastructure residual, not a licence to quote a device market.

Participation demand sits at the secondary transition. Secondary gross enrolment is 71.7%. Secondary retention is 51.9%, up from 47.2% in 2024-25. A service that assumes every child who enters Class 1 is still in school at Class 10 is contradicted by the ministry’s own retention figure.

## Pensions show a federal price, not a single national benefit

On 21 July 2026 the Ministry of Rural Development told the Lok Sabha that central assistance under the National Social Assistance Programme pension schemes was last revised in 2012, that there is no proposal under consideration to revise it, and that states add between ₹50 and ₹5,700 a month. In several states and Union territories the pension actually received averages about ₹1,100. About 5.9 crore additional pensioners are on state-funded schemes, beside more than 3 crore people on the central BPL rolls. A product or a cash-flow model that uses one national pension amount will mis-state household income in both a high top-up state and a low one.
