# What is investable in Indian aviation, and what is still a speech

An investor can separate the Indian aviation and tourism record into numbers a counterparty could show an auditor, and numbers that are a minister’s expectation. Both matter. They do not belong in the same cell of a model.

## The stock that has been built

On 6 August 2026 the government told the Lok Sabha that airport passenger-handling capacity is over 580 million passengers a year, and that traffic handled in 2025-26 was about 420 million. The Airports Authority of India and private operators spent about ₹19,260 crore, ₹19,317 crore, and ₹17,840 crore in the three years through 2025-26, from their own resources. Twenty-five greenfield airports have in-principle approval, 14 are operational, and 6 more have site clearance.

That is a capacity surplus at system level, bought at a run-rate near ₹18,000–19,000 crore a year, with the latest year lower. It is not an airport-by-airport yield. It is not a 2026-27 traffic guide. The same day’s answer refused the tidy long-range line it was offered. Asked about 500 million passengers before 2030 and 1 billion by 2047, the minister of state said airports are expected to handle more than 3,000 million passengers by 2046-47. Use the sentence he gave.

Modified UDAN’s ₹28,840 crore is a ten-year ceiling from 2026-27 to 2035-36, not cash already deployed. The deployable pieces inside it are ₹12,159 crore for 100 airports from unserved strips and ₹3,661 crore for 200 heliports, and only after a state challenge. Of the previous scheme’s 679 operationalised routes, 348 were still flying in July 2026. Regional projects should be underwritten against the staying rate, not the launch rate.

## The fleet line that is a speech with a date

On 8 May 2026, at the aircraft leasing summit in GIFT City, Civil Aviation Minister Ram Mohan Naidu said India’s commercial fleet is expected to reach 1,100 aircraft by 2027 and more than 2,250 by 2035, and that carriers had outstanding deliveries of 1,640 aircraft. He called that backlog a US$50 billion opportunity for India’s leasing market over the coming decade. The 1,640 and the US$50 billion are his translation of the order book into a leasing market. They are not a DGCA table of aircraft on the register, and they are not a lessor’s contracted pipeline.

What the summit did record as company announcements, in the government’s press note, is narrower. Air India’s IFSC leasing unit planned to take a portfolio from over 50 aircraft to 76 by March 2027. IndiGo’s IFSC unit planned to go from around 75 to nearly 150. Akasa said a new GIFT City entity would support leasing of about 60 aircraft over five years. Star Air aimed to lease 6 to 8 more regional aircraft from GIFT City in 2027. Those are plans stated in public. Delivery is the investment event.

Two policy facts around that financing are firmer than the fleet path. The Protection of Interests in Aircraft Objects Act, 2025, is in force, and the minister’s account of it is that lessors can exercise IDERA deregistration rights without the delays he said had raised airline costs. A credit-line guarantee scheme of ₹5,000 crore has Cabinet approval, ATF price increases have been capped at 25 percent, and landing and parking charges have been cut by 25 percent across airports. Each of those is a term. None of them is a passenger.

## Tourism cash flows are not the airport gap

Foreign-exchange earnings from tourism in calendar 2025 were ₹2,73,638 crore, 6.62 percent below 2024 and 26.4 percent above 2019. The 2024 compendium year was the stronger one: US$35.016 billion, up 8.78 percent. An airport concession priced off 2024 inbound receipts has already met a down year in rupees. Domestic airline passengers, a different series, grew 0.64 percent in January–July 2026. The demand that fills the 580 million of capacity is not on a steep official slope in the latest months.
