# The Charging and Vehicle Rules That Gate India's EV Incentives

India's electric-vehicle rulebook is a stack of eligibility tests. A vehicle can be registered and still fail the test that releases a subsidy. A charger can be installed and still fail the test that releases PM E-DRIVE support. The outlook is the set of instruments already notified, plus the amendments the ministry keeps posting for trucks, buses, and chargers.

## Charging: one guideline, then a swapping guideline

A [Lok Sabha reply of 11 March 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2110244) says the Ministry of Power issued the Guidelines for Installation and Operation of Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure-2024 on 17 September 2024, to set standards and protocols for the charging network. The same reply says the ministry issued Guidelines for Installation and Operation of Battery Swapping and Charging Stations on 10 January 2025. The [Power Ministry page](https://powermin.gov.in/en/content/amendment-guidelines-installation-and-operation-electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure) records an amendment to the 2024 guidelines dated 11 January 2025.

PM E-DRIVE's own charging guidelines require stations to comply with that 17 September 2024 Ministry of Power instrument, or with later amendments. Battery swapping and battery charging stations are eligible for financial support only on upstream infrastructure, and only if they comply with the 10 January 2025 swapping guidelines. The subsidy base is upstream infrastructure cost, with a possible extension to the charger itself, measured against benchmark costs published by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency.

A July 2025 heavy-industries notification, [S.O. 3318(E)](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/docs/policy_document/264796.pdf), replaces the phased-manufacturing annexure for public charging stations. Stations must meet the localisation schedule in force on the purchase-order date, comply with IS 17017 as tested by a ministry testing agency, and cannot import phased-manufacturing components in CKD form from a single supplier. "Manufacture" takes the CGST Act definition. The same annexure applies to FAME II charging stations that were still to be supplied. Local content for a charger is a dated schedule, not a slogan.

FAME II had allocated ₹839 crore to public charging stations. PM E-DRIVE allocates ₹2,000 crore. As on 1 July 2026, ₹689 crore of that second line had been approved for 6,562 chargers. The rule and the sanction are both real; the sanction is a fraction of the line.

## Vehicles: advanced battery, price caps, and a certificate

FAME II's [portal](https://fame2.heavyindustries.gov.in/) told every registered OEM to certify models to AIS 156 under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, as amended, in order to claim that subsidy. PM E-DRIVE inherited the testing-agency idea and funded an upgrade of ₹780 crore. The [28 July 2026 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696) says OEMs registered under PM E-DRIVE have obtained phased-manufacturing compliance certificates for their models from those agencies.

The price and rate rules now live in [S.O. 4424(E) of 10 August 2026](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/docs/policy_document/Gazette%20Notification%2010%2008%202026%20on%20enhancement%20of%20Outlay%2011900%20cr.pdf) for two-wheelers, and in the July reply for the other segments as they stood that week. Two-wheelers from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2028 draw ₹2,500 per kWh, capped at ₹5,000 and at 15 percent of ex-factory price. The July reply's other caps, which the August gazette does not rewrite in the text extracted here, include lower-of tests for trucks (₹5,000 per kWh, 10 percent of ex-factory price excluding trailer, and a GVW cap) and for buses (₹10,000 per kWh, 20 percent of CESL-discovered cost, and a length cap). Ambulances on the portal are the lower of ₹30,000 per kWh and 35 percent of ex-factory price, for patient-transport, basic life support, and advanced life support categories defined in AIS 125.

PLI Auto adds a different legal test on the same vehicle: domestic value addition of at least 50 percent before an incentive on determined sales. A model can clear phased manufacturing for PM E-DRIVE and still wait on a separate DVA certificate for PLI.

Battery recycling sits outside both schemes. The July reply points to the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022, notified by the environment ministry on 22 August 2022. It does not report a recycling tonnage under PM E-DRIVE.

## What is still moving

The notifications list on 3 September 2026 shows a fourth phased-manufacturing amendment for N2 and N3 electric trucks and another for M2 and M3 electric buses. The regulatory outlook for those two categories is whatever those PDFs say. Older PMP tables are the baseline those amendments change.
