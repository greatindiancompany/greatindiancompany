# Future of Work in India: State Labour Rules under the Codes

## Administered twice

The future of work in India is administered at two levels. The four labour codes are central statutes, in force from 21 November 2025. Labour is also concurrent. A Lok Sabha reply on 20 July 2026 says states and Union Territories have to frame rules under the codes. As of that reply, most had finalised or published rules, including drafts that were pre-published, and a few were still pre-publishing or finalising them.

This brief names no state. A September league table would need a later official list than the 20 July reply.

## Appropriate government

The ministry's compliance handbook is written for establishments whose appropriate government is the Centre. Other establishments follow the state or Union Territory that is the appropriate government. A firm with sites in several cities can sit under central rules in one place and state rules in another. Copying one state's wage period, licence practice, or standing orders into another state is an execution error.

## What to read locally

Central Rules were notified on 8 May 2026, the 20 July 2026 reply says. That settles the central-sphere rule text. It leaves the state question open wherever a state was still finalising rules on that date. On 21 November 2025 the ministry said existing Act provisions and rules would continue during the transition. After 8 May 2026 the working approach is: use the notified central rules where the Centre is the appropriate government, and use the state text where the state is. Where a state rule is still unfinished, the transition sentence still matters. The gazette is the check.

## Portability across different rules

The ministry's gig and platform note says an Aadhaar-linked Universal Account Number is meant to carry welfare benefits across states when workers migrate. Portability of a benefit can coexist with different state rules on wages, safety, and licences. e-Shram, which the ministry launched on 26 August 2021 as a national database of unorganised workers, including gig and platform workers, is a central registry. A state labour department's rules remain the local law.

## The pattern that is legal

Rural and urban estimates, when a reader needs them, are in PLFS on mospi.gov.in. The state pattern this brief limits itself to is legal: concurrent rules, an uneven publication status as of 20 July 2026, and a portable social-security identity that runs across those rules.
