# State patterns in unorganised-worker registration and gig statutes

India does not publish a state-wise "future of work" index. What it does publish, in a form a reader can check, is the state break-up of e-Shram registrations. Alongside that, several states have written their own platform-worker statutes. Those are different objects. A large register is not a gig-worker census, and a statute on the books is not proof that a welfare fee is being collected.

## Where the unorganised register sits

e-Shram was launched on 26 August 2021. Workers register on a self-declaration and receive a Universal Account Number. The portal includes gig workers, platform workers, and migrant workers, along with the rest of the unorganised workforce. It is not a state unemployment table from the Periodic Labour Force Survey.

As on 14 July 2026, a Lok Sabha reply released on 20 July 2026 reported 31,78,31,749 registrations. The largest state totals in that annexure were:

- Uttar Pradesh: 8,45,90,721
- Bihar: 3,24,68,736
- West Bengal: 2,67,08,321
- Madhya Pradesh: 1,95,17,182
- Maharashtra: 1,86,61,199
- Rajasthan: 1,55,09,348
- Odisha: 1,37,65,895
- Gujarat: 1,25,87,229
- Karnataka: 1,11,31,253

Uttar Pradesh alone was a little over a quarter of the national register. Bihar was about a tenth. At the other end, Lakshadweep had 2,876 registrations, Andaman and Nicobar Islands 36,025, and Ladakh 36,365. Delhi had 36,73,210, Tamil Nadu 98,30,406, Kerala 61,47,650, and Telangana 47,05,625.

Read that list as administrative reach and as the geography of unorganised work, not as a ranking of labour-market health. A state with more people, more informal work, and a more active registration drive will show a larger number even if its weekly unemployment is lower. The reply does not split these totals into gig workers versus other unorganised workers. Using Karnataka's 1.11 crore, or Rajasthan's 1.55 crore, as the count of platform workers in that state invents a category the table does not have.

The same reply says the Ministry launched e-Shram microsites for states and Union Territories on 29 January 2025 so that registration, updates, verification, and analytics can follow local needs. That is the state instrument for the register. It is not a state labour code.

## Two statutes, read in full, that do not match

Rajasthan and Karnataka are worked through here because their texts are public and their fee bases differ. They are not the full list. Bihar Act 8 of 2025, whose short title is the Bihar Gig and Platform Based Workers (Registration, Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2025, extends to the whole state and comes into force on a date the state notifies in the Bihar Gazette, with power to appoint different dates for different provisions. Telangana's Platform Based Gig Workers (Registration, Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2026 (Act 21 of 2026) was appointed to come into force on 2 June 2026 by G.O.Ms.No. 7 dated 1 June 2026. This page does not summarise the fee or benefit schedule of those Acts. The pattern to copy is the method: read that state's commencement clause and its fee base, and do not borrow Karnataka's.

Rajasthan's Platform Based Gig Workers (Registration and Welfare) Act, 2023 is Act 29 of 2023. It received the Governor's assent on 12 September 2023. It extends to the whole state and applies to aggregators operating there and to work that meets the Act's definitions of gig worker and platform. It sets up a welfare board and the Rajasthan Platform Based Gig Workers Social Security and Welfare Fund. It provides a unique ID for a registered platform-based gig worker. The welfare fee is a percentage of the value of each transaction related to that worker, excluding tax, at a rate the state government notifies. If a fee that has fallen due is late, the Act sets simple interest at 12 percent a year. Payments are to be mapped onto a Central Transaction Information and Management System. Section 1(3) says the Act comes into force on a date the state notifies in the Official Gazette. Until that notification is in hand, the fee and the interest rate are design, not a demonstrated collection.

Karnataka's Platform Based Gig Workers (Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2025 is Act 72 of 2025. It was published in the official gazette on 12 September 2025 after receiving assent on 11 September 2025. Unlike Rajasthan's commencement clause, this Act deems its provisions to have come into force on 30 May 2025. It applies to aggregators and platforms providing scheduled services in the state, and to gig workers registered with the board. The welfare fee is not less than 1 percent and not more than 5 percent of the payout in each transaction, with the percentage notified by the state, including the option of different rates by category. The Act required that notification within six months of coming into force. It also says the fee collected counts as the contribution payable under section 114(4) of the Code on Social Security, 2020, with a yearly reconciliation of any difference.

Karnataka adds obligations Rajasthan's fee section does not. Contracts must be transparent, must state the right to refuse a task, and must be notified at least 14 days before a change. Aggregators must explain, in simple language, how automated monitoring and decision systems affect fares, earnings, and related conditions, and must take measures against discrimination by those systems. Covered aggregators and platforms register with the board within 45 days of commencement. The board itself is established from a date the state appoints, with its headquarters at Bengaluru.

## What most states have instead

For states without a dedicated gig statute, the live instruments are the four central labour codes, effective from 21 November 2025, and the e-Shram register with its state microsites. The central code's aggregator contribution has its own notified start date under section 114(5). A state that has not enacted a platform-worker law is not thereby unregulated, and a state that has enacted one is not thereby collecting a fee.

The pattern worth keeping is geographic and legal, not a league table. Registrations pool in the large Hindi-belt and eastern states because that is where the unorganised register has been filled. The distinctive law is in Rajasthan, which waits on a commencement notification and charges a fee on transaction value, and in Karnataka, which is deemed in force from 30 May 2025, charges a fee on payout, and writes an explicit set-off against the central contribution.
