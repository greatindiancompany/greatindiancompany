The geography India's statistical office actually publishes every month is rural and urban, for the country as a whole. The August 2026 bulletin of the Periodic Labour Force Survey does not rank states. Readers who want a state unemployment rate have to go to the annual report, and they have to keep the year and the status attached.

All of the August figures are current weekly status for persons aged 15 and above: the activity in the seven days before the interview. The bulletin was released on 15 September 2026.

## The rural–urban gap in August 2026

Rural labour-force participation was 58.2%. Urban participation was 50.4%, unchanged from July. Over the year, rural participation rose by 1.2 percentage points, from 57.0% in August 2025. Urban participation fell by 0.5 percentage point, from 50.9%.

Rural employment, the worker population ratio, was 55.8%, up from 55.4% in July and from 54.5% in August 2025. Urban employment was 47.0%, the same as July, and 0.5 percentage point below August 2025 (47.5%).

Rural unemployment was 4.1%, down from 4.5% in July and from 4.3% in August 2025. The note calls 4.1% the lowest rural rate since January 2026. Urban unemployment was 6.8%, against 6.7% in July. The overall rate was 5.0%.

Inside that gap, rural male unemployment was 4.3% and urban male unemployment was 6.1%. Rural female unemployment was 3.9%. Rural female participation was 39.4% and urban female participation was 25.4%. The urban female unemployment rate for August is described as broadly stable over the month; the note does not print a separate figure.

The rural sample was 2,11,353 persons and the urban sample was 1,58,807, within 3,70,160 persons surveyed.

## The same gap in the last full annual year

For July 2023–June 2024, in usual status (a 365-day reference, including subsidiary work), rural unemployment was 2.5% and urban unemployment was 5.1%. Rural participation was 63.7% and urban participation was 52.0%. Rural employment was 62.1% and urban employment was 49.4%.

Urban female unemployment in that annual usual status was 7.1%. Rural female unemployment was 2.1%. The weekly-status annual rates for the same year were higher in both places: rural 4.2%, urban 6.7%.

The August 2026 urban weekly rate of 6.8% sits close to that 2023–24 urban weekly rate of 6.7%. The survey methodology was modified in January 2025, so the closeness is a description of two published levels, not a claim that nothing changed in the design. How the two statuses differ is in the [key-numbers brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-key-numbers-20260330-020).

## One state the labour ministry has put on the record

State tables live in the annual report. On 1 December 2025 the Ministry of Labour and Employment, citing those reports, gave Maharashtra's usual-status unemployment for persons aged 15 and above: 4.8% in 2017–18 and 3.3% in 2023–24. Rural Maharashtra moved from 3.2% to 2.1%. Urban Maharashtra moved from 7.4% to 5.2%. The all-India usual-status rate in that reply is the familiar 6.0% to 3.2%.

Maharashtra's 3.3% is a usual-status annual rate for 2023–24. It is not the state's reading of the August 2026 monthly bulletin, which is not published as a state cut in the press note.

## The payroll map is a third geography

EPFO's provisional payroll for July 2025 concentrates net member additions in a handful of states. Maharashtra contributed 20.47% of net payroll that month. The top five states and Union territories contributed about 60.85%. Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Haryana, Delhi, Telangana, and Uttar Pradesh each contributed more than 5%.

A large share of formal-sector member additions and a low survey unemployment rate answer different questions. One counts joiners, exits, and rejoins in covered establishments. The other estimates people without work in a rural or urban sample. Using Maharashtra's payroll share as if it were Maharashtra's unemployment rate mixes those questions. The payroll definitions are in the [EPFO brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-impact-on-smes-20260330-030).
