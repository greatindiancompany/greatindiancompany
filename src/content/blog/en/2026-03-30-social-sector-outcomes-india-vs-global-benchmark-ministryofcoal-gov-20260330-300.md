# How to Benchmark India’s Social Outcomes on Its Own Series

A global league table is the wrong benchmark for these releases. The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, the Ministry of Education, and the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation each publish India against its own previous round. The 29 May 2026 NFHS-6 note says the findings sit with the Sustainable Development Goals. It does not print a World Health Organization rank or a country-by-country table. Using an outside rank here would be an invented comparison. The benchmark that is actually in the document is the column the fact sheet already prints.

## Read the NFHS-6 change as levels, then as the ministry’s relative claim

The India fact sheet, May 2026, places NFHS-6 (2023-24) beside NFHS-5 (2019-21). For children under five:

- Stunting: 29.3%, from 35.5%. Urban 23.9%, rural 30.9%.
- Wasting: 19.0%, from 19.3%. Urban 16.5%, rural 19.8%.
- Severe wasting: 5.2%, from 7.7%.
- Underweight: 31.8%, from 32.1%. Urban 25.3%, rural 33.8%.
- Overweight: 1.3%, from 3.4%.

The press-note headline says stunting fell by 17% and severe wasting by 32%. Those are relative changes on the NFHS-5 base: 6.2 points on a 35.5% stunting rate, and 2.5 points on a 7.7% severe-wasting rate. They are not 17-point and 32-point drops. A slide that treats them as percentage-point gaps will overstate the move and hide how little wasting and underweight changed.

Feeding has its own benchmark, and it splits. Early initiation of breastfeeding rose from 41.8% to 50.1%. Children aged 6-8 months receiving solid or semi-solid food and breastmilk rose from 45.9% to 59.5%. An adequate diet at 6-23 months rose from 11.0% to 15.3%. Exclusive breastfeeding under six months fell from 63.7% to 55.8%, while children under six months currently breastfeeding stayed at 95.6% (95.1% in NFHS-5). “Breastfed” and “exclusively breastfed” are different benchmarks. The press note used the first.

Service coverage that did move: institutional births 88.6% to 90.6%; four or more antenatal visits 58.5% to 65.2%; full vaccination on cards at 12-23 months 83.8% to 87.1%; rotavirus 36.4% to 85.4%; household health-insurance or financing coverage 41.0% to 60.2%. The total fertility rate is unchanged at 2.0. A sentence that says every maternal indicator rose is too broad for a flat fertility rate and a lower exclusive-breastfeeding rate.

Anaemia is not in this benchmark. The May 2026 press note and the fact-sheet nutrition lines used here do not publish a new national anaemia prevalence. NFHS-5 anaemia figures are not updated by a cell this release left blank.

## UDISE+ benchmarks participation against the previous academic year

The 7 July 2026 UDISE+ release benchmarks 2025-26 against 2022-23, 2023-24, and 2024-25. It does not benchmark test scores. Secondary gross enrolment 68.5% to 71.7%, secondary retention 47.2% to 51.9%, preparatory dropout 2.3% to 1.8%, and secondary dropout 8.2% to 7.0% are the participation gains. The middle dropout series in that table is 3.5% then 3.6%. Pupil-teacher ratios of 10, 12, 17, and 21 are reported against the National Education Policy reference of 30:1. That is an Indian policy benchmark, not an international assessment.

The release text calls foundational and preparatory retention marginally lower in 2025-26 after three years of improvement. A single line that says retention is up drops that exception.

## PLFS benchmarks a month against the previous month and the same month a year earlier

The August 2026 monthly bulletin, released 15 September 2026, uses the current weekly status for persons aged 15 and above. Labour force participation was 55.6% in August 2026, from 55.4% in July 2026 and 55.0% in August 2025. The worker population ratio was 52.8%. Unemployment was 5.0% overall and 4.1% in rural areas. Female participation rose from 33.7% in August 2025 to 34.8%, with the rural female rate at 39.4% and the urban female rate at 25.4%, down from 26.1% a year earlier. The all-India female gain and the urban female decline are both in the same note.

## What a serious comparison looks like

Compare an indicator with the same definition, the same age group, and the previous official round. Say whether the change is in points or relative to the old base. Keep rural and urban. Keep the indicator that did not move. Leave global ranks out until an Indian ministry publishes them in the same release.
