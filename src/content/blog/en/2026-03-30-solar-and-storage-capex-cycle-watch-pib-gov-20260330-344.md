# Two Capex Clocks for India's Solar Factories and Batteries

Solar manufacturing and battery storage are both capital projects. The official schemes pay them on different clocks, and the battery clock has already been reset once because equipment got cheaper.

## Clock one: the module factory

MNRE's PLI page says the incentive for high-efficiency solar modules is paid for five years after commissioning, and only on modules manufactured and sold. The outlay is Rs 24,000 crore across two tranches. Tranche I, guidelines dated 28 April 2021, is Rs 4,500 crore through IREDA, with letters of award in November and December 2021 to three bidders for 8,737 MW of fully integrated capacity. Tranche II, guidelines dated 30 September 2022, is Rs 19,500 crore through SECI, with letters of award in April 2023 to 11 bidders for 39,600 MW of fully or partially integrated capacity.

The capex cycle of the sponsor and the cash cycle of the incentive are sequential. The sponsor funds construction. Commissioning opens a five-year window. Sales inside that window are what the incentive attaches to. A delay in commissioning does not, on this page, create a holding payment. It moves the window.

SECI's solar page adds the tranche-II allocation actually assigned to those 11 manufacturers: around Rs 13,937.535 crore, disbursed over five years after commissioning. That is the implementing agency's allocation figure. It is not a report of rupees already paid. The MNRE overview updated on 8 August 2023 does not list disbursements.

## Clock two: the battery project

PIB release 2118325, dated 3 April 2025, describes the viability-gap funding scheme for battery energy storage systems, approved by the Union Cabinet on 6 September 2023. Support is for projects approved during 2023-26. Payment is in five slices: 10 percent at financial closure, 45 percent at commercial operation, and 15 percent a year for the next three years after commercial operation.

The first slice is the one that tells you whether the capex cycle has started in the scheme's own terms. In 2024-25, a provision of Rs 96 crore was made for 1,000 MWh on the assumption that 10 percent would be paid at financial closure. No project achieved financial closure. The reply says no expenditure was incurred under the scheme that year.

Price changed the size of each slice before any slice was paid. The note says the viability-gap amount fell from Rs 96 lakh per MWh, the 2023-24 estimate, to Rs 46 lakh per MWh or 30 percent of capital cost, whichever is lower, because battery costs fell. The budgetary allocation for that 1,000 MWh slice was revised from Rs 96 crore to Rs 46 crore. Scheme capacity was increased from 4,000 MWh to 13,200 MWh while remaining inside the approved budgetary allocation of Rs 3,760 crore.

Read that as a capex-cycle fact. Cheaper batteries let the same rupee envelope cover more megawatt-hours, and they also cut the grant per megawatt-hour. A model that froze the 2023 grant rate will overstate support. A model that treats the larger megawatt-hour figure as commissioned capacity will overstate steel in the ground. As of the April 2025 reply, the gate that had not been crossed was financial closure.

## What to watch on each clock

For modules, the next official fact that matters is commissioning of an awarded line, because that is what starts the five-year sale window. For batteries, the next official fact that matters is financial closure, because that is what releases the first 10 percent. Ministry of Power oversees the battery scheme. The Central Electricity Authority monitors it. MNRE and SECI run the module incentive.
