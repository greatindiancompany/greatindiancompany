# Three years of airport capital spending, and the next regional envelope

Airport capital spending in India is two envelopes, and the Lok Sabha papers of 6 August 2026 keep them apart.

The first is what the Airports Authority of India and private airport operators have already spent from their own resources. The reply on expansion of airport infrastructure puts that at about ₹19,260 crore in 2023-24, ₹19,317 crore in 2024-25, and ₹17,840 crore in 2025-26. The path is flat and then slightly lower. It is the spending that the same reply links to a system capacity of over 580 million passengers a year, against about 420 million passengers handled in 2025-26.

The second envelope is Modified UDAN, launched on 4 July 2026 and running from 2026-27 to 2035-36. The outlay is ₹28,840 crore over ten years. Inside it, ₹12,159 crore is for developing 100 airports from existing unserved airstrips, and ₹3,661 crore is for 200 heliports in hilly, north-eastern, and aspirational areas. The Press Information Bureau note of 16 July describes the airport line as inflation-adjusted support at about ₹100 crore per airport, and the helipad line at about ₹15 crore per modern helipad. Dividing ₹12,159 crore by 100, or ₹3,661 crore by 200, lands on those unit figures. They are planning allowances in the scheme note, not tendered costs for a named strip.

## Where the recent AAI works are named

The 6 August expansion reply names the major infrastructure projects then underway, without a rupee figure on each. In Andhra Pradesh: Kadapa, Rajahmundry, and Vijayawada. In Bihar: Darbhanga. In Gujarat: Keshod. In Haryana: Hisar. In Jammu and Kashmir: Jammu. In Karnataka: Belagavi and Hubballi. In Kerala: Calicut. In Ladakh: Leh. In Manipur: Imphal. In Rajasthan: Kota and Udaipur. In Sikkim: Pakyong. In Tamil Nadu: Chennai. In Uttar Pradesh: Agra and Varanasi. In West Bengal: Bagdogra and Kolkata.

The works listed are terminals, aprons, runway-end safety areas, runway extension and resurfacing, rapid-exit taxiways, and link taxi tracks. A reader looking for a passenger forecast at Vijayawada or Leh will not find it in this reply. The useful fact is the mix: large-city airside work at Chennai and Kolkata sits on the same list as Keshod, Pakyong, and Hisar.

Greenfield airports are a third count in the companion reply the same day. Twenty-five have in-principle approval, 14 have been operationalised, and 6 proposals have site clearance. Ninety-five aerodromes have been operationalised under RCS-UDAN. Those 95 are the connectivity stock. They are not a subset of the 14 greenfield airports, and the reply does not add them into one “new airport” total.

## How to watch the cycle from here

A capex watch that adds ₹17,840 crore and ₹28,840 crore is adding one year of operator spending to a ten-year scheme ceiling. They will overlap at some cities and they will not be spent on the same timetable. The scheme still has to pass aerodromes through a challenge — land, readiness, feasibility, traffic potential, clearances, and state support — before the ₹12,159 crore becomes a construction site.

The ministry’s stated check on pace is regular project reviews with the agencies doing the work. The passenger-side works named in the same reply are DigiYatra, e-gates, in-line baggage screening, common-use check-in, and automatic tray retrieval at security. Those are operating systems inside terminals, not extra runway capacity.
