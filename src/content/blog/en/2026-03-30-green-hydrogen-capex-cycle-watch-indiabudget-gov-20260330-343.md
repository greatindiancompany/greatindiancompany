# Electrolyser Manufacturing Awards, and the July 2026 Service Agreement

The electrolyser is the machine the Mission chose to subsidise on purpose. A green hydrogen plant that imports every stack is an energy story that remains an import story. The capital cycle around that machine now has two public paths: a manufacturing award, and a model service contract.

## The incentive that pays for factories

On the [MNRE mission page](https://mnre.gov.in/en/national-green-hydrogen-mission/), the Strategic Interventions for Green Hydrogen Transition programme has two legs and an outlay of ₹17,490 crore through 2029-30. One leg is an incentive for electrolyser manufacturing. The other is an incentive for green hydrogen production. The [schemes list](https://mnre.gov.in/en/hydrogen-schemes-guidelines/) shows how that split was written into guidelines: Component I manufacturing guidelines dated 28 June 2023, Tranche II manufacturing guidelines dated 16 March 2024, Mode-1 production guidelines dated 28 June 2023, and Mode-2A and Mode-2B guidelines dated 16 January 2024.

The manufacturing result stated by Minister Pralhad Joshi on 17 June 2026 is specific. Financial incentives have been awarded to 15 companies to establish 3,000 MW a year of indigenous electrolyser manufacturing capacity, with the stated purpose of reducing dependence on imported supply chains. The Press Information Bureau note of 12 November 2025 had already said that 15 firms had been awarded 3,000 MW of annual electrolyser manufacturing capacity. The company count and the megawatt figure match across those two official statements. A megawatt of awarded factory capacity becomes a capital cycle when the firm builds the line and ships stacks. The award itself is the eligibility for support.

Downstream of those factories, the same June speech says SIGHT incentives have been awarded for 8,62,000 tonnes a year of green hydrogen production. That is the production capex the stacks are meant to serve. Refinery projects under Mode-2B, listed on 11 August 2026 at 30 kilo-tonnes a year across Panipat, Bina, Vizag, and Numaligarh, are build-own-operate. The developer’s capital is the plant at the refinery gate, which may buy a stack from an awardee or contract for it another way.

## A service contract next to ownership

On 24 July 2026, MNRE published a model service agreement for “Electrolyser as a Service” and listed it on the schemes page. This brief does not paraphrase clauses that were not read line by line. The existence of the model matters because it puts a service contract next to the ownership model that the manufacturing awards assume. A refinery, a fertiliser-linked ammonia plant, or a port tenant can look at buying electrolyser performance as a service. Uptime, power, and replacement of the stack then have to live in that contract. Treating “as a service” as automatically cheaper, before reading the file, skips the only document MNRE has published for the purpose.

## Approval sits on both paths

The mission page says eligibility for competitive bidding for green hydrogen and its derivatives is to require equipment approved by the Government of India against specified quality and performance criteria. A manufacturer who holds a SIGHT award and a service provider who never sought that approval are in different positions if the tender carries the clause. A monthly fee does not cure an unapproved stack.

The cycle to watch is therefore two clocks. One is whether the 15 awardees turn 3,000 MW a year of manufacturing capacity into lines that can meet the approval rule. The other is whether the July 2026 service agreement is used by Mode-2B developers and port-hinterland plants, or whether it stays a template on the schemes page. Great Indian Company will treat a signed service contract, when one is published, as a different fact from the template’s publication date.
