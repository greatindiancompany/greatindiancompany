# India’s foreign arrivals and tourism earnings against the world table

India’s place in world tourism is a pair of shares from the Ministry of Tourism’s India Tourism Data Compendium 2025, which reports the 2024 outcome. International tourist arrivals were 20.57 million. That was 1.40 percent of the world total and 20th among countries. Foreign-exchange earnings from tourism were US$35.016 billion, up 8.78 percent from US$32.189 billion in 2023, and about 2.02 percent of world tourism receipts. On receipts, the compendium’s time series puts India 15th in 2024.

The world total beside those shares is the UN Tourism figure the ministry reprints: 1,465 million international arrivals in 2024, against 1,466 million in 2019. India’s 20.57 million was 8.89 percent above its own previous year and 14.85 percent above 2019, on the compendium’s country table. The rank and the share can rise while the country is still a small slice of the world total. Both are true of 2024.

## Three Indian counts that should not be averaged

International tourist arrivals, 20.57 million, are foreign tourists plus non-resident Indians. Foreign tourist arrivals were 9.95 million in 2024, up 4.52 percent from 2023. Non-resident Indian arrivals were 10.62 million, up 13.22 percent from 2023 and 52.15 percent above 2019. The diaspora count is larger than the foreign-tourist count. A sentence that says “India had 20.57 million foreign tourists” has added the two series together and misnamed them.

Inside the foreign-tourist 9.95 million, the compendium’s 2024 region split is Asia and the Pacific 43.00 percent, Europe 26.70 percent, and North America 23.10 percent. The top ten source countries — the United States, Bangladesh, the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Germany, France, and Singapore — were 68.64 percent of foreign tourist arrivals. December was the peak month, at 1.06 million arrivals and 10.66 percent of the year.

Air was the way in for 83.41 percent of foreign arrivals. Land was 16.05 percent and water 0.55 percent. Delhi and Mumbai airports together handled 57.2 percent of the air arrivals. Average stay was 18.12 days, longer for Canada (30.99 days), Australia (23.99 days), and the United States (23.80 days).

## What 2025 did to the earnings line

The compendium stops at 2024. The next official earnings path is the ministry’s month-by-month foreign-exchange table for January–December 2025. The year totals ₹2,73,638 crore, against ₹2,93,033 crore in 2024. That is a 6.62 percent decline in rupees, and it is still 26.4 percent above the 2019 rupee total of ₹2,16,467 crore. The weak months against 2024 were May (−21.35 percent) and June (−22.2 percent). January and December were higher than a year earlier, by 4.35 percent and 4.07 percent.

A Press Information Bureau backgrounder of 31 July 2026 describes 2025 in round figures: around 2.02 crore international tourist arrivals, and nearly US$31.7 billion in international tourism receipts. The 2.02 crore is the international series, in the same family as 2024’s 20.57 million, not the foreign-tourist series of 9.95 million. The dollar receipt and the rupee total are a pair. They are not two different years.

## The aviation rank is a different claim

Calling India the world’s third-largest domestic aviation market is a statement by the civil aviation minister, repeated in the 16 July 2026 UDAN workshop note. It is not a line in the tourism compendium, and the compendium does not publish a passenger comparison with the United States or China. The tourism benchmark that can be checked is the arrival share, the receipt share, and the Travel and Tourism Development Index 2024 rank the ministry cites: 39th overall, 6th in natural resources, 9th in cultural resources, 26th in air transport.
