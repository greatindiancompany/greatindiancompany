# Why India's Solar Schemes Differ by State

State patterns in Indian solar are written into the scheme rules. They are not a ranking this brief can build, because the official notes below do not publish a full state table of megawatts.

## The assistance rate itself changes with geography

PIB release 2042069, dated 6 August 2024, describes PM-KUSUM, launched in March 2019 and scaled up in January 2024. Component B is standalone solar pumps. Component C is solarisation of grid-connected pumps, either at the individual pump or at feeder level. For both, central financial assistance is 30 percent, and 50 percent in the North Eastern Region, hilly regions, and islands.

That single rule is a state pattern. A pump in a covered hill state and a pump on the plains are not the same central contribution, before any state top-up. The note does not list which districts count as hilly for this purpose. An operator needs the scheme guideline and the state implementing agency for that boundary.

Component A is different again. Farmers, cooperatives, panchayats, farmer producer organisations, water-user associations, or a developer can put up to 2 MW of decentralised grid-connected renewable capacity on farm land. Distribution companies buy the power at a pre-fixed levelised tariff. If the farmer leases the land, lease rent is also available. The tariff is therefore a state utility's tariff, not a national auction price. The centre pays the distribution company a performance incentive of Rs 0.40 per unit purchased or Rs 6.6 lakh per MW installed, whichever is less, for five years from commercial operation. The company may pass that incentive on to the plant owner.

## One official state count, and why it is not a league table

The same Rajya Sabha reply says 4,11,222 farmers in the country had benefited under PM-KUSUM as on 30 June 2024. It then gives one state figure: the Uttar Pradesh implementing agency reported 51,097 farmers benefited as on 29 July 2024. Those dates differ by a month. The national figure and the state figure are not a share you should compute as if they were a closed set on one day. The reply does not give the other states. Publishing a ranking from this note would be an invention.

What the reply does show is the reporting path. The state number comes from the state implementing agency. National progress is only as current as that chain. A founder comparing two states should ask each agency for the same component, the same date, and the same unit: farmers, pumps, or megawatts. This note cannot supply the missing states.

## Household rooftop is also administered through states

PM Surya Ghar's cabinet note, PIB release 2010133 of 29 February 2024, says a household applies on the national portal and selects a vendor, and that a model solar village is to be developed in each district. The state enters through the distribution company that bills the house and buys surplus power.

The later progress note, PIB release 2294208 of 4 August 2026, makes the state difference concrete without a full map. Thirty-two states and Union Territories have removed application and net-metering charges. The note does not name the states that have not. API integration is described with more than 80 distribution companies, which are state or regional utilities, not one national retailer. Utility-led aggregation had completed 1.6 lakh installations for PMAY, BPL, and SC/ST households across four states, and was sanctioned for twelve states. Again, the states are not named in the sentences this brief relies on, so they are not guessed here.

Incentives in that note also land on state institutions: Rs 3,807.6 crore released to distribution companies and about Rs 104 crore to urban local bodies. A city accelerator covers 46 cities. Government-building solarisation is split between central ministries and states, with states identifying about 3,20,000 feasible buildings.

## What not to conclude

Battery storage obligations, as MNRE's storage page describes them, fall on obligated entities and rise on a national trajectory from 1 percent in FY 2023-24 to 4 percent in FY 2029-30. That page does not give a state battery map. A state solar story that needs storage capacity by state has to come from a state regulator or the Central Electricity Authority's state filings, not from this set of notes.
