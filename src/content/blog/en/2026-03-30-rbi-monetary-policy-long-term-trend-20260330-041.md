The repo rate is a meeting-by-meeting decision. The target it serves is set for five years at a time. On 25 March 2026 the Government of India, in consultation with the Reserve Bank, renewed the inflation target at 4 per cent, with an upper tolerance of 6 per cent and a lower tolerance of 2 per cent, from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031. The August 2026 pause sits inside that renewed mandate, not beside it.

The account of the framework here is from Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta’s 22 May 2026 speech, published in the RBI Bulletin, and from the 5 August 2026 resolution of the Monetary Policy Committee.

## What was fixed in 2016, and what was renewed

The Reserve Bank of India Act was amended in May 2016. The primary objective given to monetary policy is to maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth. Section 45ZA says the Central Government shall, in consultation with the Bank, determine the inflation target in terms of the Consumer Price Index once every five years.

The first notification set 4 per cent, plus or minus 2 percentage points, for 2016 to 2021. A Gazette notification of 31 March 2021 kept that target through March 2026. The 25 March 2026 notification is the second statutory renewal, and Gupta’s speech says every feature of the framework was retained. The target is still headline CPI, still a point of 4 per cent, still a band of 2 percentage points on either side. It was not switched to core inflation, and it was not replaced by a range without a point.

The committee that must hit the target is the Monetary Policy Committee. It decides the policy repo rate. Decisions are by majority. The Governor has a casting vote in a tie. Gupta notes that the casting vote has not been used in the decade since the committee was created.

The communication habits are part of the framework, not an optional extra. After each meeting the Reserve Bank publishes the resolution, and on the fourteenth day it publishes the minutes, including each member’s vote and statement. The Governor’s statement and the press briefing sit alongside those texts. A Monetary Policy Report is published every six months. The April 2026 report is the latest of those half-yearly documents.

## What the first decade did to inflation and growth

Gupta’s speech compares the decade before inflation targeting with the decade under it. Average headline CPI inflation fell from 8.1 per cent in 2006-16 to 4.6 per cent in 2016-26. The range of inflation narrowed from 3.3–13.4 per cent to 0.3–7.8 per cent.

Growth did not pay for that decline in the way critics of a single nominal anchor often expect. Average annual GDP growth edged up from 6.8 per cent in the pre-targeting decade to 7.0 per cent in the targeting decade, once the COVID years 2020-21 and 2021-22 are left out. The range of annual growth outcomes moved from 3.1–8.5 per cent to 3.9–9.2 per cent. The floor rose. The speech treats price stability and growth as complementary under this framework.

A cross-country table in the same speech, drawn from the IMF World Economic Outlook database, puts India’s average inflation at 4.4 per cent over 2016-25 and at 2.2 per cent in 2025. The emerging-market and developing-economy average for 2025 is 5.2 per cent. The world average is 4.1 per cent. Before targeting, India’s inflation had sat above both of those averages. During the targeting years it moved below the emerging-market average and toward the world average.

Forty-eight countries now run inflation targeting, 14 of them advanced economies. Forty-seven target headline inflation. Uganda is the exception that targets core. Gupta notes that no country that adopted inflation targeting has abandoned it, though frameworks get revised.

## Why the 2026 review kept headline CPI

Ahead of the renewal, the Reserve Bank published a discussion paper on 21 August 2025 and asked four questions: headline or core as the guide, whether 4 per cent was still the right point, whether the band should be narrower, wider or removed, and whether a range should replace a point target with a band. It received 75 direct responses. On the headline-versus-core question, 66 of 72 respondents favoured keeping headline CPI. Of those 66, 41 wanted headline alone, 21 wanted headline as the formal target with core as an operational guide, and 4 wanted core as an extra explicit target.

The August 2026 resolution is written in that retained language. It says the committee will stay resolute in aligning inflation with the target. In the 5 August press conference the Governor said the target is headline inflation, not core and not core excluding a pressure item, and that the commitment is to bring headline inflation to the target over the medium term. Core is something the committee watches in order to see where headline is likely to settle. It is not the target.

## Where the August projections sit in the band

The August resolution projects CPI inflation for 2026-27 at 5.0 per cent, peaking at 5.9 per cent in the third quarter, then 5.5 per cent in the fourth, and 5.3 per cent in the first quarter of 2027-28. All of those points are above the 4 per cent target. All of them are inside the 2 to 6 per cent tolerance band. The third-quarter figure is one tenth of a percentage point under the upper edge.

That is a different inflation world from the one in which the repo rate was brought down to 5.25 per cent. In the August minutes the Governor says average inflation that year was two per cent. The rate is unchanged. The target is unchanged. The distance from the point target is what the October meeting, set for 5 to 7 October 2026, has to judge.
