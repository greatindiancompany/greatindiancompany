# Why an Indian draft offer document still waits

Teams describe an IPO delay as "regulatory." SEBI's Annual Report 2025-26, Chapter 3, measures something narrower. Most of the extra days sit with the intermediary or with another regulator, and several stops happen before SEBI's clock is the relevant one.

## The clock SEBI publishes

Table 3.6 covers applications for public issues.

| | 2024-25 | 2025-26 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applications received | 190 | 244 |
| Processed | 164 | 247 |
| Pending at year-end | 61 | 58 |
| Median working days, queries excluded | 26 | 25 |
| Average working days, queries excluded | 28 | 27 |

The footnote for 2025-26 adds the number operators actually feel. Including time when queries sat with the intermediary or with another regulator, the median was 84 working days and the average was 86. The regulator's own handling, on the excluded-query basis, was about five weeks. The full cycle was about four months. A process review that staffs only for the 25-day figure will miss the queue that matters.

Pending stock did not clear. Fifty-eight applications were still open at 31 March 2026, against 61 a year earlier, even though more files were processed than received. Carry-in is a permanent feature of the desk, not a one-year backlog story.

Open-offer documents, a different product under the takeover regulations, have their own queue. Of 156 draft letters of offer in the year's stock, SEBI issued observations on 125, none were withdrawn, and 31 were pending on 31 March 2026. Scheme-of-arrangement observations rose from 65 to 92. Those are not IPOs. They compete for the same species of staff time at the company, the exchange, and the regulator.

## Stops that happen before the clock starts

Four conditions in the same chapter and in the April circular will hold a draft even when the 25-day desk is free.

Demat. Promoter group, selling shareholders, key managerial personnel, senior management, qualified institutional buyers, and directors must hold in dematerialised form before the draft red herring prospectus is filed. A physical certificate in a promoter's name is not a disclosure issue. It is a filing defect. Chapter 4's special window for pre-April 2019 paper transfers runs only to 4 February 2027 and is for a completed purchase with a deed. It is not a shortcut for an IPO cap table.

Pledged lock-in. The 21 March 2026 ICDR amendment lets depositories record pledged specified securities as non-transferable for the lock-in when a normal lock-in cannot be created. Circular HO/49/(17)2026-CFD-POD2/I/8965/2026 of 8 April 2026 says issuers follow the depository framework: articles of association, intimation to the pledgee, and offer-document disclosure. A lender that has not been told, or an articles clause that still forbids the marking, stops the pre-issue capital condition. Invocation or release of the pledge has to preserve the remaining lock-in. That is a systems task at NSDL or CDSL and at the depository participant, not a paragraph the lawyer can add on the last night.

Merchant banker category. Only Category 1, minimum net worth ₹50 crore, may manage a main-board equity issue. Category 2, at ₹10 crore, is ineligible for that mandate. Appointing the wrong category is a restart. The compliance officer must hold a legal or company-secretary qualification. A missing qualification is a firm-level defect that surfaces in a filing review.

Abridged prospectus. The standard summary is filed with the draft and posted online. Treating it as a marketing leave-behind, produced after SEBI's observations, misses the sequence the chapter describes.

Exchange in-principle approval sits beside SEBI's observations. ICDR still requires an application to a recognised stock exchange and a designated exchange. The exchange circular for that issue is the document that converts an observation letter into a bidding schedule. SEBI's annual report does not publish a median for the exchange leg. Leaving it out of the plan is how the 84-day SEBI figure gets blamed for a delay that has moved next door.

## What speeds up, and what does not

Chapter 3 records simplifications that remove repeated disclosure, especially for qualified institutional placements, where risk factors, financial position, and business description can be summarised because the issuer is already public. QIP volume still fell by half, to ₹67,853 crore. Easier paper did not produce more deals. The bottleneck for a first-time issuer is not the QIP shortcut. It is the draft-stage set above.

Anchor allocation was also rewritten, merging the old size buckets up to ₹250 crore and reserving 40 per cent of the anchor book, 33.33 per cent for mutual funds and 6.67 per cent for life insurers and pension funds. That is a book-building constraint. It becomes a bottleneck only if the reserved buyers do not show up. It is not why a draft sits for 84 working days.

The practical order is demat and pledge marking, banker category, abridged prospectus, then a query log that assumes most of the 84 days are the issuer's to spend. SEBI's 25-day median is the residual.
