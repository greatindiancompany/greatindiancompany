# The regulatory outlook for India’s export trade rebates

The outlook for export rules in late 2026 is a stack of notices with different clocks. None of them is a forecast of the next rate. Each one tells you which document is in force, and which review the government has already scheduled.

## RoDTEP: the cap that was imposed and then withdrawn

On 23 February 2026, Notification No. 60/2025–26 restricted RoDTEP rates to 50 percent of the rates then in force. On 23 March 2026 the government restored rates and value caps for all eligible export products to the rates in force on 22 February 2026. The [PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2244147) says the new notification supersedes the 23 February notification and the 24 February corrigendum, except for actions already taken. The reason given was maritime routing and transit changes in West Asia, higher freight, and war-related trade risk on the Gulf corridor.

The legal text is DGFT’s [notification](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/1ffbd2bf-78ce-4d6e-8a59-5c0d16fdb1d0/Notification%2066%20English_0001.pdf). The rate card a shipper applies is the schedule on the [DGFT RoDTEP page](https://www.dgft.gov.in/CP/?opt=rodtep), not a percentage remembered from February. Value caps bind in rupees per unit as well as in percent of FOB. Restoring a rate without checking the cap is an incomplete reading.

## RoSCTL is not a subset of that notice

RoSCTL covers apparel and garments under chapters 61 and 62 and made-ups under chapter 63. The 22 April 2026 textiles release says the government extended both RoSCTL and RoDTEP beyond 31 March 2026. That sentence is an extension, not a merger. A line that qualifies for RoSCTL should not be assumed to sit on the RoDTEP schedule, and the reverse is also true.

## The credit rule has its own calendar

Niryat Protsahan, under the Export Promotion Mission, is export credit rather than a tax remission. The [2 January 2026 note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2210874) set a base interest subvention of 2.75 percent on pre- and post-shipment rupee export credit, on a positive list covering about 75 percent of tariff lines at the six-digit HS level, with an exporter-wise cap of ₹50 lakh per IEC for 2025–26. Additional incentive for notified under-represented markets was left subject to operational readiness. Rates are to be reviewed twice a year, in March and September, against domestic and global benchmarks. The Reserve Bank was to issue the operational guidelines. A collateral guarantee, through CGTMSE, was set at up to 85 percent for micro and small exporters and up to 65 percent for medium exporters, with outstanding guaranteed exposure capped at ₹10 crore per exporter in a financial year. Both interventions were described as pilots.

As of 24 September 2026, that September review window is the regulatory date that is actually on the calendar. This brief does not know the outcome of a review that had not been published in the notes used here. The ₹50 lakh cap is stated for 2025–26. It should not be typed into a 2026–27 plan until the next guideline says so.

## What “outlook” means here

Expect DGFT notifications to move RoDTEP rates and caps without a new Foreign Trade Policy. Expect RoSCTL to stay on the textiles side of the ledger unless a later notice says otherwise. Expect the interest-subvention parameters to be revisited on the March/September cycle, with RBI guidelines as the operating manual. Great Indian Company will not invent the next rate.
