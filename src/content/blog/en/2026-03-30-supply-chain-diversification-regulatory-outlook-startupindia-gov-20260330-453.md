# The regulatory path for imported inputs in India's manufacturing trade

Diversifying a bill of materials is a regulatory act. The new supplier's country, the Indian quality order on that input, and the question of whether the finished good is for export or for sale in India can change the landed cost more than the invoice price. The operating text is the Foreign Trade Policy 2023 and the DGFT procedures under it.

## The policy that is actually in force

DGFT notified FTP 2023 on 31 March 2023 under the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992. It took effect on 1 April 2023 and stays in force until it is amended. An Importer-Exporter Code, issued electronically on dgft.gov.in, is mandatory for goods trade. Restricted items need an authorization. The policy also points traders to merchandise statistics on commerce.gov.in, the Commerce databank, the DGCI&S portal, and the NIRYAT portal. Those are the official ledgers. A sourcing plan that skips them is guessing at the control.

## Quality orders are the input gate

Quality Control Orders issued under the Bureau of Indian Standards Act can make an otherwise cheap input non-importable for the domestic market unless it meets the order. FTP 2023, at paragraph 2.03(A), creates a narrow exemption. Inputs imported under Advance Authorisation, or by an export-oriented unit or a special economic zone unit, may come in without complying with mandatory QCOs, but only on conditions.

For Advance Authorisation, the exemption is with a pre-import condition, and the inputs have to be used as the authorization says. For EOUs, the DGFT chapter text says there is no domestic-tariff-area clearance of those inputs or of goods made from them, the exemption is for physical exports and not for deemed exports, and the unit gives an undertaking to customs. For SEZ units and developers, the exemption covers permissible imports used inside the zone for authorized operations. Removal into the domestic tariff area brings the quality order back.

Appendix 2Y of the policy lists the ministries and departments whose QCOs are covered by this exemption. Early notifications under that appendix included orders from the Ministry of Steel, DPIIT, and the Ministry of Textiles. The appendix moves. Read the current one on the DGFT portal before you assume a chemical, a steel grade, or a textile input is exempt.

The commercial point is simple. A diversified input that fails a QCO can still feed an export order through the authorization route. The same lot cannot quietly spill into Indian retail. A plant that sells both at home and abroad needs two input paths, or one path that meets the order.

## Preferences are a second license

A free trade agreement lowers duty only if the shipment qualifies and a certificate of origin is issued. DGFT's Appendix 2A lists India's FTAs and preferential trade agreements. Appendix 2B lists agencies allowed to issue preferential certificates of origin. The DGFT portal is where that issuance is meant to happen. The India-UAE CEPA, in force from 1 May 2022, is an agreement where this machinery already matters: Commerce records duty elimination on the UAE side for 97.4 percent of tariff lines. An agreement that is signed and still awaiting ratification does not give you that line.

Rules of origin are the trap in a diversification story. A product assembled in India from inputs that do not meet the origin rule will not take the preference, even if the FTA is in force. The regulatory outlook is therefore not "more FTAs." It is "can this bill of materials survive both the quality order and the origin rule."

## What to review before the next shipment

Confirm the HS line and whether it is free, restricted, or prohibited. Confirm whether a QCO applies and whether you are on the domestic path or the Advance Authorisation path. Confirm the agreement is in force and that a named agency can issue the certificate of origin. DPIIT's FDI rules matter if the diversification is funded by a land-border investor, but they do not clear the customs line. Great Indian Company splits that capital question into its own brief.
