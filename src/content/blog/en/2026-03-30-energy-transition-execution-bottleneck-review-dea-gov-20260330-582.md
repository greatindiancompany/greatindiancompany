# Where India's Energy Transition Gets Stuck

India does not lack a target. Non-fossil capacity is 300.50 GW, FY 2025-26 added 55.29 GW, and solar module nameplate is about 172 GW. The bottlenecks that still show up in official documents are physical and administrative: evacuation, the wind row, storage that the monthly sheet cannot yet see, proof of renewable consumption, and hydrogen production that is still measured in thousands of tonnes.

## Wires are planned because plants arrive first

The Central Electricity Authority's transmission report for 2030 says the gestation period of wind and solar is much shorter than the gestation period of the associated transmission, so the network has to be planned ahead. The report sizes the inter-state system for about 537 GW of renewable capacity: 50,890 circuit kilometres and 4,33,575 MVA, estimated at ₹2,44,200 crore, including offshore wind evacuation for Gujarat and Tamil Nadu. Inter-regional transfer capacity in that report was 1,12,250 MW, with a path to about 1,50,000 MW.

That is the structural bottleneck. A solar park in western Rajasthan or a wind project at Khavda can be ready before the corridor that moves the power to a load centre. CERC's 2025 connectivity amendment, which creates a non-solar-hour access framework, is an attempt to use those corridors when solar is not generating. The transmission-charge amendment of 26 June 2025 extends waiver timelines when the delay is force majeure or a missing line, which is an admission that "the line was not there" is already a category of project delay.

Intra-state wires are a second queue. Budget 2026-27 provides ₹599.99 crore of central assistance for the Green Energy Corridor, toward a cumulative 6,000 circuit kilometres. A state project can be stuck on an intra-state bay even when the inter-state plan looks complete on a national map.

## Wind is the technology row that is not keeping solar's pace

From 1 April to 31 July 2026, solar addition was 14,334 MW and wind addition was 2,042 MW. Installed wind is 58.14 GW. The Electricity Plan wanted about 73 GW by the end of 2026-27 and about 122 GW by 2031-32. The Government says about 28 GW is under implementation. Until that stock is commissioned, "under implementation" is a bottleneck indicator, not a result. Offshore wind is earlier still: a Cabinet viability-gap scheme of ₹7,453 crore, approved on 19 June 2024, covers 1 GW. It is a start, sized in ones of gigawatts, beside a solar fleet of 165 GW.

Resource geography tightens the same constraint. On the 31 July 2026 location sheet, Gujarat has 16,613 MW of wind and Tamil Nadu 12,299 MW. New wind is not free to be sprinkled evenly across states. It lands where the resource and the corridor coincide.

## Storage is required on paper and thin in the public stock

The Electricity Plan calls for 16.13 GW of storage by 2026-27, of which pumped storage is 7.45 GW and batteries are 8.68 GW, rising by 2031-32 to 73.93 GW, of which batteries are 47.24 GW. MNRE already counts 7,425.6 MW of pumped storage inside large hydro, so the pumped-storage piece of the 2026-27 marker is essentially met. The battery piece is not a line in the physical-progress table. The 10 GWh of advanced-chemistry-cell incentive reserved for grid-scale storage had, as of the 11 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply, reached the stage of invited bids, while 40 GWh of the 50 GWh programme had been awarded, largely for the wider cell industry.

Evening peaks and the 29.2 percent non-fossil share of generation in FY 2025-26 are what make this a bottleneck rather than a technology preference. Capacity can cross 50 percent while energy and the peak hour still depend on coal. Storage is the equipment that would change that gap. It is also the equipment the monthly achievement sheet does not yet report as a national total.

## Proof of consumption is an administrative bottleneck

The renewable consumption obligation for 2026-27 is 35.95 percent, with a distributed component that needs project-level data. The guidelines allow a 4 kWh per kW per day assumption when generation data are missing. The Ministry of Power had to extend FY 2024-25 compliance filing to 31 May 2026. A rule that needs an extension in its first cycle is telling you the bottleneck is measurement, not the ambition of the percentage.

On the household side the bottleneck is the last mile of the distribution company. PM Surya Ghar pays central assistance after installation and utility inspection. The 16 July 2026 Parliament annexure showed 76 lakh applications and 39 lakh installations. The scheme portal has since absorbed the net-metering agreement so that a separate agreement is not required, which removes one step. Inspection, meter, and subsidy release remain the steps that decide whether an application becomes a system.

## Hydrogen is a scale bottleneck, not an announcement bottleneck

The Mission, the SIGHT incentive, the pilots, and a hydrogen train exist. Commissioned production of about 8,000 tonnes a year, as of February 2026, against an aim of at least 5 million tonnes by 2030, is the constraint. Electrolyser capacity, the renewable electricity dedicated to it, and an industrial offtaker have to move together. A pilot fleet of 70 vehicles does not relax that constraint. It shows a use-case while the production system is still small.

## A practical order

For a project with a near-term commissioning date, ask about the bay and the corridor before asking about the 2030 target. For a system planner, ask why wind additions are a fraction of solar additions. For a distribution company, ask whether renewable-consumption data would survive an audit. Those three questions are more precise than a general worry that "execution is hard."
