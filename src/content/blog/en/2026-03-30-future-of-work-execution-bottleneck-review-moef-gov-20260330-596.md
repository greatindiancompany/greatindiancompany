# Where India's future-of-work system still stalls in practice

The architecture for a more formal Indian labour market is no longer missing. The four codes are effective. e-Shram exists. Two states have written platform-worker laws. The stalls are now inside that architecture. Each one below is a place where a public document separates a legal object from a working system.

## A contribution that cannot start itself

Section 114(4) of the Code on Social Security fixes a band: a notified rate of 1 to 2 percent of an aggregator's annual turnover, excluding central taxes, levies, and cess, and not more than 5 percent of what is paid to gig and platform workers. Section 114(5) says the Centre notifies the date on which that contribution commences. Until both the rate and the date exist, there is nothing for an accounts payable team to remit and nothing for a scheme account to receive from aggregators. Calling the code "implemented" does not close this gap. The section was drafted so that it would stay open.

Even after the date is notified, section 114(3) allows a scheme to be funded entirely by government, entirely by aggregators, partly by beneficiaries, or from corporate social responsibility money. Execution can stall again between money in and a benefit defined. Life cover, accident insurance, health, old age, and creche are the purposes the section names. They are not automatically the products a worker can claim in a given year.

## A register that is not a benefit

e-Shram's Universal Account Number is issued on self-declaration. The Lok Sabha reply of 20 July 2026 reported 31,78,31,749 registrations as on 14 July 2026, and said fifteen central schemes had been integrated or mapped so that a registered worker can see benefits. The annexure counts people on the register who are enrolled in those schemes. One Nation One Ration Card accounted for 24.46 crore such enrolments and Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY for 15.10 crore, on that date. Those schemes pre-exist e-Shram. Mapping shows linkage. It does not show that registration created the benefit, and the rows cannot be added.

The execution bottleneck is the step after the number. Verification, deduplication across schemes, and a record of what was actually paid sit outside the headline registration total. State microsites, launched on 29 January 2025, are the Ministry's tool for local registration and analytics. A microsite that only speeds up self-declaration enlarges the list. It does not, by itself, audit it.

## A survey that cannot see the category

NITI Aayog's 2022 gig report had to build an estimate — 77 lakh workers in 2020–21 — from characteristics in household data, because the Periodic Labour Force Survey does not publish a gig or platform category. Policymakers who wait for PLFS to "confirm" the gig workforce will wait indefinitely on the current questionnaire. Policymakers who treat the 77 lakh figure, or the 2.35 crore extension to 2029–30, as an administrative roll will chase people the model never identified by name. The workable list is e-Shram's occupation fields plus platform-reported workers, and both of those have the limits in the previous section.

Provident-fund payroll does not fill this hole. EPFO counts members of covered establishments. A platform worker who is not an employee is outside it by design. Using a strong EPFO month, such as the provisional net addition of 21.89 lakh in June 2025, as evidence that gig coverage is working mixes the formal covered sector with the group the code had to define precisely because they were outside that sector.

## Boards, fees, and systems that the Acts postpone

Karnataka's Act deems its provisions in force from 30 May 2025, then establishes the welfare board from a date the state appoints. Aggregators must register within 45 days of commencement. The welfare-fee percentage, inside 1 to 5 percent of payout, was to be notified within six months. Payments are supposed to hit a Payment and Welfare Fee Verification System, with quarterly self-reporting until that system works. Each of those is a separate operational gate. An Act that is in force while the board, the rate, and the verification system are at different stages will look, from outside, like non-compliance, and from inside like a sequence the statute itself staggered.

Rajasthan stalls one step earlier. The Act comes into force on a notified date. The fee rate is another notification. The Central Transaction Information and Management System is how payments and fees are supposed to become visible. Interest at 12 percent a year and recovery as land revenue are enforcement tools for a fee that is due. They do not create the commencement notification.

## The compliance promise that still needs a form

The Ministry's 21 November 2025 note promises a single registration, a single pan-India licence, and a single return, and an inspector-cum-facilitator model. It also says old rules continue in the transition. A firm cannot file "the single return" until the return exists. Until then, the bottleneck is duplicate compliance: preparing for the new frame while still meeting the old one. The note does not give a staffing figure for the facilitator role. Absence of that figure is not proof the role is unfunded. It does mean a reader cannot claim the enforcement culture has already changed.

The stalls, listed as tests rather than as complaints, are these. Is there a section 114(5) date? Is there a scheme with a funding mix and a benefit a named worker can open? Does an e-Shram record survive a check against the scheme database, or only against itself? Can a gig worker be found without borrowing NITI's model? Has the state appointed the board, notified the fee, and turned on the payment system? Can the employer point to the new single return, or only to the press note that promised it? Any one of those answered "not yet" is the current bottleneck. Several can be "not yet" at the same time.
