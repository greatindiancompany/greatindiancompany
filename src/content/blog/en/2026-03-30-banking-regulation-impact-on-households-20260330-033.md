# What protects a household deposit and a loan complaint

Household banking rules in India answer three different questions. How much of a deposit is insured if a bank fails or is frozen. Where a service complaint goes after the bank has had a chance to answer. What a borrower must be shown before a retail or small-firm term loan is signed.

## Deposit insurance is ₹5 lakh, per bank, in one capacity

The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation, a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank, insures deposits at insured banks. A [Ministry of Finance reply published by the Press Information Bureau](https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1849973) records two statutory changes.

From 4 February 2020 the insurance cover rose from ₹1 lakh to ₹5 lakh per depositor. The cover is the amount due in the same right and same capacity at all branches of one insured bank, taken together, if the bank is liquidated or fails. Principal and interest both sit inside that ceiling. The [Corporation's own guide](https://www.dicgc.org.in/guide-to-deposit-insurance) states the same ceiling of ₹5,00,000 for principal and interest, and it also applies the ceiling on the date an all-inclusive direction restricts withdrawals, or when a reconstruction or amalgamation scheme takes effect.

The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (Amendment) Act, 2021, in force from 1 September 2021, added a time-bound interim payment. If the Reserve Bank imposes all-inclusive directions that restrict withdrawals, the Corporation is liable to pay depositors up to the ₹5 lakh cover within 90 days.

Two accounts at the same bank, held in the same capacity, do not create two covers. The same person at two insured banks has two separate ceilings. The official texts do not publish, in these pages, the share of accounts that fall inside the ceiling.

## The ombudsman is a second step, with a compensation cap

The [Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/FAQDisplay.aspx?Id=153) came into force on 1 July 2026. The Reserve Bank's frequently asked questions, dated the same day, describe it as a cost-free, non-adversarial path for deficiency in service by covered regulated entities. It replaces the 2021 scheme. Complaints received before 1 July 2026, appeals from that scheme, and execution of awards already issued stay under the 2021 scheme.

Deficiency in service means a shortcoming in a service the entity must provide, whether or not the customer lost money.

Covered banks are commercial banks, regional rural banks, state co-operative banks, central co-operative banks, scheduled primary urban co-operative banks, and non-scheduled primary urban co-operative banks with deposits of ₹50 crore or more on the previous year's audited balance sheet. Certain non-bank companies, non-bank prepaid payment issuers, and credit information companies are also covered. A complaint against an entity outside that list is not maintainable under this scheme.

The customer complains to the regulated entity first. The ombudsman complaint can follow if there is no reply within 30 days, or any longer time specified by the Reserve Bank, NPCI, or card-network guidelines, or if the reply is unsatisfactory. The ombudsman complaint then has to be filed within 90 days from the later of that deadline or the entity's last communication. Commercial decisions of the entity are not maintainable.

There is no limit on the amount in dispute. Compensation for consequential loss is capped at ₹30 lakh. A further amount, up to ₹3 lakh, may be awarded for the complainant's time, expenses, harassment, or mental anguish. Complaints can be filed on the Reserve Bank's complaint portal, by email to the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre, or on paper. The scheme is jurisdiction-neutral. The toll-free contact centre on 14448 gives information. It does not accept the complaint.

## The loan document a household can actually read

For retail and micro, small, and medium enterprise term loans, the [Key Facts Statement circular of 15 April 2024](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12663&Mode=0) requires a standard statement before the contract. It applies to loans sanctioned on or after 1 October 2024 by commercial banks including small finance banks, local area banks, and regional rural banks, but not payments banks, and by the co-operative banks and non-banking companies the circular addresses.

The statement has a unique proposal number. It is valid for at least three working days if the tenor is seven days or more, and one working day if the tenor is shorter. If the borrower agrees during that window, the lender is bound by the terms on the statement. The annual percentage rate includes interest and all other charges the lender levies, and third-party charges the lender collects, such as insurance or legal fees, are part of that rate and shown separately. A fee that is not on the statement cannot be charged later without the borrower's explicit consent. Credit-card receivables are exempt.
