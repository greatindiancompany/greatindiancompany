# What to check on India's digital rails before late December 2026

Ninety days from 24 September 2026 runs to 23 December 2026. This is a list of documents that already exist on a schedule, plus one legal date that falls inside the window. It is not a list of predicted announcements. If a page has not updated, the right note is "not yet published," not a guess.

## The one dated legal event

Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology notification G.S.R. 843(E) is dated 13 November 2025. It appoints "one year from the date of publication of this gazette" as the commencement of section 6(9) and section 27(1)(d) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. One year from 13 November 2025 falls on 13 November 2026, inside this window.

Before that date, read those two clauses in the Act. This checklist does not summarise them. After the date, check whether MeitY has issued any further notice that changes commencement. The same notification already set a later date, eighteen months from publication, for sections 3 to 5, subsections (1) to (8) and (10) of section 6, sections 7 to 17, and sections 28 to 34, among the other provisions it lists. That later date falls in May 2027. It is not a 90-day item. Do not let a November headline about the Act be mistaken for full commencement.

Also confirm that the Data Protection Board notification, G.S.R. 844(E) of 13 November 2025, is the text you are using. It established the Board from the date of publication, with its head office in the National Capital Region. The Board is not a November 2026 creation. Only the two clauses above are the one-year items.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, published 14 November 2025, and the corrigendum of 16 December 2025, are the rule text to keep beside the Act. If the Board publishes a procedure or a form in this 90-day window, file it against those rules rather than against a news paraphrase.

## Payments: two new rows, compared with August

NPCI's UPI product statistics are the monthly series. The last row in hand for this brief is August 2026: 752 banks live, 24,508.96 million transactions, value of ₹29,82,355.95 crore. September's row is the first missing month as of 24 September. October's row should also appear before late December if the page keeps its usual monthly habit. This checklist does not promise a publication day.

When a row appears, record three comparisons and ignore commentary that the table cannot support:

- Volume against August's 24,508.96 million, and against the previous month.
- Value against August's ₹29,82,355.95 crore. August was already below May (₹29,90,424.21 crore) and July (₹29,87,880.49 crore). A higher volume with a lower value is a possible outcome. It is not a contradiction.
- Banks live, against 752. The count rose every month from April (713) through August. A stall would be new information. An increase would be a continuation.

Do not add app market share unless NPCI's own app-level table for that month is the source. The product-statistics rows used here do not contain it.

## Consent: August, September, and October on Sahamati's dashboard

Sahamati's public dashboard runs a month behind in the print used here. The latest row is July 2026.

| Check | July 2026 figure to beat, match, or explain |
| --- | --- |
| Accounts linked in the month | 11.68 million |
| New consents fulfilled | 44.84 million |
| Data delivered on linked accounts | 343.78 million |
| Cumulative accounts linked | 326.30 million |
| Cumulative consents fulfilled | 538.32 million |

July's consent column was far above June's 19.30 million. The first question for the August row is whether 44.84 million was a one-month spike. Sahamati's definition should stay on the note: fulfilled consents are requests against which data was delivered at once, not every fetch attempt. If someone quotes a larger "consents raised" number from a different Sahamati page, write down which series it is. The homepage and the dashboard have used different presentations.

By late December the dashboard can reasonably be expected to show at least August and September, and possibly October. If it does not, say so. Do not fill the gap with the July run-rate.

## Identity and documents: only if a new cut is published

UIDAI's saturation report used here is as on 30 June 2026: estimated live Aadhaar 1,34,76,52,046, live penetration 94.59 percent, with Nagaland at 62.41 percent. In this 90-day window, check whether UIDAI has replaced that PDF. If it has, record the new "as on" date before comparing any state. If it has not, the June report remains the state table. Do not cite a live dashboard total without writing down the day you saw it.

DigiLocker's latest parliamentary cuts are crowded. The Lok Sabha on 29 July 2026 said over 71.66 crore registered users and more than 936.03 crore documents accessible. The Rajya Sabha statement as on 31 July 2026 said more than 72.43 crore users, about 72.86 crore document-access transactions in the last three years, and 5,437 document types. A new PIB or parliamentary answer in this window should be matched to the metric, not averaged with the older one.

UMANG, as on 31 July 2026 in that Rajya Sabha statement, had more than 11.66 crore users, about 798 crore transactions in the last three years, and 2,575 services. There is no monthly UMANG statistical calendar in the material used here. Check it only if MeitY or PIB publishes a fresh count.

## Health and commerce: stocks, not a monthly tape

The frozen health anchor is 20 July 2026: 94.87 crore ABHA IDs, 5.36 lakh facilities, 10.09 lakh professionals, from the Lok Sabha reply posted by the Press Information Bureau on 24 July. The ministry says state and district detail is on dashboard.abdm.gov.in. If you open the dashboard in this window, note the date in your own record. Do not describe it as the 20 July figure.

ONDC's parliamentary anchor is July 2026: over 500 million cumulative orders, over 500 orders a minute, about 2 lakh or more active retail merchants, about 60 percent of them small sellers. There is no official promise of an October reprint. DigiDukaan was described as operating in Hyderabad and Jaipur. A 90-day check is whether a later government answer adds cities or restates the order total. Absence of a new answer means the July figures still stand, not that the network stopped.

## A short list to tick

- Read DPDP Act sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d) before 13 November 2026, and check the gazette date rather than a secondary "comes into force in November" line.
- Log the September and October UPI rows against August's volume, value, and bank count.
- Log the next Sahamati months against July's 44.84 million fulfilled consents, and keep the column definitions.
- Replace the 30 June Aadhaar saturation table only when UIDAI dates a newer one.
- Keep DigiLocker users, documents stored, and three-year accesses as three fields.
- Open the ABDM dashboard only with a date stamp, beside the 20 July parliamentary totals.
- Do not invent a September ONDC gross merchandise value. The August Lok Sabha answer did not publish one.

That is the whole watch. The value is in noticing which series moved and which publisher stayed silent.
