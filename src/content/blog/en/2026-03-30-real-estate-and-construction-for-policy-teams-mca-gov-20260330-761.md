# What Policy Teams Can See in India's Housing Record

A policy team asked for "the latest on Indian real estate" can see four public systems and one dated parliamentary reply. It cannot see a single national score for approvals, a current list of which states have enacted the Model Tenancy Act, or a construction-industry credit rate unless it opens the statistical statement rather than the press highlight. Saying so is the briefing. Filling the gaps with a round number is how the briefing goes wrong.

## What the mission system shows

[PMAY-U 2.0](https://pmaymis.gov.in/PMAYMIS2_2024/faq.html) is built so a department can see demand, sanctions, money, and walls. Cities prepare a demand assessment and a city-wise plan. Projects go through the state sanctioning committee and then the central committee. The management information system is meant to show physical and financial progress, and beneficiaries can see their own application. Geo-tagging covers five stages. Third-party quality monitors are part of the design. Complaints go to the state, to CPGRAMS, and to the mission portal.

The [ministry scheme page](https://www.mohua.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/pradhan-mantri-awas-yojana-urban-MjNzYjMtQWa) says the dashboard carries state, city, and project views of sanctioned houses, execution and occupation stage, central releases, and utilisation certificates. That dashboard is the progress record. The same page's "about 56 lakh applications," with Uttar Pradesh at 41 percent of the named leaders, is an application snapshot. Brief it as applications. Do not brief it as houses handed over.

What a team can also brief, because the FAQ states it, is the boundary of the mission: five years, 2024 to 2029; one crore additional households as the size stated on the scheme page; central assistance of ₹1.50 lakh, ₹2.25 lakh, or ₹2.50 lakh depending on the state or territory; a twenty-year lookback that bars repeat beneficiaries; no re-entry for houses curtailed after 31 December 2023; female or joint ownership; and a five-year bar on sale or rent of the assisted house. What a team cannot brief from these pages is a verified count of occupied 2.0 houses as of September 2026. That count is the dashboard's, and it is not reproduced here.

## What the regulators show, state by state

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act requires each appropriate government to notify rules, stand up a regulatory authority and an appellate tribunal, and keep a website of registered projects, promoters, and agents. Section 11 puts quarterly project information on that website. A policy team that wants stalled projects, missing certificates, or revoked registrations has to read the state register. The July 2025 central FAQ explains the duties. It does not consolidate the states into one stock.

The FAQ also marks the limits of central knowledge. Interest rates are in state rules. Project fees vary. Title-and-construction insurance under Section 16 starts when the state notifies it. A national line that says "insurance is now compulsory" overclaims the text.

## What the statistical system shows

Use the RBI House Price Index, 117.5 in Q1 2026-27, up 1.1 percent on the quarter and 3.6 percent on the year, when the question is registered residential prices in eighteen cities. Use NHB RESIDEX when the question is assessment prices, under-construction primary stock, or a wider set of 50 cities on the FY 2024-25 base that began with the quarter ended March 2026. Use the 31 August 2026 credit release when the question is whether housing loans and commercial real estate were still growing inside bank credit. Remember the December 2025 definition break before you call the year-on-year rate a clean cycle.

Use the [25 July 2022 PIB reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1844644) only for what it was: a statement that, on information then with the ministry, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, and Assam had revised tenancy laws along the lines of the Model Tenancy Act circulated on 7 June 2021. Commission a fresh state-law check before anyone is told that the model "has been adopted."

How those state differences are designed is in the [state-pattern brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-state-wise-patterns-mca-gov-20260330-321). Great Indian Company publishes this note so a policy reader can see the record that exists, and stop where it ends.
