The useful way to read an RBI policy meeting is to separate the rate the committee sets, the forecast it publishes, and the liquidity and bank-rate figures that show whether that rate is reaching the market. All of the figures below are from the 5 August 2026 resolution, the Governor’s statement of that day, or the August 2026 Bulletin.

## The corridor the MPC set

At its meeting of 3 to 5 August 2026 the Monetary Policy Committee voted unanimously to keep the policy repo rate under the liquidity adjustment facility at 5.25 per cent. The standing deposit facility rate remains 5.00 per cent. The marginal standing facility rate and the Bank Rate remain 5.50 per cent. The stance remains neutral. The next meeting is 5 to 7 October 2026.

The corridor is symmetric. Banks can park surplus at 5.00 per cent and borrow from the marginal standing facility at 5.50 per cent. The Governor’s statement says the Reserve Bank will conduct two-way liquidity operations so that the weighted average call rate lines up with the repo rate.

## The forecast printed in the resolution

| | 2026-27 | Path given in the resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real GDP | 6.7 per cent | Q1 7.0; Q2 6.4; Q3 6.5; Q4 6.8. Q1 of 2027-28 at 7.3. Risks evenly balanced. |
| Headline CPI | 5.0 per cent | Q2 4.7; Q3 5.9; Q4 5.5. Q1 of 2027-28 at 5.3. Risks evenly balanced. |
| Core CPI | 4.3 per cent | Core excluding precious metals expected to be lower in the near term. |

The resolution also records the latest inflation print it had: CPI inflation at 4.4 per cent in June 2026, after 16 months below the 4 per cent target. Core inflation was unchanged at 3.9 per cent in May and June. Core excluding precious metals was 2.3 to 2.5 per cent in that period. Realised headline inflation in the first quarter was 3.9 per cent, 30 basis points under the June projection, according to the Governor’s statement.

The inflation target those projections are judged against is 4 per cent, with a tolerance band of 2 to 6 per cent, in force through March 2031. The third-quarter projection of 5.9 per cent sits inside that band and close to its top.

## Liquidity after the meeting

The August Bulletin, released on 25 August, measures system liquidity as average daily net absorption under the liquidity adjustment facility. That absorption was ₹0.89 lakh crore in June, ₹1.19 lakh crore in July, and ₹3.48 lakh crore in August up to the 21st. The Bulletin says the surplus crossed ₹3.0 lakh crore for the first time in nearly four months.

Over the same August window the Reserve Bank ran sixteen variable-rate reverse repo auctions, from overnight to seven days, absorbing ₹12.68 lakh crore in total. Earlier, during July and August up to the 21st, it had also run 14 variable-rate repo auctions that injected ₹4.13 lakh crore, aimed at transient shortages. Average balances under the standing deposit facility rose to ₹2.12 lakh crore in August up to the 21st, from ₹1.60 lakh crore in July. Marginal standing facility borrowings fell from ₹1,094 crore to ₹857 crore.

The call rate followed the surplus. The weighted average call rate averaged 5.13 per cent in August up to the 21st, down from 5.29 per cent in July. At 5.13 per cent it was below the 5.25 per cent repo rate. The 10-year government security yield was 6.85 per cent on 21 August, against 6.84 per cent on 31 July and 6.75 per cent at end-June.

## How much of the earlier cuts reached loan rates

The Bulletin’s transmission table covers the easing cycle from February 2025 to June 2026, when the repo rate was reduced by 125 basis points.

| | Change, February 2025 to June 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Policy repo rate | −125 basis points |
| External benchmark-based lending rate (32 domestic banks) | −125 basis points |
| One-year MCLR, median | −50 basis points |
| Weighted average lending rate, fresh rupee loans | −80 basis points |
| Weighted average lending rate, outstanding rupee loans | −91 basis points |
| Weighted average domestic term deposit rate, fresh deposits | −63 basis points |
| Weighted average domestic term deposit rate, outstanding deposits | −51 basis points |

The same table shows the pause months. In June 2026 the repo rate was unchanged, the fresh-deposit rate rose 16 basis points, and the fresh-loan weighted average rose 2 basis points. The external benchmark rate was unchanged in both May and June, which is what a formula linked to the repo rate should do when the repo rate is on hold.

For the preceding tightening cycle, May 2022 to January 2025, the same table records a 250 basis point rise in the repo rate, a 250 basis point rise in the external benchmark rate, and a 182 basis point rise in the fresh-loan weighted average.

The April 2026 Monetary Policy Report is the latest half-yearly report published before this October meeting. It is the document that sets out the operating procedure behind these tables. The October meeting is the next date on which the committee will either confirm this corridor or replace it.
