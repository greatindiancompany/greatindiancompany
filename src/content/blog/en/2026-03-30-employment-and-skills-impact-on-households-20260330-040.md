The household story in India's official labour statistics is mostly a story about women, and it changes with the window you open.

In the annual Periodic Labour Force Survey, usual status counts the activity that occupied the major part of the previous 365 days, plus any subsidiary economic activity of 30 days or more. On that definition, for persons aged 15 and above, female labour-force participation rose from 23.3% in 2017–18 to 41.7% in 2023–24, an increase of 18.4 percentage points. Male participation rose from 75.8% to 78.8%, an increase of 3.0 percentage points. All-India participation rose from 49.8% to 60.1%.

## Where the rise sits

Rural female participation in usual status rose from 24.6% in 2017–18 to 47.6% in 2023–24. The last step was steep: 41.5% in July 2022–June 2023 to 47.6% in July 2023–June 2024. Urban female participation rose from 20.4% to 28.0% across the seven annual rounds, and from 25.4% to 28.0% in the last year.

Female employment, the worker population ratio, rose from 22.0% to 40.3% over the seven years, and from 35.9% to 40.3% in the last year. Rural female employment in 2023–24 was 46.5%. Urban female employment was 26.0%.

Unemployment among women did not fall in that last year. Usual-status female unemployment rose from 2.9% to 3.2%, while male unemployment edged from 3.3% to 3.2%. Both finished at 3.2%. That match hides the urban gap: urban female unemployment was 7.1%, rural female unemployment was 2.1%.

A household in a town and a household in a village are not facing the same official unemployment rate, even when the national female rate equals the national male rate.

## The calendar-year note that qualifies the rise

On 9 April 2025 the National Statistics Office published calendar-year indicators for January–December 2024, built by combining the four quarters. These overlap the July–June annual year, and they are not the same cut.

In usual status, all-India female participation was 40.3% in calendar 2024, against 41.3% in calendar 2023. Rural female participation was 45.8%, against 47.3%. All-India female employment was 39.0%, against 40.1%. The July–June annual series through June 2024 still shows a rise. The calendar-year comparison through December 2024 shows a small easing. Both are official. A sentence that uses only one of them is incomplete.

In the weekly-status section of that same calendar note, the office writes that a decline in unpaid helpers in household enterprises seems to have contributed to the drop in the worker population ratio and in participation among rural females. The share of those helpers fell from 19.9% to 18.1% between 2023 and 2024. Part of the measured rural female labour force is work inside the household enterprise without a wage. When that share moves, the participation rate moves with it.

## The week of August 2026

The monthly bulletin uses only the current weekly status. In August 2026, female participation was 34.8% overall, 39.4% in rural areas, and 25.4% in urban areas. A year earlier the three rates were 33.7%, 37.4%, and 26.1%. Female employment was 33.0%, up from 32.0% in August 2025.

The 41.7% usual-status rate from 2023–24 and the 34.8% weekly-status rate from August 2026 are not a seven-point loss of women's work. One counts a year, including subsidiary days. The other counts a reference week, after the survey methodology was modified in January 2025. The definitions are set out in the [key-numbers brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-key-numbers-20260330-020).

## The formal-job slice of the same households

EPFO's provisional payroll for July 2025 recorded about 2.80 lakh new female subscribers, and net female payroll of about 4.42 lakh, 0.17% above July 2024. That is enrolment and rejoining inside covered establishments. It is a thin slice of the participation measured by the survey, which includes self-employment, casual work, and unpaid helpers. The payroll definitions are in the [EPFO brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-impact-on-smes-20260330-030).
