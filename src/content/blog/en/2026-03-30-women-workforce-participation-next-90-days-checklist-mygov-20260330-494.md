# The next official prints to read on women’s work

This checklist is dated 24 September 2026. It says which document to open, which cell to copy, and which comparison is valid. It does not forecast the next rate. The latest full quarter in hand is April–June 2026. The latest month cited here is June 2026. Anything posted after those notes should be read against them, not against a memory of the 2017–18 rate.

## Keep two anchors on the desk

Write these down before the next release arrives.

- Usual status, women, age 15 and above, calendar 2025: labour force participation 40.0 percent, worker population ratio 38.8 percent, unemployment 3.1 percent. Rural participation 45.9 percent, urban 27.7 percent. Source: PLFS Annual Report 2025, released 27 March 2026.
- Current weekly status, women, age 15 and above, April–June 2026: labour force participation 33.2 percent. Figure 3 of that press note: rural 37.2 percent, urban 25.0 percent. Female worker population ratio 31.3 percent. Urban female unemployment 8.7 percent. Women employed in the quarter, on MoHFW population projections: 16.4 crore. Source: quarterly bulletin, 10 August 2026.

The June 2026 monthly bulletin sits inside that quarter: female weekly participation 32.7 percent, rural 36.6 percent, urban 24.8 percent. A new monthly bulletin replaces June. It does not replace the annual usual-status rate.

## When the next monthly bulletin is posted

MoSPI has been releasing a monthly PLFS bulletin on the weekly status for rural and urban India since the 2025 redesign. For the new month, copy three female cells only: all-India, rural, and urban labour force participation, age 15 and above. Set them beside June’s 32.7, 36.6 and 24.8, and beside the quarter’s 33.2, 37.2 and 25.0.

A movement of a few tenths in one month is the grain of this series. The April–June quarter itself was 1.5 percentage points below January–March on the all-India female rate, and that whole move was the rural cell (39.2 to 37.2). One soft month does not revise the annual usual-status 40.0.

Do not average the new weekly print with 40.0. Do not compare it with the July 2017–June 2018 figure of 23.3 percent. That older figure is real, and it belongs to the previous sample design.

## When the July–September 2026 quarterly bulletin is posted

Copy the same female triplet, plus the female worker population ratio and urban female unemployment. The comparison quarter is April–June 2026, not a quarter from 2024. Rural quarterly estimates begin with April–June 2025. Also copy the share of regular wage employees, and label it all workers unless the bulletin prints a female column. In April–June 2026 that all-worker share was 16.1 percent rural and 49.3 percent urban.

If the bulletin includes selected States, keep the state cell in the weekly status. A state usual-status rank from the annual report is a different column.

## Payroll and the unorganised register, on their own calendars

EPFO payroll is monthly and gendered. The anchored year is 2024–25: 26.9 lakh net female subscribers. July 2025 was about 2.80 lakh new female subscribers and a net female addition of about 4.42 lakh. A new month updates the flow. It does not update the participation rate.

e-Shram, in the five-year note, stood at 31.89 crore registrations, 54.28 percent women, with the age profile as on 20 August 2026. Eligibility still excludes EPFO and ESIC members and income-tax payers. If a later note updates the stock, replace 31.89 crore and keep the exclusion in the sentence.

## Scheme counts that are already stale enough to date

Palna approvals were 11,395 Anganwadi-cum-creches across 34 States and Union Territories as of March 2025, against a 2024–25 target of 17,000. A MoWCD update after March 2025 replaces that pair. Until it does, quote the date. NAPS women’s participation was 22.84 percent in 2024–25, and 24.80 percent of apprentices engaged in 2025–26 up to 31 October 2025. The next MSDE reply replaces those, scheme by scheme.

## A close that stays inside the documents

After each release, one paragraph is enough: the female weekly rate, rural and urban; whether it moved with the quarter or against it; and a reminder that usual status for 2025 remains 40.0 percent on a sample MoSPI says may not match earlier years. That paragraph is the whole of the “what changed” claim the sources support.
