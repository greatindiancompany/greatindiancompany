A state-wise pattern is only as good as the table that publishes it. MeitY has not published a ranking of States by AI-policy quality, by DPDP readiness, or by personal-data breaches. The DPDP Act is a Central Act. Section 10's power to notify a Significant Data Fiduciary sits with the Central Government, and that section is not yet in force. A State cabinet note does not replace it.

## What the Centre has published about places

Two official lists name places, and they are not the same list.

The IndiaAI compute note of 13 March 2026 says shared GPUs are served from data centres including Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Noida, and Jamnagar. That is a list of where empaneled compute was being provided. It is not a list of State AI laws, and it is not a share of national capacity.

The PIB backgrounder of 13 August 2026, writing about data centres more broadly, names Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Delhi NCR, and Gujarat, and says newer investment destinations include Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and West Bengal. The same backgrounder gives installed-capacity megawatts. An earlier PIB note, on 10 February 2026, gives a different megawatt figure and city shares, and says India hosts nearly 20 percent of the world's data while holding 3 percent of data-centre capacity. Those two notes do not agree closely enough to treat either megawatt figure, or the world-data share, as a single audited census. This brief does not pick one. The place names are still useful as the cities the notes chose to mention.

On centres of excellence, the numbers are counts without a named map. The Rajya Sabha reply of 24 July 2026 records 58 AI Centres of Excellence and 27 India Data and AI Labs. The 13 August backgrounder says that as of August 2026, 58 centres had been approved across States and Union Territories, and 22 had been initiated across 13 States and Union Territories. The backgrounder does not name the 13. Until it does, a map that colours 13 States is an invention.

## What a State has published for itself

Tamil Nadu's Safe and Ethical Artificial Intelligence Policy, 2020, is a state government document. It tells that government's agencies to evaluate AI systems before public rollout with a six-factor TAM-DEF frame (transparency and audit, accountability and legal issues, misuse protection, digital divide and data deficit, ethics, fairness and equity) and a DEEP-MAX scorecard. It is a procurement and rollout guide for Tamil Nadu public systems. It does not notify anyone as a Significant Data Fiduciary under section 10, and it does not set the DPDP penalty.

Telangana's IT department has published its own AI framework and a later strategy document that describes an AI Advisory Council as being set up to guide research, public policy, talent, and responsible adoption. That is the document's design. This brief is not citing minutes that show the council has sat, and it is not treating an export target written into an earlier state note as a result that has been measured.

Other States have strategy pages. Unless the page is the instrument you are applying, it is not a compliance score, and it is not comparable with Tamil Nadu's procurement test just because both use the word "ethical."

## What still applies in every State

The duties that are already national do not vary by the State AI policy. CERT-In's six-hour incident report and the 180-day log rule apply to the entities named in the 28 April 2022 directions, wherever in India they operate. The Reserve Bank's payment-data storage rule applies to payment-system providers, not to the State where the server room is fashionable. When the DPDP fiduciary duties begin on 13 May 2027, they are Central duties. A State policy can add a procurement condition for that government's own buying. It cannot waive section 8.

The [policy-team note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-for-policy-teams-meity-gov-20260330-750) separates MeitY, CERT-In, the Reserve Bank, SEBI, and the States. The [quarter note](/blog/ai-and-data-governance-what-changed-this-quarter-meity-gov-20260330-110) has the July and August counts with their dates.
