# Where an Indian issue or a listed holding fails

A scenario map is useful when each branch is a failure the rulebook or the last published year has already exhibited. The branches below are that kind. They are not probabilities.

## The file stalls before a price band exists

In 2025-26 SEBI received 244 public-issue applications and carried 61 in. It processed 247. Fifty-eight were still pending at year-end. The median clock was 25 working days if days spent waiting on the intermediary or another regulator are excluded, and 84 working days if they are included. The average including that wait was 86. The scenario in which "SEBI is slow" and the scenario in which the issuer has not answered are different, and the table separates them.

Two pre-filing failures sit in front of that clock. Holdings of the promoter group, selling shareholders, key managers, senior management, qualified institutional buyers, and directors have to be in demat before the draft is filed. Pledged shares that cannot take a conventional lock-in have to be marked non-transferable by the depository for the lock-in period. The 21 March 2026 ICDR amendment and the 8 April 2026 circular exist because a pledge used to break the lock-in. If the articles, the lender intimation, or the offer-document line is missing, the file is not "in the market." A category 2 merchant banker cannot manage a main-board issue at all. That is an eligibility stop, not a market stop.

## The deal lists, and the rupee was an exit

The decade review in Chapter 3 finds 63 per cent of IPO proceeds paid to selling shareholders. In 2025-26, 19 pure offers for sale still raised ₹50,849 crore, and mixed deals carried another ₹57,735 crore of sales beside ₹58,166 crore of fresh capital. The risk for a long-only buyer is paying a primary price for paper that does not enter the company. The risk for the issuer's employees and creditors is the opposite case: a fresh issue whose objects are debt repayment and general corporate purposes, so the plant they were told about is not the use of proceeds. The objects section is the only document that sorts these. The national total does not.

A related supply risk is the six-tier path to 25 per cent minimum public shareholding. A large firm may list a smaller slice and sell more later. Early holders wear the later supply. The regulation sets the timetable. The scenario is simply that the timetable is honoured.

## The listed company misses LODR, or the heir cannot transfer

The exchange procedure for LODR breaches is not theoretical. In 2025-26 the exchange-wise table shows promoter demat accounts frozen for 486 companies at BSE, 99 at NSE, and 41 at MSEI, with provisional fines and a note that the March quarter had not yet been billed. Suspensions under the procedure were 22, 8, and 1. A promoter who treats financial-result dates as soft dates is in this table's population.

Transmission is the household version of the same operational risk. The 23 July 2026 circular cuts documentation for uncontested deaths and refuses the shortcut where claims compete. A nominee who cannot produce a verifiable death certificate, or an estate that is disputed, does not get Quick Transmission Processing. The holding stays stuck. That is a designed outcome.

## Liquidity and the foreign bid can gap

Chapter 5's March 2026 stress test asks how many days the largest mid-cap and small-cap schemes would need to sell 50 per cent of the portfolio. The top 10 mid-cap schemes averaged 17 days. The top 10 small-cap schemes averaged 38 days. The top five were 23 days and 51 days. An IPO that lists into the small-cap universe, or a fund that bought it, inherits that liquidity. The test is a simulation SEBI requires. It is not a prediction that those days will be needed.

The price scenario that did occur is in Chapters 1 and 4. Nifty 50 fell 15.2 per cent from the 2 January 2026 close of 26,328.6 and finished the financial year down 5.1 per cent, or 13.9 per cent in dollars. Foreign portfolio investors sold a net USD 19.7 billion of equities, mostly in the secondary market. Domestic institutions bought a net ₹8.5 lakh crore. The scenario to keep is a repeat of that split: the primary book can still clear, because domestic funds and SIPs are large, while the aftermarket price is set by foreign selling the fund cannot fully replace. SIP support itself churns. Of the movement in accounts, 7.2 crore were opened and 6.8 crore stopped or matured.

Debt is a separate ceiling. If a foreign investor's India view is expressed in bonds, [RBI circular RBI/2026-27/07 of 6 April 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=13366) is the binding limit, at 6, 2, and 15 per cent of the relevant stocks, with absolute caps that step up on 1 October 2026. Hitting that cap does not cancel an equity IPO. It does remove one debt bid. One debt-security default sat inside mutual fund schemes in 2025-26, and the chapter says defaults and restructurings were 0.5 per cent of debt-scheme assets, up from 0.002 per cent. That is a credit scenario, small in the year's count and no longer negligible in the ratio.

Use the branch that matches the question. A draft that will not move is a process scenario. A pop on listing day that fades is often an offer-for-sale and aftermarket scenario. A promoter freeze is a LODR scenario. They do not share a remedy.
