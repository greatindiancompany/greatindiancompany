# Where Public Money Sits in India's Energy Transition

An investment implication starts with a distinction the headlines skip. A scheme outlay is a multi-year ceiling. A budget estimate is what the Union Budget provides this year. Internal and extra-budgetary resources are what a public enterprise plans to raise and spend on its own balance sheet. Treating all three as "government spending this year" will mis-size every project.

The figures below are from Demand No. 71 of the Union Budget 2026-27, the scheme notifications, and the Central Electricity Authority's National Electricity Plan. They are provisions and estimates, not money already disbursed in FY 2026-27.

## This year's MNRE budget is a rooftop-and-farm budget

Net of recoveries, the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy's 2026-27 allocation is ₹32,914.67 crore, of which ₹32,911.14 crore is revenue and ₹3.53 crore is capital. The gross figure before ₹11,700 crore of recoveries is ₹44,614.67 crore. Recoveries are largely amounts met from the Sovereign Green Fund, so the net number is the one to use when asking what the Budget adds.

Inside the net total, the shape is clear:

- PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, ₹22,000 crore
- PM-KUSUM, ₹5,000 crore
- Solar power (grid), ₹1,775 crore
- Interest and bond-issuing expenses, ₹1,764.35 crore, including repayment of extra-budgetary resources
- Green Energy Corridor, ₹599.99 crore
- National Green Hydrogen Mission, ₹600 crore net
- Wind power (grid), ₹500 crore
- Bio-energy programmes together, ₹275 crore
- Human resources development and training, ₹40 crore

PM Surya Ghar alone is about two-thirds of the Ministry's net budget. KUSUM is the next slab. Grid solar, wind, hydrogen, and bio-energy are small lines beside them. A founder looking for a large central subsidy for a new utility-scale solar plant will not find it on this page. A vendor building residential rooftop systems, or a firm in the solar-pump chain, is looking at the lines that actually dominate the estimate.

For scale, MNRE's own FY 2025-26 review said expenditure of ₹24,176.68 crore was incurred against a budget estimate of ₹26,549.38 crore and a revised estimate of ₹25,301.22 crore. The Ministry has been spending most of what Parliament provides. The 2026-27 estimate is larger, and most of the increase is PM Surya Ghar, whose revised estimate for 2025-26 was ₹17,000 crore and whose budget estimate for 2026-27 is ₹22,000 crore.

## Envelopes that dwarf this year's line

The hydrogen line is the cleanest example of envelope versus annual provision. The National Green Hydrogen Mission's initial outlay is ₹19,744 crore: ₹17,490 crore for the SIGHT incentive programme, ₹1,466 crore for pilots, ₹400 crore for research, and ₹388 crore for other components. Budget 2026-27 provides ₹600 crore net. One year of the Budget is about 3 percent of the Mission envelope. That can be normal for a back-loaded incentive, which pays on production. It is a poor reason to model ₹19,744 crore as cash arriving in FY 2026-27.

The solar production-linked incentive is similar. The programme outlay is ₹24,000 crore, including ₹4,500 crore in tranche I and ₹19,500 crore in tranche II. Incentives run for five years after the factory is commissioned, on modules made and sold. The Cabinet, while approving tranche II, said the scheme would bring direct investment of around ₹94,000 crore. That was an estimate attached to the approval, not a measured investment stock.

The advanced-chemistry-cell programme under the Ministry of Heavy Industries is ₹18,100 crore for 50 GWh. A Lok Sabha reply on 11 August 2026 said 40 GWh had been awarded to four firms and the remaining 10 GWh was earmarked for grid-scale stationary storage, for which bids had been invited. Again, the outlay is the incentive pool, not equity in those factories.

## Public enterprises are a second balance sheet

The same Demand No. 71 lists investment plans financed by internal and extra-budgetary resources, not by the Ministry's net ₹32,915 crore:

- Indian Renewable Energy Development Agency: ₹40,064.74 crore
- Solar Energy Corporation of India: ₹2,824.86 crore

IREDA's plan is larger than the Ministry's entire net budget. It is a financing book. It tells you the public renewable lender expects to intermediate a large volume of project and manufacturing credit. It does not tell you the interest rate, the loss experience, or which projects will be sanctioned.

SECI's number is the corporation's own investment plan. SECI is also the implementing agency for solar-module PLI tranche II, which is a different role from investing ₹2,825 crore of its own resources.

## The generation plan's fund estimate is another species of number

The National Electricity Plan (Volume I, Generation), notified in May 2023, estimates ₹14,54,188 crore for generation-capacity addition in 2022-27 and ₹19,06,406 crore for 2027-32. PIB summarised the two periods as about ₹33.60 lakh crore. The 2027-32 figure excludes advance spending on plants that would be commissioned after 31 March 2032. This is a system-wide capital estimate covering coal, gas, nuclear, hydro, solar, wind, biomass, pumped storage, and batteries. It is not an MNRE budget, and it is not a commitment that the money has been raised.

A transmission estimate sits beside it. The Central Electricity Authority's plan for integrating renewable capacity on the inter-state system by 2030 put the additional line length and substation capacity at 50,890 circuit kilometres and 4,33,575 MVA, at an estimated cost of ₹2,44,200 crore. The Green Energy Corridor line in this year's Budget, ₹599.99 crore, is central financial assistance for intra-state corridors, described in the Budget as support toward a cumulative 6,000 circuit kilometres. Inter-state and intra-state money should not be added as if they were one cheque.

## What an investor can conclude

Public money in FY 2026-27 is concentrated on households and farms, on a lender's balance sheet, and on multi-year incentive pools that pay after factories produce. The large gigawatt targets live in the Electricity Plan and in mission documents. Anyone underwriting a project still has to find the offtaker, the tariff, and the transmission date. Those are not in Demand No. 71.
