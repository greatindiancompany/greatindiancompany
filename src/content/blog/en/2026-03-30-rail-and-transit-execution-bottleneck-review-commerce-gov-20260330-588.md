# Why Sanctioned Rail Kilometres and Open Terminals Are Not the Same as Capacity

The binding constraints on Indian rail are already numbered in ministry replies. They sit between a sanction and a train that can run.

## The open book is mostly doubling and new line

As on 1 April 2026 the sanctioned construction book was 40,000 km across 514 projects. Commissioned length to March 2026 was 12,583 km. Inside that:

- New lines: 3,361 km commissioned of 16,634 km
- Doubling: 6,177 km of 19,379 km
- Gauge conversion: 3,045 km of 3,987 km

Gauge conversion is no longer the bottleneck the other two are. Doubling is the largest remaining length, which matters because a single-line section is where freight and coaching trains take paths from each other. Expenditure to March 2026 was ₹3,05,253 crore against a sanctioned cost of about ₹8.31 lakh crore. Commissioned length was 12,583 km against 40,000 km sanctioned. The reply prints both pairs and does not convert them into a unit cost.

The commissioning pace the ministry does publish — 8.32 km a day over 2014-26, against 4.2 km a day in 2009-14 — explains how the network grew. It does not clear the gap between 40,000 km sanctioned and 12,583 km commissioned.

## Corridors moved the bottleneck off the trunk

The Eastern and Western dedicated freight corridors are commissioned: 1,337 km from Ludhiana to Sonnagar, and 1,506 km from Dadri to Jawaharlal Nehru Port. About 443 trains a day run on them. The 7 August 2026 reply says diverting freight onto the corridors created paths on the conventional network. The constraint the ministry then points to is the terminal and the first and last mile. That is why the Gati Shakti terminal policy and the Joint Parcel Product–Rapid Cargo Service exist. CONCOR, a railways public enterprise, is using the parcel scheme for door-to-door service on Delhi–Kolkata and Mumbai–Kolkata, and a pilot at Sonik goods shed.

Terminal execution has its own gap. By 7 August 2026, 142 terminals were commissioned, with estimated capacity of 224 million tonnes a year, and 146 million tonnes were handled in 2025-26. In-principle approvals covered 310 further locations. The policy clock is 24 months from approval to construction. An approval stock of that size is a pipeline of possible capacity. It is not 310 open gates.

## New sleeper trains wait on a path

The 11 March 2026 Lok Sabha reply listed the conditions for more Vande Bharat sleeper services: capacity of the section, an available path, rolling stock, matching infrastructure, maintenance of track and other assets, and occupancy. Two sleeper services were running by 23 July 2026. The chair-car fleet, at 162 services, shows the product can be repeated when those conditions are met. The sleeper count shows they are not met on most long-distance routes yet.

Dankuni to Surat does not relieve any of this until the detailed project report becomes a sanctioned, then commissioned, corridor. The August reply stops at the report being taken up.

The rupee side of the same book is in the [capex note](/blog/rail-and-transit-capex-cycle-watch-commerce-gov-20260330-348). Where a private terminal can still be the answer is in the [opportunity landscape](/blog/rail-and-transit-opportunity-landscape-commerce-gov-20260330-628).

Great Indian Company treats unopened kilometres and unbuilt terminals as the execution problem, because that is what the tables show.
