# August 2026 retail inflation as a demand signal

The price signal for Indian retail is the Consumer Price Index, not a company’s same-store sales. For August 2026, on the series with base 2024=100, the National Statistics Office put year-on-year inflation at 4.82% (provisional). Rural inflation was 5.23%. Urban inflation was 4.31%.

Food inflation, on the Consumer Food Price Index, was 5.95%. Rural food inflation was 6.13% and urban food inflation was 5.64%. The combined CPI index stood at 108.74. July’s final combined reading was 4.45%, so the provisional August print is higher than the previous month’s final print. August itself can still be revised. The September 2026 release is scheduled for 12 October 2026, or the next working day.

## Price pressure and spending pressure are different sentences

A higher CPI means the basket costs more than it did a year earlier. It does not, by itself, say households bought more units. Real PFCE, from the national accounts, is the volume-side companion. The August price print and the April–June consumption print also cover different months.

The Reserve Bank’s resolution of 5 August 2026, written before this August CPI, said headline inflation had risen to 4.4% in June after sixteen months below target, led by food and fuel. Core inflation, CPI excluding food and fuel, was unchanged at 3.9% in May and June. Excluding precious metals, the resolution put core inflation at 2.3–2.5% in that period, and said that pattern suggested demand pressures remained contained. The MPC’s full-year CPI projection for 2026-27 was 5.0%, with the high quarter marked as Q3 at 5.9%.

For an operator, August says shelf prices, especially food and especially in rural markets, were rising faster than the urban all-items index. For an investor, the RBI’s own split is the one to keep: food and fuel were doing the lifting in the early-summer narrative, and the Bank had not described a broad demand-pull inflation.
