# Jobs and Skills in India's Climate Adaptation Work

No environment ministry, disaster authority, Press Information Bureau, or India Meteorological Department page cited here counts "adaptation jobs" in India. A headcount in a pitch deck comes from somewhere else. What these pages do specify is work that public programmes assume someone can do.

## Heat needs more than a forecast desk

IMD's declaration rule is a skill: apply the temperature floors for plains, coast, and hills, then the departure or absolute test, at two stations, for two days. The 6 April 2021 IMD annexure on PIB adds a colour step from a normal day to an alert on which people are told to act. The National Disaster Management Authority's October 2019 guidelines exist so states and cities can write heat action plans around that warning. NDMA's heat page lists dehydration, heat cramps, heat exhaustion, and heat stroke, which pulls primary health workers into the same week as the meteorologists. Its heat resources include a cool-roof guide for house owners. That is a building skill, not a software category.

## Water and farms are field skills

The National Water Mission, in the ministry's FAQ of 1 December 2021, includes a goal of raising water-use efficiency by 20 percent, plus a public water database and attention to over-exploited areas. The National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture is described there as operational from 2014-15, covering soil and moisture, soil health, and on-farm water management. The same FAQ says the Soil Health Card scheme, in implementation since February 2015, tells a farmer the nutrient status of a field. Those sentences assume irrigation managers, soil-testing staff, and local water governance. They do not promise an income gain.

## Livelihood aims are not filled posts

The Green India Mission objectives in that FAQ include higher forest-based livelihood income for about 3 million households living in and around forests, alongside a greening target. Read the household figure as a mission objective printed in the December 2021 FAQ, not as jobs delivered. On the coast, a PIB note of 3 February 2022 names livelihood work rather than a vacancy list: integrated farming in the Kaipad wetlands of north Kerala, habitat and livelihood work in the Gulf of Mannar, and dairy practice for heat and cyclone stress in Andhra Pradesh.

## When the work shows up

The calendar is seasonal. Heat-plan roles matter before March, because IMD's FAQ places heat waves mainly from March to June. Flood and cyclone roles follow IMD warnings and the NDMA guidelines, not a standing national vacancy series.
