# From the 2017–18 rate to the 2025 reading

The multi-year story of women’s labour force participation in India is a documented rise on one survey design, then a level MoSPI describes as stable on the next design. Joining them into one uninterrupted climb ignores the warning the Ministry printed with the 2025 report.

## The July–June series, through 2023–24

The annual report for July 2023–June 2024, released on 23 September 2024, is the seventh report on the design that began in 2017. For women aged 15 and above, usual status (principal plus subsidiary), the all-India labour force participation rate in that report’s table is:

- 2017–18: 23.3 percent
- 2018–19: 24.5 percent
- 2019–20: 30.0 percent
- 2020–21: 32.5 percent
- 2021–22: 32.8 percent
- 2022–23: 37.0 percent
- 2023–24: 41.7 percent

Rural women moved from 24.6 percent in 2017–18 to 47.6 percent in 2023–24. Urban women moved from 20.4 percent to 28.0 percent. The rise is mostly rural. Men’s all-India rate over the same years moved from 75.8 percent to 78.8 percent. The gap narrowed because women’s rate rose, not because men’s rate fell.

The worker population ratio for women rose from 22.0 percent in 2017–18 to 40.3 percent in 2023–24. Unemployment for women on usual status was 5.6 percent in 2017–18 and 3.2 percent in 2023–24. In 2022–23 it had been 2.9 percent, so the last step of that series was a small rise in the unemployment rate alongside a larger rise in participation. More women were in the labour force, and the share of that force without work edged up in the final year of the old design.

On the weekly status in 2023–24, female participation was 35.6 percent against the usual-status 41.7 percent. The long reference year was already higher than the seven-day rate, by the subsidiary and seasonal work the usual status includes.

## Calendar 2025, after the redesign

From January 2025 the sample changed and the survey year became January–December. The 2025 annual report gives female usual-status participation of 40.0 percent, male 79.1 percent, and all persons 59.3 percent. Rural women were at 45.9 percent and urban women at 27.7 percent. The female worker population ratio was 38.8 percent. Female unemployment was 3.1 percent.

The report says participation remained stable compared with 2024. In the same report’s calendar comparison, built with the warning that 2025 may not be strictly comparable with results through December 2024, the 2024 all-India female usual-status rate is 40.3 percent. The press note’s language is stability, not a reversal of the 2017–18 to 2023–24 rise, and not a claim that 40.0 continues that rise point for point.

The weekly rate for women in calendar 2025 was 34.3 percent. The April–June 2026 quarter, on the weekly status, was 33.2 percent. A mid-term path that uses only usual status will sit near 40 percent on the new design. A path that uses only the weekly status will sit in the mid-30s and will move with the quarter, as the rural cell did between January–March 2026 (39.2 percent) and April–June 2026 (37.2 percent).

## What else moved inside the level

Job type moved more quietly than the headline rate. The 2025 press note says the self-employed share of women workers was 64.2 percent, against 66.5 percent in its 2024 comparison, and the regular-wage share was 18.2 percent, against 16.6 percent. Rural regular-wage employment for women workers was still 9.3 percent. The participation rise of the earlier years landed heavily in rural self-employment. The question for the next several years is whether the regular-wage share keeps edging up, particularly outside cities.

The reason code for being outside the labour force has not become a minor item. In 2025, 44.4 percent of women outside the labour force named child care or personal commitments in home-making. The Time Use Survey for 2024 still shows women participants spending 289 minutes a day on unpaid domestic services, against 88 minutes for men. A participation rate can stay near 40 percent on usual status for a long time if that time account stays in place.

Nominal earnings for women rose between the 2024 and 2025 readings the press note compares: about 7.2 percent in regular wage work, to ₹18,353, and about 8.8 percent in self-employment, to ₹6,374. The level gap between those two lines remains the larger fact, because most women workers are on the self-employment line.

## How to tell the path in one paragraph

From 2017–18 to 2023–24, on a consistent July–June design, women’s usual-status participation rose from 23.3 percent to 41.7 percent, and the rise was rural. From 2025, on a larger sample and a calendar year, MoSPI reports 40.0 percent and calls the rate stable against 2024. Weekly participation is lower, at 34.3 percent for 2025 and 33.2 percent in April–June 2026. Regular wage work is still 18.2 percent of women workers. That is the trajectory the releases support.
