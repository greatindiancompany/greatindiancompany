# Where India’s Social Outcome Gaps Still Sit for Capital

Capital that follows India’s social-sector headlines will crowd the indicators that already moved and miss the ones that barely did. The official series, read together, separates a large new public outlay from a short list of stubborn outcomes. Nothing below is a return, a valuation, or a fund allocation. It is the map of where public money and public results currently diverge.

## The large new public cheque is rural wages

The Ministry of Rural Development has put ₹95,692.31 crore as the central share for VB-G RAM G in 2026-27, and says that with the estimated state share the programme outlay is expected to exceed ₹1.51 lakh crore. That is wage employment plus four classes of assets: water, core rural infrastructure (including schools and Anganwadis), livelihood infrastructure, and weather protection. An investor underwriting “rural consumption” or “village works” is looking at a statutory 125-day household guarantee and a notified wage floor of ₹300, with the average notified wage at ₹327.4. The investment question is execution after 1 July 2026, not the repealed MGNREGA printout. Early person-days are an output. Households completing long spells of paid work, and assets that stay in use, are the outcomes the Act describes and the early releases do not yet score.

## Nutrition money is meeting a split result

NFHS-6 (2023-24), released on 29 May 2026, is the outcome ledger for the Poshan years. Stunting in children under five fell from 35.5% to 29.3%. Severe wasting fell from 7.7% to 5.2%. Wasting moved from 19.3% to 19.0%. Underweight moved from 32.1% to 31.8%. Children aged 6-23 months on an adequate diet rose from 11.0% to 15.3%.

A stunting decline of that size is the result a long-horizon nutrition investor wanted to see. A wasting rate still near one in five, an underweight rate still near one in three, and an adequate-diet rate still near one in six say the supplementary-nutrition and counselling system has not finished the job. Poshan Tracker scale — almost 8.94 crore beneficiaries as of 31 July 2026 — shows reach. Reach is the input. The fact-sheet rates are the outcome. Capital that prices only the stunting headline is pricing the best line in the block.

The India fact sheet also shows exclusive breastfeeding under six months at 55.8%, against 63.7% in NFHS-5, while the share currently breastfeeding stayed high at 95.6%. A maternal-nutrition or infant-formula thesis that ignores that split will misread the ministry’s own table. The 29 May press note highlighted current breastfeeding, not the exclusive-breastfeeding decline.

## Health financing expanded faster than the hardest clinical residuals

Household coverage by a health insurance or financing scheme rose from 41.0% to 60.2%. Institutional births are 90.6%. Full vaccination on cards for children aged 12-23 months is 87.1%, up from 83.8%. Those are coverage outcomes. The same release does not publish a new national anaemia prevalence; the May 2026 fact-sheet compendium’s nutrition block reports anthropometry and feeding, and the press note does not give an anaemia rate. Anyone marking the anaemia trade as “updated by NFHS-6” is ahead of the document.

Caesarean births at 27.2%, and 54.1% inside private facilities, are a cost and quality signal for hospital investors. They are not, by themselves, a health gain.

## School capital is meeting a secondary-retention gap

UDISE+ 2025-26 reports pupil-teacher ratios already inside the National Education Policy’s 30:1 reference (10, 12, 17, and 21 by stage) and a teacher count of 1,02,73,020. The outcome that is still open is staying on. Secondary retention is 51.9%. Secondary dropout is 7.0%. Single-teacher schools still number 1,00,843. Computer access is 69.9%. Money aimed at “more teachers in the average school” is aimed at a ratio the ministry already reports as improved. Money aimed at the secondary cliff, the remaining single-teacher schools, and the schools without a computer is aimed at the residual.

## The pension rate is the deliberate non-increase

On 21 July 2026 the rural development ministry said the central NSAP pension rate was last revised in 2012 and that no revision is under consideration. States top up between ₹50 and ₹5,700, and in several states the amount received averages about ₹1,100. More than 3 crore people are on the central BPL pension rolls and about 5.9 crore more are on state schemes. A credit or insurance product built on “pension income” has to use the state schedule. The Centre has published that it is not raising its own rate.
