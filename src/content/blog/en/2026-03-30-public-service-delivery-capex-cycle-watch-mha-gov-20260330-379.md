# What to watch in the service-delivery build-out

A capex cycle for citizen services in India is a set of appropriations, not a project finance timeline. The [6 August 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635) of a Lok Sabha reply says the Digital India programme is an umbrella. Central ministries, departments, and states each carry their own budgetary provisions. The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology implements eight Central Sector sub-schemes under the programme, and the reply says no state-wise allocation is made for those sub-schemes. Anyone waiting for one national "service delivery capex" printout will wait through several budget documents.

## Three stocks that are not the same project

Connectivity is one stock. The same reply says about 2.21 lakh gram panchayats had been made service-ready under BharatNet as of June 2026. A service portal that assumes a gram panchayat can complete a form at home is assuming this network, or a mobile network, is actually usable there. BharatNet is a communications build. It is not the certificate workflow.

Assisted access is a second stock. As of May 2026 the reply records 5.01 lakh functional Common Service Centres, 3.91 lakh of them at gram panchayat level, delivering over 800 services through village-level entrepreneurs. A PIB note of 27 June 2026, [The Digital India](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jun/doc2026627904001.pdf), describes the public-internet pillar as more than 6.5 lakh Common Service Centres and 1.6 lakh post offices. The August parliamentary reply and the June note do not use the same centre count. Watch the dated sentence. Do not smooth them into a growth rate.

Platforms are a third stock. DigiLocker and UMANG are operating systems with published scale in the August reply: over 71.66 crore DigiLocker users and 2,575 UMANG services for individuals. Their build-out shows up as issuer onboarding, new departmental services, and state integrations, not as a fresh greenfield plant. The reply's Tamil Nadu line, 17 state services on UMANG beside the separate e-Sevai portal, is a better capex tell than a national user total. Integration work is unfinished when the state portal and the national app list different services.

## What a budget reader can actually track

Track MeitY's eight Central Sector sub-schemes as MeitY spend, and refuse to allocate that spend across states using a population key the reply does not provide. Track state information-technology and district e-governance budgets for e-District workflows, because the 2022 Lok Sabha description of e-District already said states implement the project. Track BharatNet service-ready gram panchayats when the question is whether a rural counter can reach the portal. Track Common Service Centre functionality when the question is assisted delivery.

DARPG's NeSDA cycle is a quality watch, not a capex release. The [28 May 2026 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266244) says the 2025 portal collects data from states, Union Territories, and central ministries, and that DARPG will hold review meetings during the cycle. A state that reports a service portal with end-to-end delivery is describing an asset that already functions. A state that reports an information page is describing a gap. Neither report is a tender value.

## What this watchlist will not do

It will not convert Rs 52 lakh crore of Direct Benefit Transfer, the cumulative scheme figure in the August reply, into government capital expenditure. That figure is benefits credited to people. It will not convert DigiLocker document counts into a storage-capex series. The platform's own description says issued documents are fetched from issuers, which is a reference to the issuer's repository, not a promise that every file is a new government data-centre build.
