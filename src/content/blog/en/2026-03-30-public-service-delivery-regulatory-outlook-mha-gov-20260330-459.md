# Which service-delivery rules bind, and which only score

A citizen service in India sits under at least four different kinds of rule. One gives an electronic document legal standing. One tells a scheme how to pay. One tells a department how to take a complaint. One scores the portal and does not, by itself, create a penalty. Mixing them up is how a compliance memo becomes fiction.

## Issued documents have a rule; uploads do not inherit it

DigiLocker's [paperless-governance note](https://blog.digilocker.gov.in/digilocker-an-initiative-towards-paperless-governance/) says issued documents in the system are deemed at par with original physical documents under Rule 9A of the Information Technology (Preservation and Retention of Information by Intermediaries Providing Digital Locker Facilities) Rules, 2016. The note says those documents are fetched from the issuing agency. The legal claim is about issuer-originated records, not about every file in an account.

Railway circular CC/33/2018, [hosted by DigiLocker](https://img1.digitallocker.gov.in/assets/img/circulars/CC_33_2018.pdf), applies that distinction to a real counter. A passenger who shows Aadhaar or a driving licence from the Issued Documents section may be treated as having shown identity. A document the user uploaded is not identity for that purpose. Any product copy that says "DigiLocker accepted" without saying "issued, not uploaded" is looser than the circular.

## DBT has a payment framework, and Aadhaar has a statute beside it

The [DBT Mission about page](https://dbtbharat.gov.in/static-page-content/spagecont?id=1) points to the electronic payment framework in office memoranda of 13 February 2015 and 19 February 2015. It applies to Central Sector and Centrally Sponsored Schemes and to schemes that transfer cash to an individual beneficiary. The page says Aadhaar is not mandatory in DBT schemes, and that it is preferred because it helps reach the intended person.

UIDAI's [DBT FAQ](https://uidai.gov.in/en/308-faqs/direct-benefit-transfer-dbt.html) states the neighbouring rule. Under section 7 of the Aadhaar Act, 2016, the Central or a State Government may require Aadhaar for a benefit funded from the Consolidated Fund of India or of the state. The FAQ also says cash benefits go to the one account the person chose when giving consent to seed Aadhaar, and that the bank seeds that account in the NPCI mapper. "Not mandatory" on the Mission page and "may be required for this scheme" under section 7 can both be true. The scheme guideline is the document that decides.

The Aadhaar Payments Bridge operating procedure on the DBT site tells banks that a wrong mapping produces a wrong credit, and that the bank is liable to make it good to the department that sent the money. That is an operational duty, not a citizen-charter slogan.

## Grievance redress is a process with an appeal

[CPGRAMS](https://pgportal.gov.in/) is the 24x7 portal for a grievance about service delivery, linked to central ministries and to states, and also offered inside UMANG. The portal says the government does not charge a filing fee. After closure, a complainant who rates the resolution "Poor" can file an appeal, and the appeal is tracked with the same registration number. DARPG coordinates and reviews. The department that owns the service redresses. A NeSDA score does not close a grievance.

## NeSDA scores; it does not legislate

The [28 May 2026 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2266244) presents NeSDA 2025 as a biennial assessment. The portal, launched with nodal officers on 25 May 2026, collects data. Parameters include information security and privacy, end service delivery, status tracking, and accessibility. The release asks nodal officers to review the assessment guidelines and to submit data during the cycle. It does not create a new offence for a missing service. The proposed list is 59 mandatory services for every state and Union Territory and 43 for central ministries. "Mandatory" here is the assessment list DARPG proposes to score. Read the guidelines the release tells nodal officers to review before treating the list as a statute.
