# Registration is complete. The rest of the chain is not.

The tempting sentence about India's agri chain in 2026 is that the big targets have been hit. Ten thousand farmer producer organisations are registered. More than 1,600 mandis are on a national screen. The infrastructure fund has mobilised well over a lakh crore of investment. Each of those sentences is backed by a reply. Each of them stops at a threshold that is not the same as a working chain.

## The FPO register is ahead of the FPO balance sheet

As on 1 March 2026, 10,000 FPOs had been registered under the central scheme launched in 2020. That is the target the scheme was named for. The money that makes a registered body a buyer and seller of produce is a different stock, and the latest figure for it is earlier. As on 31 December 2025, matching equity grant of Rs 430.77 crore had gone to 6,557 FPOs, and credit-guarantee cover of Rs 662.71 crore had gone to 2,671 FPOs. The scheme's own ceilings are Rs 15 lakh of equity grant per FPO and Rs 2 crore of guaranteed project loan.

The gap is not proof that 3,443 FPOs were refused. The reply does not say how many had applied. It does say the grant and the guarantee had not reached the full register by the turn of 2026. Management assistance, up to Rs 18 lakh over three years, is the other unfinished support, and the February reply does not publish how many FPOs were drawing it.

A second register tells the same story from the market side. e-NAM had 4,724 FPOs onboarded as on 28 February 2026. That is not 10,000, and the two schemes do not share one definition, so the difference is not a backlog statistic. It is evidence that "formed" and "trading on the national mandi platform" are separate administrative facts. The April 2026 note describes an FPO path that avoids the yard: store in a WDRA-accredited warehouse, take an electronic negotiable warehouse receipt, and sell on e-NAM. That path exists as a design. The February stock of 4,724 is the count of organisations actually on the platform.

## The mandi platform is wide, and still a state-by-state build

Integration of 1,656 mandis in 23 states and 4 Union territories is a real network. The March 2026 annexure also shows how partial it is. Assam is on it with 3 mandis. Gujarat is on it with 144, Rajasthan with 173, Uttar Pradesh with 162. Inter-state bidding needs a State Unified Licence, which is a state decision described in the April note, not a switch the Centre throws. Until that licence exists, a farmer's lot is visible only inside the state's own buyers. The Centre's tool for the physical gap at the gate is a grant of up to Rs 75 lakh per mandi for assaying, weighing, and grading. Rajasthan had 134 mandis using AI and machine-learning assaying. That is a bottleneck being worked in one state, and a capability the reply does not claim for the other 1,522 mandis.

## Cold chain completions lag the national stock they are meant to join

As of 30 June 2026, the integrated cold-chain scheme had approved 409 projects and completed 311, leaving 98 ongoing. Completed projects had created 9.236 lakh metric tonnes. Grant sanctioned was Rs 3,089.08 crore and grant released was Rs 2,540.89 crore, so even the approved book is not fully paid. The Department of Agriculture figure cited alongside, 402.87 lakh metric tonnes of cold storage as on 31 January 2026, shows the scale of the stock these projects join. Ninety-eight unfinished projects are an execution queue. They are not the country's cold-chain gap by themselves. The June 2025 AIF mix points at a related queue: 2,454 cold-store projects inside a sanction list dominated by 30,202 custom hiring centres. Debt is flowing. Refrigerated capacity is a minority of the project count on that date.

One eligibility rule keeps a further class of projects out on purpose. Since 28 August 2024, AIF funds primary and secondary processing only when they are a single integrated unit. Standalone secondary processing is ineligible. A mill that does not also do the primary step cannot use this loan. That is a designed bottleneck, not a delay.

## Income support is not the missing pipe

PM-KISAN's continuation to 2030-31, and the Rs 4.47 lakh crore already transferred through 23 instalments, shows the state can execute a transfer at national scale. The chain's slower stocks are the ones that require a bank, a mandi board, a warehouse accreditation, or a grant release. The bottleneck review is the comparison between those stocks and the registers that are already full.
