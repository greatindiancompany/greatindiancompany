## A league table is not a map

State logistics performance and the physical trade corridor are related, and they are published by different arms of government. DPIIT's LEADS report sorts states by how users and indicators judge logistics. Railways, the road ministry, and the ports ministry publish where track, highways, and port links are. A state can rank well because services and rules are easier, while a neighbouring state sits on the mineral railway. Read both.

## LEADS 2024, by the groups DPIIT released

On 3 January 2025 the Commerce and Industry Minister released LEADS 2024, described as the sixth edition. Performance is grouped, not stacked into a single all-India rank list in that release.

Coastal achievers were Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Odisha, and Tamil Nadu. Coastal fast movers were Andhra Pradesh and Goa. Coastal aspirers were Kerala and West Bengal. Landlocked achievers were Haryana, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, and Uttarakhand. Landlocked fast movers were Bihar, Himachal Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, and Rajasthan. Landlocked aspirers were Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand. In the North-East, Assam and Arunachal Pradesh were achievers, and Manipur was the aspirer. Among Union Territories, Chandigarh and Delhi were achievers.

The pillars in that edition were logistics infrastructure, logistics services, the operating and regulatory environment, and sustainable logistics. The secretary of DPIIT said objective indicators had increased, including access to terminals and speed on key road corridors. The minister asked states for action plans, city-level plans, and public-private partnerships for multimodal hubs, and asked them to make land available for inland container depots through bidding.

By 16 September 2025, DPIIT said 27 states and Union Territories had formulated state logistics policies, 14 had action plans, and 19 had granted industry status to logistics. Nine policies were still in draft. Industry status is a state decision with tax and incentive consequences. It is separate from a LEADS label.

## Where the commissioned corridors run

The Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor is Ludhiana to Sonnagar, 1,337 km, described by Railways in March 2025 as mostly for mineral traffic from eastern India. The Western corridor is Dadri to JNPT, 1,506 km, described as giving western ports faster access to the northern hinterland. Both were reported as commissioned on 7 August 2026. The states named by the alignments are Punjab and the eastern mineral belt on one side, and Uttar Pradesh through to Maharashtra's JNPT on the other. Dadri is the northern junction that ties the western port system to the hinterland the ministry highlights.

The Budget proposal of 1 February 2026 names a further east-west line from Dankuni in West Bengal to Surat in Gujarat, through Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, and Maharashtra. That state list is the proposal's geography. The August 2026 reply says the detailed project report is being updated. It is not a state-wise construction progress table.

## Parks and port roads, with their dates

On 15 February 2022 the road ministry said 35 multimodal logistics parks were identified under Bharatmala Phase I. Jogighopa in Assam was under construction with the state as an equity partner. Bids had been invited for Nagpur with JNPT at Sindi, for Chennai at Mappedu with Chennai Port and SIPCOT, and for Bengaluru with KIADB. That is a four-site snapshot from 2022. The September 2025 DPIIT note says parks are being developed and that multi-agency planning is still being harmonised. It does not replace the four site names with a completion list.

Port and coastal roads under Bharatmala, as on 31 October 2024, were 18 projects, 424 km awarded and 189 km constructed, serving major and minor ports in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and West Bengal. Kerala and West Bengal are coastal aspirers in LEADS 2024 and are also named in that port-road sentence. The logistics label and the road programme are answering different questions about the same coastline.
