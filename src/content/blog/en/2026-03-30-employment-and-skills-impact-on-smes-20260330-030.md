Smaller employers meet India's jobs statistics in two places that do not measure the same thing. The Periodic Labour Force Survey describes who is working or seeking work in the population. The Employees' Provident Fund Organisation describes members and establishments inside a social-security system. A hiring plan that treats one as the other will mis-size both the market and the compliance bill.

## The ceiling decision of 16 September 2026

The Union Cabinet approved a higher wage ceiling for mandatory EPFO coverage, from ₹15,000 a month to ₹25,000 a month. The Press Information Bureau note of 16 September 2026 says a fresh employee joining above ₹15,000 has not been brought in automatically, and may sit outside mandatory provident fund, pension, and the linked insurance cover. The new ceiling is meant to pull the ₹15,000–₹25,000 band into that statutory frame.

The ministry expects more than 51 lakh additional employees to come under mandatory coverage. That sentence is an expectation. The same note says the Ministry of Labour and Employment and EPFO will take the statutory and administrative steps to implement the decision. Until those steps are done, the ₹25,000 ceiling is an approved proposal, not a deduction already running on a payroll.

The ceiling was last raised to ₹15,000 in September 2014, after a decade at the previous level. The Expenditure Finance Committee recommended the present proposal on 16 June 2026. The annual government outgo is estimated at about ₹11,339 crore, against existing budgetary support of about ₹10,250 crore. The five-year estimate is about ₹56,696 crore.

The same note reports the latest EPFO scale as about 7.98 crore contributing members in about 7.68 lakh contributing establishments, and about 82 lakh pensioners under the Employees' Pension Scheme.

For an employer, the operational question is whether people paid between the old ceiling and the new one will become mandatorily covered, with provident fund, pension, and Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance as the scheme rules provide. The note also says wider coverage can support retention. It does not publish a compliance checklist or a start date.

## What a monthly payroll addition counts

EPFO's provisional payroll for July 2025, released on 23 September 2025, showed a net addition of 21.04 lakh members, 5.55% above July 2024. New subscribers were about 9.79 lakh. About 5.98 lakh of those new subscribers were aged 18–25, which is 61.06% of new subscribers that month. Net addition in the 18–25 band was about 9.13 lakh.

About 16.43 lakh members who had exited earlier rejoined, 12.12% above July 2024. The release says these are people who moved to another covered establishment and transferred their balance rather than taking a final settlement. Net addition is built from first-time joiners, exits, and rejoins. A month in which rejoins are large is a month of movement inside the covered sector as well as a month of first enrolments. The release marks the whole table provisional, because establishments file and revise electronic challans after the report is generated, and because exit dates are marked later.

Around 40.21% of the net membership addition was in the industry class EPFO labels "expert services." Maharashtra accounted for 20.47% of net payroll. The top five states and Union territories accounted for about 60.85%, roughly 12.80 lakh net members. Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Haryana, Delhi, Telangana, and Uttar Pradesh each added more than 5%.

That geography is where covered establishments are adding members. It is not a ranking of state unemployment. The rural and urban survey rates are a different map, set out in the [regional PLFS brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-regional-view-20260330-070).

## The year underneath one month

At its 2 March 2026 meeting, the Central Board of Trustees approved EPFO's annual report for 2024–25. During that year the organisation brought 2,86,894 new establishments under coverage and enrolled 1,22,89,244 new members. Contributions were ₹3,35,628.81 crore. The interest rate declared on EPF deposits for 2024–25 was 8.25%. The board also recommended 8.25% for 2025–26, which still required government notification before credit.

The same meeting approved notification of new EPF, EPS, and EDLI schemes aligned with the Code on Social Security, 2020, so the existing schemes can be replaced. For an employer, that is a second moving part beside the wage ceiling: the scheme text itself is being re-issued, on a timetable the board has approved and the ministry still has to notify.

Apprentices are a third door, run by the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship rather than by EPFO payroll. How that scheme is counted is in the [Skill India brief](/blog/employment-and-skills-policy-explainer-20260330-060).
