# What the GST rate recast changed for retail

The retail tax change to read is the 56th GST Council meeting of 3 September 2025, chaired by the Union Finance Minister. The Press Information Bureau record says the Council moved the four-rate structure to a two-rate design: a merit rate of 5%, a standard rate of 18%, and a special de-merit rate of 40% for a select few goods and services.

Timing is part of the decision. Rate changes on services took effect on 22 September 2025. Rate changes on goods took effect the same day, with a stated exception. Pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, chewing tobacco such as zarda, unmanufactured tobacco, and bidi stayed on the existing GST rates and compensation cess until loan and interest obligations under the compensation cess account are discharged.

The Council also decided that GST on pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, unmanufactured tobacco, and chewing tobacco such as zarda would be levied on retail sale price rather than transaction value.

## What a shop can and cannot infer

The press note is a rate architecture, plus annexes of item changes. It does not say that every product on a shelf moved to 5% or 18%. A founder pricing a SKU still has to find that SKU in the annex and in the rate notification that followed. A policy reader who quotes “GST on retail is now 18%” has collapsed the merit list, the standard list, and the de-merit list into one number.

The Council’s stated aims include lower rates on common-use goods and a simpler grid. The PIB note does not publish a measured change in household consumption caused by the recast. The Reserve Bank’s August 2026 resolution later treated the continuing effect of GST rationalisation as one support for urban demand. That is a policy judgment in the monetary statement, not a MoSPI estimate of extra PFCE.

## The marketplace piece that is still a statute

Separately from the 2025 rate recast, section 52 of the CGST Act requires an e-commerce operator, where it collects the consideration, to collect tax at source on the net value of taxable supplies made through it by other suppliers, at a notified rate not exceeding 1%, and to file a monthly statement. The rate recast did not repeal that collection duty. Operators should read the current notification for the rate actually in force.
