# What changed in India’s bank credit growth

The change is a reacceleration, and it is no longer a retail story.

Scheduled commercial banks’ non-food credit grew 19.1% year on year as on 31 July 2026. A year earlier, on the comparable fortnight of 25 July 2025, growth was 9.9%. At the end of March 2026 the same non-food series was already at 15.9%, against 10.9% on 4 April 2025. The Reserve Bank’s Annual Report for 2025-26 records the same 15.9% figure for bank credit to the commercial sector, against 10.9% the year before.

That is a different cycle from 2024-25. The Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India, released on 29 December 2025, said deposits and credit of scheduled commercial banks still grew in double digits that year, but more slowly than the year before. In the detailed chapter, bank credit of scheduled commercial banks excluding regional rural banks rose 11.5% in 2024-25. Stripping out a merger, credit growth was 12.5% in 2024-25 against 16.0% in 2023-24.

## Where the faster growth sits

The sectoral release splits the book of 41 banks that account for about 95% of non-food credit. Between the March 2026 and July 2026 fortnights, personal loans did not speed up. They grew 16.2% at both dates. The step-up was elsewhere.

| Segment | Year-on-year growth, 31 March 2026 | Year-on-year growth, 31 July 2026 | Year-ago rate in the July release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agriculture and allied activities | 15.7% (10.4% a year earlier) | 17.0% | 7.3% |
| Industry | 15.0% (8.2%) | 20.0% | 6.5% |
| Services | 19.0% (12.0%) | 22.9% | 10.2% |
| Personal loans | 16.2% (11.7%) | 16.2% | 11.9% |

Industry moved from a recovery against a weak base to the fastest of the four broad sectors by July. The July note says credit to large and medium industries grew at an accelerated pace, while micro and small industries grew steadily. Inside industry, infrastructure, basic metals, engineering, chemicals, petroleum and coal products, and textiles were the buoyant lines.

Services were already the fastest major sector in March, at 19.0%, and reached 22.9% by July. The Reserve Bank names the same three supports at both dates: loans to non-banking financial companies, trade, and commercial real estate.

Personal loans stayed at 16.2%. The mix inside that segment changed. In March, vehicle loans and loans against gold jewellery were robust and housing was steady. By July, housing and vehicle loans were still in double digits, while credit-card outstanding and loans against gold jewellery had decelerated.

## How to read the 19.1% without mixing series

The 19.1% and the 15.9% are non-food credit from the fortnightly Section-42 return, which covers all scheduled commercial banks. The sector rates come from a separate return and a 41-bank sample. From 31 December 2025, the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025 redefined the last reporting fortnight as the last day of the month. Year-on-year rates from December 2025 compare that month-end with the old fortnight date a year earlier. A gap versus another RBI credit series is often a coverage difference, not a revision.

Two other official prints describe the same year and should not be averaged into 19.1%. The June 2026 Financial Stability Report says scheduled commercial banks’ credit grew 14.5% during 2025-26 on supervisory data. The annual Basic Statistical Return for March 2026 says outstanding credit, including regional rural banks, grew 14.1%. Both confirm a faster year than 2024-25. Neither is the July non-food rate.

## What this means for a reader

A founder raising working capital, a lender setting sector limits, or an investor underwriting a bank is looking at a broad credit upswing that has tilted toward industry and services since March 2026. Household and personal-loan growth is solid and no longer the margin of acceleration. The year-ago industry rate of 6.5% means part of the 20% print is a base effect. The level of growth, across agriculture, industry, and services together, is still a real change from the slower 2024-25 book.
