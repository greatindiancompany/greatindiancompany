# Where India's cyber system is still thin

A bottleneck review should use gaps the publishing institution has already marked. Several of those gaps are explicit. Others appear when two official series are placed side by side and the reader refuses to treat them as one statistic.

## Two counts that do not measure the same thing

For 2023, CERT-In handled 15,92,917 cyber security incidents. NCRB, in Crime in India as quoted to the Lok Sabha on 24 March 2026, recorded 86,420 cyber-crime cases registered that year, and 1,104 persons convicted. The conviction column is not a clearance rate of the cases registered in the same year. Cases and convictions move on different clocks, and the reply prints them as separate columns.

The distance between 15,92,917 and 86,420 is also not a measure of police failure. CERT-In counts incidents it handled, dominated in its own 2023 table by vulnerable services and scanning. NCRB counts crimes registered by police, involving communication devices as medium or target. Most technical incidents never become a criminal case. The bottleneck is that a national picture has to be assembled from ledgers that were not built to match.

The reply adds two harder limits. CERT-In does not maintain estimated financial loss. NCRB does not maintain the amount recovered. Anyone managing a national cyber programme without a loss series is managing incidents and cases, not a rupee outcome.

## Investigation capacity is local, and the supplements are finite

Police and public order are state subjects. The Centre's financial assistance under the CCPWC scheme, as stated in that reply, is ₹132.93 crore released, laboratories commissioned in 33 states and Union territories, and more than 24,600 police, prosecutors and judicial officers trained. The Delhi digital investigation support centre had helped in more than 13,417 cases up to 31 January 2026. A second centre opened in Assam on 29 August 2025.

Those are real additions. They are also a description of a system that still depends on state agencies to turn a portal complaint into an FIR. Seven joint teams, for Mewat, Jamtara, Ahmedabad, Hyderabad, Chandigarh, Vishakhapatnam and Guwahati, exist because hotspots cross jurisdictions. Multi-state cases are an acknowledged coordination problem, which is why the teams and the Samanvaya platform were built.

A standard operating procedure for the reporting portal and the financial-fraud reporting system was issued on 2 January 2026. Its job, as the reply describes it, is a uniform, victim-facing handling process and a coordination mechanism with states. A procedure dated January 2026 is evidence that common handling was still being written down then.

## Money revised down, duties not yet commenced

Cyber Security Projects in MeitY's demand were budgeted at ₹782 crore for 2025-26 and revised to ₹600 crore. The demand does not explain the revision in the note reproduced with the line. Undershoot of a scheme estimate is an execution fact. The following year's budget estimate of ₹790 crore asks the system to spend at the higher level again.

On personal data, the bottleneck is legal timing as much as staffing. Rules on security safeguards and breach notices commence eighteen months after 13 November 2025. Until that date, a data fiduciary cannot be told that those particular rules are already in force. The Data Protection Board's establishment budget was revised from ₹5 crore to ₹2 crore in 2025-26, with ₹10 crore estimated for 2026-27, for salary and establishment. A new board on a small establishment line, waiting on a later commencement for much of its caseload, is an institutional ramp, not a finished tribunal.

## The baseline says it is not enough

CERT-In's MSME document is direct. The fifteen controls are a minimum. Threats change, and each firm has risks tied to its sector, size and data. Firms are told they must go beyond the baseline and that they remain responsible for doing so. Empanelled auditors grew from 176 in the 2023 annual report to 237 in the March 2026 reply, so the supply of named auditors increased. A longer panel does not audit every MSME. The document recommends an annual baseline audit. It does not report how many firms have had one.

The thin points are matched ledgers, loss data, state investigation, scheme expenditure against estimate, and a personal-data rulebook that is still walking through its own commencement schedule.
