# What to open before the December aviation and tourism prints

The next ninety days from 24 September 2026 run to late December. Nothing in the July and August papers schedules a single decision for that window. What they do provide is a short list of documents that will either confirm the summer’s picture or break it. Open them in this order.

## 1. The next DGCA monthly note, read as a sentence

The Directorate General’s traffic note carries one sentence that is enough for a first pass: passengers carried by domestic airlines for the year to date, the year-ago total, the annual growth rate, and the monthly growth rate. Through July 2026 that sentence ended at 984.03 lakh, against 977.79 lakh, annual growth 0.64 percent. Monthly growth means the newest month against the same month a year earlier.

When the August note is the latest one in hand, subtract July’s cumulative from the new cumulative before comparing it with May. May was 153.91 lakh. July was 119.99 lakh. A strong festival month that is still below May is a seasonal recovery inside a flat year, which is the pattern January–July already shows. Write down IndiGo’s passenger count and the Air India group’s count for that month. In June those were 89.20 lakh and 32.22 lakh. In July they were 80.82 lakh and 28.75 lakh.

## 2. Keep airport throughput in a separate column

If a ministry reply or an Airports Authority release restates 2025-26 traffic, the last official wording is “about 420 million” passengers handled, against capacity of over 580 million a year, given to the Lok Sabha on 6 August 2026. That figure includes international passengers and counts airport movements. It will not match 984.03 lakh. Updating one column with the other column’s number is the error this checklist is for.

## 3. The tourism snapshot, rupees first

The Ministry of Tourism’s foreign-exchange table for January–December 2025 sums to ₹2,73,638 crore, down 6.62 percent from ₹2,93,033 crore in 2024 and up 26.4 percent from 2019. The next monthly snapshot is useful if it shows whether 2026 is tracking the weak summer of 2025 (May and June were down 21.35 and 22.2 percent) or the firmer December. Read international arrivals, foreign tourist arrivals, and non-resident Indian arrivals as three lines. In 2024 they were 20.57 million, 9.95 million, and 10.62 million. The PIB’s “around 2.02 crore” for 2025 is the international line.

## 4. Modified UDAN filings, not the launch speech

The scheme has been launched. The next observable step is a state nomination under the challenge: encumbrance-free land, infrastructure readiness, technical feasibility, traffic potential, clearances, and a letter of state support. A workshop photograph is not that filing. Also watch whether any award list shows an airline being barred for missing a start window. That penalty is in the 16 July scheme comparison, and it has not yet appeared as a published case list.

## 5. Treat one number as unfinished

Viability-gap funding for 2025-26 was ₹738.13 crore, marked provisional, in the 23 July reply. The two closed years beside it are ₹807.02 crore and ₹628.42 crore. Until the provisional tag is lifted, do not build a three-year trend that treats ₹738.13 crore as final. The route stock to carry forward is 348 routes still operational, out of 679 operationalised, not the lifetime total on its own.
