## Separate the operating base from the horizon

The mid-term trajectory of India's trade corridors has a base that already runs and a set of dated targets. The base is the commissioned Eastern and Western Dedicated Freight Corridors, 1,337 km and 1,506 km, with about 443 trains a day in the 7 August 2026 Rajya Sabha reply. The horizon is a logistics-rank target, a waterway programme, a modal-share target, and a maritime vision for 2047. Each horizon item has an author and a year. None of them is automatic because the first two rail corridors are open.

## To 2030: rank, cost method, and state policy

DPIIT, on 16 September 2025, set the National Logistics Policy objectives as logistics costs at global benchmarks, an Logistics Performance Index rank in the top 25 by 2030, and a data system for decisions. The same note says India stands 38th on the World Bank index and credits the state LEADS exercise with supporting that rise. The path from 38th to the top 25 is a policy objective on that page. It is not a published year-by-year rank path.

The cost side of the same horizon is unfinished measurement. The NCAER framework unveiled on 3 January 2025 is a method, built with a task force from NITI Aayog, the transport ministries, and MoSPI, combining export-import and domestic cargo. In September 2025 DPIIT said the assessment itself was in its final stages. A mid-term cost trajectory cannot be drawn until that assessment is published. What can be drawn is institutional: 27 states and Union Territories with logistics policies, 19 with industry status for the sector, and commodity plans that are finished for coal, finalised for cement, and still draft for steel, fertiliser, and food processing.

## Five years of waterways, and a 2047 modal share

The Union Budget 2026-27 proposes to operationalise 20 national waterways over the next five years. The summary of 1 February 2026 says this starts with National Waterway 5 in Odisha, connecting the mineral areas of Talcher and Angul and the industrial centre of Kalinga Nagar with Paradeep and Dhamra. The same statement proposes a Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme and an increase in the share of inland waterways and coastal shipping from 6 percent to 12 percent by 2047.

That 6-to-12 path sits on a decade in which, according to the 27 March 2025 Sagarmala note, coastal shipping grew 118 percent and inland-waterway cargo rose 700 percent. Growth in the past decade and a share target for 2047 are both official. They measure different things, and the note does not convert the 118 percent and 700 percent into the 6 percent base. Use the Budget sentence for the share, and the Sagarmala sentence for the decade's growth.

Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047, as summarised in that Sagarmala note, includes 4 million gross registered tonnage of shipbuilding capacity, 10 billion metric tonnes of annual port handling, and a place among the top five shipbuilding nations by 2047. Sagarmala is described as a pillar of that vision. Sagarmala 2.0 is described with ₹40,000 crore of budgetary support aimed at leveraging ₹12 lakh crore over the next decade. The identified Sagarmala book on 19 March 2025 was about 839 projects and ₹5.79 lakh crore, with 272 projects completed at about ₹1.41 lakh crore. The mid-term port trajectory is the rate at which that identified book is completed, plus whatever Sagarmala 2.0 finances. The 2047 tonnage figures remain targets in the vision.

## The rail horizon after commissioning

With the first two dedicated corridors commissioned, the rail horizon in the official record is the Dankuni–Surat proposal, 2,052 km and nearly ₹1.20 lakh crore in the 1 February 2026 briefing, with report preparation and updation confirmed on 7 August 2026. A second horizon is any later decision on the three reports that were unsanctioned on 19 March 2025. A mid-term rail map that draws those lines as operating track is ahead of the replies.

Hinterland access is already part of the operating design. The Railways replies say the Western corridor gives western ports faster reach into the northern hinterland, and that the Eastern corridor is oriented to mineral traffic. The Budget's waterway start in Odisha points mineral and industrial cargo at Paradeep and Dhamra. Those are the two official hinterland stories for the next several years: rail from the west coast northward, and a proposed waterway from Odisha's mineral belt to its ports.
