Founders meet India’s fiscal system in three places: the tax return, the payment that does or does not arrive from a government buyer, and the interest rate buried in the next term loan. The Union Budget for 2026-27 speaks to all three. It does not rank sectors for you, and it does not know your working capital.

## The return you will actually file

The Income-tax Act, 2025, takes effect on 1 April 2026. The Finance Ministry’s fiscal statement says the rewrite was guided by simpler text, no major policy change, and no change in tax rates. Finance Act, 2025, is a separate statute; that is where the statement locates the slab relief under the new regime. If a vendor tells you the new Act itself cut your rate, ask for the section. The Ministry’s own description says the Act was built not to do that.

GST is the closer operational system. After the Council’s rate changes of 22 September 2025, the structure the government describes is a merit rate of 5 percent, a standard rate of 18 percent, and a 40 percent demerit rate on a short list. Classification disputes were the point of the simplification. They have not disappeared for goods that sit on a boundary. The notification, not a summary, decides the boundary.

Registration: low-risk applicants, and applicants whose output-tax liability on supplies to registered persons will not exceed ₹2.5 lakh a month, are to be registered within three working days. The goods threshold of ₹40 lakh, the composition limit of ₹1.5 crore outside special-category states, and quarterly filing up to ₹5 crore of turnover are the older thresholds a [Press Information Bureau note of 30 June 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2279318&lang=1&reg=3) still records. Special-category states can sit below the ₹40 lakh goods threshold. Know which threshold applies to your state before you decide you are “out.”

Refunds are the cash-flow line operators under-read. In the GST system’s provisional report for August 2026, gross collections were ₹1,99,853 crore, up 14.8 percent on the previous August, and refunds were ₹31,795 crore, up 67.9 percent. Net collections rose 8.3 percent, to ₹1,68,057 crore. If you export or you sit in an inverted rate, the refund pipe matters more than the gross headline. The report says the figures are provisional.

## If the government is your customer

Just-in-time payment is the phrase the fiscal statement uses for SNA SPARSH. Funds are meant to leave the Consolidated Fund when the payment is due, across 50 centrally sponsored schemes the statement says had been onboarded. If your invoice sits on a scheme that is not on that list, the old float — money released early into an agency account, or not released at all — can still be your reality. Ask the buyer which pipe the scheme uses. The Budget line is not the pipe.

The large Union heads in the Key Features booklet are transport and defence, each near ₹6 lakh crore on the major-item list, then rural development. A startup that does not sell into those systems should not build a plan on “capex of ₹12.22 lakh crore.” That capital total includes ₹2.0 lakh crore of loans to states. The buyer may be a state department. The state’s own budget, and the Reserve Bank’s compilation of state budgets, is the map for that buyer.

## The borrowing you do not control

The Centre’s gross dated borrowing for 2026-27 is estimated at ₹17.20 lakh crore, and net at ₹11.73 lakh crore. Interest payments are estimated at ₹14.04 lakh crore, which is more than the capital line. The previous year’s primary issuances, through 27 January 2026, cleared at a weighted average yield of about 6.65 percent. That yield is a record. Your spread over it is a bank’s decision. What you can know is that the government is still a large, regular borrower, so the risk-free leg of your rate has a published supply calendar.

You do not need a debt-sustainability model. You need to know that the Budget’s working rule is a slightly lower deficit, 4.3 percent of GDP after 4.4 percent, in service of a lower debt ratio, and that the revenue deficit is not improving this year. A government that is still running a revenue deficit is still borrowing for more than capital assets. That is the macro fact. It is not a reason to delay a product decision by itself.

The useful monthly habit is short. Read the GST gross, refund, and net trio when the monthly PDF is posted. Read the Controller General’s capital-expenditure and net-tax percentages when the month is posted. Ignore commentary that cites a securities regulator for either number. Those prints are the Finance Ministry’s system and the accounts controller.
