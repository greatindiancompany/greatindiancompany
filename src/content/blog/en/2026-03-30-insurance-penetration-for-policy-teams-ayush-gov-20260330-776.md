# Who Sets Insurance Penetration Policy in India

Insurance penetration is a statistic IRDAI publishes. The laws that change the market around that statistic are administered by the Department of Financial Services in the Ministry of Finance. Mixing those two roles produces bad policy notes: a regulation is described as a statute, or a scheme enrolment is added into the GDP ratio.

## The department and the authority

DFS, on its [about page](https://financialservices.gov.in/about-us), is the nodal department for banking, financial services, and insurance. It administers the Insurance Act, 1938, the Life Insurance Corporation Act, 1956, the General Insurance Business (Nationalisation) Act, 1972, and the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 1999. It handles legislative and policy matters for IRDAI, as it does for the Reserve Bank of India and the pension regulator.

IRDAI's job, as DFS restates it on the [insurance overview](https://financialservices.gov.in/beta/en/page/insurance-overview), includes protecting policyholders, orderly growth of the industry, soundness of the firms it regulates, claim settlement, and grievance handling. The Authority works from Hyderabad. The penetration series lives in the [IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25](https://irdai.gov.in/en/document-detail?documentId=8375620): 3.7 percent of GDP in 2024-25, life 2.7 percent, non-life 1 percent.

A policy team that wants the ratio moved has two levers, and they are not the same document. Parliament and DFS change the Act and the foreign-investment rules. IRDAI changes regulations, product conditions, and the rural-obligation design. The 2025 amendment, described by the [PIB note of 23 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2254950&lang=1&reg=3), is the first kind of lever: FDI from 74 percent to 100 percent, one-time intermediary registration, a higher share-transfer threshold, a lower net-owned-fund bar for foreign reinsurers, a policyholder education fund, disgorgement, and a higher penalty ceiling. The March 2024 consolidated regulations are the second kind.

## Schemes are a third book

PIB reports government protection schemes next to the commercial market. They are not a substitute for the penetration ratio, and their enrolment figures should stay in their own sentences.

- PMJJBY: ₹2 lakh life cover, annual premium ₹436, 26.88 crore gross enrolments and 10,45,450 claims disbursed as of February 2026.
- PMSBY: accidental cover at an annual premium of ₹20, 57.11 crore enrolments and 1.76 lakh claims disbursed as of February 2026.
- Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY: up to ₹5 lakh per family per year, 43.52 crore cards as of 28 February 2026.
- ESI: 3.84 crore insured persons and 14.91 crore beneficiaries as on 31 March 2025.
- PMFBY: 93.98 crore applications and claims of ₹1,94,505.9 crore paid as of 13 March 2026.

IRDAI's social-sector obligation does point insurers at PMJJBY, PMSBY, and named welfare schemes. That link means scheme design and commercial obligations meet. It does not mean the card count is the penetration ratio.

## A clean policy note

State the ratio from the annual report, with the year. State the statutory change from PIB or DFS, with the instrument. State scheme coverage from PIB, as enrolments or cards. Say which institution can move the next step. The GST exemption on individual life and health policies from 22 September 2025 is a tax decision, recorded by PIB, whose effect on the premium ratio can show up only in a later annual report.
