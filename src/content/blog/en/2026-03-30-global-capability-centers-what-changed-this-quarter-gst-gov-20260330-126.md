# What Changed for Global Capability Centers in India

A global capability centre is a captive unit: a foreign company doing technology, operations, or research for itself from India. This quarter's official record moved on exports and on foreign equity. It did not gain a centre census.

## The release inside this quarter

On 18 September 2026 the Reserve Bank published the 2025-26 round of its Survey on Computer Software and Information Technology Enabled Services Exports (Press Release 2026-2027/1147).

Software services exports, excluding sales through overseas commercial presence, were **US$ 221.4 billion**, up **8.2 percent** from **US$ 204.7 billion** in 2024-25. Adding services delivered by foreign affiliates of Indian companies, the wider total was **US$ 239.3 billion**, up **9.5 percent** from **US$ 218.6 billion**.

Inside that export total:

- Computer services were **US$ 153.4 billion**, **69.3 percent** of the total, against **67.4 percent** a year earlier.
- IT services were **US$ 147.0 billion**. Software product development was **US$ 6.4 billion**, down from **US$ 6.8 billion**.
- IT-enabled services were **US$ 68.0 billion**. Business process outsourcing was **US$ 56.0 billion**. Engineering services were **US$ 12.0 billion**, up from **US$ 10.8 billion**.
- Off-site delivery was **91.7 percent** of exports (**US$ 203.0 billion**). On-site delivery was **8.3 percent** (**US$ 18.4 billion**).
- The United States took **54.1 percent** (**US$ 119.7 billion**). Europe took **31.8 percent**, and the United Kingdom **15.4 percent** of the total.

The Bank contacted **7,569** companies and received answers from **2,363**, covering about **89 percent** of estimated software services exports. The survey sorts activity, country, invoice currency, and mode of supply. It does not sort captive centres from third-party exporters. A reader who pastes US$ 221.4 billion into a slide titled "GCC revenue" is changing the object the Bank measured.

The previous round, published on 4 November 2025, had put 2024-25 growth at **7.3 percent** to US$ 204.7 billion on the same excluding-commercial-presence basis. The new information is a faster print, a higher computer-services share, and a still-dominant off-site mode.

## The FDI table placed in July

On 20 July 2026 the Ministry of Finance answered Lok Sabha Unstarred Question No. 10 and reproduced the DPIIT FDI factsheet for March 2026. Equity inflow into **Computer Software & Hardware** was **USD 13,946 million** in 2025-26, after **USD 7,814 million** in 2024-25 and **USD 7,973 million** in 2023-24. Cumulative equity from April 2000 to March 2026 was **USD 124,644 million**.

That 2025-26 sector line is about **78 percent** above the 2024-25 line. It is the largest sector in the top-ten table in that answer. The services sector, which DPIIT defines to include financial services, outsourcing, and R&D, was **USD 10,010 million** in 2025-26.

The same answer states gross FDI inflow of **USD 94.84 billion** in 2025-26, against **USD 80.61 billion** in 2024-25, and net FDI of **USD 6.95 billion**, against **USD 0.96 billion**. The Ministry links the net figure to repatriation and to overseas direct investment by Indian entities. Gross inflow, net inflow, and the computer-software equity line are three different balances. A captive that reinvests earnings can grow while fresh equity is quiet, and the reverse can also happen.

## What did not change

The government text that actually states a centre count is older than this quarter. On 25 February 2025, PIB Delhi reported remarks by the Secretary, Ministry of Labour and Employment, during an International Labour Organization visit to an HSBC centre in Gurugram. The Secretary highlighted "over 1,700 GCCs", **1.9 million** professionals, and **$64.6 billion** in revenue as of 2024, and a projection of about **$105 billion**, around **2,400** centres, and over **2.8 million** people by 2030. The note names Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Mumbai, and the National Capital Region. It does not name a statistical office or a survey frame.

The September 2026 RBI release does not update those counts. Neither does the DPIIT sector table. Until a statistical agency publishes a register of captives, the February 2025 remarks remain a ministerial statement, and the export and equity series remain the series that moved.
