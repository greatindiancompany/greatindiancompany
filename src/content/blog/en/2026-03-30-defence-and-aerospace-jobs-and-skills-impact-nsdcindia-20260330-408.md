# Defence notices name places and firms, not a national jobs total

## The claim these pages will not support

A workforce plan needs a headcount, a skill, and a place. The Ministry notices below publish some of those pieces. They do not publish one national total of people employed in defence and aerospace factories. Anyone who needs that total has to get it from a later official series. It is not hiding in the export release or the positive-list release.

## Counts that are about firms and trainees of a scheme, not payrolled staff

The Department of Defence Production's iDEX page describes a scheme with budgetary support of Rs 498.78 crore over five years, from 2021-22 to 2025-26, launched in May 2021. The stated objective is financial support to nearly 300 startups, MSMEs, and individual innovators, and to about 20 partner incubators, through the Defence Innovation Organisation. The iDEX FAQ sets the grant ceiling at Rs 1.5 crore for DISC and Open Challenges, and at Rs 25 crore for ADITI. Both depend on approval of the product development budget. The startup's matching contribution is at least half of total project cost.

"Nearly 300" is a support target for firms and innovators. It is not 300 factories and it is not a jobs multiplier. The Defence Innovation Organisation is a section 8 company under the Department, formed by Hindustan Aeronautics and Bharat Electronics. iDEX is its executive arm. Hiring, if an order follows, is the firm's and the user's, not an automatic outcome of the grant.

The FY 2025-26 budget release adds a different rupee figure: Rs 449.62 crore allocated that year to iDEX, including the ADITI sub-scheme. That is an annual budget line. It is not a second copy of the Rs 498.78 crore multi-year envelope. As of this note, the multi-year window printed on the scheme page runs through 2025-26, which has closed. A skills partnership should ask whether a successor window has been notified before treating the grant as open.

## Counts that are about industry participation

A PIB note of 20 November 2025 says 788 industrial licences have been issued to 462 companies, and that 16,000 MSMEs are part of the widening ecosystem. Licences and MSME participation measure firms allowed in, or firms present. They do not measure shifts worked.

The same note says that, as of October 2025, the Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu corridors had attracted investments over Rs 9,145 crore. The February 2022 parliamentary reply is more explicit about employment and still does not count it. It says the corridors may create employment, and it points to state policies that include an employment-promotion title in Uttar Pradesh. "May create" is the government's verb in that reply. It is not a filled vacancy.

## One employment figure that is real, and narrow

The 1 February 2025 budget release says the Border Roads Organisation has created substantial employment by employing 70,000 local youths. That sentence is about border-road works, local hiring, and longer-run employability. It is not employment in aircraft, ship, or munition factories. Quoting 70,000 as the defence-industry workforce misreads the paragraph.

The same release says capital investment in defence manufacturing will provide greater job opportunities. That is a direction. No vacancy count is attached.

## Where a skills reader should go instead

Match a skill to a door that is public. SRIJAN lists parts the public producers want Indian firms to make. The corridor nodes are Aligarh, Agra, Chitrakoot, Jhansi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Chennai, Coimbatore, Hosur, Salem, and Tiruchirappalli. iDEX challenges name a problem a service or a public undertaking wants solved. Company recruitment for Hindustan Aeronautics, Bharat Electronics, the shipyards, and the firms created from the ordnance factories sits on those companies' own notices.

Great Indian Company leaves the headcount blank on purpose. A training programme built on a blank will not survive contact with a factory.
