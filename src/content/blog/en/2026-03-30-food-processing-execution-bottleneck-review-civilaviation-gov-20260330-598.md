# Where food processing projects slow down

The ministry describes its schemes as demand-driven. Demand is not the same as completion. The files that take longest are the ones that need land of a certain size, a bank that will lend, a state that will share the cost, or a promoter who can finish inside a grant clock that shrinks when it slips.

## The park model slowed on land, and new sanctions stopped

Mega food parks were built as a hub and spoke: a central processing centre linked to collection near the farm. A reply placed on the ministry site, referring to the position as of 28 February 2025, says the scheme required at least 50 acres, which often deterred projects in hilly and difficult areas. The 15th Finance Commission redesign took the logical next step. New mega food park projects are not sanctioned. Only committed liabilities remain. The annual report still records grant released to ongoing parks, Rs 8.32 crore between 1 January and 31 December 2024, and lists 24 parks as operational.

Stopping new parks removes one bottleneck and leaves another: the parks already approved have to be finished and filled. About 114 units operate in the parks the report calls operational. A park that is "approved" and a park that has processors in it are different stages, which is why the report bothers to name the operational sites.

## Cold chain slows on the clock and on documents

As on 31 December 2024, 108 integrated cold-chain projects were under implementation and 286 were complete, out of 394 taken up. The report's phase notes say projects drop out when promoters withdraw or when the ministry cancels them for want of documents or justification. That is an administrative bottleneck, and it is quantified by a history of cancellations going back to the 11th Plan.

For projects that stay in, the bottleneck becomes time. Farm-level infrastructure is mandatory, plus at least one of processing, a distribution hub, or refrigerated transport, unless the project is a standalone irradiation facility. The build is supposed to finish in 24 months, or 30 in difficult areas. Each month after that can cost up to 5 percent of the instalment. A promoter waiting on a power connection or a term-loan disbursement is burning grant eligibility, not only calendar time.

The ministry's scheme management system is the tool it built for this queue. It covers cold chain, clusters, processing capacity, testing labs, Operation Greens, and research, from online application to grant release, and the dashboard is meant to show pendency and delay. A project that cannot be seen on that system is a project the ministry's own monitoring design treats as stuck.

## PMFME slows where credit and the state meet

The micro scheme's subsidy is credit-linked. The September 2025 FAQ says the beneficiary brings at least 10 percent and the balance is a bank loan. District resource persons exist to help with the project report and the sanction. If the bank does not sanction, the ministry's 35 percent does not start. That is the central execution gate, and it is why the year-end review can report 1,72,707 loans sanctioned and, in a separate sentence, subsidies released.

The second gate is fiscal sharing. The Centre bears 60 percent in most states, 90 percent in North Eastern and Himalayan states, and 100 percent in Union territories without a legislature. A centrally sponsored scheme moves at the speed of the state nodal agency as well as the speed of the ministry. Incubation centres show the lag in physical form: 76 approved with an outlay of Rs 208.11 crore, and 23 completed, inaugurated, or commissioned, as of the 9 January 2026 review.

One District One Product can slow a branding file without slowing the basic subsidy. The subsidy is available for non-ODOP products. Branding and marketing money is where the district product gets priority, and changing the district product requires a state recommendation and ministry approval. A unit that waits to switch its product before applying for the capital grant is waiting on a step the FAQ says is unnecessary for that grant.

## What execution review should measure

Count completed assets against approvals: 1,185 of 1,618 PMKSY projects operational in the January 2026 review; 286 of 394 cold-chain projects complete at end-2024; 23 of 76 incubation centres commissioned. Then look at the gate in front of the unfinished set. For parks, it was land, and new approvals have stopped. For cold chain, it is documents, the 24-month clock, and the delay cut. For micro units, it is the bank and the state share. A review that stops at "projects approved" will miss every one of those gates.
