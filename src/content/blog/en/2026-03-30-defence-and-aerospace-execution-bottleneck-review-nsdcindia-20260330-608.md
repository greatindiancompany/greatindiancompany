# A positive-list item still has to be developed, accepted, and bought

## The gap the Ministry has already printed

The 18 August 2026 release gives two counts from the same system. Up to June 2026, SRIJAN had offered more than 33,000 defence items for indigenisation. More than 15,700 items have been indigenised, with an estimated import-substitution value of about Rs 9,000 crore over five years. Defence public sector undertakings had placed procurement orders, including in-house production, of about Rs 10,000 crore on domestic vendors up to March 2026.

The offered pool is larger than the indigenised pool. That difference is the public shape of the bottleneck. This note does not invent the average number of months between an offer and an accepted item. The release does not publish that average. What it does publish is the route: Make procedures and in-house development by the defence public sector undertakings and the Coast Guard, with industry and especially MSMEs participating, and procurement from Indian industry only after indigenous development succeeds.

## Acceptance is the slow sentence

"Upon successful indigenous development" is the clause that takes time. Success is a technical and user judgment. The press note does not define it as a number of days, a single laboratory, or a waiver of trial. A firm that has been listed as interested on SRIJAN has entered the offer. It has not been told that the part is accepted.

The sixth list adds 405 items with an estimated business potential of Rs 3,070 crore. Potential is the Department's word. It becomes revenue only if development succeeds and a purchase follows. Sixteen of the items are Coast Guard items and 389 are from defence public sector undertakings, so the accepting organisation is not the same desk in every case.

## Revenue buying has its own manual

Capital acquisition is not the only slow door. A PIB note of 20 November 2025 says the Defence Procurement Manual 2025 took effect on 1 November 2025 for revenue goods and services worth about Rs 1 lakh crore. The note highlights lower liquidated damages, 0.1 percent per week, on indigenisation projects, guaranteed orders for indigenous products for up to five years, and removal of the no-objection certificate formerly required from the Ordnance Factory Board.

Those clauses are aimed at delay and at uncertainty of follow-on orders. Their presence in the manual is an official admission that damages, one-off orders, and an extra certificate were friction. Publishing a friendlier clause does not, by itself, prove the friction is gone. A firm should read the manual text that applies to its tender, not stop at the PIB summary.

## Innovation funding stops short of an order

iDEX is a parallel path for a startup that is not yet a production vendor. The FAQ is explicit that the Defence Innovation Organisation does not assure a minimum pilot quantity or commercial quantities. The nodal agency decides, under the Defence Acquisition Procedure. The bottleneck for an iDEX team is often the step after the prototype: a service or a public undertaking choosing to buy. Budgetary full use of the capital outlay, reported on 1 April 2026 at Rs 1.86 lakh crore, shows that the Ministry can spend an appropriation. It does not show that any one prototype was selected.

## Export and space add gates after the factory works

A part that has been indigenised for an Indian buyer may still need a Department of Defence Production export authorisation, and a check against the SCOMET list, before it leaves the country. A launch or satellite activity needs IN-SPACe authorisation even when the hardware exists. Execution risk in this industry is a sequence of acceptances. Great Indian Company treats the 33,000-to-15,700 gap as the one the Ministry has quantified, and leaves the unstated months unstated.
