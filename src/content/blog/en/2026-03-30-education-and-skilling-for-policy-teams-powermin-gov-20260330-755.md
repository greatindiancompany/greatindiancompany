## Two ministries, one learner

Policy teams get a cleaner picture if they stop treating “education and skilling” as one scheme with one budget.

The Ministry of Education owns the National Education Policy 2020, Samagra Shiksha (the school scheme the policy identifies as the successor to Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan), the All India Survey on Higher Education, and the university and technical regulators cited in Parliament. On 10 August 2026 that ministry reported a higher-education gross enrolment ratio of 30 in 2023-24, female participation of 31.2, and enrolment of 4.50 crore. It also reported NCrF adoption and the University Grants Commission rule allowing up to 50% of credits from skill courses.

The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship owns the Skill India Programme. The 7 February 2025 Cabinet note makes that a central-sector scheme of three parts: PMKVY 4.0, PM-NAPS, and Jan Shikshan Sansthan, with an overlay outlay of Rs 8,800 crore for 2022-23 to 2025-26. A central-sector scheme is not the same fiscal object as a school-education programme shared with the states.

## Where the two systems are required to meet

NEP 2020 tells schools and colleges to bring vocational education into the mainstream and to count vocational students in the gross enrolment ratio. The 3 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply says PMKVY has been implemented with the Department of School Education and Literacy, and that schools are used as skill hubs outside class hours. The concrete project in that reply is 1,200 skill labs in selected Navodaya and Eklavya schools, funded by the National Skill Development Fund, not by a PMKVY state quota.

State governments are not given PMKVY trainee targets. They are inside other parts of the system. NEP 2020 asks them to build State Institutes of Open Schooling and to use inclusion funds for access barriers, including for girls and transgender students. State Skill Development Missions join Kaushal Samiksha Kendra inspections. A state policy note that waits for a PMKVY target letter will wait indefinitely. A note that tracks proposals, skill labs, apprenticeship stipends, and school retention is using the instruments that exist.

## Questions a policy brief should leave blank

Do not fill a state league table from these replies. Do not cite 4.43% of GDP as the spending level for the current year; NEP 2020 records that level, and a later ratio is not in the August replies. Do not describe a Higher Education Commission as the body that issued the August 2026 credit rules. The reply still names the University Grants Commission, the All India Council for Technical Education, and NCVET. Do not mark the “50% of learners exposed to vocational education by 2025” line as done or failed. The later replies do not publish that percentage.
