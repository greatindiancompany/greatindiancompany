State education and skill departments do not need a new national policy to decide what to do this year. The National Education Policy 2020 is already the design. The binding constraints are the latest census, the latest college survey, and the budget column that was actually revised. A useful state note is built from those, for one state, rather than from the national average repeated in a larger font.

## Put secondary retention on the first page

The national UDISE+ 2024-25 picture is a briefing on where to look, not a substitute for the state table. Secondary-stage retention was 47.2%. Secondary dropout was 8.2%. Transition from middle to secondary was 86.6%. Middle-stage gross enrolment was 90.3% and secondary-stage gross enrolment was 68.5%. Early-stage dropout is no longer the national emergency it was: preparatory dropout was 2.3%. A state plan that still spends its narrative on "bringing children into Class 1," without a secondary-retention target, is managing the problem the census says has largely moved.

The state table is on the UDISE system. Publish the state's own retention, dropout, and transition for the secondary stage, with the stage definition written out, Grades 9–12. If the state looks better than 47.2% retention, say so and name the districts that pull it down. If it looks worse, the national average is not a comfort. Single-teacher schools and computer access belong on the same page. Nationally, single-teacher schools fell by around 6% and computer access reached 64.7% of schools. A district with a thin teacher posting and no computer cannot run the vocational or digital period the policy asks for, however clean the state gross enrolment ratio looks.

NIPUN Bharat's horizon is this academic year, 2026-27, for foundational literacy and numeracy by the end of Grade 3. The mission was launched on 5 July 2021 under Samagra Shiksha. A state team should already have a baseline against the mission's Lakshyas. UDISE will not supply the reading score. Commissioning or publishing that learning evidence is a state action, not a wait for Delhi. The policy text had said 2025. The mission guidelines say 2026-27. Use the mission year in the state order, and do not reopen the argument in the monitoring format.

## Higher education: equity gaps are the state job

AISHE 2023-24 puts all-India gross enrolment at 30 for ages 18–23, female gross enrolment at 31.2, Scheduled Caste at 27.8, and Scheduled Tribe at 22.8. The policy aim is 50% by 2035, including vocational education. A state higher-education department can do three concrete things with the new release. Replace older ratios in the state plan. Publish the state's ratio beside the tribal and caste ratios, not only the gender parity index, which is 1.08 nationally and can hide a tribal gap. And check whether the state's public colleges are the ones growing. The survey is self-reported and voluntary. Chase non-responding institutions. A missing college makes the state's ratio look like a policy failure when it may be a form that was not filed. In 2023-24, nationally, 59,533 of 64,756 registered institutions responded. States will vary around that.

PM-USHA's multi-year window, as stated in Parliament, ran through 2025-26, with an outlay of ₹12,926.10 crore including earlier liabilities. The annual sponsored provision in the higher-education demand for 2026-27 is ₹1,850 crore, after a revised ₹800 crore. State projects should be reconciled to whichever instrument is actually open, not to the launch speech of 2023.

## Skill missions: proposals, not just targets

The skill ministry's 2025-26 centrally sponsored provision was revised from ₹3,049.90 crore to ₹367 crore because PM-SETU, launched on 4 October 2025, was still preparatory. Funds for ITI upgradation wait on a special purpose vehicle, and as of 1 April 2026 state proposals had not reached the national committee. A state skill department's bottleneck may be its own unsent proposal. The progress list in that reply is a checklist a chief secretary can ask for by name: cluster identified, state steering committee formed, budget head created, expression of interest floated, request for proposals floated. Thirty-five states and Union Territories were on the first of those steps. Being on step one is not the same as having an approved upgrade.

PMKVY targets are approved from agency proposals that are supposed to reflect a skill gap. A state that forwards targets without a district view of who is unemployed and who is already trained will get activity without a location logic. Youth unemployment was 10.2% in 2023-24. That national rate does not tell a district which trade to fund. The apprenticeship portal does tell you which establishments have engaged apprentices. Use it. Optional trades exist so the state is not stuck with a designated list that misses local industry.

Samagra Shiksha and the skill hubs inside schools are the join. More than 7,000 skill hubs were reported inside schools, colleges, and ITIs. A state school secretary and a state skill secretary who do not share the secondary-retention map will place hubs where a proposal was easy, not where the cohort is leaving. The join is an administrative choice. The census already shows where the cohort thins.

Publish the state note with dates and sources: UDISE year, AISHE year, budget estimate versus revised estimate, and the "as on" date of any scheme count. Readers, including the state's own legislature, can then see what moved. A note that only says the state is committed to the National Education Policy does not give them that.
