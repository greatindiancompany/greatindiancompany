The investment implication of India’s logistics programme is narrower than the launch speeches. Money and milestones still sit inside the ministry or state that owns the asset. PM Gati Shakti does not write the cheque.

The [written Lok Sabha reply of 11 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297818) says the master plan is a planning framework. Approval and implementation stay with the relevant central ministry, department, or state, under that body’s own scheme and budget. The reply then says no quantified targets or expenditure requirements have been set under Gati Shakti itself. An investor who underwrites “Gati Shakti capex” as if it were a single vehicle is underwriting a metaphor.

## The only investment table that was printed

The Network Planning Group, which reviews projects for multimodal fit and last-mile gaps before they proceed, had evaluated 396 projects as on that date. Estimated cost: about ₹18.66 lakh crore. Sanctioned: 256. Under implementation: 198. The gap between sanctioned and under implementation is 58 projects. That gap is the live execution question. Estimated cost is the annex’s cost column, not equity drawn and not debt sanctioned by a bank.

The annex rows that parse cleanly are large enough to matter:

| Ministry | Projects evaluated | Sanctioned | Under implementation | Estimated cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Road transport and highways | 171 | 108 | 53 | ₹9,01,668 crore |
| Railways | 162 | 102 | 102 | ₹5,43,315 crore |
| Housing and urban affairs | 31 | 17 | 17 | ₹2,88,614 crore |

Roads are the awkward row. More than half of the sanctioned roads projects in this set were not yet under implementation. Rail, in the same table, shows every sanctioned project as under implementation. A blended “infrastructure is being built” sentence hides that split. The smaller ministries share the rest of the 396. Their annex cells do not line up cleanly enough in the public HTML to cite ministry by ministry. Use the [starred-question PDF](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS327_tYLfn2.pdf) if you need a cell, and do not reconstruct a missing one.

## What a three-year highway or rail figure will and will not support

The starred reply, same day, says that in the three years it is describing the Centre approved 1,115 national highway projects of about 18,493 km, costing about ₹4.75 lakh crore, and constructed 32,370 km of national highways. It sanctioned 237 railway projects spanning 9,703 km — 40 new lines, 17 gauge conversions, 180 doublings — at about ₹1,90,333 crore.

Those are sanction and construction claims for networks, not a freight-yield forecast. A doubling project can raise capacity and still leave the cargo on the road if the siding, the terminal, or the customer’s rack does not exist. The reply says railway surveys for new lines, gauge conversion, and doubling are now taken up under the Gati Shakti framework so that multimodal links are planned with the line. That is a process claim. It is not a modal-shift percentage.

The same reply cites an Indian Institute of Management Bangalore study that every rupee of national-highway spending is associated with a ₹3.2 rise in GDP. Treat that as the study the minister placed on the table. It is not a project IRR, and it is not a logistics-cost elasticity you can drop into a park model.

## Where the asset actually sits

Highway corridors and multi-modal logistics parks under Bharatmala are [roads-ministry documents](https://morth.nic.in/en/bharatmala). Port connectivity programmes are ports-ministry documents. A logistics company’s revenue is a third thing: it depends on whether the asset, once under implementation, changes the shipper’s door-to-door time. The August papers do not price that time.

If you are placing capital, ask three questions of any “Gati Shakti” slide. Which ministry sanctions it? Is it in the 198 under implementation, or only in the 256 sanctioned? What operating series — port tonnes, a railway loading commodity, a warehouse standard — would have to move before your revenue does?
