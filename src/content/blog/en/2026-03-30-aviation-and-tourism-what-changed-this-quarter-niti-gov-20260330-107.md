# What the July and August aviation record changed

The quarter that matters for a founder, a state cell, or an investor is not a slogan about Indian aviation growing. It is the stack of documents the Ministry of Civil Aviation put on the record between the Modified UDAN launch on 4 July 2026 and the Lok Sabha answers of 6 August 2026.

## A new scheme, with a start date

Prime Minister Narendra Modi launched Modified UDAN on 4 July 2026 from Jodhpur. On 16 July, at Vigyan Bhawan, Civil Aviation Minister Ram Mohan Naidu walked airlines, airports, and states through the rule changes. The Press Information Bureau note of that day is the public comparison: viability-gap funding can now run five years instead of three, tapered from year three; airlines without an air operator permit cannot bid; the old cap of 40 subsidised seats is lifted on aircraft with more than 80 seats; and aerodromes are to be prioritised in a challenge mode before airlines design routes around them.

The same week’s Lok Sabha starred question, answered on 6 August, states the money and the target. The scheme runs from 2026-27 to 2035-36 with an outlay of ₹28,840 crore. That includes ₹12,159 crore to develop 100 airports from unserved airstrips and ₹3,661 crore for 200 heliports in hilly, north-eastern, and aspirational areas. The stated aim is 120 new destinations and 4 crore passengers over ten years.

## The old scheme’s score, on two dates in July

Two written replies, both from Minister of State Murlidhar Mohol, stop the habit of quoting a single UDAN total.

On 23 July he told the Lok Sabha that 679 RCS routes had been operationalised since the scheme began, and that 348 were still operational. Viability-gap funding to airlines was ₹807.02 crore in 2023-24, ₹628.42 crore in 2024-25, and ₹738.13 crore in 2025-26, the last figure provisional.

On 30 July, against a date of 10 July 2026, he said those 679 routes connected 95 airports, including 17 heliports and 2 water aerodromes, and had benefited 168 lakh passengers. Flights operated under the scheme stood at 3.58 lakh.

A reader who keeps only “679 routes” has the lifetime start list. A reader who keeps “348 still flying” has the network. Both numbers are his, ten days apart, and they answer different questions.

## Airport capacity, stated as a stock

On 6 August, in the reply on expansion of airport infrastructure, Mohol said expansion and modernisation had raised passenger-handling capacity to over 580 million passengers a year, while traffic handled in 2025-26 was about 420 million. Capital spending by the Airports Authority of India and private airport operators was about ₹19,260 crore in 2023-24, ₹19,317 crore in 2024-25, and ₹17,840 crore in 2025-26. The money came from their own resources. It is not a budget head inside the ₹28,840 crore UDAN outlay.

The same day’s reply on airport development did something equally useful with a long-range claim. The question asked whether traffic would approach 500 million passengers before 2030 and 1 billion by 2047. The answer on the table does not adopt those two figures. It says Indian airports taken together are expected to handle more than 3,000 million passengers by 2046-47.

## What did not change in these papers

The July and August replies do not publish a new DGCA airline-share table, a new foreign-tourist total, or a state-wise list of which 348 routes are the ones still flying. Those sit in other series. The quarter’s aviation record is the scheme restart, the split between routes started and routes still flown, and a capacity figure that sits above the passengers airports actually handled last year.
