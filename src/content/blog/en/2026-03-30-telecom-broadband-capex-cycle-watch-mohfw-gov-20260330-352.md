# Where India's Broadband Capital Is Actually Committed

Capital in Indian broadband is visible in programme design, in the radio network operators have already built, and in the backhaul choice between fibre and microwave. TRAI does not publish a single national private-capex series in the documents used here. This note does not invent one. It marks the commitments that are written down.

## The public fibre design

The Amended BharatNet Programme, approved in August 2023, is the largest written broadband capital plan. PIB's 21 December 2024 note prices the design improvement at Rs 1,39,579 crore. The money is aimed at a specific plant list: ring-topology fibre from block to gram panchayat for 2.64 lakh gram panchayats, fibre onward to about 3.8 lakh non-gram-panchayat villages when demanded, routers for an IP-MPLS network, ten years of operations and maintenance paid against a service-level agreement, power backup at gram panchayats and blocks, and a remote fibre monitoring system at the block. The ten-year maintenance clause means a large share of the figure is operating cost contracted up front, not only cable in the first construction season.

Phases I and II, the network already counted as online, are a separate and smaller sum. The 12 February 2026 PIB reply puts that historic build at nearly 2.14 lakh of 2.56 lakh gram panchayats and an approximate cost of Rs 42,000 crore. Spending still to come is the upgrade to ring topology, the villages beyond the gram panchayat, and the decade of uptime, not a second copy of the first build.

PIB's 13 February 2025 note records two submarine systems already laid: 2,312 km between Chennai and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, and 1,869 km between Kochi and Lakshadweep. Those are sunk public connectivity assets, funded to let 4G and 5G move onto the islands. They are not a template for mainland tower capex.

## Radio already in the ground

The same December 2024 PIB note, speaking as of October 2024, counts 4,62,084 5G base transceiver stations across 779 districts and 24,96,644 4G stations across 783 districts. TRAI's microwave-spectrum recommendations of 10 December 2025 date the introduction of 5G mobile networks to October 2022 and say access providers deployed them primarily in the newly acquired 3500 MHz band. The same recommendations say the quantity of access spectrum in use nearly doubled with that band, and that 5G densification has been heaviest in urban areas.

Subscriber-side evidence that radio capex is still being turned into customers: 5G fixed wireless access reached 13.21 million lines at end-July 2026, up from 12.94 million a month earlier, with a slight rural majority. That is customer-premises equipment and a radio site, not a new inter-city fibre pair.

## Backhaul is the swing item

TRAI's December 2025 microwave paper says operators have invested to fibre-connect towers, and that right-of-way reform, including the 2024 rules, was meant to speed that work. It also says microwave backhaul is faster to deploy and often cheaper where fibre to a new 5G site is slow, difficult, or uneconomic. Urban densification therefore pulls both fibre crews and microwave licences. A capex watch that counts only BharatNet route kilometres will miss the backhaul spectrum bill. A watch that counts only spectrum auctions will miss the ring-fibre programme.

The next large private cash decision is whether the Department of Telecommunications accepts TRAI's 24 February 2026 auction recommendations. Until a notice inviting applications exists, that cash is a recommendation. How to keep it off the public-outlay line is covered in the [investment brief](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/telecom-broadband-investment-implications-mohfw-gov-20260330-232).
