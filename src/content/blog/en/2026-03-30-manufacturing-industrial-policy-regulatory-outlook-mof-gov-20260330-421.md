# Rules that shape manufacturing in India: procurement, quality orders, and FDI

A factory in India can be inside a PLI scheme and still lose a tender, fail a quality order, or need a government-route FDI approval. The regulatory outlook is the stack of those rules, as DPIIT and Parliament have written them. This is not legal advice. It is a map of which document to open.

## Licensing still exists, for a narrow set of cases

DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 says the department handles industrial policy at the Centre, including the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951, grant of industrial licences, and acknowledgement of Industrial Entrepreneur Memoranda. Most new capacity is not a licence case. The report is the right reminder that "delicensing" was never a claim that every product is unregulated. The report is the [DPIIT Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/3d9c9c2daeefb97bb9ce964370938b71.pdf).

## Procurement local content

The Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) Order, 2017, issued on 15 June 2017 and last amended on 19 July 2024, applies to central government procurement of goods, services, and works. The annual report sets out the working rules:

- Imported items bought locally from resellers, and royalties or technical charges paid out of India, are excluded from local content.
- Class-I means at least 50 percent local content. Class-II means at least 20 percent and under 50 percent. A nodal ministry can set a higher floor.
- Unless a global bid is invited, only Class-I and Class-II suppliers are eligible.
- For tenders under Rs. 200 crore, a global tender enquiry needs the approval designated by the Department of Expenditure.
- Where the nodal ministry has notified sufficient local capacity, only Class-I suppliers may bid.
- Purchase preference for the lowest Class-I supplier applies within a 20 percent margin of the lowest bid, for 50 percent of quantity on divisible items and 100 percent on indivisible items.

The report says a PLI manufacturer of an item is treated as a deemed Class-II local supplier unless its local content already meets the Class-I level, subject to a proviso the report states only in part. Bid on the order, not on this summary.

## Quality control orders

For industries in DPIIT's own domain — light engineering, consumer industry, cement, paper, rubber and linoleum, leather and footwear, and explosives — the department issues Quality Control Orders under the BIS Act and, for explosives, the Indian Explosives Act, 1884. The annual report says 83 QCOs covering more than 355 product standards had been issued, plus one explosives QCO covering 15 products. It splits the pace: 5 QCOs for 40 standards from 1987 to 2014, 29 QCOs for 102 standards from 2015 to 2022, and 49 QCOs for 213 standards from 2023 to the time of the report. It also says the department was coming up with more than 30 new QCOs "in this year." That sentence is the report's plan at the time of writing. It is not a count of orders notified in 2026. Check the individual order before you import or stamp a product.

## FDI rules that moved in 2026

The 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf) says most sectors are open to 100 percent FDI under the automatic route, and that more than 90 percent of FDI inflow comes through that route. It then records later limits.

Press Note 3 of 2020 still routes investment through the government if the investor or beneficial owner is from a country that shares a land border with India. The 2026 amendments described in the reply define beneficial ownership by the Prevention of Money Laundering Rules, 2005, and allow non-controlling land-border ownership of up to 10 percent on the automatic route, with reporting to DPIIT. For a list of manufacturing activities — capital goods, electronic components, polysilicon and wafers, advanced battery components, rare-earth processing, and rare-earth permanent magnets — the reply says proposals are to be decided within 60 days, with majority shareholding and control remaining Indian.

Press Note 3 of 2026, dated 23 July 2026, says the restriction on inventory-based e-commerce does not apply to exports of goods manufactured or produced in India. That is an export channel rule. It does not change domestic marketplace inventory rules for goods that are not exports.

The National Single Window System is described in the reply as the online interface for permissions. It does not replace a sectoral PLI application to the administrative ministry.
