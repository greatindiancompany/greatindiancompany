# Food Processing In India: Licence, Schedule 4, And Export Rules

## Two regimes, on purpose

A food plant in India answers to FSSAI for the safety of the food it makes or sells, and it answers to APEDA when it exports a product on APEDA's schedule. MoFPI schemes do not replace either licence. This outlook names the rules those bodies publish. It does not predict the next amendment.

## The food law

The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 consolidated food law and set up FSSAI to lay down science-based standards for articles of food and to regulate their manufacture, storage, distribution, sale, and import. Section 31 requires a licence before a person commences or carries on a food business. Petty manufacturers, retailers, hawkers, and similar businesses can be on a registration path where the regulations say so, without that exception becoming a free pass to ignore safety.

FoSCoS is the portal FSSAI uses for a new licence or registration, for renewal, and for modification. Payments and official mail for that process belong on that portal. A scheme sanction letter from MoFPI is not a food licence.

## Schedule 4 on the floor

FoSTaC trains food safety supervisors against Schedule 4 of the Licensing and Registration Regulations, 2011. The supervisor is appointed by the food business and holds a valid certificate. The published duties include preventing handling risks, supervising handlers, and giving onsite training to all handlers at least quarterly, with a record. An outlook that stops at "get a licence number" misses the continuing duty inside the premises.

## Export rules for scheduled products

APEDA's functions, under its 1985 Act, include registering exporters of scheduled products, fixing standards and specifications for export, improving packaging, and inspecting meat and meat products in slaughterhouses, processing plants, storage, and conveyances. The scheduled list on the about page covers fruits and vegetables and their products, meat, poultry, dairy, confectionery, biscuits and bakery, honey, jaggery and sugar products, cocoa and chocolates, alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages, cereals, groundnuts, peanuts and walnuts, pickles, papads and chutneys, guar gum, floriculture, de-oiled rice bran, green pepper in brine, and cashew nuts and products. Basmati rice is in the Second Schedule. The about page also says a 14 June 2021 DGFT notification placed cashew kernels, cashewnut shell liquid, and cardanol under APEDA.

Horticulture exports are expected to move through recognized pack houses, with facilities for grading, treatment, pre-cooling, cold storage, and quarantine. Organic products for export are tied to the National Programme for Organic Production, and APEDA says it is the secretariat for that programme. The about page also says APEDA monitors sugar imports. That import task is not a processing licence.

Marine products are a PLISFPI manufacturing segment. They are not on the APEDA scheduled list above. A marine exporter should not treat an APEDA fruit pack house, or an APEDA registration, as the marine export registration.
