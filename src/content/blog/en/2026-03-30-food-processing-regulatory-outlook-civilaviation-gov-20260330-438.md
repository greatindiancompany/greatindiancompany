# The rules a food processor has to meet

A Ministry of Food Processing Industries sanction and a Food Safety and Standards Authority of India licence answer different questions. The ministry can pay for a building, a line, or a micro-enterprise machine. FSSAI decides whether the food made there may be sold. The regulatory outlook that matters to an operator is the rulebook already in force, plus the lab and irradiation capacity the ministry has started to fund. It is not a guess about the next amendment.

## The Act and the licence

The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 is the statute. The Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011 are the working rules for who must register or hold a licence. The regulations distinguish registration from a licence, and they distinguish a state licence from a central licence. Manufacturing and processing, including sorting and grading, are listed kinds of business, as are storage, cold storage, packaging, relabelling, and transport. The right form depends on scale and on whether the business runs in more than one state. The rupee thresholds live in those regulations and in later amendments. They should be read on the FSSAI text in force, not copied from an old brochure.

Schedule 4 of the licensing regulations is the hygiene and sanitary standard a licensed business is expected to run. FoSTaC, FSSAI's training and certification programme, exists to put a named person in charge of that schedule. FSSAI's FoSTaC page says every food business with a central or state licence should have at least one trained and certified food safety supervisor for every 25 food handlers, or part of that number, on all its premises. Courses are grouped as basic, advanced, and special. A grant for machinery does not certify the supervisor.

PMFME's September 2025 FAQ treats the FSSAI licence as a document for an existing unit's file, and as something a new unit is not asked to attach on day one. Sale still needs the licence. The project report and the licence are sequential, not optional alternatives.

## Labels, including food sold online

The Food Safety and Standards (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020, in the compilation FSSAI has posted, require every pre-packaged food to carry the information those regulations list, unless a specific exemption applies. The particulars must be in English or Hindi in Devanagari script. Another language may be added if it does not contradict the English or Hindi. A label may not describe the food in a way that is false, misleading, or deceptive.

When the product is sold through e-commerce or another direct channel, the mandatory label information has to reach the buyer before the sale, except batch or lot number, best-before, use-by, expiry, and the date of manufacture or packing. Every food business must also display its registration or licence number, or a Food Safety Display Board where specified, at a prominent place on premises where food is stored, processed, distributed, or sold. Fortified and organic foods carry the marks those regulations specify.

For a small brand moving from a shop counter to a marketplace, the e-commerce clause is the piece that changes the artwork and the product page together. The marketplace listing is part of the label duty.

## Labs and irradiation are capacity, not a new offence

The regulatory system is only as usable as the labs that test against it. The Cabinet decision of 31 July 2025, as summarised in the ministry's Year End Review of 9 January 2026, includes Rs 1,000 crore inside PMKSY for 50 multi-product food irradiation units and 100 food testing labs with NABL accreditation. The review says 14 irradiation proposals were approved in 2025-26. It also records an expression of interest for the testing labs. Dates in that paragraph of the release do not line up with each other, so this brief does not treat any single closing date from that sentence as a deadline. The decision to fund the labs is the part that is clear.

FSSAI used World Food India 2025, held from 25 to 28 September 2025, to run a Global Food Regulators Summit beside the trade show. That is a convening of regulators. It is not, by itself, a new regulation.

Processed food items remain outside industrial licensing under the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951, which the ministry lists among its long-standing policy choices. Freedom from that industrial licence is not freedom from the Food Safety and Standards Act.

## What to watch without inventing a reform calendar

Through 2026-27 the live regulatory work for most firms is still licence status, Schedule 4, a FoSTaC supervisor ratio, and a label that survives an online listing. The new public money is aimed at testing labs and irradiation, which change how quickly a product can be checked and how some commodities can be treated. Until FSSAI notifies a specific amendment, a budget line is not a change in the rule.
