# How Child Nutrition Outcomes Differ Across Indian States

India’s stunting rate of 29.3% in NFHS-6 is an average across a wide state range. The May 2026 fact-sheet compendium from the International Institute for Population Sciences, released with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, prints urban, rural, and total columns for each state and Union territory it covers, beside the NFHS-5 total. Manipur is not in that compendium. District sheets followed on 20 and 21 August 2026 and are a separate download. The figures below are state totals from the May compendium, for children under five, unless a rural or urban cell is named.

## Stunting fell in the large states, and the level still splits them

The India total moved from 35.5% to 29.3%, with rural children at 30.9% and urban children at 23.9%. Among the state sheets, the higher totals include Meghalaya at 36.8% (from 46.5%), Bihar at 35.6% (from 42.9%), Gujarat at 35.3% (from 39.0%), and Jharkhand at 35.0% (from 39.6%). Uttar Pradesh is 31.5% (from 39.7%). Madhya Pradesh is 31.4% (from 35.7%). Maharashtra is 29.5% (from 35.2%). Rajasthan is 29.6% (from 31.8%), a smaller decline than the Hindi-heartland states with a higher NFHS-5 base.

Lower totals include NCT of Delhi at 16.6% (from 20.0%), Goa at 19.4% (from 25.8%), Uttarakhand at 20.0% (from 27.0%), Kerala at 20.1% (from 23.4%), Sikkim at 20.1% (from 22.3%), Punjab at 20.4% (from 24.5%), Himachal Pradesh at 20.6% (from 30.8%), and Tamil Nadu at 20.7% (from 25.0%). West Bengal is 22.4% (from 33.8%). Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and West Bengal show large declines from NFHS-5 and now sit near Kerala. Bihar’s decline is also large, and Bihar remains among the highest sheets in the compendium.

Two published exceptions moved up. Andaman and Nicobar Islands went from 22.5% to 26.7%. Ladakh went from 32.0% to 34.1%. They are small populations next to Uttar Pradesh or Bihar. They still falsify a sentence that says every geography improved.

Rural-urban gaps inside one state can exceed the India gap. Gujarat’s rural stunting is 39.5% and its urban rate is 28.2%. Jharkhand’s rural rate is 37.4% and its urban rate is 20.3%. A state average that blends those cells will send a programme to the wrong blocks.

## Wasting and underweight do not follow stunting

India’s wasting rate is almost unchanged, 19.0% against 19.3%. That national flat line hides opposite state moves. Bihar’s wasting fell from 22.9% to 19.0%, in line with its stunting decline. Kerala’s wasting is 10.9%, from 15.8%. Madhya Pradesh’s wasting rose from 18.9% to 23.8%, and its underweight rate rose from 33.0% to 39.7%, even while its stunting fell from 35.7% to 31.4%. Uttar Pradesh wasting rose from 17.3% to 19.2%, and underweight from 32.1% to 34.5%, against a stunting decline from 39.7% to 31.5%.

A state review that tracks only stunting will congratulate Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh for a real height-for-age gain and miss a worse weight picture in the same fact sheet. India’s own underweight rate barely moved, from 32.1% to 31.8%, with rural children at 33.8%. Bihar’s underweight rate did move, from 41.0% to 35.7%. Kerala’s is 17.8%, from 19.7%.

## How to use the sheets without over-reading them

Each state page is a sample survey for 2023-24, not a Poshan Tracker extract and not a ranking the ministry has certified as a league. Parentheses on a fact sheet, where they appear, mark a rate based on a small number of cases. This note does not quote those parenthetical cells. District compendiums are the right next cut for a block-level decision, and IIPS directs readers to its publication section, with guest login, rather than to a single open spreadsheet.

Programme response still runs through the state. Mission Poshan 2.0 leaves day-to-day implementation with state governments. A national hot-cooked-meal or take-home-ration design that ignores a state where wasting rose will spend against the wrong problem.
