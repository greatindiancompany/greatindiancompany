A comparison of India's cybersecurity resilience with another country is one control, one year, and one publisher. A country rank that mixes a survey, an index, and a statute is not a comparison. This page prints no rank and no score.

## What can sit on the same line

Put two countries on one line only when all four match:

- **The control.** Incident-report timeliness, a security test before network connection, a trusted-source rule for telecom equipment, or log retention. Not "cyber maturity."
- **The unit.** Hours, share of new elements certified, or findings still open. Not a colour on a chart.
- **The year.** The same twelve months. A 2013 policy next to a later statute is two documents, not a trend.
- **The publisher.** One office wrote both numbers. An Indian licence condition and a foreign vendor survey do not share a publisher.

India's telecom slice has instruments you can open: telecom cyber security rules under the Telecommunications Act, 2023, trusted-source rules for equipment, and security testing through the National Centre for Communication Security. The Department of Telecommunications site is [dot.gov.in](https://www.dot.gov.in/). Another country's telecom regulator is the matching shelf. A global index, if you use one, stays in its own table. Do not average it with a rule.

L.E.K. insights were paired with an earlier draft of this file. A consulting index is not a second country. It is omitted.

## What usually gets compared by mistake

CERT-In is India's national incident-response agency under the Information Technology Act. It is not the Department of Telecommunications, and it is not a stand-in for another country's telecom ministry. Critical-information-infrastructure protection is a further office. Banks, market infrastructure, and insurers follow their own regulators. A single "India versus peers" sentence that adds those shelves together is not a benchmark.
