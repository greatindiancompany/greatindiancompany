# India's cyber clocks, and the foreign links CERT-In has signed

The primary Indian documents used here do not publish a rank for India on a global cybersecurity index. A comparison that invents one is not a reading of those documents. Two comparisons are in the record: India's own reporting clocks differ from each other, and CERT-In has named the foreign agencies it worked with in 2023.

## Three Indian clocks for one kind of bad day

A body corporate under the CERT-In directions of 28 April 2022 reports specified cyber incidents within six hours of noticing them or being brought to notice.

A telecommunication entity under rule 7 of the Telecommunications (Telecom Cyber Security) Rules, 2024 reports a security incident affecting its network or service to the Central Government within six hours, with a description of the affected system. Within twenty-four hours it furnishes, as applicable, users affected, duration, geography, functional impact, remedial measures, and any other information it considers relevant. The Central Government may then inform the public, or require the entity to do so, if it decides disclosure is in the public interest.

A data fiduciary, once DPDP Rule 7 is in force, intimates each affected person without delay, in plain language, through the user account or a registered mode of communication. It also intimates the Data Protection Board without delay with a description of nature, extent, timing, location and likely impact, and within seventy-two hours — or longer if the Board allows on a written request — it sends the detailed account, including findings about who caused the breach if it has them, remedial measures, and a report of the intimations already given to people.

These clocks answer different questions. Six hours to CERT-In is an incident-response notice. Twenty-four hours in the telecom rules is a fuller operational picture. Seventy-two hours to the Board is a detailed personal-data account after an immediate intimation. A group that runs a telecom network, holds customer data, and is also a body corporate can owe all three. Copying a single "72-hour breach policy" from a foreign template will miss the six-hour duties already in force.

Log location is part of the same comparison inside India. CERT-In requires the 180-day ICT logs to be maintained within Indian jurisdiction. DPDP Rule 6, when commenced, sets a one-year floor for specified logs and personal data unless another law requires otherwise. A foreign parent company's global log store does not, by itself, meet the jurisdiction line in the CERT-In direction.

## Where a foreign framework is incorporated by an Indian regulator

IRDAI's 2023 guidelines say the applicability of the NIST framework to all regulated entities is provided in Annexure I of those guidelines. That is an Indian regulator pointing insurers and intermediaries at a named external framework. It is not a score, and the annexure is the document to read for how IRDAI uses it.

SEBI's CSCRF is an Indian framework that superseded SEBI's own earlier cyber circulars. It is not written as a country ranking.

## Cooperation CERT-In recorded, by name

In 2023 CERT-In signed a bilateral cybersecurity agreement with the Egyptian Computer Emergency Readiness Team and renewed agreements with the Information System Authority of Estonia and the National Cyber Security Centre of the United Kingdom, for information sharing and incident resolution.

It convened the APCERT Internet of Things security technical working group and, as a member of the APCERT drill working group, contributed to the design and execution of the APCERT drill that year. It took part as a player in four international drills and contributed to planning and scenario development for three international exercises.

It ran a G20 cybersecurity exercise and drill on 31 January 2023 for 400 participants, including people from more than 12 countries online, and a banking-sector exercise with the Reserve Bank on 5 June 2023 for more than 200 participants from the IMF, the Bank for International Settlements, central banks and CERTs of G20 members, plus Indian and foreign bank executives. From 2 to 4 October 2023 it ran Cyber-Maitree in Dhaka for organisations of the Government of Bangladesh.

CERT-In is a CVE Numbering Authority, partnered with the CVE Program, for vulnerabilities in products designed, developed and manufactured in India. That is a role on a global vulnerability list, limited to that product scope.

Use those names and clocks. Skip any league table these documents do not contain.
