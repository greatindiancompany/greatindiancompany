# What policy teams should protect in India's payments system

India's financial digitalization policy is no longer a slogan about cashless payments. It is a set of choices that are already written down: an interoperable payment rail, a rule that only regulated entities lend, a consent artefact that does not leave a copy with the intermediary, and a central-bank token designed to behave like cash. A policy team protects those choices by publishing the boundary of each one. It does not help the public by treating them as a single app.

## Interoperability, and the institution that makes it possible

UPI works because a person can use one participating app to reach another bank. That is the design MeitY states on the [Digital India page](https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/initiative/unified-payment-interface-upi/), and it is why the Press Information Bureau's April 2026 note can talk about inclusion and speed in the same breath. The August 2026 factsheet puts the scale at 741 banks and 2,365.8 crore transactions in July 2026. The April note puts January 2026 at 21.70 billion transactions and says banks on the network rose from 216 in 2021 to 691 by that month.

The policy tension is concentration of the switch beside competition at the edge. Many apps, one system operator. NPCI's [product statistics](https://www.npci.org.in/product/upi/product-statistics), including uptime and incident disclosures, are the public window on that operator. A policy team that celebrates interoperability should also be able to point to where an outage would be reported. Extending acceptance abroad, which the August factsheet counts as 11 countries, is a corridor policy. It should not be described as if those corridors were inside the domestic July total.

Inclusion tools are part of the same choice. The August factsheet records UPI 123PAY for feature phones and UPI Lite for small values, with the limits it states for those products. The April note places this on the JAM base of Jan-Dhan, Aadhaar, and mobile, with direct benefit transfer as the practice that made account credits familiar. Protecting inclusion means keeping a path that does not assume a new smartphone, without pretending that a feature-phone product has a published state-wise user count. It does not, in these notes.

## Conduct rules are the consumer-protection half

Scale without conduct rules was the problem the Reserve Bank named in August 2022 and then wrote into the [Digital Lending Directions, 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12848). The principle to protect is simple enough to say in public: the app is not the lender, the money moves between the borrower and the regulated entity, and a filing of digital lending apps on the Centralised Information Management System is not a Reserve Bank endorsement. The directions say the Reserve Bank does not verify that filing. A directory that citizens treat as a licence will undo the disclosure. Policy communication should repeat the non-endorsement as often as it repeats the existence of the list.

Two further protections are easy to blur. Default loss guarantee is capped at five per cent of the amount disbursed from a fixed pool, so a platform guarantee is not deposit insurance and not a full risk transfer. Authentication, under the [directions of 25 September 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12898), requires two distinct factors for domestic digital payments unless an exemption applies, with the issuer compensating the customer if it fails that duty. The compliance date was 1 April 2026. Card issuers still have cross-border card-not-present work due on 1 October 2026. A policy line that says "UPI is safe" is incomplete if it skips the factor rule and the issuer's liability.

Account aggregators are the data protection model already in financial law, updated as on 6 September 2024 in the [2016 directions](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=10598). Explicit consent, no retention of the financial information, no access to login credentials, revocation that works. The Digital Rupee, in the [concept note](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationReportDetails.aspx?ID=1218) and the retail pilot, is a separate public good: a token with no interest, issued and redeemed by the Reserve Bank, distributed by banks. Policy teams should resist two mergers. Do not merge e₹ with bank deposits. Do not merge an industry credit protocol with a licence. The Open Credit Enablement Network can carry messages. It cannot decide who is allowed to lend.
