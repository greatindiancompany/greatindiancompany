## The change that matters

India's trade corridors are no longer a single announcement. The official record now has an operating east-west freight railway, a budget proposal for a third dedicated corridor, and a separate logistics-policy layer that does not build track. A reader who treats those as one project will misread both capital and timing.

## Three dated statements

On 19 March 2025, the Ministry of Railways told the Lok Sabha that the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor runs from Ludhiana to Sonnagar (1,337 km) and the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor runs from Jawaharlal Nehru Port (JNPT) to Dadri (1,506 km). Of the combined 2,843 km, 2,741 route km (96.4 percent) were commissioned and operational, and work on the balance section had been taken up. The same reply said detailed project reports for three further corridors were under examination and that none of those three had been sanctioned.

On 1 February 2026, the Union Budget proposed a dedicated freight corridor connecting Dankuni in the east with Surat in the west. The same day's ministerial briefing described a 2,052 km alignment through Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, and Maharashtra, integration with the Western corridor, and nearly ₹1.20 lakh crore. It also said the existing corridors were handling around 400 freight trains a day.

On 7 August 2026, the Railways Minister told the Rajya Sabha that both the Eastern corridor (1,337 km) and the Western corridor (1,506 km) have been commissioned, and that about 443 trains a day were being operated on them. The reply says the Dankuni–Surat corridor was announced in the Union Budget 2026 and that preparation and updation of its detailed project report has been taken up. DFCCIL, the corporation that builds and operates the corridors, records trial freight runs on the JNPT–New Saphale (Vaitarna) section on 31 March 2026 as the point at which the Western corridor was completed.

The March 2025 gap and the August 2026 commissioning statement sit on the same two corridors. The Dankuni–Surat line is a later proposal. It is not the sanction of the three reports that were still under examination in March 2025. Those reports covered an East Coast corridor from Kharagpur to Vijayawada, an East-West alignment via Palghar, Bhusawal, Nagpur, Kharagpur, and Dankuni (plus Rajkharsawan–Kalipahari–Andal), and a North-South sub-corridor from Vijayawada through Nagpur to Itarsi.

## What moved with the trains

The August 2026 reply also resets the terminal count. Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals stood at 142 commissioned, with an estimated handling capacity of 224 million tonnes a year, about ₹10,000 crore of private investment, and 146 million tonnes handled in 2025-26. In March 2025 the same policy was described with 97 commissioned terminals and in-principle approval for 277 proposals. Use the later count for the present stock, and the earlier count only as the prior official mark.

Indian Railways' freight loading is given in that August reply as 1,670 million tonnes in 2025-26, and the minister described Indian Railways as the second largest freight-carrying railway. That is system-wide loading. It is not a tonne figure for the dedicated corridors alone.

## What this quarter does not settle

The August reply says the dedicated corridors enable double-stack container trains, higher axle-load trains, faster access from western ports to the northern hinterland, and new terminals along the corridor. Those are stated effects of the project design. The reply does not publish a corridor-only logistics-cost percentage. A separate DPIIT exercise with NCAER, described on 16 September 2025 as being in its final stages, is the official cost study still to be read when it is released.

## How to use the sequence

Founders and operators can plan against commissioned Eastern and Western track, and against terminals that already handle freight. Investors can separate that operating base from Dankuni–Surat, which is in detailed-project-report work. Policy teams can keep the March 2025 "not yet sanctioned" sentence attached to the three older reports until a later Railways reply retires it.
