A scenario map is useful when each branch starts from a number the government has already published, and when the branch ends in a document that would show the slip. What follows is that kind of map for 2026-27. It does not assign probabilities. The fiscal statement does not either.

## The base the other cases move from

The base case written into the Budget is a fiscal deficit of 4.3 percent of GDP (₹16.96 lakh crore), a revenue deficit of 1.5 percent, Central debt of 55.6 percent, and nominal GDP growth of 10.0 percent over the first advance estimates of 2025-26. Gross tax revenue is ₹44.04 lakh crore. Non-tax revenue is ₹6.66 lakh crore. Capital expenditure is ₹12.22 lakh crore. Interest is ₹14.04 lakh crore.

The statement says the previous consolidation, from 6.7 percent of GDP in 2021-22 to 4.4 percent in the 2025-26 revised estimates, came from keeping receipts on the budgeted path and rationalising expenditure without dropping the growth focus. That is the mechanism. A slip is a failure of one of those two, or of the GDP base under the ratio.

## Branches that are already named

**Nominal GDP below 10 percent.** The first advance estimates cited in the same papers put nominal growth in 2025-26 at 8 percent. The Budget steps up to 10 percent. If the outcome is closer to the recent estimate than to the assumption, debt and deficit ratios rise even when rupee totals are met. This shows up in the next revised estimates, not in a single monthly account.

**Tax revenue short of ₹44.04 lakh crore.** Direct taxes are 61.2 percent of the gross tax estimate. Indirect taxes are ₹17.07 lakh crore, and CGST alone is ₹10.19 lakh crore, up 6.3 percent on the revised estimates. The new Income-tax Act is described as carrying no rate change. A miss is then a miss on incomes or on GST collections and compliance, including the effect of the 2025 rate rationalisation. The GST monthly report is the early warning for the indirect piece. The Controller General’s net-tax row is the warning for what the Centre keeps after devolution.

**Non-tax and disinvestment depending on conditions.** Non-tax revenue in the first quarter was already 56.7 percent of its estimate, but that pace was slower than the previous year’s 64.0 percent. Other capital receipts are estimated at ₹80,000 crore and, the statement says, depend on market conditions. June had booked ₹24,941 crore. A weak second half on this line widens the deficit unless spending is cut.

**Interest rates.** Interest estimates “depend on the prevailing domestic and international interest rates.” The 2025-26 programme, through 27 January 2026, cleared at a weighted average yield of about 6.65 percent. A higher clearing yield does not change this year’s stock of debt overnight. It raises the cost of the ₹17.20 lakh crore gross dated programme and of the bills that roll. The interest row in the monthly accounts, against ₹14,03,972 crore, is the place the cash cost appears.

**Subsidies.** June had already charged ₹53,034 crore of urea subsidy, 45 percent of the estimate, and ₹1,14,812 crore of major subsidies in total, 28 percent of the estimate. Food and fertiliser move with procurement and import prices. The Budget’s ₹4.11 lakh crore major-subsidy estimate is the cap written down. A commodity spike shows up as a higher share of that estimate earlier in the year.

**State capital loans and state deficits.** ₹2.0 lakh crore of the Centre’s capital line is SASCI support. If states cannot ground the projects, the Centre can still book a loan while the asset lags. Separately, the Reserve Bank’s January 2026 study said sixteen states had budgeted deficits above 3 percent of their own product, and that elevated debt and contingent liabilities remain the state-level vulnerability even where interest-free central loans have eased debt service. A state stress case is not a Union default case. It is a case where the consolidated public sector borrows more, or spends the Centre’s capital support more slowly, than the Union speech implies.

**The global setting the statement already used to withhold targets.** The deviation note cites uneven global growth, shifting trade policy, and geopolitical risk. The framework names high tariffs on exports to the United States as a risk to sentiment, capital inflows, and the currency. None of those is given a rupee value. The observable fiscal response the statement chose was to skip rolling deficit targets for two years. If that risk shows up in taxes, it will show up in the gross tax row first.

## What would not, by itself, be a slip

A fiscal deficit in September that is not equal to half of 4.3 percent. The accounts controller says the in-year figure is mistimed. June was 18.2 percent of the estimate, in line with 17.9 percent a year earlier, while the revenue deficit was only 0.4 percent of its estimate. That pattern can reverse by December without the annual target having moved.

A debt ratio quoted at 55.1 percent instead of 55.6 percent. The lower figure excludes NSSF securities that are liabilities of the states. Switching definitions is not consolidation.

The map is a set of official tripwires. The next revised estimates are the scoreboard the Budget itself will have to meet.
