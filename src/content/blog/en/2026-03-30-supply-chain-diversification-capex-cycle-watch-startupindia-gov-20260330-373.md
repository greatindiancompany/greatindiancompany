# Watching the capex cycle in India's manufacturing trade diversification

The most misread number in India's manufacturing diversification story is Rs 1.97 lakh crore. That figure, in the Press Information Bureau note of 2 August 2023, is the incentive outlay for Production Linked Incentive schemes across 14 sectors. It is not a census of machines ordered, sheds built, or vendor tools bought. A capex watcher who books the outlay as expenditure will see a boom the documents have not recorded.

## Three different piles of money

Keep three piles apart.

The first is the incentive envelope. The 2 August 2023 Lok Sabha reply says the schemes were announced to draw investment and technology, to build scale, and to support manufacturing and exports. Incentives of that design are paid against performance rules set by each implementing ministry. Until a later official note reports claims paid, the outlay is capacity for incentive, not capex incurred.

The second is the applicant's own capex. The same reply says the schemes should have a cascading effect: anchor units set a supplier base, and most ancillary units are expected in the MSME sector. At that date, 733 applications had been selected and 176 of the beneficiaries were MSMEs, in bulk drugs, medical devices, pharma, telecom, white goods, food processing, textiles, and drones. Selection is a gate. It is evidence that firms asked to be inside the scheme. It is not a commissioning certificate.

The third is public capex around the plant: corridors, parks, and the mineral chain. PIB's 27 November 2025 note describes NICDC plug-and-play parks and freight-corridor work, including World Bank-linked financing it states as USD 1.96 billion for the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor and rail logistics, and USD 375 million for Ganga waterway development. Those are infrastructure spends around manufacturing, not PLI disbursements. The National Critical Mineral Mission, approved by the Union Cabinet on 29 January 2025, has its own provisions: Rs 500 crore for critical-mineral processing parks and Rs 1,500 crore for recycling, as stated in the PIB note of 24 March 2025. That is input-side capex, and it is a budget provision, not a park already producing cathode material.

## Where the cycle is heavy

Not every PLI sector is equally capex-heavy. Advanced chemistry cell batteries, high-efficiency solar PV modules, specialty steel, and electronics components sit closer to a multi-year asset cycle than a cut-and-sew line. The official list puts them in the same 14-sector frame as food products and textiles. A capex dashboard that averages them will miss the cycle. Watch the capital-heavy sectors for land, power, and vendor-tool orders, and watch the labour-heavy sectors for utilization and export orders.

The 2023 reply also said schemes were at various stages of implementation and that the Cabinet had not approved new sectors as of that day. A cycle can stall because a sector's guidelines are still being executed, even while the national outlay looks large. Check the latest PIB note before assuming the sector list is frozen, and before assuming it has expanded.

## What to put on a quarterly capex page

Four lines, each with a date: incentive outlay (Rs 1.97 lakh crore as stated in August 2023, until a newer note replaces it); selected applicants versus any later figure for investment claimed or incentive released; public corridor and park projects you can tie to a PIB or DPIIT note; and the mineral-processing provision if your bill of materials depends on lithium, cobalt, or similar inputs. Leave a blank rather than fill it with a forecast.
