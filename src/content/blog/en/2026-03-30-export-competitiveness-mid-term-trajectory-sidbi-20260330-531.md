# The medium-term path of India’s goods and services trade

The medium term in India’s trade account is visible in two Reserve Bank vintages and one customs cumulative. It is not a straight line from a slogan.

## The year that has a full BoP print

On 8 June 2026 the RBI published preliminary figures for 2025–26. The current account deficit was US$ 25.2 billion, 0.6 percent of GDP, against US$ 22.9 billion, also 0.6 percent of GDP, in 2024–25. The ratio did not widen. The dollar deficit did, slightly.

Goods exports on that account were US$ 446.1 billion and goods imports US$ 783.4 billion, a deficit of US$ 337.3 billion. A year earlier the goods deficit was US$ 286.9 billion. Services exports were US$ 421.3 billion and services imports US$ 204.7 billion, a surplus of US$ 216.6 billion, up from US$ 188.8 billion. Net invisibles, which combine services, primary income, and secondary income, were US$ 312.0 billion, against US$ 264.0 billion. The Bank attributed the rise mainly to net services and net personal transfers.

The path inside that year was not smooth. The January–March 2026 quarter was a current account surplus of US$ 7.1 billion, 0.7 percent of GDP, smaller than the US$ 13.7 billion surplus a year earlier. The goods deficit in that quarter was US$ 83.4 billion, against US$ 59.3 billion. Net services were US$ 60.4 billion, against US$ 53.3 billion. A year can show a small current account deficit while one quarter is in surplus and the goods gap is still large.

## The next quarter widened the goods gap again

April–June 2026–27, published on 1 September 2026, showed a goods deficit of US$ 86.1 billion, against US$ 68.9 billion a year earlier, and net services of US$ 51.6 billion, against US$ 47.9 billion. The current account deficit was US$ 4.2 billion, 0.5 percent of GDP. Services are still the offset. They are not yet the whole external account. Goods credits in that quarter were US$ 132.0 billion. Services credits were US$ 106.2 billion. On a BoP basis, merchandise exports remained the larger credit line in the quarter, and the much larger debit.

The customs quick estimate is the higher-frequency check, not a replacement. The 15 September 2026 DGCIS annex puts April–August 2026–27 merchandise exports at US$ 215,912 million, up 17.8 percent from US$ 183,212 million. That is five months of customs value. It will be revised, and it will not equal the BoP goods credit when the July–September account is published.

## An aim stated in one year is not the policy target for the next

FTP 2023 was written as an open-ended policy, not as a five-year export number. Launch remarks on 31 March 2023 referred to overall exports crossing US$ 750 billion and an expectation of US$ 760 billion that year. Those sentences are 2023. Later backgrounders have talked about larger aims for later years. Quoting one of them as “the” medium-term target, then judging it by adding BoP goods credits to BoP services credits, mixes a political aim with an accounting identity the aim may not have used.

The trajectory that the accounts themselves support, through the June 2026 year and the September 2026 quarter, is this: the goods deficit is still the wide line; the services surplus and secondary income keep the current account deficit near a small share of GDP; customs merchandise exports in the first five months of 2026–27 are above the previous year. Anything tighter than that is a forecast, and this brief does not make one.
