# How Climate Adaptation Differs Across Indian States

States are not a scoreboard. They are different hazard geographies using two different public objects: a plan, and sometimes a project file.

## Two counts that are not the same map

On 21 July 2025, the environment ministry told the Lok Sabha, through the Press Information Bureau, that 34 States and Union Territories have prepared State Action Plans on Climate Change, in line with the national plan, "to address State specific actions." The plans are posted on the ministry's [state action plan page](https://moef.gov.in/state-action-plan-on-climate-change). The reply does not rank them.

On 5 August 2024, PIB reported adaptation-fund projects sanctioned in 27 States and Union Territories. A state can have a plan and still be absent from that project count. The ministry's [list of approved NAFCC documents](https://moef.gov.in/index.php/list-of-approvals-projects-under-nafcc-concept-note-and-project-documents-details-dpr) is organised by state, from the Himalaya to the coast. Open the file. Do not substitute a private index.

## The hazard is why the plans diverge

The national missions set a common frame. State-specific actions are what the July 2025 reply says the plans are for. The National Disaster Management Authority explains the physical split. Heat guidance follows IMD's tests, which are written for plains, coasts, and hills separately, and which the IMD heat FAQ locates mainly over northwest, central, east, and north peninsular plains from March to June. Flood guidelines describe monsoon riverine flooding: the authority's flood page says 80 percent of precipitation falls from June to September. Urban flooding has its own September 2010 guideline because city drainage is not a river flood. Cyclone guidelines address the coast. Glacial-lake outburst flood guidelines, dated October 2020, belong with the Himalayan system, which is also a national mission.

## The coast has an extra line

A PIB release of 3 February 2022 says the hazard line has been demarcated for the entire mainland coast, and it names adaptation-fund work in Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Andhra Pradesh. A hill state's plan is not organised around that line. Averaging "state performance" into one number erases the difference the ministry and the disaster authority both preserve.

## How to read one state

Pick the state. Read its plan on the ministry page. See whether an adaptation-fund file exists. Then match the hazard guideline to the geography: heat, river flood, urban flood, drought, cyclone, or a glacial lake.
