Households do not place money in the standing deposit facility. Banks do, at 5.00 per cent. What a household sees is the carded deposit rate, the rate on a loan that has already been booked, and the currency notes that leave or re-enter the banking system. The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 18 September 2026 and the Governor’s statement of 5 August 2026 publish those channels separately from the dealing-room surplus.

## The card rates in the latest week

For the week ended 11 September 2026 the supplement reports a savings deposit rate of 2.50 per cent. Term deposits of more than one year are reported in a range of 6.00 to 6.75 per cent. The base rate range is 8.40 to 10.00 per cent. Overnight MCLR is 7.80 to 8.00 per cent. The policy repo rate on that page is 5.25 per cent, the standing deposit facility is 5.00 per cent, and the marginal standing facility is 5.50 per cent.

The year-ago column, the week ended 12 September 2025, shows the savings rate already at 2.50 per cent. Term deposits above one year were then 5.85 to 6.60 per cent. The base rate was 8.50 to 10.30 per cent. Overnight MCLR was already 7.80 to 8.00 per cent. Over the year the policy rate fell from 5.50 to 5.25 per cent, the bottom of the published term-deposit range rose from 5.85 to 6.00 per cent, and the top rose from 6.60 to 6.75 per cent. The savings rate did not move.

CPI inflation was 4.4 per cent in June 2026, the August resolution says, after sixteen months below the 4 per cent target. A savings rate of 2.50 per cent and a June CPI print of 4.4 per cent are both published. A balance left in a savings account through that month did not match the price rise the index recorded. The Committee projects CPI inflation for 2026–27 at 5.0 per cent, with the third quarter at 5.9 per cent. That projection is the Bank’s, not a promised deposit rate.

## How much of the rate cut reached deposits and loans

The Governor’s statement of 5 August, in the Bulletin dated 25 August, measures pass-through from February 2025 to June 2026 after a cumulative 125 basis point cut in the repo rate. The weighted average domestic term deposit rate fell 63 basis points on fresh deposits and 51 basis points on outstanding deposits. The weighted average lending rate fell 80 basis points on fresh rupee loans and 91 basis points on outstanding rupee loans. The statement says fresh lending transmission moderated in recent months because credit demand was robust.

The June statement had measured the same averages through April 2026: fresh term deposit rates down 85 basis points, outstanding term deposit rates down 50, fresh lending rates down 83, outstanding lending rates down 89. Households opening a new deposit after April were looking at a cumulative decline that, by the August footnote, had narrowed. Households with an older outstanding loan were looking at a cumulative decline that had widened, from 89 basis points through April to 91 through June. The two sides of the household balance sheet did not receive the hold in the policy rate in the same way.

The 10-year government security par yield in the week ended 11 September 2026 was 7.05 per cent, against 6.57 per cent in the week ended 12 September 2025. The published term-deposit range of 6.00 to 6.75 per cent sits under that par yield and over the 5.25 per cent repo rate. The supplement reports the yields. It does not report how many households switched from deposits to small savings or to mutual funds.

## Currency is the household instrument that changes liquidity directly

Notes in circulation are a household and firm choice that drains or feeds bank reserves. The Bulletin extract dated 11 September 2026 puts currency with the public at ₹41,94,110 crore as on 31 August. Over the fortnight that stock fell by ₹13,005 crore, or 0.3 per cent. Over the year it rose by ₹4,75,121 crore, or 12.8 per cent. In 2026–27 so far it was up ₹1,28,622 crore, or 3.2 per cent.

Time deposits with banks, in the same money-stock table, were ₹2,53,01,766 crore as on 31 August, up 17.1 per cent year on year and up 8.1 per cent in the financial year so far. Demand deposits were ₹36,02,950 crore, up 18.7 per cent year on year and down 6.3 per cent in the financial year so far. The year’s addition has been in time deposits and in currency, while demand deposits are lower than at the end of March.

The Governor’s August statement treats the monsoon months as a season in which currency usually returns to banks and adds to system liquidity, along with a drawdown of government cash balances. The fortnight to 31 August is one published decline in currency with the public, of ₹13,005 crore, inside a year in which that stock is still 12.8 per cent higher. A family putting festival cash back into a deposit does the thing the statement describes. The rate that deposit earns is the card on the supplement, not the 5.00 per cent the bank can earn overnight at the standing deposit facility.

The call rate for the week ended 11 September averaged 4.97 per cent, under the standing deposit facility rate and above the 2.50 per cent savings card on the same page. Term deposits were offered from 6.00 per cent. Households cannot place funds in the call market. The surplus shows up in the wholesale print. The household offer is the carded range.
