The liquidity story of 2026 is a corridor that stopped moving, a reserve requirement that finished a cut announced in 2025, and an overnight rate that in the latest published week traded through the floor. The Monetary Policy Committee has left the policy repo rate at 5.25 per cent at every meeting this calendar year. The operating machinery around that rate is the revision the Reserve Bank finalised on 30 September 2025.

## The rate has been held since December

On 5 December 2025 the Committee cut the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.25 per cent and kept a neutral stance. The standing deposit facility moved with it to 5.00 per cent. The marginal standing facility rate and the Bank Rate moved to 5.50 per cent. One member, Prof. Ram Singh, preferred an accommodative stance. The resolution that was adopted stayed neutral.

The four meetings since then left that set of rates where it was. The resolutions of 4–6 February, 6–8 April, 3–5 June and 3–5 August 2026 each record a unanimous hold at 5.25 per cent, with the same 5.00 and 5.50 bounds, and the same neutral stance. The August resolution is the 62nd meeting of the Committee. It projects real GDP growth for 2026–27 at 6.7 per cent and CPI inflation for the year at 5.0 per cent, with inflation peaking in the third quarter. The resolution set 19 August 2026 as the publication date for the minutes. The next meeting is scheduled for 5–7 October 2026.

The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 18 September 2026 still shows those policy rates for the week ended 11 September, and it shows the fixed reverse repo rate unchanged at 3.35 per cent. Under the 30 September 2025 framework the floor of the corridor is the standing deposit facility, 25 basis points below the repo, and the ceiling is the marginal standing facility, 25 basis points above it. The fixed reverse repo rate remains on the statistical page. It is not the bound the framework names.

## The cash reserve ratio completed its path to 3 per cent

The cash reserve ratio did change, on a timetable set before this year. The notification of 6 June 2025, RBI/2025-26/46, reduced the ratio by 100 basis points in four equal tranches of 25 basis points, to 3.0 per cent of net demand and time liabilities. Banks were required to maintain 3.75 per cent from the fortnight beginning 6 September 2025, 3.5 per cent from 4 October, 3.25 per cent from 1 November, and 3.0 per cent from 29 November 2025.

The supplement dated 18 September 2026 records the result. For the week ended 11 September 2026 the cash reserve ratio is 3.00 per cent. For the week ended 12 September 2025 it was 3.75 per cent. The statutory liquidity ratio is 18.00 per cent in both weeks. A footnote on the same page records a measurement change that affects how those fortnights are drawn: from 15 December 2025, under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025, a fortnight runs to the 15th and the last calendar day of the month, rather than to alternate Fridays.

## Absorption is running through auctions, not through bond sales

The revised framework, Press Release 2025-2026/1201, kept the overnight weighted average call rate as the operating target. It discontinued the 14-day variable rate repo and variable rate reverse repo as the main tool for transient liquidity. Transient gaps are to be handled mainly with 7-day operations, and with other operations from overnight out to 14 days. Durable liquidity stays with open market operations, variable rate repo and reverse repo beyond a fortnight, and foreign-exchange swap auctions.

The Bulletin extract dated 11 September 2026 shows how that division looked in practice at the start of the month. From 31 August through 6 September the outright open market columns, sale and purchase, are blank on every date. Absorption is in the variable rate reverse repo and the standing deposit facility. On 4 September the reverse repo book is ₹6,02,394 crore and the standing deposit facility is ₹2,28,290 crore, and net absorption is ₹8,30,561 crore. On the weekend of 5 and 6 September the variable rate reverse repo column is blank, the standing deposit facility is still above ₹2.4 lakh crore, and net absorption falls to ₹2,64,000 crore and ₹2,45,922 crore.

The Governor’s statement of 5 August 2026, published in the Bulletin of 25 August, puts a wider frame on the same surplus. Since the June meeting, system liquidity measured by the net position under the liquidity adjustment facility averaged a daily surplus of ₹1.0 lakh crore. The weighted average call rate over 6 June to 31 July traded 6 basis points above the repo rate, averaging 5.31 per cent.

## The latest call-rate print is the part that moved

That summer average and the latest weekly print are different. In the supplement dated 18 September 2026, the weighted average call rate for the week ended 11 September is 4.97 per cent. The same row of the table lists the standing deposit facility at 5.00 per cent and the repo rate at 5.25 per cent. The week’s average therefore sits 3 basis points under the published floor and 28 basis points under the policy rate. The path inside the table is a slide: 5.20 per cent in the week ended 28 August, 5.07 per cent in the week ended 4 September, then 4.97 per cent.

A hold on the policy rate is compatible with that print. The neutral stance leaves the desk free to absorb or to inject. In the first week of September the published operations were absorbing, and they were doing it with reverse repo auctions and the standing facility, with no outright open market purchase or sale on the dates the extract lists.
