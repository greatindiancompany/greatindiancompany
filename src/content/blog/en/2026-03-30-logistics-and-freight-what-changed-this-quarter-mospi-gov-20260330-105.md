The freight fact that moved this quarter is a pair of statements laid in the Lok Sabha on 11 August 2026. One is the reply to Starred Question 327 on logistics efficiency under PM Gati Shakti. The other is a written reply, issued the same afternoon, on what Gati Shakti itself is allowed to spend. Read together, they are the public record for July–September 2026. They are not a truck-rate index.

## The policy stack, as the ministry described it

The [starred reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS327_tYLfn2.pdf) from the Ministry of Commerce and Industry treats PM Gati Shakti, launched in October 2021, as the planning frame, and the National Logistics Policy, launched in September 2022, as the services frame. It then lists what that stack had produced by the date of the answer.

The lines worth keeping:

- A 2025 assessment by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade with the National Council of Applied Economic Research puts logistics cost at about 7.9 percent of GDP. The reply calls that comparable to advanced economies. It does not publish the worksheet on the floor of the House.
- The reply says India’s place in the latest World Bank Logistics Performance Index is 38th, six places higher than the previous mark it is using.
- Twenty-nine states and Union Territories have formulated their own logistics policies. Thirty-two have granted the sector industry status.
- The Unified Logistics Interface Platform is described as operational. The Logistics Data Bank is described as tracking export-import containers across ports, container freight stations, and inland container depots.
- A Service Improvement Group and the Logistics Ease Across Different States exercise are described as standing institutions. Sectoral plans are named for coal, steel, fertilizers, cement, pharmaceuticals, and food.
- Logistics courses are in more than 100 universities and institutes. Sixty-eight qualification packs for logistics skills are operational, with digital literacy built into them.

Those are stocktakes. They become “this quarter” only because they were placed on the table on 11 August 2026.

## The sentence investors usually skip

The [written reply the same day](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297818) says Gati Shakti is an integrated planning framework. Projects are still approved and paid for by the ministry or state that owns the scheme. The minister’s words are that no quantified targets or expenditure requirements have been set under the Gati Shakti initiative itself.

That is the change in interpretation, if not in asphalt. A corridor announcement on the master-plan portal is not a budget line. The Network Planning Group had, as on that date, evaluated 396 infrastructure projects with an estimated cost of about ₹18.66 lakh crore. Of those, 256 were sanctioned, and 198 of the sanctioned projects were under implementation. The annex puts the roads ministry at 171 projects evaluated and 53 under implementation, and the railways at 162 evaluated and 102 under implementation.

The same starred reply adds a three-year construction note that the written reply does not repeat: 1,115 national highway projects of about 18,493 km, costing about ₹4.75 lakh crore, approved, and 32,370 km of national highways constructed; 237 railway projects of 9,703 km sanctioned, at about ₹1,90,333 crore, covering 40 new lines, 17 gauge conversions, and 180 doubling works. “Last three years” is the reply’s window, dated from 11 August 2026. It is not a fiscal-year label you should invent.

## What these papers do not update

They do not give a door-to-door freight rate, a coastal-versus-overseas split for August, or a state ranking. They also do not say that 7.9 percent of GDP is a measured fall from the “13–14 percent” line the Prime Minister used when he launched the policy on 17 September 2022. One figure is a 2025 assessment cited in 2026. The other is a 2022 speech. Until a method note connects them, they are not a before-and-after.

The ministry homepage that used to sit on this brief is the wrong shelf. Freight for this quarter is the August replies, plus the roads ministry’s own [Bharatmala page](https://morth.nic.in/en/bharatmala) when the question is highway corridors rather than a national cost ratio.
