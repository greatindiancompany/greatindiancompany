# How Foreign Trade Policy 2023 changed India’s export trade rules

On 31 March 2023 the Commerce and Industry Ministry launched Foreign Trade Policy 2023 and described it as dynamic and open-ended. That design choice is the policy shift. FTP 2015–20 had already been patched in place. The 2023 text said later revisions would be made when required, with feedback from trade built into the update cycle, rather than waiting for the next five-year book.

## From incentive language to remission

The launch note set four pillars: incentive to remission; export promotion with exporters, states, districts, and Indian missions; ease of doing business, lower transaction cost, and e-initiatives; and emerging areas, including e-commerce, districts as export hubs, and the SCOMET control list.

“Incentive to remission” is the line operators still misread. Remission pays back duties and taxes that stay embedded in an export. It is not a reward layered on top of the price. The live instrument is RoDTEP, Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products, notified by Commerce and administered through the duty-credit scrip system. The Directorate General of Foreign Trade keeps the rate schedules on its [RoDTEP page](https://www.dgft.gov.in/CP/?opt=rodtep). Apparel and made-ups sit under RoSCTL, a separate textiles rebate, so a garment exporter who files RoDTEP for a line that belongs in RoSCTL is in the wrong scheme.

The rates themselves move. On 23 March 2026 the government restored RoDTEP rates and value caps to those in force on 22 February 2026, withdrawing a 50 percent restriction imposed by Notification No. 60/2025–26 of 23 February 2026. The [notification](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/1ffbd2bf-78ce-4d6e-8a59-5c0d16fdb1d0/Notification%2066%20English_0001.pdf) is the policy, not a blog summary of it.

## What the 2023 text actually changed for firms

Advance Authorisation and the Export Promotion Capital Goods scheme continued, with more of the work pushed onto a rule-based system in the regional offices. The launch said that during 2023–24 the issue, revalidation, and export-obligation extension steps would be covered in phases, with risk-flagged files examined by hand and the rest expected on an automatic route at the start.

The courier cap for e-commerce exports was raised from ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh per consignment, with a later revision left open. Faridabad, Mirzapur, Moradabad, and Varanasi were added as Towns of Export Excellence, on top of 39 existing towns, with a stated aim of supporting handloom, handicraft, and carpet exports. Merchanting trade — goods moving from one foreign country to another through an Indian intermediary, without touching an Indian port — was written in, subject to RBI guidelines, and excluded for CITES and SCOMET items.

A one-time amnesty addressed default on export obligations under EPCG and Advance Authorisation: customs duty exempted in proportion to the unfulfilled obligation, with interest capped at 100 percent of those duties, and no interest on the additional and special additional customs duty portions. That was a clean-up of old files, not a new export subsidy.

At the launch, the minister said overall exports had crossed US$ 750 billion and were expected to cross US$ 760 billion that year. Those sentences belong to 31 March 2023. They are not a target printed for 2026–27, and they are not a licence to add later merchandise and services series together and call the sum “the FTP target.”

## How to use the shift

Read FTP 2023 as a standing rulebook plus a stream of DGFT notifications. The competitive question for a firm is whether the embedded-tax remission, the authorisation route, and the district or e-commerce facility it can actually file are in force this month.
