# Five public doors into India's defence and space industry

## A door is not a market size

The notices below tell a private firm where to apply. They do not publish a total addressable market for Indian defence and aerospace. Production of Rs 1,50,590 crore in FY 2024-25 is an output figure. It is not a pool of open contracts. The opportunities that follow are entry points, each with the limit the issuing page already states.

## SRIJAN and the positive lists

SRIJAN, opened in August 2020, is where defence public sector undertakings and service headquarters offer items to Indian industry, including MSMEs and startups. The 18 August 2026 release says more than 33,000 items had been offered up to June 2026. The sixth positive list adds 405 items, estimated business potential Rs 3,070 crore, of which 389 are from defence public sector undertakings and 16 from the Coast Guard. After development succeeds, those items are to be bought from Indian industry.

This door fits a component, material, or sub-assembly firm that can build to a named line. It fits poorly as a strategy slide that stops at "Atmanirbhar". The work is the line on the portal.

## A reserved slice of the modernisation budget

The 1 February 2025 budget release provisions Rs 27,886.21 crore for procurement from domestic private industry in FY 2025-26. That is 25 percent of the domestic share. The domestic share itself is Rs 1,11,544.83 crore, or 75 percent of the Rs 1,48,722.80 crore modernisation budget.

The rupee is an earmark inside a budget, not a tender a named startup has won. It does tell a private manufacturer that the Ministry wrote a private-industry slice before the year began, separate from purchases from defence public sector undertakings. On 1 April 2026 the Ministry reported that the wider capital outlay, raised to Rs 1.86 lakh crore at revised estimates, was fully used. Use of the appropriation is not a published split of how much of the private earmark was contracted.

## iDEX, including ADITI

The iDEX scheme page sets support at Rs 498.78 crore from 2021-22 to 2025-26, aimed at nearly 300 startups, MSMEs, and individual innovators, and about 20 incubators. The FAQ caps DISC and Open Challenge grants at Rs 1.5 crore, and ADITI grants at Rs 25 crore, subject to the product development budget. The FY 2025-26 budget line was Rs 449.62 crore including ADITI. The FAQ withholds any promise of a production quantity. This door is for a prototype the services or a public undertaking might later buy. It is the wrong door for a firm that needs a multi-year production contract on day one.

## Corridors, if the firm needs land

Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu remain the two notified corridors, with eleven nodes between them. As of October 2025, a PIB note puts combined investments attracted above Rs 9,145 crore, with 289 memoranda and a much larger potential figure. A firm that needs a plot and a state incentive starts with UPEIDA or TIDCO and then checks the date of the investment claim. A software or design firm with no land need can ignore the corridor and still use SRIJAN or iDEX.

## Space, under a different promoter

The Indian Space Policy 2023 separates the roles. IN-SPACe authorises and promotes non-government space activity. ISRO's stated focus is research and development of new space technology. NewSpace India Limited commercialises technologies and platforms created with public expenditure. A startup that wants to launch, operate a satellite, or use a government range is in the IN-SPACe line. A firm that wants to sell a spare for a military aircraft is in the SRIJAN line. The policy does not merge them.

The November 2025 note adds a scale marker for the industry already inside the gates: 788 industrial licences issued to 462 companies, and 16,000 MSMEs described as part of the ecosystem. Those are signs of a crowd at the door. They are not a guarantee of work. Great Indian Company would rather a founder pick one door and read its limit than collect all five logos.
