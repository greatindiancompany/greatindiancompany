# How cyber work is split between the Centre and the states

There is no official state ranking of cybersecurity resilience in the documents this brief uses. The Lok Sabha reply published on 24 March 2026 gives one geography a superlative, names a handful of places for specific programmes, and then explains why a national league table would describe the wrong institution.

## Investigation sits with the states

The reply states that police and public order are state subjects under the Seventh Schedule. States and Union territories are primarily responsible for prevention, detection, investigation, complaint reporting, recovery, victim support and prosecution of crimes, including cyber fraud, through their law-enforcement agencies. The Centre supplements that work with advisories and financial assistance.

Complaints on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, which is part of the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre in the Ministry of Home Affairs, are converted into FIRs and taken through chargesheets, arrests and resolution by the state or Union territory agency concerned. A portal report is not itself an investigation.

## The one place CERT-In singled out

The same reply says that, as per CERT-In, the highest number of cyber incidents reported, including from sectors, are from the National Capital Territory of Delhi. It does not publish the state-by-state table behind that sentence. Using Delhi as a ranked "least resilient" state would add a judgement the reply does not make. Delhi also concentrates government, telecom and financial systems that report into a national agency. The reply does not separate those effects.

## Places named for a job, not a score

Cyber Bharat Setu, a CERT-In programme on cybersecurity culture in states and Union territories, had participation in 2025 from Madhya Pradesh, Tripura, Uttarakhand and the Jammu and Kashmir administration. That is a participation list for one programme.

Seven Joint Cyber Coordination Teams have been constituted for Mewat, Jamtara, Ahmedabad, Hyderabad, Chandigarh, Vishakhapatnam and Guwahati. The reply describes them as covering the country on the basis of cyber-crime hotspots and areas with multi-jurisdictional issues. They are a coordination map of hard problems, not a resilience ranking of those cities.

Cyber forensic-cum-training laboratories under the Cyber Crime Prevention against Women and Children scheme have been commissioned in 33 states and Union territories. The National-Digital Investigation Support Centre, earlier the National Cyber Forensic Laboratory (Investigation), was set up in New Delhi on 18 February 2019 and in Assam on 29 August 2025. Up to 31 January 2026 the Delhi centre had assisted state and Union territory agencies in more than 13,417 cyber-crime cases. Assistance from a national lab is a capacity fact. It does not score the other states.

## What the Centre sends to every state

The reply lists tools that are not state-specific: CERT-In alerts and advisories; the National Cyber Coordination Centre sharing threat information with state governments; an automated threat-exchange platform; Cyber Swachhta Kendra for malicious programmes and vulnerable services; mock drills and workshops with state organisations; and joint training with industry partners.

I4C runs State Connect, Thana Connect and peer-learning sessions. The Samanvaya platform is described as a management-information and analytics layer for interstate linkages, and the Pratibimb module maps criminal and infrastructure locations for jurisdictional officers. The reply says this has led to arrest of more than 21,857 accused and more than 1,49,636 cyber-investigation assistance requests. Those totals are national.

A state pattern that the record supports is institutional: the same national pipes (portal, helpline 1930, threat exchange, labs) land in police systems whose legal responsibility is local, with extra coordination structures where cases cross borders. A state pattern the record does not support is a numbered ranking of which state is "ahead" on resilience.
