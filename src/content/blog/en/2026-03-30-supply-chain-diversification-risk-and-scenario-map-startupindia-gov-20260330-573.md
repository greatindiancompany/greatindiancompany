# Risk scenarios for a manufacturing line still tied to one trade source

A risk map is a set of ways the line stops, tied to rules that already exist. It is not a probability table. None of the official notes below assign odds. Use the scenarios to see which document you would need on the day the single source fails.

## The input meets a quality order

You qualify a second-country input and then find a Quality Control Order blocks it for sale in India. FTP 2023, paragraph 2.03(A), lets Advance Authorisation holders, EOUs, and SEZ units import inputs without meeting mandatory QCOs, but the goods are for export production, with pre-import conditions and limits on domestic clearance. Scenario: the domestic order book cannot use the new input, and the export order can. Firms that blended both markets in one purchase order discover the split only at the port. The mitigation that exists in the rule is to split the lanes, or to buy an input that meets the order. Appendix 2Y lists which ministries' orders are inside the exemption, and it changes.

## The preference is refused

The customer priced the part as if an Indian FTA rate applied. The agreement is either not in force, or the bill of materials fails the origin rule, or nobody can issue the certificate. DGFT Appendix 2A is the agreement list. Appendix 2B lists agencies for preferential certificates of origin. The India-UAE CEPA is in force from 1 May 2022; many later texts are not in the same position. On 13 May 2026 the Commerce and Industry Minister said five of nine recent agreements were still expected to enter into force over the following 12 months. Scenario: a contract signed against a "concluded" FTA ships at the non-preferential rate. The mitigation is to price both rates until the certificate is in hand.

## The critical mineral never becomes an Indian input

Localization of a battery, magnet, or electronics line still depends on lithium, cobalt, or another mineral processed somewhere else. The National Critical Mineral Mission, approved on 29 January 2025, is the official response: domestic production, overseas assets, processing, and recycling. KABIL has an exploration agreement for five lithium brine blocks in Catamarca, Argentina, over 15,703 hectares, and an MoU with Australia on lithium and cobalt. The 24 March 2025 PIB note also records Rs 500 crore for processing parks and Rs 1,500 crore for recycling. Scenario: the overseas block and the MoU proceed, and the Indian processing park does not, so the plant still imports the intermediate. Owning a foreign resource is not the same as having the input next to the factory.

## The capital is on the wrong route

The operating company looks Indian, and the beneficial owner sits in a country that shares a land border with India. Paragraph 3.1.1(a) of the Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2020 puts that investment on the government route. Scenario: equipment is ordered on the assumption of an automatic-route close, and the approval is still open. Manufacturing as an activity is on the automatic route only when that paragraph does not catch the investor.

## The node, not the factory, is the bottleneck

The DPIIT-NCAER study, reported on 27 November 2025, puts 2023-24 logistics cost at 7.97 percent of GDP and Rs 24.01 lakh crore, and says smaller firms pay more. It also says the first and last 50 kilometres of a roughly 600-kilometre haul can dominate. Scenario: the second source manufactures on time and misses the vessel because the last mile or the port queue did not move. Corridor projects such as the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor change some nodes and leave others untouched. A backup plant on the same weak node is not a diversified route.

## How to use the map

Assign each scenario an owner: customs and quality, the origin desk, the mineral buyer, the company secretary, the logistics lead. Review them when DGFT amends an appendix or when a PIB note updates the mineral mission or a PLI scheme. Great Indian Company keeps the map descriptive. It does not sell a probability.
