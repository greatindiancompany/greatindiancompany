# Domestic airline traffic through July 2026

The number a fare, a fleet plan, or a tourism season should be checked against is the Directorate General of Civil Aviation’s monthly line: passengers carried by domestic airlines. It counts a passenger once on the journey. It is not the airport throughput series, which counts arrivals and departures and is the series behind the “about 420 million” passengers Indian airports handled in 2025-26.

## The cumulative line, then the months inside it

DGCA’s own summary sentence, repeated in each monthly note, gives a year-to-date total, the year-ago total, an annual growth rate, and a monthly growth rate. Through 2026 those cumulative lines read:

- January–March: 437.31 lakh, against 431.98 lakh, annual growth 1.23 percent, monthly growth −0.87 percent.
- January–April: 575.49 lakh, against 575.13 lakh, annual growth 0.06 percent, monthly growth −3.47 percent.
- January–May: 729.40 lakh, against 715.70 lakh, annual growth 1.91 percent, monthly growth 9.49 percent.
- January–June: 864.04 lakh, against 851.74 lakh, annual growth 1.44 percent, monthly growth −1.03 percent.
- January–July: 984.03 lakh, against 977.79 lakh, annual growth 0.64 percent.

“Monthly growth” in that sentence is the latest month against the same month a year earlier. It is not the change from the previous month. June’s −1.03 percent means June 2026 against June 2025. May’s +9.49 percent means May 2026 against May 2025.

Subtracting successive cumulative totals gives the single months. April was 138.18 lakh. May was 153.91 lakh. June was 134.64 lakh. July was 119.99 lakh. July 2025, by the same subtraction on the year-ago cumulatives, was 126.05 lakh, which is the 4.8 percent decline DGCA reported for July.

## What the May peak was

On 16 July the civil aviation minister told a UDAN workshop that India had been recording about five lakh domestic passengers a day, and that May was the highest monthly domestic traffic, serving over 1.5 crore passengers. The DGCA cumulative lines put May at 153.91 lakh, which is 1.54 crore. The daily remark and the monthly total describe the same peak at two resolutions. They do not make May a run-rate for the year. June and July both came off that peak, and the January–July growth rate is 0.64 percent.

## Who carried the June and July passengers

DGCA’s airline table for June, read against the 134.64 lakh month, is concentrated. IndiGo carried 89.20 lakh passengers in June, a 66.3 percent share, up from 64.9 percent in May. The Air India group carried 32.22 lakh, a 23.9 percent share, down from 25.6 percent in May. Akasa Air carried 8.61 lakh, a 6.4 percent share. SpiceJet carried 2.63 lakh, a 1.9 percent share.

July volumes, from the same DGCA release that reported the 119.99 lakh month, were lower at each of those carriers: IndiGo 80.82 lakh, the Air India group 28.75 lakh, Akasa 6.65 lakh, SpiceJet 1.87 lakh. A share calculated from those July counts will not match a share someone quotes from June. Use the month that matches the decision.

## How to read the next note

When the August, September, or October note appears, write down four figures before forming a view: the new year-to-date total, the annual growth rate printed beside it, the monthly growth rate (same month last year), and the single month obtained by subtracting the previous cumulative. Then check whether IndiGo’s passenger count and the Air India group’s count moved together. A year-to-date rate that stays near zero while one month spikes is a season, which is what May was inside a 0.64 percent seven-month total.
