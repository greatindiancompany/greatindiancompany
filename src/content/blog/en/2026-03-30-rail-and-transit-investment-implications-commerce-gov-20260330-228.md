# Where Railway and Metro Capital Is Going in FY 2026-27

Railway capital and metro capital are raised in different places. Mixing them produces a single “infrastructure” number that neither ministry publishes.

## The railway book is still mostly public budgetary support

On 1 April 2026 the Ministry of Railways said gross budgetary support for rail capital investment had risen from ₹29,055 crore in 2013-14 to ₹2.78 lakh crore in 2026-27. The Budget Estimate figure given in that reply is ₹2,78,030 crore, of which ₹79,072 crore is allocated to construction of new tracks. New track is a large slice of support. It is not the whole of it.

The same reply estimated expenditure on safety-related activities in 2026-27 at ₹1,20,389 crore, of which ₹46,189 crore is for track infrastructure. That safety estimate is reported separately from the new-track allocation. This brief does not add the two lines together.

As on 1 April 2026, the sanctioned construction book was 514 projects — 169 new lines, 29 gauge-conversion projects, and 316 doubling projects — totalling 40,000 km and about ₹8.31 lakh crore. Expenditure up to March 2026 was ₹3,05,253 crore, and 12,583 km had been commissioned. Most of the sanctioned length is still ahead of the railway. The [capex cycle note](/blog/rail-and-transit-capex-cycle-watch-commerce-gov-20260330-348) splits that book by category.

## Private capital shows up at the terminal, not on the trunk

The 7 August 2026 freight reply put private investment mobilised through commissioned Gati Shakti cargo terminals at about ₹10,000 crore. An April reply had put the figure near ₹9,183 crore when 128 terminals were commissioned. The later stock is 142 terminals. Wagon schemes add industrial rakes, not a second railway. The dedicated freight corridors themselves are described as commissioned public works. The Dankuni–Surat corridor is a detailed project report announced in Union Budget 2026. It is not, in that reply, a sanctioned construction outlay an investor can underwrite.

Coach manufacturing is a smaller, specific public outlay. The 1 April 2026 release said manufacture of 120 Vande Bharat trainsets has been taken up at the Marathwada Rail Coach Factory at Latur, set up at a cost of ₹685.79 crore.

## Metro capital sits with urban affairs

The 15 March 2026 PIB note on metro rail, drawing on the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, said that since 2014 the government had sanctioned 38 metro projects covering 1,051 km at an estimated cost of ₹3.44 lakh crore. The annual metro budget in that note is ₹29,550 crore for 2025-26, against ₹5,798 crore in 2013-14. The Metro Rail Policy routes central participation through an equal-equity special-purpose vehicle with the state, not through the railway gross budgetary support line.

An August 2025 PIB note had stated a 2025-26 metro budget of ₹34,807 crore. The March 2026 note states ₹29,550 crore. Both are PIB texts. A capital plan should cite the later note and keep the earlier one visible, rather than average them.

## What this means for a capital allocation

Public budgetary support builds track, safety, and the freight corridors. Private money, on the published record, has gone into cargo terminals and wagons. Metro money is an urban-affairs sanction book with its own budget line. A pitch that treats “India rail capex” as one pool is not reading the releases. The [investor brief](/blog/rail-and-transit-for-investors-commerce-gov-20260330-788) stays inside those three pools.

Great Indian Company writes this so an investor can see which rupee is budgetary, which is a terminal, and which is still a project report.
