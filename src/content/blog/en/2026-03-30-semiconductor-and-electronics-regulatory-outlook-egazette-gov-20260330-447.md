Semiconductor and electronics incentives in India are a stack of named notifications. A homepage does not tell an applicant the rate, the ceiling, or whether the window is open. This brief lists the instruments that do, as they stood for a reader on 24 September 2026. Where a later amendment exists and this page has not restated its new date, the instruction is to read that amendment on the MeitY scheme page before filing.

## SPECS: capital expenditure on components and fabs

The Scheme for Promotion of Manufacturing of Electronic Components and Semiconductors was notified as Gazette Notification No. CG-DL-E-01042020-218992 dated 1 April 2020. MeitY's [scheme page](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/scheme-for-promotion-of-manufacturing-of-electronic-components-and-semiconductors-specs-AMxIDOtQWa) states a financial incentive of 25 percent of capital expenditure. The list covers electronic components, semiconductor and display fabrication units, ATMP units, specialised sub-assemblies, and capital goods for those goods. Applicants are entities registered in India. The scheme applies to new units and to expansion, modernisation, and diversification.

The [guidelines of 1 June 2020](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/SPECS-Guidelines-01062020.pdf) say applications were receivable up to 31 March 2023, three years from notification. Incentives apply to investment made within five years of acknowledgement. MeitY's page also lists an addendum dated 5 April 2023 and an amendment dated 10 July 2023. Those later documents are part of the scheme's text. This brief does not paraphrase them. An investor who needs to know whether a 2024 or 2025 purchase still qualifies should read the addendum, not the original three-year sentence alone.

## Design Linked Incentive: a separate gazette, implemented by C-DAC

Notification No. CG-DL-E-21122021-232049, dated 21 December 2021, is the [Design Linked Incentive](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Notification-Design-Linked-Incentive-Scheme.pdf). It is aimed at domestic companies, startups, and MSMEs designing integrated circuits, chipsets, systems on chip, systems, and IP cores. C-DAC receives applications, evaluates them, and sets up the design infrastructure, including the national design-tool grid.

The rates in the notification are specific. Product-design support reimburses up to 50 percent of eligible expenditure, ceiling ₹15 crore per application. Deployment support is 6 percent to 4 percent of net sales over five years, ceiling ₹30 crore per application. The [guidelines dated 30 December 2022](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Guidelines-for-Design-Linked-Incentive-DLI-Scheme-30.12.2022.pdf) add a gate: product-design applications are for startups and MSMEs, and deployment applications are for domestic companies, startups, and MSMEs. Claims follow milestones in the approval letter. The notification opened applications for three years from 1 January 2022 and allows the Minister to extend that window. A startup should confirm the live window with C-DAC or the India Semiconductor Mission before treating the original three years as the close.

"Domestic company" in the notification is an ownership test: beneficial ownership of more than 50 percent by resident Indian citizens, or by Indian companies that are themselves so owned. The status has to be held for three years after a claim. That test is why a foreign parent's design subsidiary does not automatically qualify.

The same 21 December 2021 date is when [PIB, on 29 February 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2010132), says the wider Programme for Development of Semiconductors and Display Manufacturing Ecosystem was notified, with an outlay of ₹76,000 crore. The design gazette is one instrument inside that day. Fab and packaging approvals have since been announced as Cabinet decisions under the programme. Their project-level fiscal support is set at appraisal. It is not restated as a fresh percentage in each approval release.

## PLI 2.0 for IT hardware

The Cabinet approved PLI Scheme 2.0 for IT Hardware on 17 May 2023 with a budgetary outlay of ₹17,000 crore. MeitY notified it as Gazette Notification No. CG-DL-E-30052023-246165 dated 29 May 2023. The [scheme page](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/production-linked-incentive-scheme-pli-2-0-for-it-hardware-wM0MDOtQWa) states an average incentive of around 5 percent on net incremental sales, over the base year, for six years. The target segment is laptops, tablets, all-in-one PCs, servers, and ultra-small-form-factor devices. The page also says semiconductor design, integrated-circuit manufacturing, and packaging are included as incentivised components of this scheme. The sales incentive still attaches to the target-segment goods. A chip, by itself, is not the product the 5 percent is calculated on.

The [operating guidelines of 14 July 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/03/Guidelines_PLI-Scheme-ITHW-min.pdf) block a double count. Investment that has already received a SPECS incentive is not counted toward the PLI incremental-investment threshold. The same bar applies to a contract manufacturer's SPECS-backed investment. A group that has used both schemes needs two ledgers.

## The Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme

On 28 March 2025 the Cabinet approved the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme. It was notified on 8 April 2025 as Gazette Notification No. CG-DL-E-08042025-262341. The [26 April 2025 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620) records an outlay of ₹22,919 crore and a tenure of six years with one year of gestation, from 2025-26 through 2031-32. Incentives are turnover-linked, capex-linked, or hybrid, and part of the payout is tied to employment. Applications opened on 1 May 2025 at ecms.meity.gov.in. For sub-assemblies and bare components the original window was three months. For the supply-chain and capital-equipment segment it was two years.

MeitY's [scheme page](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/electronics-component-manufacturing-scheme-UTM1IjMtQWa) lists a 7 July 2025 addendum and an amendment described as an extension of the application window. The original three-month close is therefore not a safe assumption for segments A, B, and C. The amendment is the document that says whether those segments were reopened, and until which date.

## Semicon 2.0 is a Cabinet decision first

On 15 July 2026 the Cabinet approved Semicon 2.0 with an outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore. The [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) is the decision: six pillars, the status of the 12 earlier manufacturing units, and the design-scheme status. A Cabinet release is the authority for the outlay and the pillars. It is not, by itself, the claim form. Rates, eligible expenditure, and application windows for the new pillars — especially machines and materials — sit in the notification MeitY issues afterwards. Until that notification is posted, the enforceable claim formulas remain the ones in the gazettes above.
