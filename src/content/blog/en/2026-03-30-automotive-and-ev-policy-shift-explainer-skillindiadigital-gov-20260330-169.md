# How India's EV Demand Incentive Moved from FAME II to PM E-DRIVE

India did not replace one electric-vehicle subsidy with another on a single day. FAME II stopped taking new demand-incentive sales, a short bridge scheme covered part of 2024, and PM E-DRIVE then absorbed that bridge and kept being amended. The policy a buyer or OEM should use is the latest gazette, not the launch press note.

## FAME II closed on a date and a fund

The [Ministry of Heavy Industries page](https://www.heavyindustries.gov.in/en/fame-ii) records Phase II of Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of Electric Vehicles with an outlay of ₹10,000 crore for three years from 1 April 2019. About 86 percent of that support was for demand incentive. The original support envelope was 7,000 electric buses, 5 lakh electric three-wheelers, 55,000 electric four-wheelers including strong hybrids, and 10 lakh electric two-wheelers, with room to move numbers across segments. Incentives were for advanced-battery vehicles that were registered. Three-wheelers, four-wheelers, and buses were aimed mainly at public or commercial use. Private registered two-wheelers were included as the mass segment.

The [FAME II portal](https://fame2.heavyindustries.gov.in/) adds the operating close. Incentives covered vehicles sold and registered until 31 March 2024. The outlay was enhanced from ₹10,000 crore to ₹11,500 crore. The scheme was fund-limited as well as date-limited: subsidies for two-, three-, and four-wheelers ran until 31 March 2024 or until funds ran out, whichever came first. OEMs were told to certify models to AIS 156 under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules to stay eligible.

A later [PIB reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2110244) says ₹839 crore of FAME II was allocated to public charging stations. That is a separate line from the vehicle subsidy.

## The 2024 bridge was then folded in

PM E-DRIVE was notified on 29 September 2024 as S.O. 4259(E). The [scheme portal](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/) says implementation was framed from 1 October 2024 to 31 March 2026, and that EMPS-2024 — the Electric Mobility Promotion Scheme for electric two- and three-wheelers from 1 April 2024 to 30 September 2024 — was subsumed, so the effective start is 1 April 2024. The Cabinet note on [pmindia.gov.in](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/cabinet-approves-pm-electric-drive-revolution-in-innovative-vehicle-enhancement-pm-e-drive-scheme-with-an-outlay-of-rs-10900-crore-over-a-period-of-two-years/) stated an outlay of ₹10,900 crore over two years.

On 7 August 2025 the ministry extended the scheme to 31 March 2028 inside that same ₹10,900 crore, and said the two-wheeler, e-rickshaw, e-cart, and L5 terminal date stayed 31 March 2026. [PIB's note of 8 August 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2154408) is explicit that the scheme is fund-limited: if money for a sub-component is exhausted early, claims stop. A 27 March 2026 amendment then moved registered two-wheelers to 31 July 2026 and e-rickshaws and e-carts to 31 March 2028, and recorded that L5 had closed on 26 December 2025 because its target was met.

## The August 2026 amendment is the current design

[S.O. 4424(E) of 10 August 2026](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/docs/policy_document/Gazette%20Notification%2010%2008%202026%20on%20enhancement%20of%20Outlay%2011900%20cr.pdf) is the shift that matters for anyone still using the July parliamentary reply. The outlay becomes ₹11,900 crore. The scheme window is 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2028. Registered two-wheelers are supported up to 45,79,120 vehicles with ₹2,767 crore. From 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2028 the rate is ₹2,500 per kWh, capped at ₹5,000 per vehicle and at 15 percent of ex-factory price. The FY 2024-25 rate stays the higher ₹5,000 per kWh cap of ₹10,000. L5 remains closed. The last date for any claim is 31 December 2027. The portal's allocation table now sums to ₹11,900 crore, with administration at ₹55 crore.

Demand incentive and manufacturing incentive are different instruments. PM E-DRIVE pays down the vehicle or the charger. PLI Auto, covered in the investment brief, pays later for domestic value addition. Reading one as a substitute for the other will mis-state both cash timing and eligibility.
