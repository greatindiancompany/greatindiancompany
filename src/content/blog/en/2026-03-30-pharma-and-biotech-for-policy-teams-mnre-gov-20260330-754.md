# Which Ministry Holds Which Pharma and Biotech Lever

Policy teams get a cleaner review when each lever stays with the ministry that publishes it. Indian pharma and biotech currently runs through at least three Union homes, plus state governments. Collapsing them into one “health manufacturing” brief produces recommendations the responsible department cannot carry out.

## Chemicals and fertilizers: production and shared estates

The Department of Pharmaceuticals, in the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, holds the manufacturing-incentive and park instruments. Its schemes index is the control list. On it: the bulk-drug PLI for critical key starting materials, intermediates, and APIs; the pharmaceuticals PLI; the medical-devices PLI, which is a neighbour and not a drug scheme; the Scheme for Promotion of Bulk Drug Parks; medical-device parks; the Strengthening of Pharmaceuticals Industry scheme; the Scheme for Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma MedTech Sector, with a gazette dated 1 October 2025; the Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana; and a consumer-awareness and price-monitoring scheme.

The 6 February 2026 Lok Sabha reply, given by the Minister for Chemicals and Fertilizers, is the department’s public status board for the two drug PLIs and the three bulk-drug parks. Parks are joint with states. The reply names the state implementing agencies: Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation, Himachal Pradesh Bulk Drug Park Infrastructure Ltd., and Andhra Pradesh Bulk Drug Infrastructure Corporation. A central review that ignores those agencies is reviewing a grant ceiling without a works owner. The reply is also explicit that Tamil Nadu does not have a bulk-drug park under this scheme. Policy language that promises a park in every pharmaceutical state is ahead of the approval.

Jan Aushadhi is on the same index and serves a different objective: retail access to generic medicines through kendras. A production incentive and a retail-access scheme can be complementary. They answer different questions, and a monitoring dashboard should not add their budgets into one “pharma support” line without showing both names.

## Health: licence, trial, and manufacturing practice

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare holds the Drugs and Cosmetics Act framework. CDSCO, in the Directorate General of Health Services, describes its responsibilities as new drugs, clinical trials, standards, import quality, and coordination of state drug control organisations. State licensing authorities remain the manufacturers’ day-to-day licensor for a large part of the catalogue. SUGAM is the central electronic route and a store of state licence details.

Revised Schedule M, G.S.R. 922(E) of 28 December 2023, is a health-ministry amendment of the Drugs Rules. It sets premises, plant, and quality-system requirements. A chemicals-ministry subsidy for technology upgradation may help a unit pay for that standard. The standard itself is not rewritten by the subsidy guideline. Policy teams coordinating MSME upgrading should put the health-ministry gazette and the Department’s upgradation guidelines in the same folder and keep the authors distinct.

Clinical research policy lives here too. The New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019, including ethics committees and six-monthly SUGAM status reports, are the trial regime. BioE3 does not replace them when a biotherapeutic enters a human study.

## Science and technology: incubation and biomanufacturing policy

The Department of Biotechnology holds biotechnology parks and, through BIRAC, bio-incubators. Its National Biotechnology Parks Scheme note lists parks and centres in Lucknow, Hyderabad, Chennai, Guwahati, Cochin, and Bengaluru as support already given at the time of the note. The Cabinet approval of BioE3 on 24 August 2024 sets biomanufacturing themes and calls for biomanufacturing and bio-AI hubs and biofoundries. A policy team writing a “single window for biotech” should say which of those themes has an appropriation, a guideline, and a state partner. The August 2024 note establishes the policy. It is not itself a park allotment order.

Environment and price sit beside these three. Common effluent plants and zero-liquid-discharge systems in the park designs need environmental clearance and consents; the February 2026 reply already records an environmental clearance in January 2026 for additional land at the Andhra Pradesh park. Scheduled-drug prices are the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority’s regime. An incentive to manufacture does not, by itself, reset a ceiling price.

A practical coordination note names, for each recommendation, the department, the legal instrument, and the last dated status. The regulator’s half of that note is [the CDSCO split](/blog/pharma-and-biotech-regulatory-outlook-mnre-gov-20260330-434). Great Indian Company publishes the map so a policy reader can assign the action to the office that holds the file: [greatindiancompany.com](https://greatindiancompany.com).
