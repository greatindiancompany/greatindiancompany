# The Regulatory Outlook for India's Energy Transition

The outlook from here is mostly implementation. The statutes and the big notifications are already on the book. What a distribution company, an open-access consumer, a module maker, or a generator has to live with in FY 2026-27 is written down. The work is proving compliance, getting a connection, and meeting a quality order.

## Consumption obligations are the live demand rule

The Ministry of Power's renewable consumption obligation notification of 27 September 2025, issued under the Energy Conservation Act, supersedes the October 2023 notification. MNRE has said this single framework subsumes state renewable-purchase targets, so the design intent is one obligation rather than a central target stacked on a separate state target.

For 2026-27 the Bureau of Energy Efficiency's operational guidelines set out a 35.95 percent total for distribution licensees, split into 1.97 percent wind, 1.34 percent hydro, 2.70 percent distributed renewable energy, and 29.94 percent other renewable sources. Wind and hydro count from projects commissioned after 31 March 2024. Distributed renewable energy is generation from projects of 10 MW or less, including rooftop. Where generation data are missing, the guidelines allow an assumption of 4 kWh per kW per day. Open-access consumers and captive users are on the total percentage for the year, not on a separate invention of their own.

Two adjustments matter for state regulators. In the hilly and North-Eastern states named in the guidelines, and in Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand, the distributed component is half the table value, and the rest moves into the other component. A licensee that serves only urban consumers carries 75 percent of the distributed component.

The outlook is not a new percentage. It is whether obligated entities file usable data, whether state commissions' open-access and tariff regulations sit cleanly beside the central notification, and which of the three compliance routes an entity uses: renewable electricity itself, certificates including virtual power purchase agreements, or the buyout price set by the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission. CERC has issued virtual-PPA guidelines. The buyout price is a CERC number, and it will keep being read as a ceiling on the cost of the obligation.

The Ministry extended the deadline for filing FY 2024-25 compliance details to 31 May 2026. That extension is a clue to the administrative load. The first full cycles are where the rule becomes real.

## The carbon market runs on intensity targets

The Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, notified on 28 June 2023 and amended on 19 December 2023, creates certificates of one tonne of CO2 equivalent. The compliance track specifies obligated entities. The Environment Ministry notifies their greenhouse-gas intensity targets. The Bureau of Energy Efficiency runs measurement, reporting, and verification, and accredits carbon verification agencies. The detailed compliance procedure was published in July 2024.

The outlook here is the arrival of notified intensity numbers for each cycle, the quality of annual verification, and whether certificates trade at a price that changes plant decisions. The scheme does not set India's national emissions cap. A firm that is not an obligated entity does not get a target from this notification merely by operating in India.

## Grid rules are about when you may connect

CERC's third amendment in 2025 to the Connectivity and General Network Access regulations introduced a non-solar-hour connectivity framework, so that transmission built for solar can carry other power when the sun is down. The fourth amendment to the sharing-of-transmission-charges regulations, issued on 26 June 2025, sets the inter-state transmission waiver trajectory for renewable and battery projects, including extra time when delay comes from force majeure or from the transmission system not being available.

Green Energy Open Access remains the retail-side rule from June 2022: 100 kW and above, no floor for captive consumers, a fifteen-day decision by the nodal agency, and Grid-India as the central nodal agency. State commissions still determine retail tariffs, cross-subsidy surcharge, and additional surcharge within the bounds of those rules. A national open-access portal does not erase a state charge.

## Factories are on a standards calendar

The Solar Systems, Devices, and Components Goods Order, 2025, notified on 27 January 2025, replaced the 2017 quality-control order and picks up current Indian Standards for modules, storage batteries, and inverters, including module-efficiency standards. Domestic-content monitoring runs through a National Institute of Solar Energy portal. Import monitoring runs through the Renewable Energy Equipment Import Monitoring System launched in October 2025.

The Approved List for modules is already the gate for many government-linked projects. MNRE's FY 2025-26 review said an ingot-and-wafer pathway under that list had been announced for June 2028. That date is the regulatory bridge between today's module factories, about 172 GW of nameplate capacity at 31 March 2026, and a deeper domestic chain. Cell-factory equipment has a parallel window: basic-customs-duty exemption on capital goods for lithium-ion cells used in battery storage runs until 31 March 2028.

GST on renewable devices and parts is 5 percent from 22 September 2025, down from 12 percent. That is a tax fact, already in force, and it belongs in project models now.

## What "outlook" should mean in a note like this

Expect tighter proof of renewable consumption, a carbon-certificate compliance cycle for named entities, connection rules that care about non-solar hours, and a 2028 marker for ingots and wafers. Expect state regulators to keep writing the retail tariffs and open-access charges those national rules sit inside. A new unnamed national scheme is not required for any of that work to fill the next year.
