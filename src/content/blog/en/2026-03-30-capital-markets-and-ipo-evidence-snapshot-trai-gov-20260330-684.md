# FY26 figures worth keeping from SEBI's own tables

This page is a snapshot, not an argument. Every figure is from SEBI's Annual Report 2025-26 or from the Handbook of Statistics press release of 17 August 2026. The year is April 2025 to March 2026 unless a stock is dated 31 March 2026. Primary-market tables are sourced in the report to BSE and NSE. Demat tables are sourced to NSDL and CDSL. If a later handbook revises a cell, the later cell wins.

## Primary equity and the routes around it

Total primary-market resource mobilisation was ₹13.6 lakh crore, 4.4 per cent below the previous year.

Equity public issues plus rights issues were ₹2,34,872 crore from 506 issues, against ₹2,10,190 crore from 464. The narrative rounds the latest year to ₹2.3 lakh crore and calls the change 11.7 per cent.

IPOs were 366 issues and ₹1,88,616 crore. Of those, offer-for-sale only was 19 issues and ₹50,849 crore. Fresh issue only was 214 issues and ₹21,866 crore. Deals that did both were 133 issues and ₹1,15,901 crore, of which ₹58,166 crore was fresh capital and ₹57,735 crore was offer for sale.

Follow-on public offers were 1 issue and ₹88 crore. Rights issues were 139 and ₹46,168 crore. The largest rights issue was about 54 per cent of rights money.

Main-board IPOs were 109 and ₹1.8 lakh crore, median size ₹760 crore. SME listings were 257 and ₹11,587 crore, average size ₹45.1 crore. Of the SME issues, 197 were in the ₹10 crore to ₹25 crore band and 57 were ₹25 crore or more.

Seventy-eight issues above ₹500 crore raised ₹2,01,619 crore, 85.8 per cent of public-and-rights money. Financial services were 28.6 per cent of that money, metals and mining 12.0 per cent, consumer services 11.3 per cent. Private-sector issuers were 504 of 506 issues and 98.8 per cent of the amount.

Qualified institutional placements were 36 issues and ₹67,853 crore, against 91 and ₹1,35,597 crore. Preferential allotments were ₹1,48,219 crore, up 76.3 per cent.

Public-issue applications received were 244. Processed were 247. Pending at year-end were 58. Median processing time was 25 working days excluding query time and 84 working days including it.

## Debt, trusts, and the social exchange

Debt issues were ₹9,11,078 crore across 1,967 issues, down 8.4 per cent. Private placement was 98.8 per cent. Public debt issues were ₹11,343 crore across 43 issues, up 39.2 per cent. Listed commercial paper issuance was ₹16,68,834 crore, against ₹15,48,091 crore. Municipal bonds were 14 issues and ₹1,756 crore, against one issue and ₹100 crore.

InvITs mobilised ₹21,026 crore. REITs raised ₹9,300 crore in three issues. Net assets at March 2026 were ₹6.4 lakh crore for InvITs and ₹2.4 lakh crore for REITs. Five social-stock-exchange non-profits raised ₹21.9 crore, against ₹14.8 crore from six issues.

Open offers closed were 122, offer size ₹35,185 crore, against 71 and ₹15,457 crore. Buyback offer size was ₹19,238 crore across 17 tender offers. Open-market buybacks had ended on 1 April 2025. Average utilisation was 99.9 per cent of offer size.

Registered primary intermediaries on 31 March 2026: 240 merchant bankers, 80 registrars, 60 bankers to an issue, 25 debenture trustees.

## Secondary market, demat, and households

Nifty 50's closing high in the chapter is 26,328.6 on 2 January 2026. The index then fell 15.2 per cent from that peak. The financial-year change was minus 5.1 per cent, or minus 13.9 per cent in US dollars. MSCI Emerging Markets rose 24.7 per cent. MSCI World rose 17.2 per cent. Market capitalisation was ₹411.6 trillion, also stated as ₹411.6 lakh crore. India is described as the fifth-largest stock market on that measure.

Domestic institutions bought a net ₹8.5 lakh crore of equities. Their ownership was 17 per cent. Foreign portfolio ownership was 15.8 per cent. Mutual funds contributed ₹6.4 trillion of the domestic buying. Chapter 2 states foreign portfolio net equity outflows of USD 19.7 billion, concentrated in the secondary market.

Demat accounts at NSDL and CDSL were 22.5 crore, with 18.8 crore opened since April 2020. Unique demat accounts were 12.2 crore, against 3.3 crore at the start of that comparison. The 31 March 2026 table shows 444 lakh accounts at NSDL, 1,801 lakh at CDSL, and 1,218 lakh unique accounts across both.

Mutual fund assets were ₹73.7 lakh crore, up 12.2 per cent. Unique mutual fund investors were 6.14 crore in Table 5.9, which the chapter rounds to 6.1 crore, up 13.2 per cent. Tier III was 3.39 crore of that total. SIP accounts were 10.45 crore. Average net monthly SIP inflow was ₹16,413 crore. SIP assets were ₹15,10,943 crore. Gross mutual fund mobilisation was ₹157.5 lakh crore. Net inflows were ₹7.4 lakh crore. Registered asset managers were 54, five of them public sector.

The handbook that sits beside the annual report, press release 47/2026, contains 153 tables, 85 annual and 68 quarterly or monthly. Use it when the question is a time series. Use the chapters above when the question is the year the narrative explains.
