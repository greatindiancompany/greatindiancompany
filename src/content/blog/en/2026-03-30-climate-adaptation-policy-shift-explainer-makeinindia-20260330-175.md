# How India's Climate Adaptation Policy Is Organised

India's adaptation policy is a stack of documents with different jobs. Reading them as one "climate announcement" hides which office can actually move.

## One plan, two tasks

The National Action Plan on Climate Change was released on 30 June 2008. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change FAQ of 1 December 2021 says the plan aims to help the country adapt and to make the development path more ecologically sustainable. That FAQ lists eight missions. Solar energy and enhanced energy efficiency are mainly mitigation instruments. Water, sustainable agriculture, the Himalayan ecosystem, Green India, and sustainable habitat carry most of the adaptation load. Strategic knowledge is there to support both.

On 21 July 2025, Press Information Bureau reported a Lok Sabha reply that describes nine missions. Human health is the area added to the earlier list. The reply says the missions have been revised to align with India's Nationally Determined Contribution, and that resilience work includes agriculture, water resources, coastal regions, health, and disaster management. A note that still says "eight missions" is using the 2021 FAQ. A note that says "nine" should cite 21 July 2025.

## Principles that still apply

The December 2021 FAQ states the plan's principles in plain terms: protect the poor through development that is sensitive to climate change; pursue growth and poverty alleviation without dropping ecological sustainability; prefer efficient demand-side management; deploy technologies for adaptation and mitigation; use market, regulatory, and voluntary mechanisms; and implement through civil society, local governments, and public-private partnerships. Those are design tests. The FAQ does not report a score against them.

## The state layer and the fund

The 21 July 2025 reply says 34 States and Union Territories have prepared State Action Plans on Climate Change for state-specific actions. The ministry posts those plans on its [state action plan page](https://moef.gov.in/state-action-plan-on-climate-change). A state plan is the translation layer. It is not a clearance.

The National Adaptation Fund for Climate Change is the project instrument. A PIB release of 5 February 2018 describes it as a Central Sector Scheme set up in 2015-16, for concrete adaptation activities in agriculture, animal husbandry, water, forestry, tourism, and related sectors. States prepare proposals with NABARD, the National Implementing Entity, and the State Steering Committee on Climate Change must approve a proposal before it is considered. A PIB reply of 5 August 2024 says the fund was made a non-scheme in November 2022. Briefing it as an open annual scheme window uses a pre-2022 description.

## Rules that sit beside the missions

Hazard planning is not inside a single mission PDF. The National Disaster Management Authority publishes separate guidelines for floods, urban floods, drought, cyclones, heat-wave action plans, and glacial-lake outburst floods. On the coast, a PIB release of 3 February 2022 points to the Coastal Regulation Zone Notification, 2019, and to a hazard line demarcated for the mainland coast under the Integrated Coastal Zone Management project.

Mission LiFE, named in the July 2025 reply, is described there as a citizen contribution. It does not replace a state plan, a fund file, or a hazard guideline.
