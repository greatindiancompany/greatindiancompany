# The Pharma Capex the Government Has Actually Published

Pharma capex in India is discussed as if one national cycle number existed. The official record publishes something narrower and more useful: the cost of common infrastructure in three bulk-drug parks, the central grant ceiling, and the existence of private projects whose own capital cost is not in the parliamentary table.

## Three stacks, from the 6 February 2026 reply

Each approved park has a total project cost and, inside it, a common-infrastructure cost. Central assistance is limited to the lower of ₹1,000 crore or 70 percent of that common-infrastructure cost (90 percent in Himachal Pradesh and the other hilly and North-Eastern cases the reply names). The reply also states a grant-in-aid of ₹1,000 crore to each park. The scheme allocation is ₹3,000 crore, and the tenure runs through FY 2026–27.

Gujarat, at Jambusar in Bharuch, shows a total project cost of ₹2,507.02 crore and common infrastructure of ₹1,457.01 crore, on 2,015.02 acres, through the Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation. The gap between total project cost and common-infrastructure cost is other physical infrastructure in the state project. It is still a park number. It is not the sum of tenants’ reactor halls.

Himachal Pradesh, at Haroli in Una, shows ₹1,923 crore total and ₹1,118.46 crore of common infrastructure, on 1,405.01 acres, through Himachal Pradesh Bulk Drug Park Infrastructure Ltd. Andhra Pradesh, at Nakkapalli in Anakapalli, shows ₹1,876.66 crore total and ₹1,438.89 crore of common infrastructure, on 2,001.80 acres, through Andhra Pradesh Bulk Drug Infrastructure Corporation.

Add those three total project costs and you have a public park-programme figure. Do not relabel it “Indian pharma capex.” Company plants approved under the PLI sit outside those totals unless a specific project disclosure puts them inside a park.

## What the PLI side does and does not capitalise

The March 2020 cabinet decision authorises ₹6,940 crore for the bulk-drug incentive over eight years. That rupee figure is an incentive envelope on incremental sales, not a grant equal to plant cost. The February 2026 reply counts 48 approved greenfield projects for 33 bulk drugs. It does not publish the capital expenditure of those 48 projects. The pharmaceuticals PLI envelope is ₹15,000 crore for 55 approved applicants, greenfield and brownfield. That envelope is also an incentive pool, grouped in the 3 March 2021 gazette by the applicant’s FY 2019–20 global manufacturing revenue. Brownfield approvals can be incremental equipment on an existing site. Treating every approval as a new campus overstates the building cycle.

The cabinet text of 2020 did attach an expectation of ₹46,400 crore of incremental sales to the bulk-drug incentive. Sales are not capex. A cycle watch that needs investment committed by approved applicants should use the approved-applicant annexures and any later Department review that states investment, and should label the year of that annexure. The schemes page lists an approved-applicant file for the bulk-drug PLI dated 6 November 2023 and one for the pharmaceuticals PLI dated 3 May 2023. Read the file before quoting a commitment.

## Where the spending is visible this year

The February 2026 stage notes are a capex-progress report of a specific kind. Gujarat has awarded civil and utility packages, including effluent treatment, solvent recovery, and disposal facilities, and describes civil works as an advanced stage of completion. Andhra Pradesh has awarded infrastructure tenders and was still acquiring additional land, with environmental clearance for that land in January 2026. Himachal Pradesh has awarded road and water works and has floated, rather than completed, the zero-liquid-discharge and steam tender. Spending follows awarded contracts. A floated tender is a procurement step.

For plants outside the parks, the Department’s Strengthening of Pharmaceuticals Industry scheme, including the revamped technology-upgradation assistance tracked on the schemes page, is a separate capital instrument aimed at existing industry and common facilities. Its guidelines, not the park table, are the source for that subsidy design. Revised Schedule M, notified by the health ministry on 28 December 2023, is a reason many existing units have a compliance capex plan. The gazette sets the manufacturing-practice standard. It does not price the upgrade.

Watch the next official release of park instalments and the next applicant list. Those two documents move the only national capex picture that is currently public.
