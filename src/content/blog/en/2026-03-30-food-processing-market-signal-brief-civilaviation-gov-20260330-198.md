# Reading the food processing market from official series

Food processing in India throws off several official percentages that sound like one market. They are not. Gross value added, the Annual Survey of Industries, foreign direct investment, agri-food exports, and the APEDA processed basket each answer a different question. This brief is a way to read them together without adding them up.

## Value added has outgrown farming, on the ministry's constant-price series

The ministry's Annual Report for 2024-25 says that over the nine years ending 2023-24, food processing grew at an average annual rate of about 6.55 percent, against about 4.43 percent for agriculture and allied activities, both at 2011-12 prices. In 2022-23 the sector was 7.93 percent of manufacturing GVA and 8.45 percent of GVA in agriculture, again at those constant prices.

The Year End Review of 9 January 2026 gives the level, not the share: GVA in food processing rose from Rs 1.34 lakh crore in 2014-15 to Rs 2.24 lakh crore in 2023-24, on first revised estimates. A rising level and a single-year share can move differently. Use the 7.93 percent figure only for 2022-23, and the rupee levels only for the years the review names.

## Employment signals a large organised base and a larger unincorporated one

On the Annual Survey of Industries for 2022-23, as reprinted by the ministry, registered food processing engaged 22.96 lakh persons, 12.41 percent of employment in registered manufacturing. The same report puts unincorporated food processing at 46.57 lakh workers in 2022-23, 15.21 percent of unregistered manufacturing employment, from the Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises.

The January 2026 review cites a later ASI year. It says food processing accounted for 12.83 percent of employment in the registered or organised sector in ASI 2023-24. It does not restate the headcount. A one-year move from 12.41 to 12.83 percent is a share of a changing manufacturing total, not proof that 0.42 percent of India's workforce changed jobs.

Registered factories numbered 42,801 in ASI 2022-23. Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Punjab, and Maharashtra hold the largest shares of those factories. That is a plant map, not a consumption map.

## Exports need two denominators

The annual report puts agri-food exports, including processed food, at US$46.43 billion in 2023-24, about 11.7 percent of India's merchandise exports of US$395.39 billion that year.

The January 2026 review says the share of processed food inside agri-food exports rose from 13.7 percent in 2014-15 to 20.4 percent in 2024-25. That is a composition shift inside farm and food exports. It is not a claim that processed food is a fifth of all Indian exports.

APEDA's administrative report for 2024-25 uses a third basket: products APEDA monitors. Processed value-added products in that basket were US$8,222 million in 2024-25, 28.67 percent of APEDA product exports of US$28,682 million. The largest lines inside that processed group were miscellaneous preparations (US$1,477 million), cereal preparations (US$934 million), processed vegetables (US$897 million), pulses (US$855 million), groundnuts (US$795 million), and processed fruits, juices, and nuts (US$722 million). APEDA cites DGCIS as the source for these trade figures. Pulses and groundnuts sit in APEDA's processed value-added table; a reader who pictures only packaged snacks will mis-read the US$8.2 billion.

Farmer Connect, an APEDA portal, reports a wider 2024-25 picture that should not be forced into the same cell: agricultural products of US$51.91 billion, 11.86 percent of merchandise exports of US$437.70 billion, with APEDA-monitored products at US$27.90 billion on provisional-or-PC data. When two APEDA pages differ by a few hundred million dollars, quote the page and the label rather than averaging them.

## Investment inflow is a stock over a decade, not a quarterly print

The Year End Review says the sector attracted US$7.33 billion of FDI equity inflow from April 2014 to March 2025. The annual report states the policy that sits under that flow: 100 percent FDI on the automatic route for food processing, subject to sectoral rules, and 100 percent FDI on the government route for trading, including e-commerce, in food products manufactured or produced in India. The second permission is about selling Indian-made food, including online. It is not a blanket approval for any food retail.

## What a market signal is, on this evidence

The usable signals are these. Processing value added has grown faster than farming on the ministry's nine-year average. Organised factories are a modest share of units next to the unincorporated workforce. Processed food is a rising slice of agri-food exports, while APEDA's own processed table is a defined list with DGCIS values. Foreign equity is permitted on two different routes and has a published decade total. None of these series is a retail same-store number, and none of them is a forecast.
