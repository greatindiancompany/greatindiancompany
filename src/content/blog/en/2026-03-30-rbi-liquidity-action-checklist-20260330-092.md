An operator tracking rupee liquidity needs a fixed order of Reserve Bank pages and a refusal to mix their definitions. The pages below are the ones that carried the September 2026 prints. The questions under each page are the ones those prints can answer.

## Each morning, the money-market release

The release “Money Market Operations” is the daily sheet. The issue dated 21 September 2026 covers 20 September. Read it in this order.

1. Check the calendar before the volumes. 20 September 2026 was a Sunday. Overnight market volume is reported at zero, and the call, triparty and market repo lines are blank for that reason. A zero call volume on a Sunday is not a frozen market.
2. Read the standing facilities. That day’s marginal standing facility is ₹82 crore at 5.50 per cent. The standing deposit facility is ₹91,747 crore at 5.00 per cent. Net liquidity from the day’s operations is an absorption of ₹91,665 crore. Placement at the floor, rather than borrowing at the ceiling, is the signal in those two lines.
3. Read outstanding variable rate operations, not only the day’s auction. On 20 September the outstanding reverse repos were ₹2,22,629 crore maturing 21 September, and ₹52,449 crore plus ₹2,39,276 crore maturing 7 October, each at a 5.24 per cent cut-off. Net liquidity including outstanding operations was an absorption of ₹6,04,964.24 crore.
4. Read the reserve and government lines at the foot. Cash balances of scheduled commercial banks with the Reserve Bank were ₹8,15,649.80 crore on 20 September. The average daily cash reserve requirement for the fortnight ending 30 September was ₹8,21,989 crore. The requirement is a fortnightly average. The Centre’s surplus cash balance reckoned for auction on 18 September was zero. Net durable liquidity as on 31 August was a surplus of ₹10,66,303 crore.

Keep the durable stock and the day’s net absorption as separate lines. The footnote on the release defines net liquidity as repo plus marginal standing facility plus standing liquidity facility, minus reverse repo, minus standing deposit facility.

## When an auction is announced, read the notice before the result

The framework of 30 September 2025 says the Bank will give at least one day’s notice of a variable rate operation, and may still announce on the same day when circumstances warrant. The notice carries the notified amount, the tenor, the window and the reversal date. The result carries offers, acceptance and the cut-off.

The 3-day variable rate reverse repo on 18 September was notified for ₹2,25,000 crore. Offers and acceptance were both ₹2,22,629 crore. The cut-off and the weighted average were 5.24 per cent. Compare that cut-off with the repo rate of 5.25 per cent. A cut-off one basis point under the policy rate is absorption priced near the middle of the corridor, not at the 5.00 per cent floor. The result release is Press Release 2026-2027/1142.

## Once a week, the statistical supplement

The supplement dated 18 September 2026 is the rate page. For the week ended 11 September, read the corridor and the reserve ratios before any commentary: repo 5.25, standing deposit facility 5.00, marginal standing facility 5.50, cash reserve ratio 3.00, statutory liquidity ratio 18.00. Then read the weighted average call rate, 4.97 per cent for that week, against the floor. Then the 91-day and 364-day bill yields, 5.21 and 5.91, and the 10-year par yield, 7.05. The carded savings, term deposit, MCLR and base-rate ranges on the same page are the retail and loan-card print. The call rate beside them is the dealing-room print.

The extract dated 11 September is the flow page. Use the liquidity-operations table for the split between variable rate reverse repo, the standing deposit facility and outright open market operations. In the dates 31 August to 6 September the open market sale and purchase columns are blank. Use the government loan lines for the frictional piece: state government loans and advances were ₹27,940 crore on 4 September, up ₹24,824 crore on the week, and central government loans were zero. Use the banking table for deposits and credit as on 31 August, and the money-stock table for currency with the public.

A footnote on the rate page records that, from 15 December 2025, a fortnight ends on the 15th and the last calendar day of the month. Cash reserve maintenance follows that fortnight. Do not compare a new fortnight’s average with an alternate-Friday fortnight from 2024 as if the windows matched.

## At each policy meeting, three sentences

The resolution states the repo rate, the two walls and the stance. The current setting, from the meeting of 3–5 August 2026, is 5.25 per cent, walls at 5.00 and 5.50, stance neutral. The next meeting is 5–7 October 2026. The Governor’s statement, published in the Bulletin, is where the average daily surplus under the liquidity adjustment facility is given in lakh crore, separate from the durable-liquidity stock on the daily release. The August statement puts that average at ₹1.0 lakh crore since the June meeting, and states the objective as aligning the weighted average call rate with the repo rate.

A week’s work is then short. Did the call rate sit inside 5.00 to 5.50, and how far from 5.25? Was the day’s net injection positive or negative? Is the reverse repo book growing or maturing? Did state or central cash move? Did the cash reserve balance sit near the fortnight’s average requirement? Those five questions use only lines the Reserve Bank already totals.
