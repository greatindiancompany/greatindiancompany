The policy repo rate is not a staff recommendation that the Governor announces. Under the Reserve Bank of India Act it is a vote of the Monetary Policy Committee. The 3 to 5 August 2026 meeting is a clean example, because the rate did not change and the minutes show exactly what each member was voting on.

## Who sits, and what they vote on

Section 45ZB of the Act constitutes the committee. The August minutes, published under Section 45ZL, name the six people who attended the 62nd meeting. Sanjay Malhotra, Governor, chaired it. The other internal members were Poonam Gupta, Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, and Indranil Bhattacharyya, the Executive Director nominated by the Central Board. The external members were Nagesh Kumar, Saugata Bhattacharya and Ram Singh.

The committee’s job is to set the policy repo rate required to achieve the inflation target, keeping growth in mind. The target in force, renewed by Gazette notification on 25 March 2026, is 4 per cent consumer-price inflation, with a tolerance band from 2 to 6 per cent, through 31 March 2031. At the 5 August press conference the Governor said the target is headline inflation. Core inflation is a way to read the underlying path. It is not a second target.

Votes are by majority. The Governor has a casting vote if the committee ties. In the decade of the framework that casting vote has not been needed. The August vote did not need it either. All six members voted to keep the repo rate at 5.25 per cent. The minutes, required on the fourteenth day, were published on 19 August 2026. They contain the resolution, the vote of each member, and each member’s statement.

## What “5.25 per cent, neutral” actually fixes

The repo rate is the rate under the liquidity adjustment facility. Once it is set, two other rates follow in this operating framework. The standing deposit facility rate sits 25 basis points below the repo, at 5.00 per cent. The marginal standing facility rate and the Bank Rate sit 25 basis points above it, at 5.50 per cent. The August resolution states those three consequences in the same paragraph as the vote.

The stance is a separate sentence. The committee “decided to continue with the neutral stance.” Neutral is not a forecast that the next move is zero. In her August statement Gupta says a neutral stance signals that the future course is data dependent. Bhattacharyya says a pause preserves flexibility on timing and does not imply an extended pause. The Governor, at the press conference, refused the labels dovish and hawkish and said the rate was the one that fitted the growth-inflation dynamics and the outlook the committee had published.

The rate the market is supposed to track is the weighted average call rate. The Governor’s 5 August statement says liquidity operations will be run so that this call rate lines up with the repo rate. In the August Bulletin that call rate averaged 5.13 per cent up to 21 August, below the repo rate, because the liquidity surplus had widened. The corridor can be unchanged while the call rate drifts inside it. That drift is an operating outcome, not a second vote.

## What the committee publishes, and what it does not decide

Each meeting produces a resolution the same day, with the rate, the stance, the growth projection and the inflation projection. The August resolution projects real GDP growth for 2026-27 at 6.7 per cent and CPI inflation at 5.0 per cent, with inflation peaking at 5.9 per cent in the third quarter. Those projections are the committee’s conditional intermediate picture. They are not a promise.

Fourteen days later the minutes show the disagreement that a unanimous vote can hide. Gupta writes that the scope for further easing does not seem to exist, and that a case for a hike may emerge during the year. Malhotra writes that a supply shock calls for a rate response when it generalises, unanchors expectations, or persists, and that this evidence is limited so far. A reader who has only the resolution will see a hold. A reader who has the minutes will see the tests each member is using for October.

Two things that happened on 5 August were not the committee’s rate vote. The Governor announced draft guidelines on resuming licences for urban co-operative banks, a review of the credit monitoring arrangement for rural co-operative banks, and a proposal to harmonise the rules on interest rates on advances across regulated entities. Those are Reserve Bank measures announced with the statement. They are not MPC resolutions.

The meeting calendar for 2026-27 is fixed: 6–8 April, 3–5 June, 3–5 August, 5–7 October, 2–4 December, and 3–5 February 2027. A Monetary Policy Report is published twice a year. The April 2026 report is the current one. The October meeting is the next time the rate, the stance and the projections can change together.
