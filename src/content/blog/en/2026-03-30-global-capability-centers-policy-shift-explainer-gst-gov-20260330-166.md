# The Policy Stack Around India's Captive Technology Hubs

A captive hub in India is touched by foreign-investment policy, by the way software exports are measured, and by the personal-data law. Those are separate instruments. Treating them as one "GCC policy" hides which document actually binds the company.

## Entry is an FDI question

The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade maintains the consolidated FDI policy. Its automatic route is the route on which a person resident outside India does not need prior approval of the Reserve Bank or the Central Government. The [Make in India FDI page](http://www.makeinindia.com/policy/foreign-direct-investment) lists **IT & BPM** among activities open to **100 percent** foreign investment on the automatic route, and it points readers back to DPIIT for the text that governs.

On 20 July 2026 the Minister of State for Finance told the Lok Sabha that most sectors, other than certain strategically important ones, are open for 100 percent FDI on the automatic route, and that more than **90 percent** of FDI inflow is received on that route. The answer lists the National Single Window System among the measures the government relies on. DPIIT's annual report describes that system, at nsws.gov.in, as the online interface for permissions and as a portal used to seek government approval for FDI where approval is still required.

A captive set up as an Indian company in IT or business-process work will usually meet the automatic-route description. It still has to be checked against the current sectoral text, security conditions, and any case the policy puts on the government route, including beneficial-ownership cases. The computer-software equity series is the statistical shadow of this policy, not the policy itself. In the DPIIT factsheet reproduced in that Lok Sabha answer, Computer Software & Hardware took **USD 13,946 million** of equity in 2025-26.

## Promotion infrastructure is STPI, under MeitY

Software Technology Parks of India is an autonomous society under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. It was registered on 5 June 1991 to run the Software Technology Park and Electronics Hardware Technology Park schemes, to provide data communication, and to support exporters. Its annual report for 2024-25 records **67** centres in operation, **59** of them in Tier II and Tier III cities, and exports by STPI-registered IT/ITeS units of **₹10,69,270.59 crore** in 2024-25, marked tentative, up **13.35 percent** from **₹9,43,312.66 crore**.

That is an export-facilitation and incubation network. Registration under the STP scheme, or use of STPI for Softex attestation, is a compliance and infrastructure choice. It is not a licence that defines a company as a global capability centre.

STPI has also published a note arguing for a comprehensive national policy on global capability centres. The documents used here do not include a notified statute that creates a separate legal category called a GCC.

## Two meanings of the same letters

MeitY and STPI also use "GCC" for **Government Community Cloud**: a cloud stack for government workloads, with empanelment rules and data-centre racks. A tender, a corrigendum, or a status report that says GCC in that sense is about community cloud, not about a multinational's captive engineering centre. Policy readers should expand the letters before they file the document.

## Data protection is the binding new conduct rule

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act 22 of 2023) received assent on 11 August 2023. Section 3 applies it to processing of digital personal data inside India, and also to processing outside India when that processing is connected with offering goods or services to people in India. A captive that processes personal data on Indian soil is inside that clause whether or not the parent sells to Indian customers.

Section 16 lets the Central Government restrict, by notification, transfers of personal data for processing to specified countries. It also leaves stricter laws in force. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, were notified on 13 November 2025. Their commencement is staggered: Board-related rules from publication, Consent Manager registration one year later, and the operative notice, security, rights, cross-border, and Significant Data Fiduciary rules eighteen months after publication. The calendar and the localisation clause are set out in the [regulatory outlook](/blog/global-capability-centers-regulatory-outlook-gst-gov-20260330-446).

## What a policy shift would look like

A real shift would be a notified change in one of these instruments: a press note amending the FDI route, a new RBI reporting line that separates captives, an order under the data-protection rules naming countries or data categories, or a statute that defines a captive centre. A speech that repeats an industry centre-count is not that instrument. The count in the PIB note of 25 February 2025 is a ministerial highlight, and it sits outside this stack.
