# Four official series for Indian consumer demand

One number cannot stand in for Indian retail demand. Four official series cover four different questions. Each figure below is tied to the release that printed it.

## Private final consumption expenditure

Question: how much did households consume in the national accounts, and how fast did that volume grow?

Release: MoSPI press note, 31 August 2026, base year 2022-23. In April–June 2026-27, real PFCE grew 7.1% to ₹44,74,278 crore. Nominal PFCE was ₹49,08,179 crore, or 55.6% of nominal GDP. The estimate is indicator-based and open to revision. The next quarter is scheduled for 30 November 2026.

PFCE is the spending aggregate. It is not turnover of organised retail chains.

## Consumer Price Index

Question: how fast did the retail basket’s price rise?

Release: NSO, August 2026, base 2024=100, provisional. Combined inflation was 4.82%. Rural was 5.23% and urban was 4.31%. Food inflation (CFPI) was 5.95%. July’s final combined print was 4.45%. September’s index is due on 12 October 2026 or the next working day.

CPI is a price change. It is not a statement that households bought more goods.

## Household Consumption Expenditure Survey

Question: what does a household spend per person, and how does that differ by place?

Release: HCES 2023-24 factsheet, fieldwork August 2023–July 2024. Without imputing free welfare items, average MPCE was ₹4,122 rural and ₹6,996 urban. Food was 47.04% of the rural budget and 39.68% of the urban budget. Sikkim was the highest state on this set (₹9,377 rural, ₹13,927 urban). Chhattisgarh was the lowest (₹2,739 rural, ₹4,927 urban). The factsheet also publishes a second set that imputes those free items. The two sets are not interchangeable.

## Unincorporated trade

Question: how large is the non-corporate shop economy?

Release: ASUSE 2025 press note, 6 May 2026, reference period January–December 2025. Other retail trade was 26.49% of unincorporated non-agricultural establishments and 27.55% of their workers. The sector as a whole had 7.92 crore establishments and about 12.81 crore workers. Companies and limited liability partnerships are outside the survey. A companion labour-force fact, from PLFS 2025, puts the wider group “trade, hotel and restaurant” at 12.9% of usual-status workers. That group includes accommodation and food service.

A one-page reading uses all four and refuses to average them. Volume is PFCE. Price is CPI. The household budget is HCES. The unincorporated shop is ASUSE.
