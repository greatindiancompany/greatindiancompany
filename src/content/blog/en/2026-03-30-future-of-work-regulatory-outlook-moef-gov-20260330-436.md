# Future of Work in India: Regulatory Outlook for Employers

## Already on the book

From 21 November 2025, four codes are in force: the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. The Ministry of Labour and Employment presented them as the rationalisation of 29 central labour laws.

A Lok Sabha reply on 20 July 2026 states that Central Rules under the codes were notified on 8 May 2026. For an establishment whose appropriate government is the Centre, the outlook is to read the notified rules.

## Geography still splits the rulebook

The same reply says states and Union Territories must frame rules because labour is concurrent, that most had finalised or published them, including pre-published drafts, and that a few were still doing so. A national outlook overstates how settled shop-floor rules are until that state's gazette is read. The 21 November 2025 announcement said existing Act rules would continue during the transition. That sentence remains the relevant one where a state rule is not yet the operative text.

## Recognition, then a scheme

Gig work, platform work, and aggregators are defined. The ministry's note sets aggregator contributions at 1 to 2 percent of annual turnover, capped at 5 percent of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers, and describes an Aadhaar-linked Universal Account Number. The 20 July 2026 reply says those definitions exist for formulating schemes on life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity, and old-age protection. A platform's outlook is to budget the contribution design and to track which scheme notification has been issued. e-Shram, launched by the ministry on 26 August 2021, is the registration rail described for unorganised workers, including gig and platform workers.

The ministry's comparison describes ESIC as voluntary below 10 employees and mandatory for a hazardous process even with one employee, on a pan-India frame. That is a coverage rule for establishment design.

## Duties already specific enough to operate

Appointment letters for workers. A national floor wage. Overtime, in the central-sphere handbook, at not less than twice the normal wage where a minimum wage is fixed and hours run past the normal limit. Women permitted to work at night and in all types of work, with consent and required safety measures. Safety committees where an establishment has 500 or more workers. An inspector-cum-facilitator role, which the ministry describes as guidance and compliance support. A National OSH Board for harmonised safety standards.

For IT and ITES, the sector note adds salary release by the 7th of the month.

## The live variables

Statutes and central rules are published. State rules and gig-scheme notifications are the open items. An employment forecast is not one of those items.
