# What is already in force in labour law, and what still needs a notification

The regulatory question in September 2026 is no longer whether India will consolidate its central labour laws. That consolidation was given effect on 21 November 2025. The question is which duties are live because the statute says so, and which duties are still a rate, a scheme, or a date that a government has to notify.

## The layer that has commenced

The Ministry of Labour and Employment announced on 21 November 2025 that the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 were being made effective that day, rationalising 29 central laws. The Economic Survey 2025–26, in its preface, records the same step and adds that rules were expected to be in place over the following months. Paragraph 1.40 of the Survey describes the consolidation as a simplification of compliance, a widening of security, and a recognition of gig and platform workers so they can be registered and included in social-security schemes.

The Ministry's commencement note lists design changes it presents as part of that effective framework. Minimum wages are a right for all workers, not only for scheduled employments. Appointment letters are mandatory. Fixed-term employees get benefits equal to permanent workers and qualify for gratuity after one year. Women may work at night and in all types of work, subject to consent and safety measures. Workers above 40 are to receive a free annual health check. ESIC is described as pan-India, voluntary below 10 employees and mandatory for hazardous processes even with one employee. Compliance is described as a single registration, a single licence, and a single return.

Those are the items a compliance calendar should already contain, read against the actual commencement notifications. They are not a reason to ignore the next paragraph of the same press note.

## The layer the commencement note itself left open

The 21 November note says the government will engage the public while it frames rules, regulations, and schemes under the codes. During the transition, the relevant provisions of the existing labour Acts and their rules, regulations, notifications, standards, and schemes continue. "The codes are in force" and "every old rule has been repealed in operation" are not the same sentence. Where a matter still depends on a rule, the old instrument is the one the Ministry told readers would continue.

For gig and platform work the statute is more specific than the press note. Section 114 of the Code on Social Security allows schemes for life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity, old-age protection, and creche. Section 114(4) tells aggregators in the Seventh Schedule to pay a notified rate of at least 1 percent and at most 2 percent of annual turnover, excluding central taxes, levies, and cess, with a cap of 5 percent of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers. Section 114(5) then says the date of commencement of that contribution shall be notified by the Centre. A code that is effective, and a levy whose start date is still a separate notification, can both be true. Outlook writing that skips section 114(5) will tell platforms they are already in default for a sum nobody has been told to start paying.

The Seventh Schedule, which identifies who is in that contribution, covers ride sharing, food and grocery delivery, logistics, e-marketplaces, professional services, healthcare, travel and hospitality, content and media, and any other goods and services platform. Section 152 lets the Centre amend that schedule by notification. The list is stable until it is not, and the amendment path is public.

## State law is ahead of the Centre in one state and behind a notification in another

Karnataka's Platform Based Gig Workers (Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2025 deems itself in force from 30 May 2025, months before the central codes' commencement date. Its welfare fee, between 1 and 5 percent of payout per transaction, had to be notified within six months of commencement. The Act also builds a board from a date the state appoints, requires aggregator registration within 45 days of commencement, and requires transparency for automated monitoring systems. As of this brief, the legal fact to track is whether those notifications and the board's appointment are on the gazette record. The Act's text does not complete that step by itself.

Rajasthan's 2023 Act is drafted differently. Section 1(3) brings it into force on a date the state notifies. The fee rate is also a notification, expressed as a percent of transaction value excluding tax. The outlook for Rajasthan is binary in a way Karnataka's is not: find the commencement notification, or do not describe the Act as operating.

The same binary applies elsewhere, and the list is longer than these two. Bihar's Act 8 of 2025 comes into force on a notified date, and different provisions may be appointed on different dates. Telangana's Act 21 of 2026 was appointed to come into force on 2 June 2026. An outlook that names only Rajasthan and Karnataka will miss a statute that is already in force and another that may still be waiting on its notification.

## How to read the next year of Gazettes

Three kinds of paper change the outlook. A rule under one of the four codes replaces the transition fallback for that subject. A notification under section 114(5), together with a notified rate under section 114(4), starts the central aggregator contribution. A state notification starts or prices a welfare fee, and in Karnataka's case determines how that fee is reconciled with the central contribution.

Social-security coverage, which the Ministry put at more than 64 percent in 2025 against about 19 percent in 2015, is a statement about the breadth of schemes already counted. It is not evidence that the gig-worker scheme in section 114 has been funded. The outlook is a stack: codes effective, rules and schemes still the Ministry's own unfinished list as of the Economic Survey's early-2026 text, one state Act deemed in force, another waiting on its commencement date, and a central gig levy that the statute refuses to start until a date is notified.
