# Risks that can move India’s export trade

A scenario map with made-up probabilities is not a risk tool. The risks below are the ones named in a dated official note, or visible as a concentration in a dated table. What happens next is not scored here.

## Routing and freight on the West Asia corridor

On 23 March 2026 the government said developments in West Asia had changed maritime routing and transit, raised logistics costs, and disturbed shipping schedules for consignments moving to or through the region. The response in that note was not a new logistics subsidy. It was restoration of RoDTEP rates and value caps, withdrawing the 50 percent restriction of 23 February 2026, for eligible products from 23 March 2026. The risk that was official, then, was a freight and schedule shock large enough to justify giving back a rebate cut within a month.

The August 2026 destination annex does not prove that shock is still the cause of any one market. It does show the Gulf readings. Merchandise exports to the United Arab Emirates were US$ 2,378 million in August 2026, down 26.1 percent from US$ 3,217 million a year earlier. Saudi Arabia was US$ 625 million, down 17.5 percent from US$ 757 million. April–August exports to the UAE were down 10.6 percent. A founder who ships through that corridor should watch freight invoices and the RoDTEP cap together. The cap is a rupee ceiling. A higher freight bill is not rebated by RoDTEP, which remits embedded duties and taxes, not ocean freight.

## Concentration in the buyer, not only in the commodity

In the same annex, the United States accounted for 19.8 percent of April–August 2026–27 merchandise exports, at US$ 42,790 million, up 6.2 percent from a year earlier. August alone was up 21.8 percent. A large, growing buyer is a commercial fact and a concentration fact. The annex also shows sharp gains to Singapore (up 96.6 percent in April–August) and China (up 38.7 percent). Diversification in the table is real. It has not removed the US share.

NITI Aayog’s 16 September 2026 Trade Watch note said exports to FTA partners rose 36.3 percent and imports from them 10.0 percent in that edition’s assessment, and that Tanzania and South Africa had entered the top ten markets alongside strong growth to Singapore. Preferential agreements change the tariff. They do not, by themselves, change a 19.8 percent share in five months.

## The goods deficit can widen while the current account looks calm

In April–June 2026–27 the goods deficit was US$ 86.1 billion, against US$ 68.9 billion a year earlier. Net services were US$ 51.6 billion. The current account deficit was US$ 4.2 billion, 0.5 percent of GDP. The risk for an exporter is not the current account ratio. It is a policy or currency reaction if the goods gap keeps widening, and a financing risk if services or remittances slow. Portfolio investment was a net outflow of US$ 9.6 billion in that quarter, and reserves fell US$ 8.1 billion on a BoP basis. Those are financial-account facts sitting next to the trade facts. They are not a forecast of the rupee.

## A border measure on metals

The Trade Watch note flags the European Union’s carbon border adjustment as a reason to improve the competitiveness of steel and aluminium exports. Metals exports were US$ 34.8 billion in 2025, and iron and steel, articles of those metals, and aluminium were about 78 percent of that. A carbon measure at the border is a cost that a rebate of embedded Indian duties does not cancel. Firms in that slice need the EU rule and the Indian energy and process data, not another RoDTEP percentage.
