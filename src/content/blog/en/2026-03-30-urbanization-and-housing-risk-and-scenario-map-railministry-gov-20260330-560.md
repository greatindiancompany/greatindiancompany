# Urban Housing Risks That Follow from Published Rules

## A scenario needs a condition, not a probability

Nothing in the MoHUA, PIB, or Census material used here assigns a probability to house prices, to migration, or to a state’s reform compliance. A useful scenario is a published condition that, if it holds, changes a decision. Four of those conditions are already on the page.

## The baseline risk: planning on 2011 until 2027 tables exist

MoHUA’s urban page still describes 31.16 percent urban as on the 2011 table, 377.1 million people, and a decade in which census towns, not statutory towns, supplied most of the 2,774 new towns. Any site, tariff, or “urban demand” model that needs a 2026 population is using something other than the official urban count. Census 2027’s reference date is 1 March 2027, with Phase I built to record housing condition and amenities during 2026. The risk is not that the census might happen. PIB has published the decision and the ₹11,718.24 crore outlay. The risk is acting, before the tables, as if a newer rate were already official, or acting, after the tables, as if a census-town reclassification were the same thing as a municipal boundary change.

## The subsidy risk: the loan can fall out of the release test

Under the Interest Subsidy Scheme, PIB’s 24 July 2025 text pays the subsidy in five yearly instalments only while the loan is active and more than 50 percent of principal is outstanding. A borrower who prepays, or a lender who structures a short amortisation, can lose later instalments even though the sanction looked eligible. Income, loan size, property value, and carpet area are eligibility risks at origination. The principal test is a risk during the life of the loan. The closed CLSS book, ₹58,885 crore already released to 25,04,220 beneficiaries, does not carry this forward. Those accounts are finished.

A second subsidy risk is administrative and dated only to December 2024. The PIB note then recorded in-principle approval for more than 6 lakh houses under the 2.0 scheme, from 29 States and Union Territories that had signed memoranda, and it required a demand survey by 31 March 2025 and an affordable housing policy by March 2025. A state that never signed, or never surveyed, is not in that count. This brief does not know, from a later national table, how that list moved. The scenario to underwrite is “show the memorandum and the sanction order,” not “the crore is evenly spread.”

## The mission-closure risk: assets without a central budget

The Smart Cities page says the central budget closed on 31 March 2025 and that no new city will be added. Command centres exist in all 100 cities. Advisory No. 27 of 10 June 2025 asks states to decide the future of the special purpose vehicles. The risk for a vendor is a contract that assumed a sixth extension of central funding. The reply of 6 August 2026 shows the pattern of leftover work is local: Silvassa, Port Blair, and Kavaratti were still well short of full project completion on the 31 March 2026 annex, while Indore, Surat, and Pune were at 100 percent. A national “97 percent done” line, which is the minister’s 7,790 of 8,064, is true and still hides a city that has not finished.

AMRUT 2.0 adds a reform risk. From the third year, the October 2021 guidelines tie uninterrupted funds to property tax and user charges. Non-revenue water below 20 percent, and reuse targets of 20 percent of city demand and 40 percent of industrial demand at state level, are published standards. Missing them is a funding and service risk the guideline itself describes. The indicative outlay’s printed period ended with financial year 2025-26.

## The legal risk: fifty registers, one Act

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act is central. Authorities, rules, and registers are state-level. Section 4’s requirement that seventy percent of allottee collections stay in a separate scheduled-bank account is a promoter risk wherever registration applies. It is also a buyer risk where a project that should be registered is not. MoHUA’s advisory of 31 July 2026 tells authorities they may add four months to registration and completion for registered projects whose completion date falls on or after 28 February 2026, citing force majeure. The scenario to underwrite is whether that state’s authority has issued the order. A four-month shift in a completion date is a timing fact. It is not a change in carpet area, in the separate-account rule, or in the subsidy caps.
