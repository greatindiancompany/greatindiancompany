# Four ways the women’s workforce figures get misread

The risk in this subject is less a missing dataset than a confident reading of the wrong cell. Each scenario below is a way a published number gets used as if it were a different published number. The remedy in each case is a label MoSPI or PIB has already printed.

## Treating the weekly quarter as a reversal of the annual rate

Calendar 2025 usual-status participation for women aged 15 and above is 40.0 percent. The current weekly status for that year is 34.3 percent. April–June 2026, on the weekly status, is 33.2 percent, down from 34.7 percent in January–March 2026. Rural women in that quarter were 37.2 percent, against 39.2 percent the quarter before.

A note that says women’s participation “fell from 40 percent to 33 percent” has compared a 365-day rate, including subsidiary work, with a seven-day quarter. Usual status counts someone whose economic activity runs 30 days or more alongside another principal activity. The weekly status asks about the last seven days. Seasonal rural work can leave the weekly rate and remain inside the annual rate. The April–June press note shows the quarterly dip in the rural female cell, with the urban female cell at 25.0 percent after 25.4 percent.

The check: if both numbers are weekly, compare them. If one is usual status, say so and do not subtract.

## Stitching 23.3 percent to 40.0 percent as one slope

The July 2017–June 2018 female usual-status rate was 23.3 percent. The July 2023–June 2024 rate was 41.7 percent. Those two sit on the same design, and the rise is in MoSPI’s table, year by year. The calendar-2025 rate of 40.0 percent sits on the redesigned sample. MoSPI’s endnote says the 2025 estimates may not be strictly comparable with earlier annual reports or with the calendar results it shows for 2022–2024.

A chart that draws a straight line from 23.3 to 40.0 reports a rise the Ministry has asked users not to treat as strictly continuous, and it also drops the 41.7 percent point that belongs to the old design’s last year. The honest mid-term sentence is the one in the trajectory brief: a large rise through 2023–24, then a 2025 level the Ministry calls stable against its 2024 comparison, with the break marked.

## Reading unemployment as the constraint

Usual-status unemployment for women in 2025 was 3.1 percent, the same as for men. Urban women were at 6.4 percent. Youth unemployment is higher: for persons aged 15–29 the usual-status rate was 9.9 percent, and for urban women in that age group it was 18.9 percent. Those cells matter for young urban jobseekers. They are not the reason the all-ages female participation rate is 40.0 percent rather than close to the male 79.1 percent.

People outside the labour force are not unemployed. Unemployed means in the labour force and without work. Among women outside the labour force, 44.4 percent named child care or personal commitments in home-making. The Time Use Survey for 2024 puts women participants at 289 minutes a day on unpaid domestic services and men at 88, with participation of 81.5 percent and 27.1 percent. A risk model that “solves” women’s participation by targeting the 3.1 percent unemployment rate is aimed at the smaller number.

## Adding EPFO women to e-Shram women

In 2024–25, EPFO recorded 26.9 lakh net female subscribers. e-Shram, in the five-year note, had 31.89 crore registrations, 54.28 percent of them women. e-Shram eligibility excludes EPFO members, ESIC members, and income-tax payers. The two stocks are defined to be disjoint. Adding the female EPFO flow to 54.28 percent of e-Shram produces a number neither system publishes.

A related version of the same error is to treat 20.0 crore women employed on usual status in 2025 and 16.4 crore women employed in the April–June 2026 week-status quarter as a loss of 3.6 crore jobs. The first is a year, including subsidiary work. The second is a quarter on a seven-day status. Both use MoHFW population projections. They are different employment concepts.

## What a careful scenario looks like

A forward scenario that stays inside the sources has branches MoSPI has already made visible, not probabilities nobody has published.

- Weekly rural participation moves with the agricultural calendar, as it did from 39.2 percent in January–March 2026 to 37.2 percent in April–June, while the annual usual-status rate stays near the 40 percent level of 2025.
- Urban weekly participation stays near 25 percent, which is where it has sat in the recent quarterly triplet, and urban female unemployment stays above the rural rate.
- The care reason, 44.4 percent, and the time-use gap, 289 minutes against 88, limit how far participation can rise while unpaid domestic time stays at the 2024 survey’s level.
- The regular-wage share of women workers, 18.2 percent overall and 9.3 percent in rural areas, rises or stalls independently of the participation headline.

Each branch can be confirmed or retired by the next bulletin’s labelled cell. None of them requires a figure that is not already in a MoSPI or PIB table.
