The market signal that MeitY and Parliament actually publish is electronics production and exports. It is a large assembly base. It is not a published figure for India's share of global wafer starts. This brief keeps those two ideas apart. The page is current to 24 September 2026.

## Two official snapshots of the same industry

On 26 April 2025, when the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme guidelines were launched, [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2124620) gave a closed financial-year series. Domestic production of electronic goods had gone from ₹1.90 lakh crore in 2014-15 to ₹9.52 lakh crore in 2023-24. Exports had gone from ₹0.38 lakh crore to ₹2.41 lakh crore. The note calls the production compound rate more than 17 percent and the export compound rate more than 20 percent. It says that in 2024-25 electronics became the third-largest exported commodity, and that India had become the world's second-largest mobile manufacturing country.

On 22 July 2026 the Minister of State answered Lok Sabha question 483 with a later pair of years. The [reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf) puts electronics production at about ₹1.9 lakh crore in 2014-15 and about ₹13.11 lakh crore in 2025-26, and electronics exports at about ₹38,000 crore and about ₹4.24 lakh crore. Mobile-phone production is about ₹18,000 crore then and about ₹6.27 lakh crore in 2025-26. Mobile-phone exports are about ₹1,500 crore then and about ₹2.59 lakh crore. The reply says India manufactures around 99.2 percent of the mobile phones sold in the country, and that mobile phones are the single largest exported item.

Read as a series, the 2023-24 production figure of ₹9.52 lakh crore and the 2025-26 figure of about ₹13.11 lakh crore are two dates, not a conflict. The same is true of exports, ₹2.41 lakh crore in 2023-24 and about ₹4.24 lakh crore in 2025-26. The later reply is the one to cite for 2025-26. The April 2025 note is the one to cite for the 2023-24 base and for the rank claims about export commodities and mobile manufacturing.

The reply's employment line is narrower than it first looks. It says industry estimates that electronics manufacturing now provides employment to nearly 25 lakh people. That is labeled as an industry estimate. It is also an electronics-manufacturing figure, not a headcount of the semiconductor plants.

## What this scale is made of

At the 26 April 2025 launch, the Minister described the sequence the government has been running: finished products first, then modules, then components, then the materials that go into components. He said finished goods account for 80 to 85 percent of the value chain. That sentence is the useful market signal inside the growth numbers. The export boom in the parliamentary table is concentrated in mobile phones. A phone exported from India can still carry an imported chip, an imported display, and an imported passive.

The schemes aimed at that gap are explicit about product lists. [PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/production-linked-incentive-scheme-pli-2-0-for-it-hardware-wM0MDOtQWa), approved on 17 May 2023 with an outlay of ₹17,000 crore, targets laptops, tablets, all-in-one PCs, servers, and ultra-small-form-factor devices. The [Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/electronics-component-manufacturing-scheme-UTM1IjMtQWa) targets display-module and camera-module sub-assemblies, passives, printed circuit boards, enclosures, and the capital goods used to make them. Those lists are the government's own statement of which parts of the bill of materials are still the opportunity.

## What the approved chip plants say they will serve

Cabinet releases name end markets. They do not size them. The 29 February 2024 approvals cover high-performance compute at 28 nm, power-management chips, automotive and consumer electronics, and telecom. The 12 August 2025 compound and packaging approvals name missiles and defence equipment, electric vehicles, railways, fast chargers, data-centre racks, solar inverters, and also mobile phones and set-top boxes. The 5 May 2026 display approval names televisions, commercial signage, phones and tablets, in-car displays, and micro-displays for glasses and watches. The customer list is wide. A wide list is not a forecast of offtake.

## The chip-market estimate that PIB labels as industry's

The [7 February 2026 PIB budget note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=1&PRID=2224839) says that, as per industry estimates, the Indian semiconductor market was about $38 billion in 2023, $45 billion to $50 billion in 2024-25, and was expected to reach $100 billion to $110 billion by 2030. Those sentences are in an official note, and the note attributes them to industry estimates. They are not the production census in the Lok Sabha reply. A pitch that quotes the $100 billion figure should say whose estimate it is, and should not place it next to ₹13.11 lakh crore of electronics production as if the two were the same statistic.

Approved investment is also not a sales number. About ₹1.64 lakh crore is the commitment attached to 12 manufacturing projects. It does not measure chips sold.
