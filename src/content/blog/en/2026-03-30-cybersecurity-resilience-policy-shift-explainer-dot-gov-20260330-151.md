# How India's cybersecurity duties came to be stacked

The useful way to read India's cybersecurity policy is as a stack, not as a single reform. Each layer names a different institution, a different trigger, and a different clock. A company that updates only one policy document will miss the others.

## The national incident layer

CERT-In says in its Annual Report 2023 that it has been operational since January 2004 and is designated under section 70B of the Information Technology Act, 2000 as the national agency for incident response. Its listed functions include collecting and sharing incident information, forecasts and alerts, emergency measures, coordination, and guidelines.

The directions of 28 April 2022 turned part of that mandate into a standing operating duty for service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations. Two duties are explicit in the direction text: report the cyber incidents listed in its annexure within six hours, and enable logs of ICT systems for a rolling 180 days, kept within Indian jurisdiction and produced to CERT-In with an incident report or when directed. Reporting channels named in the direction are email at incident@cert-in.org.in, phone 1800-11-4949 and fax 1800-11-6969.

That direction did not replace sector regulators. It sits underneath them.

## The critical-infrastructure layer is a different agency

The National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre, a unit of the National Technical Research Organisation, states that it was created under section 70A and designated as the national nodal agency for critical information infrastructure protection by a gazette notification of 16 January 2014. The Information Technology Act's explanation, as NCIIPC's identification guidance restates it, covers a computer resource whose incapacitation or destruction would have a debilitating impact on national security, economy, public health or safety. Section 70 allows the appropriate government to notify a protected system.

NCIIPC's public site groups its work under banking and financial services, power and energy, government, telecom, health, transport, and strategic and public enterprises. Its identification guidance says the basic responsibility for protecting a critical information infrastructure system lies with the agency that runs it. Frequency of ordinary malware incidents is not the test for this layer. Impact on those national functions is.

## Sector layers, written by the regulator that already supervises the firm

The Reserve Bank of India's Information Technology Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices Directions, 2023, were issued on 7 November 2023 and took effect on 1 April 2024. They consolidate IT governance, information-security risk, cyber incident response, business continuity and information-systems audit for commercial banks, specified NBFCs, credit information companies and named All India Financial Institutions. They tell regulated entities to notify CERT-In and the Reserve Bank of incidents as per regulatory requirements. Housing finance companies continue to report cyber incidents to the National Housing Bank.

SEBI's Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework of 20 August 2024, circular SEBI/HO/ITD-1/ITD_CSC_EXT/P/CIR/2024/113, supersedes a listed set of earlier SEBI cyber circulars. It sorts regulated entities into market infrastructure institutions, qualified, mid-size, small-size and self-certification categories, and it requires a security operations centre, with small-size and self-certification entities mandated onto a Market SOC run by BSE and NSE. The glide path in that circular was 1 January 2025 where a cyber circular already existed, and 1 April 2025 where the framework was new.

IRDAI's Information and Cyber Security Guidelines, 2023, reference IRDAI/GA&HR/GDL/MISC/88/04/2023 of 24 April 2023, apply to insurers including foreign reinsurance branches, a named list of intermediaries, and the Insurance Information Bureau. They superseded the 2017 insurer guidelines and later circulars that had extended cyber expectations to intermediaries. The guidelines place an Information Security Risk Management Committee over the information and cyber security policy, and they point to a NIST framework annexure.

The Telecommunications (Telecom Cyber Security) Rules, 2024, made under section 22 of the Telecommunications Act, 2023, give telecommunication entities their own policy, audit, SOC and incident-reporting duties, including a six-hour report and a twenty-four-hour follow-up. The October 2025 amendment extends parts of the identifier regime to entities that use telecommunication identifiers to identify customers or deliver services, even when those entities are not licensees.

## The personal-data layer is a commencement schedule

The DPDP Act was enacted on 11 August 2023. The rules notified on 13 November 2025 do not switch every duty on at once. Security safeguards, breach intimation to affected people and to the Data Protection Board, and the extra duties of a significant data fiduciary are in the rule text, and they are on the eighteen-month commencement clause. Consent Manager registration is on the one-year clause.

A policy shift worth explaining to a board is this: one event can be a CERT-In incident, a sector-regulator incident, a telecom security incident, a personal-data breach, and, if a protected system is involved, an NCIIPC matter. The statutes did not merge those reports into one form.
