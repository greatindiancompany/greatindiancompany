Ninety days from 24 September 2026 runs to about 23 December 2026. That window will not produce a new National Education Policy. It can change whether a budget line is spent, whether an ITI upgrade proposal has moved, and whether a training calendar for the rest of the financial year is real. The checklist below is documents to open and questions to answer. It does not invent meeting dates.

## Read the surveys you will otherwise misquote

Open the AISHE 2023-24 report, released on 8 July 2026, at [aishe.gov.in](https://aishe.gov.in/), not only the press note. Write down the all-India gross enrolment ratio of 30, the female ratio of 31.2, and the Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe ratios of 27.8 and 22.8. Then find the state table and record the state you actually operate in. If your note still uses a higher-education ratio from before this release, replace it this week. The two survey years were published together. You do not need to wait for another national release to fix a stale slide.

Open the UDISE+ 2024-25 note of 28 August 2025 and the [dashboard](https://udiseplus.gov.in/). Record, for the same state, secondary-stage retention and secondary-stage dropout, not only the gross enrolment ratio. Nationally those were 47.2% retention and 8.2% dropout at the secondary stage. If you run a programme aimed at "keeping adolescents in school," retention is the number that tells you whether the problem is still there. The academic year 2026-27 is also the year NIPUN Bharat set for foundational literacy and numeracy by the end of Grade 3. Ask the state mission what Grade 3 evidence it will publish this year. Do not assume a national score will appear on a particular date.

## Follow the money that was revised once already

The 2026-27 budget estimates are in force. The 2025-26 revised estimates are the warning. Samagra Shiksha was revised from ₹41,250 crore to ₹38,000 crore. PM SHRI was revised from ₹7,500 crore to ₹4,500 crore. The skill ministry's net provision was revised from ₹6,100.10 crore to ₹2,703.54 crore, and its centrally sponsored schemes from ₹3,049.90 crore to ₹367 crore.

Before December, check whether any supplementary demand or revised estimate for 2026-27 has been tabled, and whether PM SHRI, Samagra Shiksha, the Skill India Programme (₹2,800 crore budgeted), or PM-SETU (₹6,140.50 crore budgeted) has moved. The source is [indiabudget.gov.in](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/eb/sbe92.pdf), not a retelling. If nothing has been revised yet, write that down too. Absence of a cut is information.

## Ask one question about PM-SETU

On 1 April 2026 the skill minister told the Rajya Sabha that PM-SETU proposals from state steering committees had not yet been received for national approval, and that release of upgradation funds depends on a special purpose vehicle. By late 2026 that sentence may be out of date. It is the right question to re-ask: has any state proposal been approved, and has any special purpose vehicle been formed so that funds can be released?

The administrative steps already counted in April were clusters identified in 35 states and Union Territories, state committees in 32, budget heads in 25, expressions of interest in 19, and requests for proposals in 12. If you are an industry partner, the actionable check is whether the state you care about is among those that floated a request, and whether that process has since produced a contract. The five trainer institutes named for capacity work are in Bhubaneswar, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kanpur, and Ludhiana. A national "ITI modernisation" claim that is not one of those tracks is still a press line.

## Training operations, not new slogans

PMKVY 4.0's published activity markers to re-check on the [Skill India Digital Hub](https://www.skillindiadigital.gov.in/) are the scale already stated: 27.08 lakh candidates trained as on 7 December 2025, and more than 5.44 lakh in future-oriented roles as on 30 June 2026. See whether the hub's public counts have been refreshed, and whether your own centre's numbers match what the hub shows. The highest common-norms training cost cited for new-age roles was ₹49 per hour. If a budget you are bidding against assumes a higher public rate, ask where that rate is notified.

Apprenticeship contracts are worth a separate look before year-end hiring plans freeze. Cumulative engagement under NAPS was 51.30 lakh up to 31 December 2025. Completions are much smaller. If your model depends on apprentices finishing assessment in this financial year, use the completion series, not the engagement series.

Parliament's winter session, if it follows the usual calendar, is when written answers update scheme counts. This checklist does not assume a session date. It does say: if an answer on PMKVY, NAPS, PM-SETU, or AISHE is tabled before 23 December, replace the dated figure you are using and keep the "as on" date in the sentence.

Leave power-sector and generic consulting links out of the file. They do not regulate these schemes and they do not publish these ratios.
