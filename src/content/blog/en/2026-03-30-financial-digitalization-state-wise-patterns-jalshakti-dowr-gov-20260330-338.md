# What India's payments data will and will not say about states

A state-wise league table is the slide people expect from a note on Indian financial digitalization. The official monthly UPI series does not give them one. Treating a national total as if it were a map, or treating a bank's volume as if it were a state's, produces a pattern the publisher did not print.

## The cuts that exist

NPCI's [UPI product statistics](https://www.npci.org.in/product/upi/product-statistics) are a national monthly series: banks live, volume, and value. The Press Information Bureau factsheet of 24 August 2026 reads July 2026 as 741 live banks, 2,365.8 crore transactions, and ₹29.87 lakh crore. An earlier PIB note of 11 April 2026 reads January 2026 as 691 banks, against 216 banks in 2021, with 21.70 billion transactions and ₹28.33 lakh crore. The rise in participating banks is a coverage fact. It is not a ranking of Maharashtra against Bihar.

Bank-wise volume files, which NPCI links from its statistics section, are the next official cut. They answer which member banks are originating and receiving payments. A national bank's count mixes every state it serves. A regional rural bank's count is closer to a geography, and still is not a state domestic product. This brief does not reprint a bank-wise table, because the honest instruction is to open the current file rather than freeze a month.

## The only geography the Digital Rupee launch actually named

The retail Digital Rupee pilot is one of the few Reserve Bank documents that lists places. The [press release of 29 November 2022](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=54773) started the closed user group in Mumbai, New Delhi, Bengaluru, and Bhubaneswar, with State Bank of India, ICICI Bank, Yes Bank, and IDFC First Bank. It then named a later set: Ahmedabad, Gangtok, Guwahati, Hyderabad, Indore, Kochi, Lucknow, Patna, and Shimla, plus more banks. That list is a pilot design from 2022. It is not a 2026 adoption ranking, and it is not evidence that states absent from the first wave lack digital payments. UPI was already a national system by then. The wholesale pilot, started on 1 November 2022, was about settlement of secondary-market government securities among nine banks. It has no state pattern at all.

## Inclusion tools are not a state index either

The August 2026 factsheet is specific about who the later UPI products were for. UPI 123PAY was built for feature phones, through an interactive voice response number, an app, a missed-call path, and proximity sound. MeitY's [Digital India page on UPI 123PAY](https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/initiative/upi-123pay/) is the programme description of that choice. The factsheet says the per-transaction limit for UPI 123PAY was raised to ₹10,000, and that UPI Lite's wallet limit was raised to ₹5,000 with a per-transaction limit of ₹1,000. Those are product limits, not state budgets.

The April 2026 PIB note places UPI on top of a wider inclusion stack it calls the JAM trinity: Jan-Dhan accounts, Aadhaar, and mobile connectivity, with direct benefit transfer as the use that made digital credits familiar. That is a national architecture story. It does not publish a state-by-state count of UPI users in this note, so this brief will not invent one.

A policy reader who needs a geographic cut should ask a narrower question that a real series can answer. Which banks are live. Which pilot cities were named for e₹-R at launch. Whether feature-phone and low-value tools exist so that a smartphone assumption is not baked into the product. A heat map of "digitalization by state" that does not cite a column labelled by state is not that question.
