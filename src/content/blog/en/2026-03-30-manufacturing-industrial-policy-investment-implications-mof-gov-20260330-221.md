# How to read PLI investment against the incentive outlay

An investment memo that uses one "PLI number" is usually mixing three official series. The 28 July 2026 reply to [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1423](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1423_Rd4xWf.pdf) puts all three kinds of fact on one page, which makes the mix easier to see.

## The three series

**Incentive outlay.** The reply says the 14 PLI schemes were approved with an outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore. That is the ceiling of incentive money the government has approved for the schemes. It is not capital expenditure by companies, and it is not money already paid.

**Actual investment reported under the schemes.** As on 31 March 2026 the same reply says actual investment was over ₹2.40 lakh crore. The 21 July annexure, [PIB PRID 2287008](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287008), totals ₹2,40,138 crore across the 14 lines. Reported project investment is already larger than the incentive outlay. That is what the design implies: the incentive is a payment on production, while the investment is the applicant's capital. It is not a return multiple, and it is not profit.

**Incentive disbursed.** The 28 July reply says cumulative incentive disbursement as on 31 March 2026 was ₹35,354 crore. Dividing that disbursement by the ₹1.91 lakh crore outlay is arithmetic on this page, not a ratio in the reply. ₹35,354 crore is a little under one-fifth of ₹1.91 lakh crore. Most of the approved incentive had not been paid by that date. Do not book undisbursed outlay as cash a company has received.

A fourth series often gets added by mistake. In the same Lok Sabha reply, manufacturing FDI is stated as USD 19.04 billion in FY 2024-25, up from USD 16.12 billion in FY 2023-24. Gross FDI inflows in FY 2025-26 are stated as USD 94.53 billion. Those are balance-of-payments style inflows. They are not PLI investment, they are in dollars, and they cover a different question. Leave them in a separate line of the memo.

## What the sales figure is

The reply says production and sales under the schemes were over ₹22.66 lakh crore as on 31 March 2026, and exports over ₹15.2 lakh crore. Cumulative sales are not value added, not profit, and not the incentive. A company can report sales under a scheme and still be waiting on a claim. The 27 March 2026 Rajya Sabha reply, [PIB PRID 2246089](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2246089), is the place to see an earlier cut: as on 31 December 2025, sales were over ₹20.41 lakh crore and exports over ₹8.3 lakh crore. Use the later date for a stock, and both dates if you want to see movement.

## Where a diligence file should go next

The annexure shows concentration. Solar modules, pharmaceutical drugs, and automobiles and auto components are the three largest investment lines. A fund looking at "Indian manufacturing incentives" in the abstract is mostly looking at those three plus specialty steel and large-scale electronics. IT hardware, drones, medical devices, and ACC batteries are in the table at much smaller reported investment. Absence from the top of the table is not the same as absence of a scheme.

DPIIT's Annual Report 2024-25 describes the India Industrial Land Bank as the GIS layer for industrial areas, and the National Industrial Corridor programme as the node-building track. Reported PLI investment does not tell you whether a plot in a corridor node is allotted. The August 2024 corridor approval of an estimated ₹28,602 crore, in [PIB PRID 2050136](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2050136), is public project cost for nodes. It is a fifth series. Do not fold it into the ₹2.40 lakh crore.
