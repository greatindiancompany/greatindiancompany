## How to read this page

Every figure below is tied to a dated official release. Later replies replace earlier ones for the same item. Where a release gives an aim or a minister's expectation, it is labelled that way. This page does not fill gaps with a logistics-cost percentage. DPIIT said on 16 September 2025 that its cost assessment with NCAER was still in the final stages, after unveiling a measurement framework on 3 January 2025.

## Dedicated rail freight

- Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor: Ludhiana to Sonnagar, 1,337 km. Western Dedicated Freight Corridor: Dadri to Jawaharlal Nehru Port, 1,506 km. Combined scope stated as 2,843 km.
- 19 March 2025: 2,741 route km (96.4 percent) commissioned and operational. Average trains per day: 247 in 2023-24, 352 in 2024-25 up to February 2025, and 371 in February 2025. Eastern corridor described as mostly mineral traffic. The project described as enabling double-stack container trains and faster northern-hinterland access from western ports.
- Three further reports under examination on that date, none sanctioned: East Coast, Kharagpur–Vijayawada; East-West, Palghar–Bhusawal–Nagpur–Kharagpur–Dankuni and Rajkharsawan–Kalipahari–Andal; North-South, Vijayawada–Nagpur–Itarsi.
- 31 March 2026: DFCCIL records trial freight runs on JNPT–New Saphale (Vaitarna) as completion of the Western corridor.
- 1 February 2026: Budget proposes a corridor from Dankuni to Surat. Ministerial briefing the same day: 2,052 km via Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, and Maharashtra; nearly ₹1.20 lakh crore; existing corridors around 400 freight trains a day.
- 7 August 2026: both the 1,337 km and 1,506 km corridors reported commissioned; about 443 trains a day; Dankuni–Surat report under preparation and updation. Indian Railways freight loading 1,670 million tonnes in 2025-26. The minister described Indian Railways as the second largest freight-carrying railway.

## Terminals and wagons, 7 August 2026

- Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals: 142 commissioned; estimated capacity 224 million tonnes a year; about ₹10,000 crore private investment; 146 million tonnes handled in 2025-26. The 19 March 2025 mark was 97 commissioned and 277 in-principle approvals.
- Wagons: about 275 special-purpose rakes and 397 general-purpose rakes operational, plus about 54 industry-owned automobile rakes.

## Roads, parks, and ports

- Bharatmala, approved 2017 for 34,800 km. As on 31 October 2024: 26,425 km awarded, 18,714 km constructed. NHAI expenditure ₹4.72 lakh crore up to 30 November 2024. Port and coastal connectivity roads: 18 projects, 424 km awarded, 189 km constructed. Coastal states named: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal. Reply dated 18 December 2024.
- 15 February 2022: 35 multimodal logistics parks identified under Bharatmala Phase I. Jogighopa then under construction with Assam as equity partner. Bids invited for Nagpur (JNPT, Sindi), Chennai (Mappedu, Chennai Port and SIPCOT), and Bengaluru (KIADB).
- Sagarmala, note dated 27 March 2025: launched March 2015; about 839 projects, about ₹5.79 lakh crore identified; 272 projects completed as of 19 March 2025, about ₹1.41 lakh crore. Coastline 7,500 km; potentially navigable waterways 14,500 km. Coastal shipping up 118 percent in a decade. Inland-waterway cargo up 700 percent. Ro-Pax ferries: more than 40 lakh passengers. Nine Indian ports said to be in the world's top 100, Visakhapatnam in the top 20 container ports. Sagarmala 2.0: budgetary support of ₹40,000 crore, aim of leveraging ₹12 lakh crore over the next decade. Sagarmala Development Company Limited set up August 2016.
- 1 February 2026: proposal to operationalise 20 national waterways over five years, starting with National Waterway 5 in Odisha (Talcher, Angul, Kalinga Nagar to Paradeep and Dhamra). Proposed Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme. Inland waterways and coastal shipping share to rise from 6 percent to 12 percent by 2047. Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision targets cited in the Sagarmala note: 4 million gross registered tonnage of shipbuilding capacity and 10 billion metric tonnes of annual port handling, among the top five shipbuilding nations by 2047.

## Logistics institutions

- National Logistics Policy launched 17 September 2022. PM GatiShakti launched 13 October 2021.
- 16 September 2025: Unified Logistics Interface Platform, more than 30 systems, over 160 crore transactions as of August 2025. Logistics Data Bank, more than 75 million export-import containers, 101 inland container depots. LEADS linked to India's 38th place on the World Bank Logistics Performance Index. Policy target: top 25 by 2030. E-Logs: more than 35 associations, 100 of 140 issues resolved. State logistics policies formulated in 27 states and Union Territories; industry status in 19; 14 action plans; nine policies in draft. More than 65,000 professionals trained from 2023 to 2025.
- LEADS 2024 released 3 January 2025. Coastal achievers: Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Odisha, Tamil Nadu. Coastal fast movers: Andhra Pradesh, Goa. Coastal aspirers: Kerala, West Bengal.

## Westward and northward agreements

- IMEC memorandum, published September 2023: India, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, the European Union, France, Germany, Italy, and the United States. Eastern corridor, India to the Arabian Gulf. Northern corridor, Arabian Gulf to Europe. Ship-to-rail, plus electricity and digital cable and a clean-hydrogen pipe. On 16 April 2025 the commerce minister said the corridor would cut logistics costs by up to 30 percent and transportation time by 40 percent. That sentence is the minister's expectation.
- INSTC dry run, reported 20 March 2015: Nhava Sheva–Bandar Abbas–Tehran–Bandar Anzali–Astrakhan, and Nhava Sheva–Bandar Abbas–Baku. Gaps then named: irregular sailings to Iran, bill-of-lading insurance, and non-vessel-owning common carriers.
