# The Export and Local-Content Benchmarks India Publishes for Autos

A comparison of India's electric-vehicle share with China's or Europe's does not appear in the Ministry of Heavy Industries reviews, the Ministry of Power charging note, or SIAM's FY 2025-26 release. Those documents benchmark India against India's own targets: export volumes, a 50 percent local-content test, a domestic charger path, and a domestic cell-capacity target. That is the benchmark a reader can audit.

## The external number SIAM prints is exports

SIAM's [14 April 2026 release](https://www.siam.in/pressrelease-details.aspx?pid=605) gives FY 2025-26 exports as the outward measure:

- Passenger vehicles: 9.05 lakh units, up 17.5 percent on FY 2024-25. SIAM names the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America as steady markets.
- Two-wheelers: 51.8 lakh units, up 23.4 percent, described as the highest financial-year exports. SIAM credits a wider product range, brand acceptance, and a weaker rupee.
- Three-wheelers: about 4.61 lakh units, up 50.1 percent, with Sri Lanka and African markets named.
- Commercial vehicles: 0.95 lakh units, up 17.4 percent, with neighbouring countries and the Middle East named.

These are export units, not a share of world trade. Passenger-vehicle and commercial-vehicle lines in the same release omit named manufacturers (BMW, Mercedes, JLR, and Volvo on passenger vehicles; Daimler on commercial vehicles). The export paragraphs do not repeat that footnote line by line. Treat the export totals as SIAM's published series, and check the release before using them as a census of every plant.

Domestic sales in the same year were 46.43 lakh passenger vehicles, 10.80 lakh commercial vehicles, 8.36 lakh three-wheelers, and 2.17 crore two-wheelers. Exports are large next to domestic sales in two-wheelers and three-wheelers, and smaller next to domestic passenger-vehicle sales. That ratio is visible in SIAM's own units. It is an India-to-India comparison of two SIAM lines.

## The manufacturing benchmark is 50 percent domestic value addition

The [23 September 2021 PLI notification](https://pliauto.in/docs/press_release/2021-09-24%20PIB%20PR%20Govt%20notifies%20PLI%20for%20Auto_PressRelease.pdf) said the auto scheme would increase India's share in global automotive trade. It did not publish the share it was starting from. The operative test that followed is domestic, and the [13 January 2026 review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2214039) states it: PLI Auto incentivises only products that achieve domestic value addition of at least 50 percent.

As of 31 December 2025, eight Champion OEM applicants held that certificate for 94 variants, and ten Component Champion applicants held it for 37 variants, out of 82 approved applicants. Cumulative determined sales were ₹32,879 crore till 30 September 2025, against an estimated ₹2,31,500 crore of incremental sales over five years. The global-trade sentence in the 2021 note is an aim. The 50 percent test and the determined-sales line are the scores the ministry has since published.

The cell programme in the same review is a capacity benchmark: ₹18,100 crore to establish 50 GWh of advanced chemistry cell manufacturing. Of that, 40 GWh had been awarded to firms and 10 GWh kept for grid-scale stationary storage. Reported investment was ₹2,878 crore till 30 October 2025, and one beneficiary had an installed 1 GWh plant in pilot production since March 2024. A 50 GWh target and a 1 GWh pilot are both in the review. They are the domestic battery benchmark, not a ranking against cell plants elsewhere.

## The charger benchmark is a domestic count

A [Ministry of Power note](https://www.powermin.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/fab513bc9acc0ad7a794454fe1e329f4.pdf) says the 2024 charging guidelines were issued to support a connected network and to help raise chargers from 34,000 then to about 1 lakh by 2030. "Then" is the note's own present tense, in a file the ministry uploaded in July 2025. It is not a September 2026 census, and it is not a comparison with another country's charger stock.

The later official charger figure to set beside it is narrower. As on 1 July 2026, the heavy-industries reply had approved ₹689 crore of the ₹2,000 crore PM E-DRIVE charging line for 6,562 chargers. That is scheme-funded public charging, not the national stock of 34,000.
