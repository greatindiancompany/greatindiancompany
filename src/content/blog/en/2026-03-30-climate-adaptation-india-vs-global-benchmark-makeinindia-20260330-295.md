# Comparing India's Climate Adaptation on the Official Record

A comparison is only as good as the column headings. On the environment ministry, disaster authority, Press Information Bureau, and India Meteorological Department pages used here, India does not publish a rank against other countries for adaptation readiness. A chart that offers one is using some other method.

## What the plan says India is trying to do

The National Action Plan on Climate Change, released on 30 June 2008, is restated in the ministry's FAQ of 1 December 2021. The FAQ says high growth is essential for living standards and for reducing vulnerability to climate impacts. The principles put protecting the poor first, and they hold growth and ecological sustainability in the same sentence. That is a domestic design choice. It is not a score against another capital.

## The global sentence that is easy to misuse

On 21 July 2025, PIB carried a Lok Sabha reply on nationally determined contributions. The reply says India has one of the lowest per capita emissions and stands out among G20 countries on track to meet its contribution. In the same text, installed solar capacity is said to have risen from 2.82 GW in 2014 to 116.25 GW in June 2025, emission intensity of GDP is said to have fallen 36 percent between 2005 and 2020, and the 50 percent non-fossil share of installed electric capacity is said to have been reached in June 2025, ahead of 2030.

Those lines answer a mitigation question. They do not rank heat-action plans, flood guidelines, or coastal hazard lines. Importing them into an adaptation scorecard changes the subject.

## The comparison the Survey note actually makes

PIB's 29 January 2026 note on the Economic Survey 2025-26 says the Survey treats adaptation as central and treats development as a form of adaptation. The international examples in that release are about energy-transition risk in a handful of European countries, tied to baseload power, transmission, and system flexibility. They are not an adaptation index.

## The institutional benchmark a reader can actually use

If the task is "what does India run, that another system might also run," the dated pieces are these. A national plan, described as nine missions on 21 July 2025. State plans in 34 States and Union Territories, same reply. A project fund whose latest cited stock is 30 projects and ₹847.48 crore of sanctioned cost, on 5 August 2024, and which was made a non-scheme in November 2022. Separate hazard guidelines from the National Disaster Management Authority. A meteorological definition of a heat wave from IMD. Those are instruments. They are not a place on a league table.
