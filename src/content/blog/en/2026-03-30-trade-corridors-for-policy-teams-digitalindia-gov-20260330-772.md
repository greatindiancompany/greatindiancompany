## Assign the corridor to the ministry that answers for it

Policy work on India's trade corridors fails when one brief is asked to cover track, parks, port dues, and a diplomatic route. The official design splits the work. PM GatiShakti, launched on 13 October 2021 and approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs on 21 October 2021, is the shared map for infrastructure. The National Logistics Policy, launched on 17 September 2022, is the services, skills, and regulatory agenda, and it uses the GatiShakti institutional frame, including the Empowered Group of Secretaries. A February 2022 road-ministry note described the digital platform as bringing 16 ministries into one view. Count that as the 2022 description of the room, and check a later notification before you publish a new headcount of ministries.

## Railways and DFCCIL

The Ministry of Railways answers for dedicated freight corridors. The stocktake a policy note can quote from 7 August 2026 is narrow and hard: Eastern corridor commissioned, Ludhiana–Sonnagar, 1,337 km; Western corridor commissioned, Dadri–JNPT, 1,506 km; about 443 trains a day; Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals at 142, with about ₹10,000 crore of private investment. The open decision is Dankuni–Surat, announced in the Union Budget 2026, with the detailed project report under preparation and updation. The tests for sanction were stated on 19 March 2025 for the earlier trio of reports: feasibility, financial and economic viability, traffic, and funds. Those three reports — East Coast, East-West via Palghar to Dankuni, and North-South via Nagpur — had not been sanctioned on that date. A policy note should not convert the Budget announcement into a sanction of the older trio.

DFCCIL is the public-sector builder and operator. Its milestone note is the place to confirm the 31 March 2026 trial that closed the last western section. Parliament replies remain the place to confirm what the minister is willing to say is commissioned.

## Roads, ports, and waterways

The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, through NHAI and the other highway companies, answers for Bharatmala, including port and coastal roads and the multimodal logistics parks identified under Phase I. The figures safe to carry are from 18 December 2024: 34,800 km approved in 2017, 26,425 km awarded and 18,714 km constructed as on 31 October 2024, ₹4.72 lakh crore spent by NHAI up to 30 November 2024, and 189 km constructed of 424 km awarded in the port and coastal road category. The park list with site partners is older, 15 February 2022. Publishing Jogighopa, Nagpur, Chennai, and Bengaluru as "current status" without a later release overstates what that note can support. DPIIT said in September 2025 that multi-agency park planning was still being harmonised. That sentence is the honest 2025 status.

The Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways answers for Sagarmala, major-port incentives, and inland waterways through the Inland Waterways Authority. The 27 March 2025 note is the programme stocktake: about 839 projects, ₹5.79 lakh crore identified, 272 completed as of 19 March 2025. The Budget summary of 1 February 2026 is the place for the proposal to operationalise 20 national waterways in five years, beginning with National Waterway 5 to Paradeep and Dhamra, and for the proposed Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme. Coastal charge discounts, priority berthing, and the bunker GST change belong in a ports note as measures already described, not as Budget ideas.

## Commerce, DPIIT, and the external corridors

DPIIT, in the Commerce and Industry ministry, answers for the logistics policy, LEADS, the Unified Logistics Interface Platform, the Logistics Data Bank, E-Logs, and the NCAER cost work. The 16 September 2025 note is the stocktake: top-25 Logistics Performance Index aim by 2030, 38th cited as the current rank, 160 crore ULIP transactions, 27 states and Union Territories with policies, 19 with industry status. The cost study was in its final stages. A policy brief that needs a cost-to-GDP number should wait for that study.

External corridors sit with Commerce as well as with the line ministries that would build any Indian landside link. The India-Middle East-Europe memorandum of September 2023 is a commitment among eight governments to arrange a ship-to-rail corridor. The International North-South Transport Corridor remains a multilateral agreement; the last fully specific Indian bottleneck list in this set is the 20 March 2015 Lok Sabha reply on the dry run. Updating that list requires a newer Commerce reply, not a paraphrase of the 2015 text as if it were this year's audit.
