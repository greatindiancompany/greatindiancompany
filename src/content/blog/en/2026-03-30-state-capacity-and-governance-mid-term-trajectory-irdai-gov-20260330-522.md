# The Mid-term Path from the Fifteenth Finance Commission Award

The medium term for Indian state capacity is the handover now under way: a five-year Finance Commission award that ran through 2025-26, a new award already used to split a capital loan, a debt stock that did not return to the old benchmark, and a civil service learning system that has scale without yet reaching its design population.

## What the 2021-26 award fixed

PIB's summary of the Fifteenth Finance Commission, published on 1 February 2021, is the document that fixed the path states have been living on. Vertical devolution was 41 percent of the divisible pool, held at the Commission's own 2020-21 recommendation. That was about one percentage point below the Fourteenth Finance Commission's 42 percent, with the adjustment explained by the creation of the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. The states' share was put at Rs 42.2 lakh crore over 2021-26. With grants, aggregate transfers were estimated at about 50.9 percent of the divisible pool. The Commission assessed gross tax revenue for the five years at Rs 135.2 lakh crore and the divisible pool, after cess, surcharge and collection cost, at Rs 103 lakh crore.

Horizontal shares were a need, equity and performance mix: income distance 45 percent, population 15, area 15, demographic performance 12.5, forest and ecology 10, and tax and fiscal effort 2.5. Revenue deficit grants were Rs 2,94,514 crore for seventeen states. Local government grants were Rs 4,36,361 crore, of which Rs 2,36,805 crore was for rural local bodies, Rs 1,21,055 crore for urban local bodies and Rs 70,051 crore for health grants through local governments. Rs 8,000 crore was for incubating new cities and Rs 450 crore for shared municipal services. Cities under a million received basic grants. Million-plus cities were put entirely on a performance-linked Million-Plus Cities Challenge Fund. Health grants through local bodies were for urban health and wellness centres, sub-centres, primary and community health centres, block public health units, diagnostics and conversion of rural sub-centres and primary centres into health and wellness centres. Total health-sector grants were Rs 1,06,606 crore.

That design is the mid-term baseline. Money for panchayats and municipalities was tied to a constitutional grant, split by population size, and partly locked to health facilities and to city performance. It was not a blank untied cheque to the state treasury.

## What is already using the next award

The Department of Expenditure's SASCI guidelines of 27 March 2026 allocate the state portion of untied capital assistance, Rs 67,000 crore out of a Rs 75,000 crore Part I, in proportion to each state's share of central taxes under the Sixteenth Finance Commission award. The full scheme is Rs 2,00,000 crore of 50-year interest-free loans, above the net borrowing ceiling. Special assistance of this kind has run since 2020-21. Over a five-year view it has changed from a pandemic-era addition into a standing capital instrument with reform parts. The trajectory is more conditions and an explicit link to the new Commission's shares, on top of tax devolution rather than instead of it.

## Debt and people, on the RBI's clock

The overview of RBI's State Finances study of 2024-25 budgets records a long consolidation and its limit. The consolidated gross fiscal deficit of states fell from an average of 4.3 percent of GDP in 1998-99 to 2003-04 to 2.7 percent in 2004-05 to 2023-24. Debt fell from 31.8 percent of GDP at end-March 2004 to 28.5 percent at end-March 2024, and RBI notes that this is still well above the 20 percent level recommended by the FRBM Review Committee in 2017. The next edition, released on 23 January 2026, puts the budget estimate of outstanding liabilities at 29.2 percent of GDP at end-March 2026, and the 2024-25 deficit at 3.3 percent of GDP, mainly because of the interest-free capital loans. The mid-term path is a deficit that can look reformed across twenty years and a stock that remains high, with a capital loan that lifts the annual deficit without being part of the normal ceiling.

On the administrative side, DoPT designed iGOT for about two crore employees. As on 6 March 2026 the platform had more than 1.51 crore users, over 4,400 courses and more than 7.7 crore completions, with MoUs in 29 states and UTs. The trajectory there is coverage of the Union and of most states, a Cabinet Secretary-level review unit, and a remaining distance to the design population, plus an admitted absence of district-level learning monitoring.

A reader taking a three-to-five-year view should hold four objects at once: 41 percent devolution and tied local-body grants through the Fifteenth award, Sixteenth Finance Commission shares already splitting the untied capital loan, a liability ratio still far from the 2017 committee mark, and a competency platform that is large and still short of the workforce it was drawn for.
