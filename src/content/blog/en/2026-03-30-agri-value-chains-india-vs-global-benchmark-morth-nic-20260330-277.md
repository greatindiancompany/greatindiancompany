# India's agri export basket, checked against itself

A global benchmark is a share of someone else's total. The documents that belong in this brief, APEDA's annual report and APEDA's product pages, state India's exports. They do not state India's share of world agricultural trade. The comparison they do support is India against its own previous year, and one product line against the rest of the Indian basket.

## The year APEDA has closed

APEDA's Annual Administrative Report for 2024-25, using DGCIS principal-commodity data, says India's agri exports in 2024-25 were USD 51,913 million. The prose of the report puts APEDA scheduled products at USD 28,601 million of that. The summary table on the same pages prints APEDA products at USD 28,590 million. Both figures are in the report. A reader should use the cell they are looking at, and should not average them.

The summary table is the clean year-on-year comparison. Agri exports moved from USD 48,757 million in 2023-24 to USD 51,913 million in 2024-25, which the table marks as 6 per cent. APEDA products moved from USD 25,667 million to USD 28,590 million, marked as 11 per cent. APEDA's share of agri exports moved from 53 per cent to 55 per cent. That 55 per cent is a share of India's agri exports, not a share of world exports.

APEDA's mandate under the APEDA Act, 1985, is scheduled products. The report says those scheduled products cover 17 categories and 802 HS codes at eight digits. Everything outside that schedule can still be an agri export in the DGCIS agri total. The gap between USD 51,913 million and the APEDA line is that outside set. Marine products, which sit with a different authority, are the usual reason a national "agri export" headline and an APEDA headline diverge. This brief does not reassign them.

## What the basket is made of, in the year APEDA has published since

APEDA's animal-products page, sourced to DGCIS, gives 2025-26 exports of animal products at USD 6,236.79 million. Buffalo meat is USD 5,098.54 million. The page itself says buffalo meat contributed over 81.75 per cent of animal-product exports in 2025-26. The rest of that page is much smaller: dairy products USD 407.18 million, poultry products including eggs and live birds USD 317.79 million, natural honey USD 207.97 million, sheep and goat meat USD 103.57 million, animal casings USD 58.91 million. The markets named for buffalo meat and other animal products are Vietnam, Egypt, the UAE, Malaysia, and Saudi Arabia.

Processed fruits, juices, and nuts, on APEDA's page for that head, were 5,27,115.69 metric tonnes and USD 662.79 million in 2025-26. Destinations named are the Netherlands, the United States, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and the United Kingdom.

Those 2025-26 lines should not be divided by the 2024-25 agri total of USD 51,913 million. They are different years. The only share that is already computed inside a single table is the 55 per cent APEDA share for 2024-25, and the 81.75 per cent buffalo-meat share inside 2025-26 animal products.

## What a processing benchmark would require, and what we have instead

A common global comparison is the share of farm output that is processed. Building it would need a processed-output numerator and a farm-output denominator for the same commodities and the same year. The documents here do not pair them.

What they do pair is capacity created under named schemes. Under the food-processing production-linked incentive, approved applicants had created around 35 lakh metric tonnes a year of processing and preservation capacity by 31 December 2025, after investing about Rs 9,207 crore. Under the cold-chain component of PM Kisan Sampada Yojana, 311 completed projects had created 9.236 lakh metric tonnes of cold storage as of 30 June 2026. The Department of Agriculture's national cold-storage stock, as cited in that reply, was 402.87 lakh metric tonnes on 31 January 2026.

Set beside those tonnes, the Third Advance Estimate for 2025-26 puts foodgrain production at 376.563 million tonnes, including rice at 154.024 million tonnes and wheat at 120.657 million tonnes. Foodgrain tonnes and cold-store tonnes answer different questions. Dividing one by the other is not a processing ratio, and it is not an international rank.

## The export scheme that follows the 2024-25 report

The 2024-25 annual report describes the Agriculture and Processed Foods Export Promotion Scheme as financial support to registered exporters for 2021-22 to 2025-26. A tender APEDA published on 1 September 2026 is the next instrument: a project-management unit for Bharat Krishi Niryat Yojana for the 16th Finance Commission cycle, 2026-27 to 2030-31. The tender says the scheme supports exporters, FPOs, food manufacturers, traders, state governments, and food-testing laboratories under three heads: export infrastructure, quality development, and market development. The tender is not an export forecast, and it does not print a scheme outlay.
