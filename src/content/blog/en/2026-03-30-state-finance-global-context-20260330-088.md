# The fiscal end-state after GST compensation

GST compensation was a transition device. It was a promise that, for a limited period, a state's revenue from the taxes merged into GST would be protected if collections grew more slowly than a rate written into law. That promise has expired. The cess that was kept on to repay the loans raised when the promise could not be funded from the cess itself has also been wound down. The end-state is ordinary fiscal federalism: own tax, a share of Union taxes, grants, and borrowing under a ceiling.

## What the statute guaranteed, and for how long

The Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act, 2017, set a transition period of five years from the date GST began. Inside that period, each state's protected revenue was its base-year revenue from the subsumed taxes, stepped up at 14 per cent a year. If actual GST revenue assigned to the state fell short of that path, the state was to be compensated. The five years ran to 30 June 2022. From 1 July 2022, states have had no statutory claim to be paid for a shortfall against that 14 per cent path.

The cess funding the compensation was levied on a short list of goods, largely luxury and demerit goods. When cess collections in 2020-21 and 2021-22 could not fund the compensation due, the gap was met with back-to-back borrowing. The Goods and Services Tax (Period of Levy and Collection of Cess) Rules, 2022, notified on 24 June 2022 as G.S.R. 468(E), then extended levy and collection of the cess up to 31 March 2026 so that those borrowings and their interest could be repaid. Extending the cess was a debt-service decision. It did not restore the compensation entitlement.

## How the cess actually ended

The 56th GST Council, meeting in September 2025, decided to end the compensation cess and to fold the tax incidence on most of those goods into GST itself. The Press Information Bureau's FAQ of 3 September 2025 says the rate changes for goods and services other than specified tobacco products took effect on 22 September 2025. For cigarettes, chewing tobacco such as zarda, unmanufactured tobacco, and beedi, the existing GST rates and the compensation cess were to continue until a later date, tied to discharge of the remaining loan and interest liabilities.

The Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) notification No. 03/2025-Compensation Cess (Rate), dated 31 December 2025, substituted nil for the remaining compensation-cess rates. The notification came into force on 1 February 2026. By that date the tobacco tail of the cess had a closedown date, ahead of the 31 March 2026 outer date in the 2022 rules. As of September 2026 both dates are past. There is no live compensation cess and no live compensation entitlement.

## What states have in place of the guarantee

Three receipt streams carry the load the guarantee used to backstop.

Own-tax revenue is the stream the state administers: state GST, state excise, stamp duty and registration, taxes on vehicles, and value added tax on petroleum products that stayed outside GST. Chart II.4 of the Reserve Bank's January 2026 state-budget study is the own-tax series. A shortfall in own tax is now the state's to absorb, or to offset with spending cuts or borrowing. There is no 14 per cent floor underneath it.

Devolution is the states' share of the divisible pool of Union taxes. The Sixteenth Finance Commission recommended that this share stay at 41 per cent for 2026-27 to 2030-31. The government's explanatory memorandum accepts that recommendation, and accepts the further recommendation that the Union disclose the net proceeds certified by the Comptroller and Auditor General under Article 279. Cesses and surcharges of the Union stay outside the divisible pool, which is why the end of one cess (the compensation cess) and the continued use of other cesses are different facts.

Grants are the third stream. Chart II.3 of the Reserve Bank study tracks the declining components of grants-in-aid. Finance Commission grants and scheme grants are designed in each award. They are programme money. They are a floor under a scheme, and they are a different legal object from the old compensation, which was a floor under a tax.

Borrowing remains available inside the net borrowing ceiling under Article 293(3), plus the interest-free capital-investment loans that sit outside that ceiling. The January 2026 study puts the consolidated gross fiscal deficit at 3.3 per cent of GDP in 2024-25 and in the 2025-26 budgets, and says the step through 3 per cent mainly reflects those capital loans. Compensation arrears are no longer the story inside that deficit.

## How to read "global" here

Readers who know other federations sometimes look for a standing federal top-up whenever a state's consumption tax disappoints. India's version of that top-up was time-bound, formula-bound, and funded by a cess. It has reached its end-state. Comparing a state's 2026 revenue to the old 14 per cent path is a historical counterfactual. It is not a claim the state can take to the GST Council. The live questions are own-tax effort, the 41 per cent devolution, the grant design for 2026-31, and the borrowing ceiling. This brief does not import another country's subnational deficit ratio. The Indian documents above are the comparison that can be checked.
