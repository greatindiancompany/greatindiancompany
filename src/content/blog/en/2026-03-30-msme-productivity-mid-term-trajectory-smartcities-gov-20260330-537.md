# The mid-term path for MSME formalisation and productivity

A mid-term path is a set of programmes with end dates and a survey with a direction. It is not a compound growth rate. Through the late 2020s, India's official MSME productivity story has four published tracks. They do not move as one number.

## Formal identity keeps widening

Udyam and the Udyam Assist Platform are the formalisation track. The Press Information Bureau note of 14 May 2026 put the combined stock, as of March 2026, above 7.9 crore: 4.72 crore on Udyam and 3.21 crore on Udyam Assist. The Ministry's 26 June 2026 note said the combined registrations had crossed 8.7 crore by June 2026. Udyam Assist, launched in January 2023, is how informal micro enterprises obtain a certificate that banks can use for priority-sector classification.

If that pace of registration is the path, the path is toward a larger identified population. It is not, by itself, a path toward higher output per worker. The enterprises newly on Udyam Assist are being counted into the credit system. ASUSE still measures activity in the unincorporated sector whether or not a unit has a certificate.

## The definition now has more investment room

Since 1 April 2025 a firm can carry more plant and machinery and more turnover inside micro, small, or medium status. The Budget 2025-26 statement of purpose was scale, technological upgradation, and capital access. Over a five-year view, the classification itself no longer forces a firm out at the old ₹1 crore, ₹10 crore, and ₹50 crore investment lines. The composite test remains: either ceiling still reclassifies the firm upward.

## Activity and productivity, as last measured

ASUSE 2025 is the latest nationwide activity print in this record, released 24 March 2026. Establishments and jobs grew faster than current-price gross value added per worker (4.54 percent, to ₹1,56,539). Internet use jumped from 26.7 percent of establishments to 39.4 percent. The honest mid-term statement is that the last published round shows broader digital use and a modest nominal productivity gain, alongside a large gain in the number of establishments. MoSPI said the full report and unit-level data were to follow in April 2026. Later tables belong on mospi.gov.in. They are not invented forward from the press note.

Nothing in these releases authorises a forecast of real productivity for 2027 or 2028. Current prices, a changed survey calendar, and a larger sample are reasons to wait for the next round rather than draw a straight line.

## The programme clock

RAMP, the World Bank-supported Ministry programme, is the coordination clock. The RAMP portal places it over 2022–27. The 26 June 2026 note says plans from all 36 states and Union Territories were evaluated, 398 proposals worth ₹3,211.75 crore were approved, more than 55 lakh MSMEs had been reached as on June 2026, and reimbursement claims stood at 50 percent of the programme target. A mid-term review asks whether approved state projects finish, and whether reimbursement catches up, before it asks whether national value added per worker has jumped.

CGTMSE's higher ₹10 crore ceiling, in force for guarantees approved on or after 1 April 2025, is the credit track for micro and small enterprises over the same years. SIDBI sits on that trust with the Ministry. The ceiling is a capacity for collateral-free limits. Conversion into sanctioned, performing credit is the execution question, taken up in the bottleneck brief.
