# What Social Sector Capital Is Buying in Anganwadis, Schools, and Villages

Social-sector “capex” in India is not one budget head. It is three public asset programmes that sit under nutrition, school education, and rural wages. Each publishes a different unit: centres to be upgraded, facilities already in schools, and works that wage employment is supposed to leave behind. Treating beneficiary counts as capital, or capital as an outcome, mixes the cycle.

## Anganwadi upgrades are a defined building programme

Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0, on the Ministry of Women and Child Development site, includes Anganwadi infrastructure as one of its verticals, beside nutrition support, early childhood care and education, and Poshan Abhiyaan. The ministry’s scheme page says 2 lakh Anganwadi centres are to be strengthened and upgraded, at 40,000 a year, with better buildings, LED screens, water purification, smart learning aids, Poshan Vatikas, and BharatNet connectivity where it is available. The scheme guidelines describe Poshan 2.0 as an integrated nutrition support programme and record that Anganwadi services under it were approved for the 15th Finance Commission period, 2021-22 to 2025-26. A capital plan for FY 2026-27 should confirm any later approval on the ministry site rather than assume that window still governs the current year. This note does not invent a successor sanction.

The stock those upgrades sit on is large and mostly already built. As of 31 July 2026 the Press Information Bureau put the Poshan Tracker network at nearly 13.99 lakh centres and 13.30 lakh workers. Upgrading 2 lakh centres is a slice of that stock, not a new national network. The outcome the building is for — growth, diet, and early learning — is measured elsewhere, in NFHS-6 and in Tracker growth monitoring, not by the upgrade target itself.

Hot cooked meals and take-home ration are recurrent spending, not capex. Poshan Maah 2026’s push for weekly menus, menu boards, and food-safety checks is an operating cycle on top of the building. A capex watch that stops at construction will miss whether the kitchen inside the new room is actually serving.

## School facilities are counted; learning from those facilities is not

UDISE+ 2025-26, released on 7 July 2026, is the facility census. Electricity 95.0%, drinking water 99.5%, girls’ toilets 98.5%, boys’ toilets 97.2%, handwashing 96.9%, library 90.5%, computers 69.9% (from 64.7%), internet 67.4% (from 63.5%), ramps with handrails 58.2% (from 54.9%). Playgrounds are 81.9%. Rainwater harvesting is 29.9%. Zero-enrolment schools fell to 5,663. Single-teacher schools fell to 1,00,843, about 3% fewer than the previous year on the ministry’s wording.

That is a capital and facility stock. The ministry presents pupil-teacher ratios of 10, 12, 17, and 21 as inside the National Education Policy’s 30:1 reference, which is a staffing ratio, not a building. The participation outcome next to this stock is secondary retention at 51.9% and secondary gross enrolment at 71.7%. A school can have a toilet, a computer, and a teacher ratio inside the policy reference, and still lose the child before Class 10. Facility capex and retention are both published. They are not the same result.

## Village wage works are the third capital pipe

Under the VB-G RAM G Act, in force from 1 July 2026, wage days are tied to assets. The ministry’s 11 May 2026 note groups works into water security, core rural infrastructure, livelihood infrastructure, and extreme-weather mitigation. Core rural infrastructure includes rural roads, public buildings, school infrastructure, Anganwadi centres, sanitation, renewable energy, and housing-related works. Livelihood infrastructure includes markets, storage, processing, livestock, and fisheries. The central share for 2026-27 is ₹95,692.31 crore, with total outlay including the estimated state share expected to exceed ₹1.51 lakh crore.

This is capital financed by a wage guarantee. Administrative expenditure is set at 9 percent, up from 6 percent, for field capacity rather than for the asset itself. Geo-tagging and Janata information boards are the published transparency tools. They do not, in the commencement notes, report a completion rate or a durability rate for 2026-27. Person-days generated in the first month, reported to the Lok Sabha on 11 August 2026 as more than 9.69 crore, measure labour absorbed. They do not measure the asset.

## How to watch the cycle

Ask three questions of any social-sector capex claim. Is the unit a building, a device, a meal, or a wage day? Which ministry’s document sets the target, and for which years? Which outcome series — NFHS body measurements, UDISE retention, or a works completion report — would show whether the asset changed the result? Until the third answer is a published number, the capital is a commitment, not an outcome.
