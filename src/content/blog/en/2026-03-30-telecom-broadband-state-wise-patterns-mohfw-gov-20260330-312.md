# Licensed Service Areas Are the Broadband Map That Matters

India does not operate telecom as 28 identical states. Access spectrum and most subscriber statistics are organised as licensed service areas. BharatNet state tables are organised as states, and a few large states run their own implementation. Using one national tele-density for a district plan mixes those maps.

## The spread TRAI will stand behind

At end-July 2026, all-India tele-density was 94.68 percent. TRAI's Press Release No. 116/2026 says nine licensed service areas sat below that average. Delhi had the highest tele-density, 368.33 percent. Bihar had the lowest, 64.19 percent. The release is explicit about the geography inside those labels. Population projections exist state-wise. Delhi's wireless count includes subscribers on local exchanges in Ghaziabad and Noida in Uttar Pradesh, and in Gurugram and Faridabad in Haryana. West Bengal's figure includes Kolkata, Maharashtra includes Mumbai, and Uttar Pradesh includes the east and west service areas. Andhra Pradesh includes Telangana, Madhya Pradesh includes Chhattisgarh, Bihar includes Jharkhand, and the North-East area bundles several states. A state dashboard that ignores those inclusions will double-count or under-count.

Circle categories in the same note show a second pattern. From July 2025 to July 2026, wireless subscribers grew in every category: the all-India yearly wireless rate was 7.02 percent. Yearly wireline growth was confined to Circle B and Circle C. Circle A and the metros (Delhi, Mumbai, and Kolkata) recorded a yearly decline in wireline subscribers. In July 2026 itself, wireline subscribers fell in Circle B and in the metros, and rose elsewhere. The fibre telephone line is a Circle C and Circle B story in the yearly data. The mobile line is a national story.

All licensed service areas except Kolkata added wireless mobile subscribers in July 2026.

## One state written out in full

The national gram-panchayat total is not a substitute for a state file. PIB's 12 February 2026 reply, answering a question that includes Tamil Nadu, gives a worked example of how the maps diverge.

Out of 2,56,000 gram panchayats, nearly 2,14,000 had been made online under BharatNet Phases I and II. Tamil Nadu implemented through its own vehicle, Tanfinet, rather than BSNL. As on 31 December 2025, 10,869 gram panchayats excluding block headquarters, out of 12,525, were service-ready. Fibre-to-the-home connections provided through BharatNet in the state numbered 808. The amended programme in Tamil Nadu is planned to provide fibre-to-the-home to 5,27,506 households over the next ten years, implemented by the state. About 55,000 route kilometres of the 3,08,907 route kilometres laid by all facility providers in the state are under BharatNet.

Service quality and permissions diverge from the national line as well. Operational optical fibre with 95 percent uptime was available in 4,325 villages of Tamil Nadu as of January 2026, against a national December 2025 baseline of 42,000 villages on the way to a 2030 target of 2.7 lakh. Anchor-institution connectivity in the state was 84.74 percent as of December 2025, against a national 68.8 percent. Rural internet subscribers per 100 population in Tamil Nadu were 54.53 as of September 2025, against the national mission baseline of 47.16. Right-of-way disposal averaged 84.9 days in the state for 2025–26, against a national average of 30.4 days. The 4G saturation project had deployed 255 base stations to cover 297 villages in the state as on 31 December 2025.

Tamil Nadu is ahead of the national anchor-institution rate and behind the national permission clock. A single "state broadband index" would hide that split.

Portability demand is a third map. In July 2026 the heaviest mobile-number-portability counts were Uttar Pradesh (East), Uttar Pradesh (West), Madhya Pradesh, and Bihar, which is a population-and-competition map, not a tele-density ranking.
