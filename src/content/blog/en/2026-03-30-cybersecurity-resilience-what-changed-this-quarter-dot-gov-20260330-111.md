# What is dated in India's cybersecurity record

India does not publish a quarterly cybersecurity score. CERT-In's public incident series, the Union Budget, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules are all dated on different calendars. A reader in late September 2026 who asks "what changed this quarter" needs that distinction first.

The quarter that contains 24 September 2026 is July–September. Nothing in the primary documents reviewed for this brief is a new cybersecurity statute notified inside those three months. The live story is older instruments whose commencement dates are still running, plus a parliamentary accounting from March 2026 that is the newest official incident series in this set.

## The newest official incident series is annual, and it is from March

On 24 March 2026, the Minister of State for Home Affairs, Shri Bandi Sanjay Kumar, gave a written Lok Sabha reply that the Press Information Bureau published the same day. CERT-In, the national incident-response agency under section 70B of the Information Technology Act, 2000, reported these totals:

| Year | Cyber security incidents tracked by CERT-In |
| --- | --- |
| 2021 | 14,02,809 |
| 2022 | 13,91,457 |
| 2023 | 15,92,917 |
| 2024 | 20,41,360 |
| 2025 | 29,44,248 |

The 2023 figure matches CERT-In's own Annual Report 2023. The reply also says CERT-In does not maintain estimated financial loss from these incidents. A higher count is not, on this record, a measured rupee loss.

## What is already in force, and what is still ahead

The [Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/) were notified on 13 November 2025 as G.S.R. 846(E). PIB's note of 14 November 2025 calls this the operationalisation of the Act enacted on 11 August 2023, and it describes an 18-month phased timeline. The rules themselves are more precise than that headline:

- Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 came into force on gazette publication.
- Rule 4, on registration and obligations of Consent Managers, comes into force one year after publication.
- Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 come into force eighteen months after publication.

From 24 September 2026, Rule 4 is the next dated gate, one year after 13 November 2025. The larger set — consent notices, reasonable security safeguards, breach intimation, and significant-data-fiduciary duties — is still on the eighteen-month clock. Treating "the DPDP law" as one switch that flipped in November 2025 will mis-time a compliance programme.

## Three earlier shifts that still govern daily work

These are not September 2026 news. They are the duties a firm is already inside.

The [CERT-In directions of 28 April 2022](https://www.cert-in.org.in/Directions70B.jsp), issued under section 70B(6), require covered entities to report specified cyber incidents within six hours of noticing them or being told of them, and to keep ICT logs for a rolling 180 days inside Indian jurisdiction. The same CERT-In page records a 27 June 2022 extension for MSMEs and for subscriber-validation by data centres, VPS, cloud and VPN providers. Read that extension for the altered dates; this brief does not restate a deadline it has not quoted from that file.

The Telecommunications (Telecom Cyber Security) Rules, 2024, G.S.R. 720(E) of 21 November 2024, require a telecommunication entity to report a security incident to the Central Government within six hours, and to furnish a fuller set of facts within twenty-four hours. India Code records a later amendment, G.S.R. 771(E) of 22 October 2025, which adds telecommunication identifier user entities and a mobile-number validation platform. That amendment is outside this quarter. It is the current text of the identifier rules.

CERT-In's [15 Elemental Cyber Defense Controls for MSMEs](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/Elemental_Cyber_Defense_Controls_for_MSME.pdf), version 1.0 dated 1 September 2025, is a baseline of 15 controls and 45 recommendations. It points MSMEs back to the six-hour reporting direction and the 180-day log rule. It is a starting set, and the document says so.

## How to use the next disclosure

When a new CERT-In annual report, a fresh Lok Sabha table, or a commencement notification appears, date it against the clocks above. A quarterly blog cycle is not one of those clocks.
