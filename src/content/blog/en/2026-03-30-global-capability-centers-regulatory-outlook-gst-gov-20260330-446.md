# Data-Transfer Rules for India's Captive Technology Hubs

A captive hub exists to move work, and often personal data, between India and a parent abroad. The binding Indian statute is the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, administered by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. The rules were notified on 13 November 2025. The design of the cross-border clause is permissive until the government specifies a restriction. That is a different regime from a blanket rule that all GCC data must stay in India.

## Who is covered

Section 3 applies the Act to processing of digital personal data inside India, whether the data were collected digitally or digitised later. It also applies to processing outside India if that processing is connected with offering goods or services to people in India. Personal or domestic processing, and personal data made public by the person concerned, sit outside the section.

A centre in Bengaluru or Hyderabad that processes employee data, customer data, or user data on Indian systems is processing inside India. The parent's location does not take that processing out of section 3. A parent that offers a service to people in India can also be in scope for processing it does abroad.

Section 16 is the transfer power. The Central Government may, by notification, restrict a transfer of personal data for processing to a country or territory it names. The section does not itself name countries. Subsection (2) keeps any stricter law in force. Sector rules that already limit a class of data are not switched off by this Act.

The Act's Schedule, read with section 33, sets monetary penalties. Failure to take reasonable security safeguards may extend to **two hundred and fifty crore rupees**. Failure to notify the Board or affected people of a breach may extend to **two hundred crore rupees**. Breach of the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary may extend to **one hundred and fifty crore rupees**. Breach of any other provision of the Act or the rules may extend to **fifty crore rupees**. Section 44 omits section 43A of the Information Technology Act, 2000, the earlier compensation provision for sensitive personal data.

## What the rules actually say about sending data out

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, were notified as G.S.R. 846(E) on 13 November 2025. Rule 1 staggers commencement:

- Rules 1, 2, and 17 to 21 from the date of publication in the Official Gazette. Those cover definitions and the Data Protection Board.
- Rule 4, on Consent Managers, one year after publication. That date is **13 November 2026**.
- Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22, and 23 eighteen months after publication. That date is **13 May 2027**. This set includes notice, security safeguards, breach intimation, and the cross-border rules.

Rule 15 says personal data processed under the Act may be transferred outside India, subject to requirements the Central Government may specify by general or special order, where the data are made available to a foreign State or to a person or entity under the control of such a State. Until such an order exists, the rule's own text does not list blocked countries.

Rule 13 is the clause that can become a localisation rule, and only for a defined class. A Significant Data Fiduciary is a fiduciary or class the Central Government notifies under section 10. Rule 13(4) requires such a fiduciary to ensure that personal data specified by the Central Government, on the recommendation of a committee, is processed so that the personal data **and the traffic data pertaining to its flow** are not transferred outside India. The committee includes officials of MeitY and may include other ministries. The rule does not name the datasets. Traffic data is in the sentence, which matters for telemetry and logs, not only for customer files.

Rule 13 is in the eighteen-month set. It does not apply on the day a company reads it in 2026. It applies from 13 May 2027, and the localisation sentence applies to data the government has specified, for fiduciaries the government has notified.

## What a captive should do with that structure

Map the datasets that routinely leave India for the parent: employee records, customer records of the parent's clients, and traffic or log data tied to those sets. Mark which of them would be painful to pin in India if a later order or a section 10 notification named them. Build the consent, notice, and breach-notice machinery against the May 2027 date, and treat 13 November 2026 as the Consent Manager date rather than as the date the whole Act's operating rules switch on.

Do not read a Government Community Cloud tender as this regime. MeitY uses those letters for a government cloud programme as well as for the industry's name for captive centres. The data-protection rules are the instrument that governs a multinational's Indian processing.
