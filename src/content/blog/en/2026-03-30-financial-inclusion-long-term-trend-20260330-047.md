India’s Financial Inclusion Index is a cumulative reading, and RBI publishes it once a year. The [17 August 2021 release](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_PressRelease.aspx?fn=2754&prid=52068) introduced it with two points already on the line: 43.4 for March 2017 and 53.9 for March 2021. The Bank said the index has no base year, so the level reflects the stock of effort to that date, on a scale where 0 is complete exclusion and 100 is full inclusion.

The annual releases after that fill in a steady climb:

- March 2022: 56.4, against 53.9 a year earlier, with growth across the sub-indices (2 August 2022).
- March 2023: 60.1, against 56.4, mainly from Usage and Quality (15 September 2023).
- March 2024: 64.2, against 60.1, mainly from Usage (9 July 2024).
- March 2025: 67.0, against 64.2, with the year’s improvement contributed by Usage and Quality (22 July 2025).
- March 2026: 70.0, against 67.0, mainly from Usage (16 July 2026).

Each of those notes says the sub-indices all grew. They do not print the Access, Usage, and Quality levels. Anyone who needs those levels has to use a document that actually publishes them.

## Two published windows, not one spliced series

RBI’s annual report covering the first index gives the only early sub-index path in that form. From March 2017 to March 2021, Access rose from 61.7 to 73.3, Usage from 30.8 to 43.0, and Quality from 48.5 to 50.7. Usage and Quality were still below the overall index. The report put the compound annual growth of the headline index over that span at 5.5 percent.

NSFI 2025–30, in its retrospect on the 2019–2024 strategy, publishes a later window: the FI-Index from 49.9 in 2019 to 64.2 in 2024. Inside that window Access moved from 67.5 to 79.3, Usage from 38.7 to 55.5, and Quality from 52.6 to 65.8. The 64.2 matches the March 2024 press release. The 2019 level of 49.9 sits between the March 2017 print of 43.4 and the March 2021 print of 53.9, which is what a cumulative index should do. It is still a second source, not a missing row from the July press cycle.

Read together, the two windows say the same thing in different years. Access was already the high sub-index. Usage rose faster from a lower base, and the recent one-line releases have started naming Usage as the main contributor. Quality moved too, and in the 2023 and 2025 notes RBI names it alongside Usage.

## The survey line underneath

The demand-side line is older than the index. The World Bank’s [Global Findex 2021 India country brief](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/4c4fe6db0fd7a7521a70a39ac518d74b-0050062022/original/Findex2021-India-Country-Brief.pdf) says account ownership rose from 35 percent of adults in 2011 to 78 percent in 2021, with most of that rise between 2014 and 2017, and with the 2021 rate essentially unchanged from 2017. Table B.1 of Global Findex 2025 then reports 89 percent for 2024. The same 2021 brief said 35 percent of accounts were inactive. The 2025 spotlight says 16 percent of account owners in India had no active account. The [global brief](/blog/financial-inclusion-global-context-20260330-087) puts that inactivity next to other countries. The [latest change](/blog/financial-inclusion-what-changed-20260330-007) is the March 2026 print.
