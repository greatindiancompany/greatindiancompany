# An evidence snapshot of India's major ports and shipping pages

A useful snapshot says where a fact lives and how old it is. India's ports and shipping pages are primary, and they are not internally tidy. The pages that carry this topic are the ministry's Ports Wing, Sagarmala division, and shipping division; the Indian Ports Association; and Press Information Bureau notes that carry a minister's reply.

## Port counts: use the Ports Wing, and notice the clash

The Ports Wing says there are 14 major ports, of which 12 are operational, and about 200 non-major ports, of which around 65 handle cargo. It names the twelve operating authorities. The Indian Ports Association, formed in 1966 under the Societies Registration Act and based in New Delhi, lists the same twelve as members. Kamarajar appears as Kamarajar Port Limited. The Sagarmala page still says "12 major ports and approximately 200 non-major ports." It does not mention 14, and it does not say that two are not yet operating. For the count, prefer the Ports Wing. Do not average the two sentences into thirteen.

The two non-operating major ports are not named on the Ports Wing page. Naming them from memory would be a guess. Leave them unnamed until the ministry names them.

## Cargo: the association's dated file, not a programme page

The association posts operational reports. The list includes major-port traffic for June 2026, dated 6 July 2026, and April to July 2026, dated 6 August 2026. That is the evidence for recent major-port cargo. This snapshot does not restate the tonnes, because the file can be replaced and a copied number cannot.

The Sagarmala page carries older planning evidence. It says the 2016 National Perspective Plan projected more than 2,500 million metric tonnes a year of traffic by 2025, that Indian port capacity was about 2,400 million metric tonnes a year, and that a roadmap aimed at more than 3,300 million by 2025. It also says coastal traffic rose 118 percent over a decade and estimates about 250 million metric tonnes of coastal traffic by 2025. Those sentences document what the programme published. They are not the August 2026 traffic note. Capacity and traffic are different units even when both are in tonnes.

## Fleet, trade share, and money

The shipping division says about 95 percent of trade by volume and 68 percent by value moves by sea. Sagarmala says more than 90 percent by volume. Quote the page you opened. The same shipping page gives the merchant fleet as on 30 April 2019: 1,411 vessels, 12.79 million gross tonnage, 17th by that page's ranking. That date is part of the fact. Strip the date and the fact becomes false.

Shipyard money has two dated bureau notes. On 27 December 2025 the ministry's scheme design was posted: 15 to 25 percent assistance, a 40 percent scrap credit, scheme life to 31 March 2036, and capacity language of about 4.5 million gross tonnage a year by 2047. On 24 July 2026 a Lok Sabha reply posted the package total of ₹69,725 crore, the fund split, the 26 May 2026 fund-manager appointment, three in-principle clusters, four in-principle brownfield yards, and a contract value of ₹15,234 crore for assistance approvals over two years. The reply also cites UN Trade and Development for 2024 and 2025 output. Attribute that output to the reply's citation. It is not a figure calculated on this page.

Yard structure on the shipping division is a count, not a financial: 61 shipyards, eight central, two state, 51 private. Recycling structure is Alang-Sosiya: nearly 98 percent of Indian recycling, 153 plots, 115 of 131 allotted plots described as Hong Kong Convention compliant.

If a sentence you need is not on one of these pages, it is not in this snapshot. Great Indian Company would rather leave a gap than borrow a generic open-data link. The series is at [greatindiancompany.com/blog](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog).
