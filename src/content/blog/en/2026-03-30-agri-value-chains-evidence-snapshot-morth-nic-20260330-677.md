# The holding and household numbers behind the chain

Two official counts describe the base of this chain, and they do not count the same thing. The Agriculture Census counts operational holdings. The Situation Assessment Survey counts agricultural households and what they earn. A value-chain plan that treats them as one denominator will mis-state both scale and income.

The tenth Agriculture Census, 2015-16, recorded 146.45 million operational holdings and 157.82 million hectares operated. The average size was 1.08 hectares. In 2010-11 the same series had 138.35 million holdings, 159.59 million hectares, and an average of 1.15 hectares. Holdings rose 5.86 percent. Area fell 1.11 percent. The census is a structural survey of who operates land. It is not a 2026 production estimate, and the Agriculture Census portal still presents 2015-16 as the latest completed round in that ten-census series.

Size classes are the part a chain designer can use:

| Class | Share of holdings | Share of operated area | Average size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marginal, below 1 hectare | 68.45% | 24.03% | 0.38 ha |
| Small, 1 to 2 hectares | 17.62% | 22.91% | 1.40 ha |
| Semi-medium, 2 to 4 hectares | 9.55% | 23.84% | 2.69 ha |
| Medium, 4 to 10 hectares | 3.80% | 20.16% | 5.72 ha |
| Large, 10 hectares and above | 0.57% | 9.07% | 17.07 ha |

Marginal and small holdings together are about 86 percent of the count and about 47 percent of the area. The four smaller classes below “large” hold almost all the farms. The large class is a thin count with a large average. Lot size, assaying cost, and the viability of a one-member equity grant of ₹2,000 all sit on this table. Uttar Pradesh had the most holdings, 23.82 million. Rajasthan had the most operated area, 20.87 million hectares. The state split is in [where holdings and mandis sit](/blog/agri-value-chains-state-wise-patterns-morth-nic-20260330-317).

## Income is a household series, and it is older

The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, answering Parliament, says the latest Situation Assessment Survey is the National Statistical Office’s 77th round, January to December 2019, covering agricultural year 2018-19. Average monthly income per agricultural household was ₹10,218, up from ₹6,426 in 2012-13. That reply does not publish a wage-versus-crop split, and it does not update the figure to 2025 or 2026. ₹10,218 is not a mandi price, not an MSP, and not the turnover of an FPO.

An operational holding and an agricultural household are different units. One holding can be operated by a household that also earns wages. One household can operate more than one parcel. The census average of 1.08 hectares cannot be divided into ₹10,218 to produce “income per hectare” without a bridge the two publications do not provide. Anyone who needs that bridge has to go back to the survey microdata, not to a blended paragraph.

These two numbers are still the right baseline for a chain, because later policy is built on top of them. MSP is a price per quintal for a crop. Procurement tonnes are what agencies buy. e-NAM tonnes are what mandis trade. None of those series tells you how many farms exist or what a household earned in the last survey the Ministry calls the latest. Use 146.45 million and 1.08 hectares for structure. Use ₹10,218, dated 2018-19, for the last official household average. Use a different page for this season’s price.
