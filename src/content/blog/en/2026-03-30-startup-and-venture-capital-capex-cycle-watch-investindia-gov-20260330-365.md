Industrial capex is money spent on plant, machinery, and buildings. Startup India’s headline corpus is a promise to buy units in other people’s funds. Watching one series as if it were the other will mis-time both a factory cycle and a venture cycle.

## Four stocks that share a name and not a meaning

DPIIT’s operational guidelines for Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0, published with the Press Information Bureau note of 25 April 2026, fix the corpus at ₹10,000 crore. Commitments to alternative investment funds are spread over the 16th and 17th Finance Commission cycles. The scheme “would not invest directly in startups”. It participates in the capital of SEBI-registered Category I and Category II funds. Those funds then invest in equity, equity-linked instruments, and debt instruments of entities DPIIT currently recognises as startups.

That single design creates four numbers a reader must keep apart.

The corpus is the size of the government pool: ₹10,000 crore for Fund of Funds 2.0, and a separate ₹10,000 crore corpus long associated with the first Fund of Funds.

A commitment is a contract with a fund, drawn later. The guidelines cap the government cheque inside each fund: 40 percent and ₹500 crore for deep-tech funds, 30 percent and ₹100 crore for smaller early-growth funds, 30 percent and ₹200 crore for tech-driven manufacturing funds, and 25 percent and ₹180 crore for sector-agnostic funds. Aggregate money from government funds of funds, Centre or state, stays within 50 percent of a fund’s corpus.

A disbursement is cash that has left the implementation agency. For the first programme, PIB said that by the end of financial year 2025-26 more than ₹7,000 crore had been disbursed to over 135 funds. The year itself disbursed 1,039+ crore rupees, after 980+ crore in 2024-25. Implementation-agency operating costs under Fund of Funds 2.0 are capped at 0.50 percent a year of commitments outstanding after redemptions, taken from the corpus. That fee is administration, not capex.

Portfolio investment is what the funds report they have put into companies. As on 31 December 2025 the Rajya Sabha annexure put that stock at ₹25,547.98 crore across 1,371 startups. PIB’s later year-end line is more than ₹26,900 crore in over 1,420 startups. Portfolio investment exceeds government disbursement because private limited partners fund the rest, which is the multiplier the scheme is built to produce. Fund of Funds 2.0 writes the multiplier into the guidelines: 1.5 times for deep tech, 2 times for early-growth micro funds, 1.75 times for manufacturing-oriented funds, and 2.5 times for agnostic funds. “Times” here means capital the fund invests relative to the government commitment. It does not mean a rise in gross fixed capital formation.

## The calendar of fund investment, not of factories

The same Rajya Sabha reply, placed on 13 February 2026, prints the year-wise amount invested in startups by funds supported under the first scheme, as on 31 December 2025, in crore rupees:

| Calendar year | Invested |
| --- | ---: |
| 2016 | 0.000 |
| 2017 | 343.520 |
| 2018 | 676.842 |
| 2019 | 1,623.555 |
| 2020 | 2,066.888 |
| 2021 | 3,491.006 |
| 2022 | 5,973.741 |
| 2023 | 3,366.478 |
| 2024 | 3,734.869 |
| 2025 | 4,271.080 |
| Total | 25,547.98 |

The peak year in this table is 2022, at ₹5,973.741 crore. The next two years are lower, and 2025 is higher than 2024 without regaining the 2022 print. That is a venture-deployment path. It is silent on whether the portfolio companies bought machines, hired contractors, or paid for inventory. DPIIT has not, in these documents, published a startup capex survey.

The manufacturing segment of Fund of Funds 2.0 is still a fund segment. Eligible funds must say, in the private placement memorandum, that they will support tech-driven innovative startups in at least one manufacturing-oriented champion sector, as the government defines those sectors. The vehicle remains an alternative investment fund with a life capped at 18 years in that segment. An 18-year fund life is patient capital. It is not an appropriation for a plant.

## What to watch if the question is physical investment

Watch drawdowns against the ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds 2.0 corpus, the half-yearly review DPIIT required in the guidelines, and the annual utilisation report that must include funds backed, companies backed, and net asset value. Watch the first scheme’s disbursement line, which was still below the old ₹10,000 crore corpus at the end of 2025-26 on the “more than ₹7,000 crore” print. Leave plant and machinery to the industrial statistics that actually measure them.
