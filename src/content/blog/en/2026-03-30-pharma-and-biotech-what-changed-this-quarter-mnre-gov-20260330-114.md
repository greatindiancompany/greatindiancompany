# What Changed in India’s Bulk-Drug Record by February 2026

A useful quarterly read of Indian pharma policy starts from two dated public texts, then stops. On 21 March 2020 the Union Cabinet approved a bulk-drug park scheme and a production-linked incentive for critical key starting materials, drug intermediates, and active pharmaceutical ingredients. On 6 February 2026 the Minister for Chemicals and Fertilizers, Jagat Prakash Nadda, gave the Lok Sabha a written status of those same schemes. The gap between those two documents is the change worth tracking. This page does not fill June–September 2026 with a fresh statistic the record does not contain.

## The product list is no longer the 2020 list

The cabinet decision identified 53 critical bulk drugs: 26 fermentation-based and 27 made by chemical synthesis. Incentive was set at 20 percent of incremental sales for fermentation products and 10 percent for chemical-synthesis products, measured over the 2019–20 base year, for six years. The financial envelope was ₹6,940 crore over eight years. The scheme applied only to those identified products.

The 6 February 2026 reply describes the same scheme with a later count: incentives on the sale of 41 identified products, made in greenfield projects. It reports 48 approved projects covering 33 bulk drugs. A 1 September 2022 Press Information Bureau note had put the then-current position at 51 approved projects, of which 14 had been commissioned. Those three counts — 53 products in 2020, 51 projects in 2022, 41 products and 48 projects for 33 drugs in February 2026 — are all official, and they are not the same number. The Department of Pharmaceuticals’ schemes page is where the corrigenda and the approved-applicant lists sit, including a list dated 6 November 2023 and a corrigendum dated 4 May 2026. This brief does not paraphrase that May 2026 corrigendum. The file itself is the next read.

## The three parks moved from proposal maps to project sheets

In 2020 the cabinet approved three parks, a ₹3,000 crore envelope, and a ceiling of ₹1,000 crore per park, with common facilities such as solvent recovery, distillation, power and steam, and a common effluent treatment plant. By 1 September 2022 the Department had given in-principle approval to Himachal Pradesh, Gujarat, and Andhra Pradesh, chosen from 13 state proposals with an advisory committee chaired by the CEO of NITI Aayog.

The February 2026 reply keeps those three states and replaces the 2022 proposal sketch with implementing agencies and costs. Gujarat’s park is at Jambusar in Bharuch, on 2,015.02 acres, through the Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation, with a total project cost of ₹2,507.02 crore and common-infrastructure cost of ₹1,457.01 crore. Himachal Pradesh’s park is at Haroli in Una, on 1,405.01 acres, through Himachal Pradesh Bulk Drug Park Infrastructure Ltd., at ₹1,923 crore total and ₹1,118.46 crore of common infrastructure. Andhra Pradesh’s park is at Nakkapalli in Anakapalli, on 2,001.80 acres, through Andhra Pradesh Bulk Drug Infrastructure Corporation, at ₹1,876.66 crore total and ₹1,438.89 crore of common infrastructure. The 2022 note had described the Andhra Pradesh site as K.P. Puram and Kodhada in the then East Godavari district, on 2,000.45 acres, and the Himachal site as 1,402.44 acres. Quote the 2026 locations when you mean the current project.

The same reply sets the scheme tenure as FY 2020–21 through FY 2026–27. Central assistance is the lower of ₹1,000 crore or 70 percent of common-infrastructure cost, and 90 percent in Himachal Pradesh and the other hilly and North-Eastern jurisdictions named in the reply. It also states a grant-in-aid of ₹1,000 crore to each approved park.

## What did not change into a finished industry

Construction status in that reply is still a works programme. Gujarat’s civil packages are described as an advanced stage of completion, with effluent treatment, solvent recovery, and disposal-facility execution underway. Andhra Pradesh has awarded road, power, water, drainage, and utility-building tenders; fencing is complete; an external water pipeline is being installed; power-design approval is in process; extra land is still being acquired, with environmental clearance for that land granted in January 2026. Himachal Pradesh has awarded road, drainage, bridge, and water tenders, and has floated the tender for zero-liquid-discharge facilities, including the effluent plant and steam system.

The pharmaceuticals PLI, a separate scheme notified on 3 March 2021 with an outlay of ₹15,000 crore, is reported in the February 2026 reply as 55 approved applicants. That applicant count also appeared in the September 2022 note. Stability of the count is a fact about those two texts. It is not a substitute for the named list on the Department’s site.

## How to use the next quarter

Treat any new Department notice, applicant list, or parliamentary reply as an update only when it carries a date later than 6 February 2026. Until that document exists in your file, leave plant-level sales, jobs, and commissioning claims out of the memo.
