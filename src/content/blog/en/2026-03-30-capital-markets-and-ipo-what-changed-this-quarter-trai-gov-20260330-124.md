# What moved in India's listing and fund rules this quarter

The quarter that matters for this note is July to September 2026. The issue-of-capital rulebook was last consolidated earlier, on 21 March 2026. What landed in this quarter is the ongoing life of a listed company, the mutual fund rulebook, and the statistical record of the year just closed.

## Listing obligations were consolidated again in July

SEBI's page for the Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015 now carries a text last amended on 14 July 2026. That is the version a listed company, a merchant banker, or a buyer of a newly listed stock should open first. LODR is the post-listing rule. It covers financial-result timelines, material-event disclosure, related-party transactions, and the corporate-governance duties that begin the day an IPO lists. The March ICDR text still governs how a company gets onto the market. The July LODR text governs how it stays there.

A related gazette change is already operational. Gazette notification SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2026/312, dated 10 July 2026, amended regulation 40(7). SEBI then issued circular HO/38/13/11(14)2026-MIRSD-POD/I/17111/2026 on 23 July 2026. The circular standardises transmission of listed securities and of mutual fund units after the death of the sole holder or of all joint holders. It adds a low-value path called Quick Transmission Processing, drops the mandatory probate of a will, allows a combined affidavit and no-objection letter, and accepts a death certificate that carries a QR code. The circular says the framework comes into force 30 days after 23 July 2026. By this note's date, that window has opened. Disputed claims are outside it and still go to a court or other legal process.

## The mutual fund regulations were amended in the same fortnight

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026, were notified on 14 January 2026 and last amended on 7 July 2026. SEBI's Annual Report for 2025-26 describes the January text as a rewrite of the layered 1996 regulations: shorter drafting, valuation rules pulled into one place, and scheme documents moved into prescribed formats. The July amendment is the text now on the legal page. Anyone comparing a scheme information document with "the regulations" should use the 7 July consolidation, not a 1996 printout.

## SEBI published the year, not a new market forecast

On 17 August 2026, SEBI press release 47/2026 announced the 21st Handbook of Statistics, for 2025-26. The release says the book has 85 annual tables and 68 quarterly or monthly tables, 153 in all, covering primary markets, secondary markets, derivatives, and investment vehicles. The same month, SEBI posted Annual Report 2025-26, including the primary-market, secondary-market, and fund-management chapters. Those chapters are the right place for last year's issuance, demat, SIP, and foreign-portfolio figures. They are a closed-year record. They are not a print of this quarter's order book.

One market-structure item in the secondary-market chapter is timed for this quarter. SEBI describes a closing auction session, from 3:15 pm to 3:35 pm, for stocks that already have derivative contracts. The report schedules that session for August 2026. Other stocks stay on the existing 30-minute volume-weighted close until the framework is extended. The operative exchange circular is the document that tells a trading desk whether the session is live on a given day.

## What did not move in this quarter

The pledged-share lock-in change is easy to mis-date. ICDR was amended on 21 March 2026 so that depositories can mark pledged pre-issue shares "non-transferable" for the lock-in. SEBI's circular of 8 April 2026, HO/49/(17)2026-CFD-POD2/I/8965/2026, told exchanges, depositories, merchant bankers, and issuers to follow the depository framework, including articles of association, lender intimations, and offer-document disclosure. That mechanism is in force. It is not a July event.

RBI's foreign-portfolio debt limits for 2026-27 were set on 6 April 2026. The half-year ceiling that runs through September 2026 is the one now in use. The higher ceiling for October 2026 to March 2027 is already printed in that same circular. Nothing in the July-September SEBI texts above replaces it.

## How to use the quarter

Read the 14 July LODR text for anything a listed company must disclose. Read the 23 July transmission circular if the question is inheritance of shares or fund units. Read the 7 July mutual fund text if the question is a scheme rule. Read the August handbook and annual report if the question is what FY 2025-26 actually raised, and keep those figures labelled as last year's. For a live IPO, the draft offer document and the exchange in-principle approval still control the timetable.
