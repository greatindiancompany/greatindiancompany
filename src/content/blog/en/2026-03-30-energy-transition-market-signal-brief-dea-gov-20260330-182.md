# Energy Transition Market Signals in India

A market signal is a fact that changes what a buyer, a lender, or a manufacturer should expect next quarter. India's energy-transition debate often treats the 500 GW aim as that signal. The sharper signals are in who actually added capacity in FY 2025-26, what happened to module factories, and which compliance rules now force a designated consumer to procure clean electricity.

## Demand showed up in more than one contract

MNRE's 8 April 2026 briefing puts solar addition in FY 2025-26 at 44.61 GW, against a 34 GW target and against 23.83 GW added in FY 2024-25. The mix matters more than the headline.

Distributed solar contributed 16.31 GW, about 36 percent of the year's solar addition: 8.71 GW rooftop and 7.67 GW under PM-KUSUM. Of the remaining 28.30 GW, the Ministry described about 15 GW as power-purchase-agreement projects and about 13 GW as commercial, industrial, and captive. Across rooftop and utility projects, it put the commercial and industrial share, including captive, at about 15 GW, roughly 34 percent of solar added that year.

That is a two-track market. One track is still a utility or SECI-style contract. The other is a company buying for its own load, which the Green Energy Open Access Rules made easier by setting the green open-access floor at 100 kW, with no floor for captive use. A developer who models India as only a central auction is describing half of last year's solar.

Wind sent a smaller but cleaner signal. Addition was 6.05 GW in FY 2025-26, against 4.15 GW the year before, which MNRE called the highest annual wind addition on record. The August 2026 PIB factsheet says about 28 GW of further wind capacity is under implementation. Installed wind on 31 July 2026 was 58.14 GW. The under-implementation stock is large relative to the installed base, which is a pipeline signal, not a commissioning date.

## The factory signal ran ahead of imports

The same FY 2025-26 review says solar module manufacturing capacity reached about 172 GW by 31 March 2026, from about 2.3 GW in 2014, with about 98 GW added during the year from a base near 74 GW. Wind turbine manufacturing capacity was about 24 GW, up from 10 GW in 2014. MNRE said 8 of 12 production-linked-incentive manufacturers had started production somewhere in the module value chain. It also reported solar-module imports of USD 758 million, against USD 2,152 million in the comparison period in that briefing.

Read the 172 GW figure as nameplate factory capacity. Installed solar generation capacity on 31 July 2026 was 164.59 GW. A country can have more module-making capacity than installed plants. The PLI scheme pays selected manufacturers for five years after commissioning, on manufacture and sale of high-efficiency modules. Tranche II, with an outlay of ₹19,500 crore, awarded letters to 11 bidders in April 2023 for 39,600 MW of fully or partly integrated capacity. Starting production is the milestone the April 2026 briefing could confirm. Full integrated output, including ingots and wafers, is a later milestone: MNRE said an Approved List of Models and Manufacturers pathway for ingot-wafer had been announced for June 2028.

Two tax changes point the same way. From 22 September 2025 the GST rate on renewable-energy devices and parts fell from 12 percent to 5 percent. From 2 February 2026 to 31 March 2028, basic-customs-duty exemption covers capital goods for lithium-ion cell manufacturing used in battery energy storage.

## Compliance is now a procurement signal

The renewable consumption obligation for 2026-27 is 35.95 percent of the relevant electricity consumption for distribution licensees, open-access consumers, and captive users. The Central Electricity Regulatory Commission has issued guidelines for virtual power purchase agreements as one way for designated consumers to meet that obligation, and it has a buyout-price route as well. MNRE has also issued a pilot contract-for-difference scheme for 500 MW of renewable projects.

These instruments tell a buyer that "I will procure when it is cheap" is no longer the whole story. A designated consumer has a percentage to cover this year. They do not tell you the tariff. Auction prices move month to month and are not restated here.

## What would confirm or weaken the signal

Confirmation would be another MNRE year in which commercial and industrial solar stays large, wind additions stay near the FY 2025-26 pace, and module imports stay well below the earlier comparison level. A weak signal would be a year in which the 172 GW of module capacity does not translate into domestic sales, or in which the 28 GW wind pipeline does not show up in the monthly wind row. The 500 GW aim does not distinguish those outcomes. The monthly sheet does.
