# What Changed in India's Household Rooftop Solar Scheme

The useful quarterly question is narrow. What did the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy put on the record about household rooftop solar, and how does that print differ from the scheme as the Cabinet approved it?

## The note that belongs to this quarter

On 4 August 2026, PIB Delhi issued release 2294208 on PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana. That date sits in the quarter this page is updated for (the page date is 24 September 2026). The release is a progress note, not a new cabinet decision.

It reports that more than 50.06 lakh households are benefiting from rooftop solar installations. It sets that against 7.94 lakh rooftop solar installations over the previous ten years. It says the scheme is benefiting one lakh households every six days, and that the daily pace rose 3.2 times over nine months, from 5,038 installations a day in October 2025 to nearly 16,328 a day in July 2026. July 2026 alone covered 5.06 lakh households, which the note calls the highest monthly figure since the scheme began.

Money movement is part of the same note. It says Rs 28,024 crore in subsidy has been released to beneficiaries through direct benefit transfer. Nearly 19 lakh households are receiving zero electricity bills. More than 12 lakh households together earned Rs 421 crore from surplus power sales in FY 2024-25, which the note translates to about Rs 3,500 a year for a household that exported power.

## What the 2024 design said, and what later operations changed

Cabinet approval is a different document. PIB release 2010133, dated 29 February 2024, set an outlay of Rs 75,021 crore, a target of one crore households, and free electricity up to 300 units a month. It described central financial assistance of 60 percent of system cost for 2 kW systems and 40 percent of the additional cost between 2 and 3 kW, capped at 3 kW. At the benchmark prices stated then, that meant Rs 30,000 for 1 kW, Rs 60,000 for 2 kW, and Rs 78,000 for 3 kW or higher. It also said households would be able to access collateral-free loans of around 7 percent for systems up to 3 kW.

The August 2026 note still cites the same Rs 75,021 crore outlay. It then describes an operating system that the cabinet note only sketched: a fully online path from application to subsidy, Jan Samarth for paperless loans, PFMS release of subsidy within 15 days, API links with more than 80 distribution companies, a digital net-metering agreement, vendor star ratings, and domestic-content checks through the DCR portal. Concessional loans are stated at 5.75 percent, benchmarked to the repo rate plus 50 basis points, sanctioned for 21.87 lakh applicants, with 17.5 lakh installations completed through loan financing.

That loan-rate line is a change in the published terms. The 2024 note said "around 7 percent." The 2026 note states 5.75 percent and the benchmark. A founder pricing a customer offer should use the later note, and should still open the national portal before quoting a rate to a household, because a press note is not the loan sanction.

## Capacity, vendors, and public buildings

Release 2294208 says 14.8 GW of rooftop solar has been commissioned under the scheme. The cabinet note had said the scheme would add 30 GW in the residential sector over the life of the systems. Those two figures answer different questions: one is commissioned capacity reported in August 2026, the other is the outcome the cabinet attached to the scheme in February 2024. They should not be subtracted as if both were measured on the same day.

The vendor base in the August note is 34,219 registered vendors, of whom 29,469 are active, and more than 2.32 lakh people trained under capacity-building programmes. Deemed approval and a feasibility waiver apply for rooftop solar up to 10 kW. Thirty-two states and Union Territories have removed application and net-metering charges. Incentives released are Rs 3,807.6 crore to distribution companies and about Rs 104 crore to urban local bodies.

Government buildings are a separate track in the same release. Central ministries had identified about 41,542 feasible buildings with a potential of 1,418 MW, of which 25,255 buildings and 872 MW were solarized. States had identified about 3,20,000 feasible buildings with a potential of 4,400 MW, of which 81,329 buildings and 1,450 MW were solarized. Together, 1.06 lakh government buildings were solarized. A payment security mechanism is described for RESCO bidders on state and UT solarization projects.

## What this quarter's note does not settle

The August release does not publish a state-by-state household table. It does say the utility-led aggregation model has completed 1.6 lakh installations for PMAY, BPL, and SC/ST households across four states, and is sanctioned in twelve states. It does not revise the module PLI outlay or the battery storage viability-gap scheme. Those sit in other notes.

Module eligibility still matters for anyone installing under a government scheme. MNRE's ALMM page, updated 16 September 2026, says only models and manufacturers on List-I may be used in government projects, government schemes, open access, and net-metering. The August rooftop note's DCR integration is the household-scheme version of that domestic-content check.

## How to use the two releases

Read 2010133 for the design: outlay, household count, assistance formula, and the 30 GW outcome the cabinet attached to the scheme. Read 2294208 for the operating report dated 4 August 2026: installations, subsidy paid, bills, vendors, and commissioned gigawatts. Quote the date with the figure.
