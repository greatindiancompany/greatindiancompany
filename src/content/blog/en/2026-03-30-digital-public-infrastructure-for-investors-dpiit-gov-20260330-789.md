# How investors should read India's digital public rails

Investors meet India's digital public infrastructure twice. First as a story about population-scale systems. Then as a company that claims to sit on those systems. The public record is strong enough to test the story. It is not strong enough to price the company. This note is the separation.

Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any security. It is a way to read the documents.

## The rails are busy. That fact is no longer the diligence

Several official counts, all dated, show use at a scale that does not need a pitch deck.

NPCI's UPI product statistics for August 2026: 752 banks live, 24,508.96 million transactions, value of ₹29,82,355.95 crore. April's row was 713 banks, 22,346.80 million transactions, and ₹29,02,988.05 crore. Volume and bank participation rose across those months. Value did not rise in a straight line. May, at ₹29,90,424.21 crore, is above August.

DigiLocker, as on 31 July 2026 in the Rajya Sabha statement: more than 72.43 crore registered users. The Lok Sabha answer of 29 July: over 71.66 crore users and more than 936.03 crore documents accessible. Allocation since inception, in that answer: ₹168.58 crore, with ₹105.14 crore spent.

Account Aggregators, Sahamati's public dashboard for July 2026: 326.30 million cumulative accounts linked, 538.32 million cumulative consents fulfilled, 343.78 million data deliveries in the month. Fulfilled consents are requests against which data was delivered at once, not every attempt.

ABHA, health ministry, as of 20 July 2026: 94.87 crore IDs, 5.36 lakh facilities, 10.09 lakh professionals.

ONDC, Commerce Ministry, as of July 2026: over 500 million cumulative orders, about 2 lakh or more active retail merchants.

A company that raises money on the claim that "India has not yet adopted digital rails" is arguing with these tables. A company that raises money on the claim that "therefore our revenue is large" is changing the subject. Adoption of the rail is not the company's take rate.

## Ask which seat the company occupies

The Reserve Bank's Account Aggregator directions of 28 November 2025 are a diligence checklist with yes or no answers.

If the company says it is an Account Aggregator, it should have a certificate of registration, net owned funds of at least ₹2 crore, and no other business. It should not be executing payments or loans. It should not be storing the financial information it pulls. It should not be outsourcing the aggregation itself. It should be able to show a consent artefact with purpose, recipient, and expiry. A data room full of bank-statement archives fails this test even if the archives came from customers. The directions say the information must not reside with the aggregator.

If the company says it uses Account Aggregator data to underwrite, it should be a financial information user, meaning an entity regulated by a financial-sector regulator. The directions name the Reserve Bank, SEBI, IRDAI, the pension regulator, and the Department of Revenue. An unregulated parent with a regulated subsidiary needs a clear statement of which entity receives the data. The July dashboard can confirm that the ecosystem delivers data at high volume. It cannot confirm that this company is allowed to receive it.

If the company says it is "the ONDC play," ask whether it is a buyer application, a seller application, a logistics provider, or a services firm around them. The ministry says ONDC is a section 8 company and not a marketplace. There is no equity claim on the network's 500 million orders unless the company can show its own orders. The reply publishes no take rate and no merchandise value. A revenue model that needs those numbers has to come from the company's own books. The public assistance programme, ₹277.35 crore through 2026-27 for small-enterprise onboarding, is not a proxy for marketplace commission.

If the company says it is an ABDM business, ask for the sandbox and the web application security audit. The ministry says both are required, and that there is no central store of health records. A model that depends on reselling a national clinical database contradicts the parliamentary reply. The 5.36 lakh facility registrations are a directory size. They are not a contract value.

## Read UPI volume and value before you read a growth adjective

Inside the five published months from April to August 2026, August is the volume high and not the value high. An investor listening to "record UPI" should ask which column. A payments company whose revenue is a function of value, or of a fee on a subset of merchants, can have a worse month in a record-volume month. The product-statistics page used here does not split person-to-person transfers from merchant payments, and it does not show app share. If a management presentation shows a market-share percentage, the source has to be something other than this national table. Do not let the national table launder an unaudited share figure.

The government said on 29 July 2026 that UPI serves more than 55.49 crore individuals and 6.5 crore merchants and connects 731 banks, and that it powers 85 percent of India's digital payments and nearly 50 percent of global real-time payments. Use the user and merchant stocks as a dated government description. The 731 banks match June, not August. The two percentages are not accompanied, in that release, by the worksheet. They are not a substitute for a company's own volume.

## Cheap public build costs are not private margins

DigiLocker's disclosed decade-scale allocation is ₹168.58 crore against tens of crores of users. MeitY's eight Digital India sub-schemes cost a few thousand crores a year: ₹5,144.45 crore allocated and ₹4,638.28 crore spent in 2025-26, on the 29 July Lok Sabha reply. These numbers explain why the shared layer can be widely used without looking like a scarce toll road in the public accounts. They do not cap what a private company can charge for a workflow on top. They also do not prove that a private company can charge anything. Account Aggregators may price their own service, because the directions require a board-approved pricing policy, and they may not price the underlying data as an asset they own.

The investment implication is location. Returns, if they exist, are in a regulated user of data, a network participant with its own orders, a health application that facilities pay, or a service that reduces the cost of joining. They are not in a claim on UIDAI, on the DigiLocker budget line, or on ONDC's section 8 company.

## Risks that belong in the memo because they are documented

Coverage. UIDAI on 30 June 2026: national live penetration 94.59 percent, Nagaland 62.41 percent, Bihar 88.29 percent, Gujarat 89.74 percent. A company whose funnel assumes Aadhaar authentication will leak users in those states. That is a distribution fact, not a macro opinion.

Connectivity. June 2026: 2.21 lakh gram panchayats service-ready under BharatNet, points of presence operational at 80,472. Rural total addressable use is closer to the second number.

Timing of general data protection. The Data Protection Board exists under a 13 November 2025 notification. Most operative sections of the 2023 Act were appointed for eighteen months after that gazette, which falls in May 2027. Sector rules, including the Account Aggregator directions, already apply. A company that says privacy law is either fully here or entirely absent is imprecise. The memo should name which duties are live for its seat.

Measurement. ONDC will not give you a seller-level national census. Sahamati's consent column is not a loan book. DigiLocker's document stock is not its three-year access count. If the deck mixes these, ask for the cell in the original table.

## What you still have to get from the company

The public record will not give you revenue, contribution margin, fraud losses, app-level UPI share, credit disbursed through consented pulls, or the share of ONDC orders that belong to one application. Those are the diligence items. The public record will tell you when a deck has used a national stock as if it were a company metric. That test is worth doing before the financial model, because a model built on the wrong stock does not become reliable when the spreadsheet is more detailed.
