# India DPI for Investors: What the Public Record Will Support

## Read structure, then stop

Investors can use the Press Information Bureau research note of 6 March 2026 to see how India's digital public infrastructure is structured. They cannot use it to see earnings. This page, updated on 23 September 2026, keeps that line. It does not add a multiple, a fee, or a forecast.

## What the record will support

Retail payments are concentrated on one interoperable rail. The note says UPI carried 21.70 billion transactions in January 2026, worth over ₹28.33 lakh crore, with 691 banks live and 81 percent of domestic retail payment volume. It cites a June 2025 IMF report for UPI as the largest retail fast payment system by volume, and the 2024 ACI Worldwide report for around 49 percent of global real-time payment volume. The UPI figures are footnoted to NPCI.

Public procurement is a sized channel. Government eMarketplace cumulative gross merchandise value exceeded ₹16.41 lakh crore as of November 2025. Micro and small enterprises are stated at 44.8 percent of cumulative order value, on orders worth over ₹7.35 lakh crore.

Open commerce has a seller base, not a sales share. ONDC, launched in 2022, had 1.16 lakh-plus retail sellers in 630-plus cities and towns as of December 2025.

Identity and issued documents are at very wide scale. More than 144 crore Aadhaar numbers had been generated as of March 2026. DigiLocker had 67.63 crore users on 5 March 2026, with over 950 crore documents issued.

Cross-border presence is a list. UPI is live in the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, France, Mauritius, and Qatar. Separately, the note says 24 countries had cooperation agreements as of February 2026. Neither list is a disclosed sales pipeline. India Stack Global is the showcase page for the platforms. A showcase is not a contract.

## What the record will not support

- A revenue pool, take rate, or merchant-discount assumption for UPI.
- A private app's share of the 21.70 billion January transactions.
- A total addressable market for firms that use the rails.
- A bridge from Direct Benefit Transfer to private margin. Cumulative transfers had crossed ₹49.09 lakh crore as of January 2026. That figure is benefits paid to recipients.
- A capital-spending cycle for the whole stack. The programme outlay the note states is e-Courts Phase III at ₹7,210 crore for 2023 to 2027.
- A state winner. The note does not rank states.

## Consent rails are adoption, not yield

If the question is consented financial data, use the Department of Financial Services table rather than the PIB note. As of 31 March 2026 that page, crediting Sahamati, reports 17 licensed account aggregators and 284.6 million linked accounts. The page says registration is voluntary. Adoption of a consent rail is still not a yield.

The supportable position is narrower. Distribution for payments, public procurement, and government APIs runs on shared rails with published participation counts. Price, margin, and share remain outside this note.
