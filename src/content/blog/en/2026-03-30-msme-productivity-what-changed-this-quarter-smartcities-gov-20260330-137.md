# What still governs MSME productivity this quarter

A reader opening this in late September 2026 does not need a fresh slogan. The rules and the survey print that govern micro, small, and medium enterprise productivity are already on official paper. This quarter is about using those releases correctly.

## The classification in force

Ministry of MSME notification S.O. 1364(E), dated 21 March 2025, has applied since 1 April 2025. It amended S.O. 2119(E) of 26 June 2020. An enterprise is classified on investment in plant and machinery or equipment and on annual turnover, together:

- Micro: investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore.
- Small: investment up to ₹25 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore.
- Medium: investment up to ₹125 crore and turnover up to ₹500 crore.

The Union Budget 2025-26 note from the Press Information Bureau said the investment limits were raised to 2.5 times and the turnover limits to 2 times so that enterprises could reach higher scale, upgrade technology, and reach capital without dropping out of the category. Crossing either ceiling moves an enterprise up. It moves down only when both investment and turnover fall below the ceiling of its present category.

## The productivity print

On 24 March 2026 the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation released Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE) 2025, covering January to December 2025. That survey is the official activity picture for unincorporated non-agricultural manufacturing, trade, and other services. It is not the Udyam register.

PIB's ASUSE note reports establishments rising from 7.34 crore in ASUSE 2023-24 (October 2023–September 2024) to 7.92 crore, gross value added at current prices up 10.87 percent, and employment up 6.18 percent, with more than 74.52 lakh jobs added and about 12.81 crore workers in the sector. Gross value added per worker, for market establishments, rose from ₹1,49,742 to ₹1,56,539, which MoSPI states as 4.54 percent. The two survey years do not share the same calendar. October–December 2024 sits between them. Treat the growth rates as the comparison MoSPI published, and read the reference periods before you annualise them.

## The registration stock

A PIB note of 14 May 2026 says that, as of March 2026, more than 7.9 crore enterprises were on the two portals: 4.72 crore on Udyam and 3.21 crore on the Udyam Assist Platform. The Ministry's 26 June 2026 MSME note says registrations across both platforms had crossed 8.7 crore by June 2026. Those are registration counts. They do not measure output per worker.

The same 14 May note records two Budget 2026-27 finance items that sit in the background of this quarter: a ₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund, and an additional ₹2,000 crore for the Self-Reliant India Fund. It also says the budget mandates the Trade Receivables Discounting System for central public sector enterprises. None of those items replaces the ASUSE productivity ratios.

## How to use this quarter

Keep three files open. The 21 March 2025 notification tells you who still counts as micro, small, or medium. The 24 March 2026 ASUSE release tells you what happened to establishments, jobs, and current-price value added in the unincorporated sector. The Udyam and Udyam Assist counts tell you how wide the formal identity net had become by March and by June 2026. Productivity work starts when those three are kept apart.

The credit architecture around that identity — the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises, run with SIDBI, and the Reserve Bank's priority-sector treatment of Udyam certificates — is the companion question. It is not a substitute for the survey.
