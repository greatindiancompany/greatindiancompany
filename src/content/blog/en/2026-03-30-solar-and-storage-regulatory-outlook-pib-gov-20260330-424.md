# Who Sets the Rules for India's Solar Modules, Rooftops, and Batteries

The regulatory outlook for Indian solar and storage is a map of institutions. Mixing them up is how a project discovers, late, that the wrong ministry holds the approval it needs.

## Modules and cells sit with MNRE

The Approved List of Models and Manufacturers is an MNRE order of 2 January 2019. The ministry's page, updated 16 September 2026, says List-I covers solar modules and List-II covers solar cells. The first module list was issued on 10 March 2021. The first cell list was issued on 31 July 2025 and is updated regularly. Listed modules are required for government projects, government schemes, open access, net-metering, and sales to government under section 63 bidding guidelines.

PIB release 2082901, dated 10 December 2024, is the amendment that set 1 June 2026 as the date from which modules in those project classes must use List-II cells, unless the bid's last submission date was before that order. Thin-film modules from integrated factories are treated as meeting the cell requirement. From the date of this brief, that effective date is in the past. The live question is whether the model and the cell are on the lists MNRE is updating, not whether the amendment is still a proposal.

MNRE also holds the scheme rules that create demand for those listed products. The high-efficiency module PLI is an MNRE scheme. PM Surya Ghar and PM-KUSUM are MNRE schemes described in PIB notes. A distribution company's interface with a household is still a state matter, but the central assistance formula and the approved-list gate are MNRE's.

## Batteries are overseen by the Ministry of Power and watched by CEA

PIB release 2118325, dated 3 April 2025, is explicit about the split. The viability-gap scheme for battery energy storage was approved by the Cabinet on 6 September 2023. The Central Electricity Authority monitors the scheme. The Ministry of Power oversees it, for timely completion and use of funds. Support applies to projects approved during 2023-26. The release also says that in 2024-25 no project reached financial closure, so no expenditure was booked.

MNRE's storage overview, updated 21 November 2023, describes a different instrument: the energy storage obligation notified by the Ministry of Power. Obligated entities move from 1 percent in FY 2023-24 to 4 percent in FY 2029-30, by 0.5 percent a year. The obligation counts only when at least 85 percent of the energy stored in the year comes from renewable sources. That is a purchase rule for utilities and other obligated entities. It is not the same document as the viability-gap grant for a battery project.

CEA appears in both places as the planner and the monitor. MNRE quotes CEA's National Electricity Plan of 2023 for storage requirements of 82.37 GWh in 2026-27 and 411.4 GWh in 2031-32. The April 2025 power-ministry reply quotes the same plan for 236 GWh of battery storage by 2031-32. Planning numbers and grant rules should be traced to the institution that issued them.

## SECI bids and trades; it does not replace the regulator

SECI's what-we-do page says it is a Renewable Energy Implementing Agency for MNRE and a Category-I trading licensee under the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission. It runs tariff-based reverse auctions and signs power purchase agreements with developers and power sale agreements with distribution companies. CERC's licence is what allows the trading. MNRE's schemes are what many of the tenders implement. State distribution companies remain the buyers on the sale agreement.

An outlook that says "SECI will regulate storage" is not what these pages say. SECI can be the bid manager for utility-scale batteries, which its page says it is. The grant rules in release 2118325 stay with the Ministry of Power and CEA. The cell and module lists stay with MNRE.

## The near-term reading list

As of 24 September 2026, three rules are already in force or already dated, and they belong to different desks. Cell listing for covered projects from 1 June 2026, with the bid-date exemption in the December 2024 note. Battery grants that still depend on financial closure before the first 10 percent. A storage obligation path that runs through FY 2029-30 and requires renewable charging of at least 85 percent.
