# The work and the skills around India's digital rails

Digital public infrastructure changes work in specific places: a counter in a gram panchayat, a classroom, a shop learning to list goods, a clinic joining a registry. It does not, in the official record, come with a national "jobs created" total. The releases that do exist count trainees, entrepreneurs at service centres, teachers' platforms, seller training hours, and registered health professionals. Those are the counts a reader can use.

## The literacy scheme has already closed

The Press Information Bureau's account of 29 July 2026 says the Pradhan Mantri Gramin Digital Saksharta Abhiyan trained 6.39 crore individuals. It also says the scheme concluded on 31 March 2024. Anyone describing PMGDISHA as a live pipeline is behind the government's own sentence.

The same passage cites the National Sample Survey Office's Comprehensive Annual Modular Survey for 2022-23. In that survey, 78.4 percent of persons aged 15 to 24 reported they could send a message with an attached file. The survey also reported that 94.2 percent of rural households and 97.1 percent of urban households possessed a telephone or mobile phone. Phone ownership and the ability to send an attachment are not the same skill as completing an Account Aggregator consent or issuing a document into DigiLocker. They are the baseline the government chose to quote. The survey year is 2022-23, so it is not a 2026 labour-market print.

MeitY's Lok Sabha reply of 29 July 2026 describes how the government now tries to build skills after that scheme closed: courses on the iGOT Karmayogi platform, training through government institutes, and workshops for cybersecurity professionals, alongside regular cyber drills. That is a civil-service and security curriculum. It is not a count of private-sector jobs.

A separate information-security effort appears in related government material on awareness workshops. This brief does not add a second trainee total on top of the 6.39 crore figure, because the PMGDISHA number is the one tied to a named, closed scheme in the 29 July account.

## The job you can see is the village-level entrepreneur

Common Service Centres are the employment model the releases actually describe. They deliver government and business services through village-level entrepreneurs, not through a single national payroll.

As of May 2026, the PIB account says 5.01 lakh CSCs were functional, including 3.91 lakh at gram panchayat level, offering over 800 services. The Rajya Sabha statement carried on 12 August 2026, referring to 30 June 2026, says 4,07,122 CSCs were functional at gram panchayat level, and that 70,069 of those were operated by women village-level entrepreneurs. The gram-panchayat stock rose between the May and June descriptions. The women-operated figure is a June stock, not a flow of new jobs that month.

A CSC operator is closer to a local enterprise than to a salaried post in a ministry. The releases list the work: government services, banking, financial services, insurance, pensions, and utility payments, plus assisted access for people who will not complete a mobile journey alone. They do not publish wages, footfall per centre, or how many centres closed. The 70,069 figure is the gender fact that is safe to repeat. It is not a measure of women's employment in the wider digital economy.

## Teachers and classrooms already have a rail

DIKSHA, the Digital Infrastructure for Knowledge Sharing, is an initiative of the National Council of Educational Research and Training under the Ministry of Education. Its about page says it was launched in 2017, has been adopted by almost all states, Union Territories, and central boards including CBSE, and supports 36 Indian languages. Each state uses it in its own way. That design choice matters for jobs: a national platform does not mean a single national training contractor.

The same page says DIKSHA is built on Sunbird, described as MIT-licensed open-source technology, and that the National Digital Education Architecture was launched on 29 July 2021. Under PM eVidya, DIKSHA is described as "One Nation, One Digital Platform." The concrete teacher programme named there is NISHTHA. NISHTHA 1.0, for elementary grades, moved online onto DIKSHA during the pandemic. NISHTHA 2.0 and 3.0 focus on secondary grades and on foundational literacy and numeracy. States have added their own capacity-building programmes. The page also points to Indian Sign Language material and audio books for children with special needs, and to QR codes that link PM eVidya television channels to the same platform.

What the about page does not give is a current count of teachers trained or courses completed. Citing DIKSHA as a skills rail is fair. Citing a headcount of teachers that the page does not publish is not.

## Seller skills are a curriculum with a budget line

The Commerce Ministry's Lok Sabha reply of 4 August 2026 treats small-trader participation on the Open Network for Digital Commerce as a training problem as much as a software problem. It reports:

- DigiDukaan, an onboarding effort for small kirana and retail traders, operating in Hyderabad and Jaipur, with field staff and outreach.
- More than 3,000 hours of virtual training and more than 200 hours of technical training in open digital sessions, attended by over 50,000 participants.
- Seller handbooks and guidebooks for first-time digital sellers, in 14 Indian languages.
- The MSME Trade Enablement and Marketing outlay of ₹277.35 crore for 2024-25 through 2026-27, covering awareness, capacity building, cataloguing, onboarding, logistics, and packaging, with a target that 50 percent of beneficiary enterprises be women-owned.

The reply's picture of the network, as of July 2026, is approximately 2 lakh or more active retail merchants, about 60 percent of them small sellers, plus approximately 10 lakh or more service providers in mobility and logistics. Those are participation stocks. The 50,000 participants are a training stock. Neither number is "jobs created by ONDC." A shop that lists on a seller app may be an existing livelihood that gains a channel. A metro or logistics provider counted among the 10 lakh may already have had riders and drivers. The official text is careful on this point when it refuses a seller-by-seller census. Readers should be equally careful about employment multipliers.

## Health professionals are registered, which is not the same as hired

As of 20 July 2026, the health ministry told the Lok Sabha that 10.09 lakh healthcare professionals were on the Healthcare Professionals Registry and 5.36 lakh facilities were on the Health Facility Registry, beside 94.87 crore ABHA IDs. A registry records people who are already in a profession and have been verified onto a digital list. It does not mean the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission employed 10.09 lakh people.

The work created around that registry, which the reply does describe, is integration work: public programmes such as PM-JAY, Nikshay, and state systems in Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Tamil Nadu are named as connected, and private applications must pass a sandbox and a web application security audit. That is a skills demand for implementers, health-IT staff, and compliance reviewers. The reply does not quantify the demand.

## What a workforce conversation can claim

It can claim that India ran a large rural digital-literacy scheme and then closed it, after training 6.39 crore people, and that a 2022-23 official survey found widespread phone ownership and a high share of young people who could send an attachment. It can claim that gram-panchayat service centres are a live, gendered enterprise layer, with 70,069 women operators in the June 2026 cut. It can claim that teachers have a national platform with state-level freedom, that ONDC has a published training effort concentrated so far in two cities for its kirana programme, and that health professionals are being listed on a registry.

It cannot claim a net change in employment from digital public infrastructure. No release cited here estimates jobs displaced at a counter, jobs added in a payments app, or wages in a CSC. The honest labour story is about which public programmes are still open, which have shut, and which numbers are registries rather than payrolls.
