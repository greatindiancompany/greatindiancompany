# What counts as evidence of manufacturing trade diversification

Evidence is a figure whose unit you can name, whose year you can name, and whose document you can open. India's diversification debate mixes four units that do not measure the same thing. This snapshot separates them. It does not declare that diversification has succeeded or failed.

## An outlay is not production

On 2 August 2023, PIB reported a Lok Sabha reply: Production Linked Incentive schemes for 14 sectors, outlay Rs 1.97 lakh crore (described there as over USD 26 billion). The purpose stated was investment, scale, and export competitiveness. That number is evidence of a policy commitment. It becomes evidence of manufacturing only when a later note reports production, investment claimed, or incentive released. This snapshot does not have that later production figure, so it will not imply one.

The same reply's count of 733 selected applications, 176 of them MSMEs, is evidence of selection at that date, in named sectors. Selection evidence answers "who was allowed into the scheme." It does not answer "what left the factory."

## A cost study is not a monthly index

On 27 November 2025, PIB reported the DPIIT-NCAER assessment of logistics cost for 2023-24. The figures are 7.97 percent of GDP, 9.09 percent of non-services output, and Rs 24.01 lakh crore. The method, as the note describes it, mixes responses from over 3,500 industry stakeholders with data from the statistics ministry, the Reserve Bank, and the GST network. That is evidence of a level for one year, under a stated method. The note also says commonly cited 13 to 14 percent figures were overestimates. Comparing 7.97 with 13 is not evidence that costs fell by five percentage points. It is evidence that the older talking point and the new study are different objects.

Smaller-firm costs, and the role of the first and last 50 kilometres on a haul of about 600 kilometres, are findings inside that study. They are better evidence for a plant decision than the GDP ratio alone. Corridor facts in the same note, such as Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor wagon turnaround moving from 15-16 days to 2-3 days, are evidence about that corridor, not about every highway.

## A trade print is not preference use

On 13 May 2026 the Commerce and Industry Minister said exports were USD 863 billion in the year ending 31 March 2026, with services up about 8.5 to 9 percent and overall exports up nearly 5 percent. That is evidence of export scale for that year, as stated by the minister. It is not evidence that the growth came from diversified supply chains. The aspirational USD 1 trillion target is a goal, not a result.

FTA evidence has to be narrower. Commerce records the India-UAE CEPA in force from 1 May 2022, duty elimination on 97.4 percent of UAE tariff lines corresponding to 99 percent of imports from India, and bilateral trade of USD 72.9 billion in 2021-22 rising to USD 84.5 billion in 2022-23. Bilateral trade evidence is not the same as evidence that shipments used the preference. Certificate-of-origin counts would be that second kind of evidence. Where this snapshot does not have them, it does not invent them. The minister's 13 May 2026 remark that four of nine recent agreements were operational is a status claim. Name the four from the Commerce list before you treat a lane as covered.

## A rule is not a flow

DPIIT's FDI circular is evidence of permission: manufacturing on the automatic route, land-border beneficial owners on the government route. It is not evidence of how much manufacturing FDI arrived. Use DPIIT's data releases for the flow, and say which release.

The honest snapshot, as of 24 September 2026, is therefore a stack: a 2023 incentive design, a 2023-24 logistics level published in 2025, a 2025 mineral mission, and a May 2026 ministerial export and FTA status. Great Indian Company would rather show that stack than a single score. The home page is [greatindiancompany.com](https://greatindiancompany.com).
