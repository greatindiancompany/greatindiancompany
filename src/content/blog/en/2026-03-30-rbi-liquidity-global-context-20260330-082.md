India’s liquidity corridor is a domestic instrument. The world enters it through the Reserve Bank’s own description of global policy, through the exchange rate, and through foreign-exchange operations, which the Bank lists as a durable liquidity tool. The resolution of 3–5 August 2026 and the Weekly Statistical Supplement are the pages that carry those links. They do not set the standing deposit facility by copying another central bank’s rate.

## What the Committee said about the world

The August resolution says the global outlook in 2026 has been marked by sharp market swings, persisting inflation concerns and shifting policy expectations. It says persistent inflation has prompted several central banks to raise rates while others remain vigilant. It says the US dollar appreciated, supported by elevated yields, a hawkish Federal Reserve tone, and a US economy it describes as relatively buoyant. It names conflict in West Asia, volatile oil prices, sticky inflation expectations and fragile public finances as downside risks. The Governor’s statement of 5 August, published in the Bulletin on 25 August, adds that global growth is projected to soften while the inflation forecast is higher for 2026 than for the previous year, and that crude prices, currencies and financial markets have moved with the conflict.

The domestic decision beside that paragraph is a hold. The repo rate stays at 5.25 per cent, the standing deposit facility at 5.00 per cent, the marginal standing facility and the Bank Rate at 5.50 per cent, and the stance stays neutral. The Committee’s reason, in the same resolution, is that headline inflation is rising mainly on food and fuel, that the rise is not yet broad-based, and that growth in 2026–27 is projected at 6.7 per cent with risks evenly balanced. CPI inflation for the year is projected at 5.0 per cent. The June statement had said major advanced-economy central banks were likely to pivot toward tightening. The August hold is the Indian answer printed next to that external description.

The resolution does not publish a Federal Reserve funds rate, and this note does not supply one. The transmission channel the Bank does quantify at home is its own. Since the June meeting the weighted average call rate averaged 5.31 per cent. System liquidity averaged a daily surplus of ₹1.0 lakh crore.

## The rupee and the forward points on the same page as the corridor

The supplement dated 18 September 2026 prints the exchange rate in the same table as the policy rates. For the week ended 11 September 2026 the rupee is ₹95.72 per US dollar, against ₹88.28 in the week ended 12 September 2025. The euro rate is ₹111.15, against ₹103.60. One-month forward premia of the dollar are 3.05 per cent, against 1.85 per cent a year earlier. Three-month premia are 3.41 per cent, against 1.97 per cent. Six-month premia are 3.49 per cent, against 2.13 per cent. The footnote attributes the spot reference and the forward premia to Financial Benchmarks India.

Over that year the corridor moved down by 25 basis points and the one-month forward premium moved up by 1.20 percentage points. Both numbers are in the table. The Governor’s August statement states the exchange-rate policy in one place: the rate is set by market forces, while the Bank curbs excessive volatility, checks speculative behaviour and prevents disorderly movements so that the rate stays consistent with fundamentals and with economic activity. Intervention under that policy, when it happens, changes rupee liquidity. The 30 September 2025 liquidity framework lists foreign-exchange swap auctions with open market operations and long-term variable rate operations as the durable liquidity instruments.

The June 2026 Governor’s statement records one such operation: a long-term USD/INR buy/sell swap of USD 5 billion in May 2026, footnoted under the durable measures taken since the April meeting. The statement gives the dollar amount. It does not give a rupee equivalent in that footnote.

## Reserves rose in foreign-currency assets, and the weekly gold line fell

The Weekly Statistical Supplement dated 11 September 2026 reports foreign exchange reserves as on 4 September. Total reserves are ₹74,24,473 crore, or US$ 785,706 million. The variation over the previous week is an increase of US$ 44,903 million. The variation since end-March 2026 is an increase of US$ 94,599 million. The variation over the year is an increase of US$ 87,438 million.

The composition matters for anyone who wants to treat the reserve increase as a rupee-liquidity injection. Foreign currency assets are US$ 648,168 million, up US$ 47,498 million on the week and up US$ 95,886 million since end-March. Gold is US$ 113,816 million, down US$ 2,594 million on the week and down US$ 1,580 million since end-March, while still up US$ 23,517 million over the year. The week’s increase in total reserves is more than accounted for by foreign currency assets, with gold moving the other way. The supplement does not split that foreign-currency increase into purchases, sales, interest and valuation. A buy/sell swap and an outright purchase do not leave the same rupee mark, and the table does not identify which one produced the week.

The August statement gives an earlier reserve level for a different date: US$ 692.9 billion as on 31 July 2026, with import cover of over 10 months and external debt cover of 90.8 per cent. The 4 September statistical level is the later weekly print, and the comparison the supplement itself publishes is its own week, end-March and year columns. On the capital-flow side, the same statement says that during June and July foreign portfolio investment turned to a net inflow of US$ 7.1 billion, mainly in debt, after outflows in April and May. For April–July as a whole it records a net portfolio outflow of US$ 4.3 billion, with equity outflows of US$ 11.6 billion and debt inflows of US$ 7.3 billion. Portfolio debt and the exchange rate are the external flows. The domestic liquidity consequence runs through whatever operations the Bank chooses to do, and those operations sit in the durable bucket of the September 2025 framework.

The corridor the money market is trading against remains 5.00 / 5.25 / 5.50. In the week ended 11 September the weighted average call rate was 4.97 per cent. The global paragraph in the resolution explains why the Committee is unwilling to treat that surplus as a reason to move the repo rate yet. The reserve and rupee tables explain why the surplus is not sealed off from the rest of the world.
