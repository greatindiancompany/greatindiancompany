The risk in India’s financial inclusion system is not a single percentage of “dormant” accounts. Three official definitions are in use, and they flag different failures.

## An account the survey calls inactive

The Global Findex 2025 defines an inactive account as one with no deposit, no withdrawal, and no incoming or outgoing digital payment in the previous year. In India, 14 percent of adults — 16 percent of account owners — had no active account. Exclude India, and the report’s figure for other low- and middle-income economies is 4 percent of account owners. The spotlight also says the gender gap in active-account ownership, among account owners, fell from 12 percentage points in 2021 to 7 percentage points in 2024.

That is a usage fact from a household survey. It does not, by itself, mean the bank has classified the account as inoperative, and it does not mean the balance has moved to the Depositor Education and Awareness Fund.

## An account the bank must call inoperative

RBI’s [circular DOR.SOG (LEG).REC/64/09.08.024/2023-24 of 1 January 2024](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12589&Mode=0), which took effect on 1 April 2024, defines an inoperative savings or current account as one with no customer-induced transaction for more than two years. Customer-induced transactions include a financial transaction done at the customer’s behest, a non-financial transaction that leaves an audit trail, and KYC updation done in person or through the bank’s own digital channels. The annex lists AePS, UPI, and a direct benefit transfer credit among those financial transactions. Interest the bank credits on its own is a bank-induced transaction, and it does not keep the account operative. The same circular tells banks to segregate zero-balance accounts opened for government-scheme credits and scholarships so that the inoperative label does not attach to them merely because they were not otherwise operated for more than two years. Balances unclaimed for ten years are a further category: they are transferred to the DEA Fund. Two years and ten years are different clocks.

On 12 June 2025 RBI amended those instructions so an authorised business correspondent can help update KYC and reactivate an inoperative account, including through biometric e-KYC. The direction says the bank remains responsible for the updation. A correspondent is a channel. The liability stays with the bank.

## An agent who can move the customer’s cash

AePS is the cash rail that sits on Aadhaar. On 27 June 2025 RBI issued [directions on due diligence of AePS touchpoint operators](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12877&Mode=0), in force from 1 January 2026. The cover note says frauds have been carried out through AePS by identity theft or by compromise of customer credentials. The directions require the acquiring bank to do individual-style customer due diligence on every touchpoint operator before onboarding, unless that due diligence was already done because the person is a business correspondent or sub-agent, and to update that KYC periodically. The bank must monitor operators on an ongoing basis and set parameters for location, type of operator, and the volume and velocity of transactions. Technical hooks such as APIs are to be limited to AePS operations.

NSFI 2025–30 adds a composition risk on the same network. It puts the share of women business correspondents at around 15 percent and asks state-level bankers’ committees to adopt voluntary plans to reach at least 30 percent by December 2028. That target is about who stands at the last mile, which matters for women customers using the [household account](/blog/financial-inclusion-impact-on-households-20260330-037). It is not a fraud statistic.

A risk register that survives contact with a supervisor keeps the three rows apart: survey inactivity, the two-year inoperative rule, and AePS operator due diligence. The [policy page](/blog/financial-inclusion-policy-explainer-20260330-057) explains the rail those operators sit on. Great Indian Company splits them so a reader does not treat a World Bank usage rate as an RBI fraud count.
