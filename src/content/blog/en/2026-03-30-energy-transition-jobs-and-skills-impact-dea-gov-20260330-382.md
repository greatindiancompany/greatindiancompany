# Jobs and Skills in India's Energy Transition

Employment claims in this sector come in two forms, and they should not be added together. One is a headcount from a training programme, with a date. The other is a mission document's estimate of jobs if a 2030 target is met. India has published both. Only the first is a record of people trained or placed.

## The solar technician pipeline is a real programme

MNRE runs the Suryamitra skill programme through the National Institute of Solar Energy. It trains people as solar PV technicians for installation, operation, and maintenance. The course is 600 hours, or about three months, and is residential. Assessment and certification sit with the Skill Council for Green Jobs. Eligibility on the Ministry's programme page is at least 10th standard plus an ITI trade such as electrician, wireman, electronics mechanic, fitter, or sheet metal. The National Institute's own note also describes the course as aimed at ITI and diploma holders, with a bar on candidates who already hold a degree, and with an explicit hope that some trainees become solar entrepreneurs rather than only employees.

The counted results are older than the capacity boom, and that age should stay visible. PIB, citing the Ministry, said that up to June 2022 a total of 51,331 candidates had been trained and 26,967 had gained employment. MNRE's programme page gives a cumulative 51,529 Suryamitras trained till December 2022. An impact assessment by the Skill Council for Green Jobs, dated December 2020, reported that more than 90 percent of trainees said their technical know-how and performance had improved, and 88 percent reported an increase in job opportunities. Those are survey responses from trainees, not a census of the solar workforce in 2026.

The programme is still the Ministry's designated route into rooftop installation. NISE has invited trained Suryamitras to on-the-job training under PM Surya Ghar. That link matters more than a fresh national jobs total, because the household scheme is where installation work is visibly growing. The PIB factsheet of 13 August 2026 says 51.58 lakh households were benefiting from rooftop solar under the scheme and 14.8 GW had been commissioned under it. Each of those systems needs a vendor, an electrician, a net-meter visit by the distribution company, and someone who can service the inverter later. The Ministry has not published a current national count of those workers on the pages used here. Inventing one from the household count would be a guess.

Budget 2026-27 provides ₹40 crore for MNRE's human-resources-development and training line, the same provision as the 2025-26 budget and revised estimates. The National Institute of Solar Energy's grant is ₹33 crore, the National Institute of Wind Energy's is ₹26 crore, and the National Institute of Bio Energy's is ₹18 crore. These are institutional budgets. They are not wage bills for the industry.

## Bioenergy has a newer training name

MNRE's FY 2025-26 review says the Ministry inaugurated the Jaiv-Urja Mitra programme under its human-resources framework, to train biomass aggregators, feedstock-depot operators, and compressed-biogas technicians. The GOBARdhan scheme approved on 6 August 2026, with an outlay of ₹23,731 crore through FY 2035-36, is the demand side of that skill: 1,929 CBG or bio-CNG plants were registered as of 13 August 2026, 217 commissioned and 357 under construction. A technician programme attached to a plant pipeline is a concrete skill bet. The Ministry has not, in the sources used here, published a count of Jaiv-Urja Mitra trainees.

## Mission job numbers are scenario arithmetic

The Cabinet note on the National Green Hydrogen Mission says the 2030 targets are likely to bring in over ₹8 lakh crore of investment and create over 6 lakh jobs, alongside at least 5 million tonnes a year of green hydrogen and about 125 GW of associated renewable capacity. "Likely" is the Cabinet's word. Commissioned production cited later by PIB was around 8,000 tonnes a year as of February 2026. Until output, exports, and factory payrolls are counted, the 6 lakh figure remains the Mission's estimate of a completed build-out.

The solar PLI approval spoke of employment as one purpose of a domestic module industry. It did not publish a placement census. The advanced-chemistry-cell programme is likewise an investment and capacity scheme, ₹18,100 crore for 50 GWh, not a jobs survey.

A different official jobs estimate sits in coal, which is part of the energy system the transition has to staff even as renewable capacity grows. The PIB factsheet of 13 August 2026 says a ₹37,500 crore coal and lignite gasification scheme approved in May 2026 is projected to generate around 50,000 direct and indirect jobs across 25 projects. That, too, is a projection attached to an approval.

## How to talk about this without a fake total

Use Suryamitra's 2022 training and placement counts when you mean people who went through that course. Use the hydrogen Mission's "over 6 lakh" when you mean the Government's estimate for 2030 if the Mission's production target is achieved. Use the ₹40 crore training line when you mean what the Union Budget set aside this year for MNRE's own skill budget. Those three sentences can all be true. They are not one workforce statistic.
