# Questions the July 2026 EV Reply Leaves Open for Policy Teams

The [28 July 2026 reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2290696) is useful because of what it refuses as much as what it counts. It gives a penetration figure, a reimbursement table, a charger sanction, and a bus-letter count. It also says, in answer to a direct question, that no assessment has been done of the scheme's effect on fossil-fuel use, vehicle emissions, or the pace of adoption. Policy teams who need an outcome sentence have to write one the ministry has not written, or they have to wait for a study the reply says does not exist.

## What was measured, and what was declined

The reply's objective statement is operational: faster adoption of electric vehicles, charging infrastructure, and an EV manufacturing ecosystem. The outlay it states is ₹10,900 crore. Thirteen days later, [S.O. 4424(E)](https://pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in/docs/policy_document/Gazette%20Notification%2010%2008%202026%20on%20enhancement%20of%20Outlay%2011900%20cr.pdf) raised that outlay to ₹11,900 crore and moved the two-wheeler terminal date from 31 July 2026 to 31 March 2028. A brief for a legislature or a state department should carry both documents. The July text is the status report. The August text is the rule that followed.

Asked whether adoption, fuel, emissions, and domestic manufacturing had been assessed, the reply says no such assessment has been done. It then offers a penetration statistic in the same paragraph: from 0.7 percent in FY 2019-20 to 8.2 percent in FY 2025-26, supported by PM E-DRIVE, FAME-II, and other policies. On manufacturing, it says OEMs have obtained phased-manufacturing compliance certificates from the ministry's testing agencies. Certificates held are a compliance fact. They are not the assessment the question asked for.

An earlier reply, on [11 March 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2110244), is the Vahan series a team can use if it wants a year path rather than two endpoints. It records electric share from 0.01 percent in FY 2014-15 to 7.31 percent in the FY 2024-25 row of that table. Because the reply is dated 11 March 2025, the FY 2024-25 row does not cover a finished financial year. Calendar 2024 on the same page is a finished year: 19,50,490 electric registrations out of 2,62,07,453, or 7.44 percent. SIAM's separate registration totals, 1.97 million in FY 2024-25, should stay in the SIAM series. The policy error is to average these into one official penetration.

## The data the scheme design does not produce

The July reply says no state-wise allocation was made for demand incentives, so state-wise data is not maintained. A parliamentary question that asks for beneficiaries by state cannot be answered from the reimbursement ledger. The national ledger as on 22 July 2026 was 23,22,878 vehicles and ₹2,281.94 crore, dominated by 20,56,831 two-wheelers. The reply also says the scheme covers Tier-II and Tier-III cities and aspirational regions as part of a pan-India design, without a special incentive. Equal rules and equal published data are not the same thing. The rules are national. The data product is national too.

Where geography exists, the reply is partial. Charging approvals of ₹689 crore for 6,562 chargers went to three oil-marketing companies and nine states, unnamed. Bus concessions are named for Surat (600) and Hyderabad (915), inside quantity letters for 13,800 of 14,000 allocated buses. A policy team that wants a state scorecard has to ask for the nine states and for prototype-approval status, because the published reply stops short of both.

The January 2026 review adds a manufacturing scorecard the July reply does not repeat in full: PLI Auto investment of ₹35,657 crore and determined sales of ₹32,879 crore till 30 September 2025, against estimates of ₹42,500 crore and ₹2,31,500 crore, and a jobs estimate revised to 1.48 lakh from the 2021 launch figure of over 7.5 lakh, with 48,974 jobs reported as generated. Cell investment of ₹2,878 crore and 1 GWh installed against 50 GWh targeted belong in the same manufacturing paragraph. They are the domestic-production record. They are still not an emissions account.

## What a next reply could close

The open questions that follow from these texts, without adding a number the texts do not contain, are concrete. Publish reimbursements after 10 August 2026 against the ₹11,900 crore ceiling. Name the nine states and three companies in the charging sanction, and say how many of the 6,562 chargers are commissioned. Report prototype approval and deployment against the 13,800 bus letters. State whether the 8.2 percent penetration will be followed by the fuel and emissions assessment the July reply said had not been done. Keep Vahan, SIAM, and scheme-reimbursement series in separate columns when that assessment is written.
