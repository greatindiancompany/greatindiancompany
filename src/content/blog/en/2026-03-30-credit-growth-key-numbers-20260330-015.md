# The bank credit numbers behind the headline rate

India’s bank credit cycle is easy to misread because the Reserve Bank publishes several growth rates that do not measure the same stock. These are the prints that matter, with the series named beside each one.

## The latest non-food rate

Non-food bank credit grew **19.1%** year on year as on the fortnight ended **31 July 2026**, compared with **9.9%** on 25 July 2025. This figure comes from the Section-42 return and covers all scheduled commercial banks. It is the cleanest single rate for “how fast is the system lending, excluding food credit.”

The previous full-year point on the same series was **15.9%** as on **31 March 2026**, against **10.9%** on 4 April 2025. So the July rate is not the 2025-26 average. It is a further acceleration in the first four months of 2026-27.

## Four sectors, one sample

Sector rates are not the Section-42 total. They come from 41 scheduled commercial banks that together account for about 95% of non-food credit. As on 31 July 2026:

- Agriculture and allied activities: **17.0%**, against 7.3% a year earlier.
- Industry: **20.0%**, against 6.5%.
- Services: **22.9%**, against 10.2%.
- Personal loans: **16.2%**, against 11.9%.

Services are the fastest. Industry has closed the gap that was still visible in March, when industry grew 15.0% and services 19.0%, while personal loans were already at 16.2%. Personal-loan growth has not been the source of the move from 15.9% to 19.1%.

The July industry note is specific about size. Large and medium industries accelerated. Micro and small industries were steady. The services note points to non-bank finance companies, trade, and commercial real estate. Inside personal loans, housing and vehicle loans stayed in double digits, and credit cards and gold-jewellery loans slowed.

## Two slower rates that are still correct

Anyone comparing July’s 19.1% with a March “bank credit” headline will find lower numbers. They are different collections.

The annual Basic Statistical Return (BSR-1) for March 2026, which includes regional rural banks and is built from account-level credit, shows outstanding bank credit up **14.1%** year on year, from **11.1%** a year earlier. On that return, personal loans grew **12.9%** and were **30.7%** of total credit. Loans to the private corporate sector grew **15.5%** and were more than a quarter of the book. Agriculture was **14.4%** and **12.9%** of credit. Industry was **12.0%** and **22.4%** of credit.

The June 2026 Financial Stability Report, using supervisory returns, says scheduled commercial banks’ credit growth accelerated to **14.5%** during 2025-26, while deposits grew **11.5%**. Public sector banks continued to outpace private sector banks. Personal loans and services remained the main drivers of the year’s growth, even as every major sector was faster in March 2026 than it had been.

Use 19.1% for the latest system-wide non-food pace. Use 14.1% when the question is the surveyed loan book, including who borrowed and at what rate. Use 14.5% when the question is the supervisory year 2025-26, set next to deposits, bad loans, and capital.

## Rates on the book, not just growth

BSR-1 also records the price of that March stock. The share of loans priced below 9% rose to **64.2%** at end-March 2026, from **43.9%** a year earlier. Term loans were **62.8%** of bank credit. Within term loans, **80.2%** carried a rate below 10%. Those shares sit beside the policy-rate cuts of 2025-26. They do not, by themselves, say how much of the latest July growth is cheap credit. The July sectoral release does not publish a rate split.

## A small rule for the next print

From December 2025, year-on-year rates compare month-end data with the old reporting fortnight a year earlier. The footnote is on every sectoral release after the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025. Read the date in the press note before comparing a new 19-point-something with an older fortnight.
