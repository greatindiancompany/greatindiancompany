# Where India's Solar and Storage Projects Stall After the Award

An award letter is the start of execution, not the end. Three official records show where Indian solar and storage work actually stops: a grant milestone nobody crossed, a gap between SECI awards and SECI commissioning, and a park scheme built because land and wires were the bottleneck.

## Financial closure stopped the battery grant

PIB release 2118325, dated 3 April 2025, is unusually precise about a stall. The Cabinet had approved viability-gap funding for battery storage on 6 September 2023. Money was supposed to move in five parts, and the first part, 10 percent, was due at financial closure. For 2024-25 the ministry provided Rs 96 crore against 1,000 MWh on that assumption, later revised to Rs 46 crore when the grant per megawatt-hour was cut. None of the projects achieved financial closure. No expenditure was incurred.

That is the bottleneck, named. It is not land in the abstract and it is not a missing tender. The scheme's own first gate did not open during that financial year. Later slices, 45 percent at commercial operation and 15 percent in each of the next three years, cannot start if closure never happens. CEA monitors the scheme and the Ministry of Power oversees it. A progress claim that skips closure and talks only about the rise from 4,000 MWh to 13,200 MWh is describing the envelope, not the build.

## Awarded solar and commissioned solar are different books

SECI's what-we-do page says that as of 1 May 2025 it had awarded over 73.8 GW, of which 45.9 GW was solar. Its solar page says that as of March 2025 it had commissioned 21.67 GW of solar. The dates are not identical, so the subtraction is not a single official "backlog" statistic. The qualitative fact is still in the two sentences: the award book is much larger than the commissioned book.

The scheme table dated 31 March 2025 shows the pattern inside individual tranches. Early ISTS tranches are largely commissioned. Later tranches, including several with 1,000 MW or 2,000 MW awarded, show commissioned capacity at zero and capacity still under implementation. The manufacturing-linked solar line on that table shows 12,000 MW awarded, 4,300 MW commissioned, and 4,234 MW under implementation. Execution review means reading that table, not averaging it into one delay story.

SECI's own investment book is small beside the auction book, and it has its own unfinished slice. As of 1 May 2025 the page reports 122.7 MW operational under SECI's ownership, 425 MW under construction, and 900 MW under tendering. Facilitation of other developers' projects and construction of SECI's own plants are different bottlenecks.

## Parks were designed around land and transmission

SECI's solar page describes the solar park scheme, notified by MNRE in December 2014 and enhanced in 2017. The design aim is at least 50 parks of 500 MW and above, toward about 40 GW. The page's status line is 53 parks and 39,323 MW of allocated park capacity. The stated benefit is developed land, clearances, transmission, water, roads, and a single zone so developers face fewer approvals. States nominate the park developer and send the proposal to MNRE.

The bottleneck the scheme is built to remove is therefore pre-construction: land and evacuation, not the module. A park megawatt allocated is not a megawatt commissioned. The page gives allocation, not a commissioned-park total in the summary table used here.

## Factories have a bottleneck the incentive itself defines

MNRE's PLI page withholds the module incentive until after commissioning, and then pays it on manufacture and sale for five years. Tranche II's 39,600 MW of manufacturing awards, issued by SECI in April 2023, do not become incentive cash on the award date. If the execution question is "why has the outlay not behaved like a construction grant," the scheme text is the answer.
