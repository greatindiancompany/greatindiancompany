# State-wise Patterns in Mandatory e-Service Saturation

State capacity in India is a pattern across states, not a national average. The cleanest recent official pattern is in DARPG's NeSDA Way Forward report for December 2025, released by PIB on 22 January 2026. The figures are data states and UTs uploaded to the dashboard as of 31 December 2025. Treat them as a self-reported map with a date, and read three columns separately.

## Three columns, three maps

The stock column: 24,090 e-services across states and UTs. Of those, 8,656 are in local governance and utility services, the sector the release singles out. States added 156 e-services in November 2025. A state can look large on this column because it has enumerated many local services, and another can look smaller because it has not.

The mandatory-list column: saturation of 59 mandatory e-services. PIB says 21 states and UTs had achieved over 90 percent saturation, and it prints these names: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Jammu and Kashmir, Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Meghalaya, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Tripura, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal. The release's own count is 21 and the printed list is what a reader should use. States that are absent from that list are not thereby scored at zero. They are absent from the "over 90 percent" sentence.

The unified-portal column is a third map. Portals providing 100 percent of services: Assam (Sewa Setu), Jammu and Kashmir (e-UNNAT), Karnataka (Seva Sindhu), Odisha (Odisha One), Madhya Pradesh (MP e-Service), Uttarakhand (Apuni Sarkar) and Kerala (e-Sevanam). Above 90 percent through an identified unified portal: e-District Chandigarh, e-District Delhi, Saral Haryana and e-Mitra in Rajasthan.

Put the columns together and the pattern appears. Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Jammu and Kashmir show up both at 100 percent portal coverage and inside the high mandatory-saturation names. Assam and Odisha are named at 100 percent portal coverage and are not in the printed high-saturation list. Delhi is named above 90 percent on its unified portal and is not in that mandatory list. A state-wise claim that uses only one column will mis-rank the others. An earlier Lok Sabha reply, published on 10 December 2025, had the October 2025 picture at 23,919 e-services, mandatory saturation of 80 percent against 66 percent in April 2023, ten states and UTs above 90 percent on a unified portal, and six at 100 percent. The January release is the later map. Quote the date with the rank.

## What else varies, officially

The December report spends a chapter on city platforms because, in DARPG's words as carried by PIB, citizen interactions largely occur at the municipal level. It points at municipal portals in Puducherry and Guwahati as practices worth showing. Local bodies are therefore not a footnote to the state portal. They are the largest service block in the count and the level DARPG says citizens actually meet.

Civil-service learning varies as well, on a different file. The 11 March 2026 PIB note on Mission Karmayogi gives iGOT figures as on 6 March 2026 for two states it describes as consistently high on enrolments: Rajasthan, 7,46,135 registered users and 43,96,651 course enrolments; Madhya Pradesh, 9,36,951 registered users and 42,78,599 enrolments. The same note says district-level monitoring of learning is not done on the platform, and that MoUs cover 29 states and UTs. A high state enrolment does not tell you which district has learned anything.

NITI Aayog's Aspirational Districts Programme, launched in January 2018, is the place the Union publishes a district pattern on purpose. It covers 112 of the most under-developed districts. NITI describes the method as convergence of central and state schemes, collaboration among central and state officers and the district collector, and competition through a monthly delta ranking on the Champions of Change portal. NITI anchors the programme. The published design says states are the drivers. That is a state-wise pattern of a different kind: the district is the unit, the state is the manager, and the ranking is a delta from a baseline rather than a level of e-services.

## How to brief a state

Name the column. "High NeSDA" can mean many services, a complete mandatory list, or one portal that actually carries the catalogue. For a project in a city, ask about the municipal portal, not only the state single window. For a workforce or scheme that depends on district staff, the iGOT state total and the Aspirational Districts delta answer different questions, and only one of them is a district number.
