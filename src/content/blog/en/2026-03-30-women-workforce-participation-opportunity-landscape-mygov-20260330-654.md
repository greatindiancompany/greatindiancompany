# Where women’s paid work is already a regular wage

The opportunity is specific. It is not “Indian women” as one pool. In usual status for 2025, urban women workers were 50.9 percent in regular wage or salaried employment. Rural women workers were 9.3 percent in that status and 70.7 percent self-employed. An employer, a training provider, or a care-service operator is looking at one of those structures, and the tools that attach to each are different.

## The urban regular-wage pool

Urban female labour force participation on usual status was 27.7 percent, and the worker population ratio was 25.9 percent. Participation is lower than in rural areas, where it was 45.9 percent. The women who are working in urban areas are much more likely to be on a wage. Casual labour was 8.7 percent of urban women workers, against 20.0 percent in rural areas. Self-employment was 40.4 percent in urban areas, against 70.7 percent in rural areas.

That is the pool in which EPFO enrolment can occur. During 2024–25, 26.9 lakh net female subscribers were added to EPFO. The stock of women employed on usual status was 20.0 crore. The payroll flow is a small fraction of the stock, which is what one would expect if covered establishments are a slice of regular-wage work and regular-wage work is a slice of women’s employment. For a firm that already runs payroll, the relevant recruitment market is this urban wage pool, not the rural self-employed majority and not the e-Shram register. e-Shram excludes EPFO members by design.

Earnings in regular wage work for women were ₹18,353 in the 2025 reading, against ₹6,374 in self-employment. The opportunity for the worker is the gap between those official levels. The opportunity for the firm is a labour supply that, in cities, is already majority regular-wage among women who work. Urban female unemployment on usual status was 6.4 percent, and for urban women aged 15–29 it was 18.9 percent. In the April–June 2026 weekly-status quarter, urban female unemployment was 8.7 percent. There is a search pool, especially among younger urban women, beside the lower all-ages rate.

## The rural structure is a different opportunity

Rural female participation is higher, and the jobs are household and casual. A product or a scheme aimed at own-account workers and helpers is aimed at the 70.7 percent. A product that assumes a salary account, a creche at the plant, and an Internal Committee is aimed at the 9.3 percent regular-wage share. Both can be real businesses. They are not the same go-to-market.

Nominal self-employment earnings for women grew about 8.8 percent between the 2024 and 2025 readings, to ₹6,374, faster in percentage terms than men’s self-employment earnings. The level remains far below men’s ₹17,914 and far below women’s own regular-wage earnings. Growth on that line is an opportunity only if the offer raises the level, not only the percentage.

## Care itself is an operating market the Ministry is funding

Palna formalises child care that the Time Use Survey still records as unpaid. Women participants spent 289 minutes a day on unpaid domestic services and 137 minutes on unpaid caregiving. PIB states that lack of day care deters women from going out to work, and that the Anganwadi-cum-creche is intended to support women’s workforce participation. As of March 2025, 11,395 such creches had been approved, against a 2024–25 target of 17,000. The funding pattern, 60:40 in the general case, is a public market with a dated approval gap. Sakhi Niwas is the related accommodation offer for women entering work, with day care for residents’ children.

Private creches sit next to the statutory duty: establishments with 50 or more employees must provide a creche under the maternity law MoLE is implementing. That duty creates demand from covered employers. It does not create demand from self-employed rural women. The public scheme is the instrument for the second group.

## Apprenticeship is the intake that has moved

Women’s share of apprentices under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme rose from 11.30 percent in 2018–19 to 22.84 percent in 2024–25. More than 10.01 lakh women apprentices have been engaged since 2016–17. In 2025–26, up to 31 October 2025, women were 24.80 percent of apprentices engaged. For an establishment that can host an apprentice, that is a supply line into the regular-wage world, with a published female share. It is still subject to the PLFS finding that 51.4 percent of women aged 15–59 with formal vocational training were in the workforce, against 83.3 percent of men. The opportunity is real at intake. Retention into work is the weaker official cell.

The map, in one pass: urban wage employment, where half of women workers already are; covered payroll, where the female EPFO flow is measured; rural self-employment, where most rural women workers are; and creche and hostel services, where unpaid time and a public approval target define the gap.
