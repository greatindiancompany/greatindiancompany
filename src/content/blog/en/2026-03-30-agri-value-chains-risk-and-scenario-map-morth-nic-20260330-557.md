# What can move in the agri chain, and which paper would show it

A risk map that invents probabilities is a forecast in disguise. The useful map names a movement that the latest official print has already made visible, and names the next document that would confirm or retire it. Four such movements are on the table in September 2026.

## The crop in the ground is uneven against its own normal

The area report of 8 September 2026, as on 4 September, is a sowing outcome, not a loss estimate. Rice at 421.82 lakh hectares is 16.37 lakh hectares below the same week of 2025 and 9.82 lakh hectares above its normal of 412.00. A scenario in which "rice area collapsed" is not what the row says. A scenario in which late sowing in Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and Madhya Pradesh pulls the crop below last year's 154.024 million tonnes is possible, and it is not yet a number. The document that would make it a number is an advance estimate for 2026-27. Until that estimate exists, the 2025-26 Third Advance Estimate stays last year's crop.

Other rows are already below normal, which is a different risk from a year-on-year dip. Pulses are 117.13 lakh hectares against a normal of 123.63. Urad is 25.15 against 29.60, even after a gain of 2.68. Oilseeds are 191.99 against 200.08. Cotton is 109.17 against 125.51. Cotton's gap to normal is the wide one. The May MSP raised medium-staple cotton by Rs 557 a quintal, to Rs 8,267. A higher floor does not restore the missing hectares. The procurement history in that same MSP release is a warning about who the public buyer has actually been: of 8,746 lakh metric tonnes of 14 kharif crops bought during 2014-15 to 2025-26, paddy was 8,418. A weak cotton or oilseed crop meets a thin public-buying record. A weak paddy crop meets a buying machine with a long history. Those are different risks, both grounded in the same Cabinet note.

## Storage is large in one ledger and thin in another

National cold-storage capacity, cited from the Department of Agriculture as on 31 January 2026, was 402.87 lakh metric tonnes. Completed projects under the food-processing ministry's integrated cold chain had created 9.236 lakh metric tonnes as of 30 June 2026. Agricultural Marketing Infrastructure, as of 30 June 2025, had sanctioned 982.94 lakh metric tonnes of storage capacity, which is godown space, with a subsidy of Rs 4,829.37 crore released. A scenario about "no storage" ignores the godown ledger. A scenario about "the cold chain is solved" ignores how small the completed PMKSY tonnes are beside the national cold-storage stock. The AIF book, as of 30 June 2025, had 2,454 cold-store projects and Rs 8,258 crore sanctioned for them, inside a much larger count of custom hiring centres. The risk to name is a mismatch of asset and crop: a warehouse for grain is not a cold room for horticulture, and the ledgers already show which one the public schemes have filled.

## The export basket has a concentration the page itself prints

APEDA's animal-products page for 2025-26 puts animal-product exports at USD 6,236.79 million and buffalo meat at USD 5,098.54 million, "over 81.75 per cent" of that basket, with Vietnam, Egypt, the UAE, Malaysia, and Saudi Arabia named as principal markets. A demand or access change in that meat line would move most of the animal-product total. Dairy, at USD 407.18 million on the same page, would not offset it. This is a composition fact. It is not a prediction that any market will close.

The all-agri total that can be set beside a previous year is still 2024-25: USD 51,913 million, up from USD 48,757 million, with APEDA scheduled products at 55 per cent of the agri total in the annual report's summary table. A 2025-26 all-agri total is not the figure used here. Concentrating the risk story on buffalo meat uses the year APEDA has broken out. Extending it to all agri exports would need the later DGCIS total.

## Scheme risk is a calendar

Three clocks create operational risk for anyone financing a project in late 2026. The food-processing PLI runs to 2026-27. PMKSY's stated outlay period runs up to 2025-26, while 98 cold-chain projects were still ongoing on 30 June 2026. The AIF note of August 2025 said new loan disbursement would end with 2025-26, even though the scheme is operational to 2032-33 and subvention lasts up to seven years. A project that needs a fresh AIF sanction, a fresh PLI slot, and a fresh PMKSY grant is leaning on three windows, two of which the documents describe as late or closed. PM-KISAN, continued to 2030-31, removes one income risk for eligible landholding families and removes none of the project-window risk.

NABSanrakshan and CGTMSE cover AIF loans up to Rs 2 crore. A project larger than that has a guarantee only on the eligible band. That cap is a written limit, and it is the credit risk the guideline chose to leave with the lender.
