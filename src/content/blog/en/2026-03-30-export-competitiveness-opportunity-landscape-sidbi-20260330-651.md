# FTA access and the live opportunity in India’s export trade

An opportunity landscape that lists every negotiation as if it were a tariff cut will send goods into a market that still charges the MFN rate. The useful split is legal status, then the lines that were already moving.

## In force is not the same as signed or concluded

A [1 September 2026 government note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2305422) says the India–UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement and the social security agreement entered into force on 15 July 2026, with zero-duty access for nearly 99 percent of India’s exports to the United Kingdom. That is the preference a shipper can test now, subject to rules of origin.

The textiles release of 22 April 2026, written before that in-force date, is still the cleaner status list for the rest of the set, and it should be read with the dates it actually uses:

- India–EFTA TEPA entered into force on 1 October 2025.
- India–UK CETA was signed in July 2025. The later note is what moves it to “in force.”
- India–Oman CEPA was recorded for December 2025.
- The India–New Zealand FTA was announced on 22 December 2025.
- The India–EU FTA was concluded on 27 January 2026.

“Announced” and “concluded” are not “in force.” The April note expected these agreements to improve preferential access for textiles, apparel, handicrafts, and technical textiles. Expectation is not a landed duty. For the EU in particular, a conclusion in January 2026 still needs an in-force notice before a price is rewritten.

NITI Aayog’s 16 September 2026 Trade Watch said exports to FTA partners rose 36.3 percent and imports 10.0 percent in that edition. Use the rate as the edition’s result. Confirm the partner list and the months in the PDF before you attribute the gain to the UK agreement, which only entered into force on 15 July, after the April–June quarter that edition covers.

## The lines that were growing without waiting for a new FTA

The July 2026 Commerce narrative already showed electronics exports at US$ 5.92 billion, up 57.40 percent, and engineering goods at US$ 12.24 billion, up 17.71 percent. Petroleum products were US$ 6.92 billion, up 67.64 percent. Those are customs outcomes for one month. An FTA can widen them. It did not create the July print.

Textiles are the slower, employment-heavy opportunity. In 2025–26, ready-made garments were ₹1,39,349.6 crore, up 2.9 percent, inside total textile and handicraft exports of ₹3,16,334.9 crore, up 2.1 percent. The UK zero-duty line matters more to a 2.9 percent garment base than to a 57 percent electronics month, because the garment base was not already repricing itself. That is a judgment about where a tariff change is large relative to recent growth, not a forecast of UK orders.

E-commerce is the other written opportunity that does not depend on a partner’s parliament. FTP 2023 raised the courier consignment cap from ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh and pointed to ICEGATE integration so courier and postal exports could claim FTP benefits. The launch also said various estimates put e-commerce export potential at US$ 200–300 billion by 2030. Those are estimates cited at the launch, not a 2026 outcome. The operational change is the cap and the claim path.

## How to pick a lane

Start with an agreement that is in force, a rule of origin you can meet, and a commodity line whose recent customs value you have read. District product lists and status-holder branding do not replace that sequence.
