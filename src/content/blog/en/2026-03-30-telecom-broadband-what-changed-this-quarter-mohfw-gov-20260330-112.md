# What Changed in India's Broadband Count by July 2026

The latest full quarterly book from the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) covers January–March 2026 and was released on 22 June 2026. The latest monthly subscription note, Press Release No. 116/2026, is dated 28 August 2026 and stops at 31 July 2026. A reader who wants "this quarter" in September 2026 should use those two dates, and should wait for the next quarterly indicator before treating April–June as a closed book.

## The count that moved

In the quarterly indicators, broadband subscribers were 1,007.35 million at end-December 2025 and 1,065.88 million at end-March 2026, a rise of 5.81 percent in the quarter. Total internet subscribers, broadband plus narrowband, were 1,092.79 million at end-March, up 6.24 percent from 1,028.61 million at end-December. Narrowband rose faster in percentage terms, from 21.25 million to 26.91 million, and remains a small slice of the total.

By 31 July 2026, TRAI's monthly note put broadband subscribers at 1,094.48 million, up from 1,087.50 million at end-June, a monthly rate of 0.64 percent. The July note is built from reports by 1,562 operators. The path from the March quarterly figure of 1,065.88 million to the July figure of 1,094.48 million is the change a reader can check without inventing an April–June total.

## Where the July growth sat

TRAI splits the July broadband book into three segments:

- Fixed wired access (DSL, fibre, Ethernet, cable modem, and internet leased line) rose from 47.71 million to 48.14 million, a monthly rate of 0.90 percent.
- Fixed wireless access, covering 5G fixed wireless, Wi-Fi, Wi-Max, radio, unlicensed-band radio, and satellite, rose from 18.36 million to 18.84 million, a monthly rate of 2.60 percent.
- Mobile wireless access, including handset, dongle, and machine-to-machine connections on 3G, 4G, and 5G, rose from 1,021.43 million to 1,027.50 million, a monthly rate of 0.59 percent.

The fast edge in July was fixed wireless. The mass of the book is still the mobile line. Inside fixed wireless, 5G fixed wireless access itself moved from 12.94 million at end-June to 13.21 million at end-July, with 6.56 million urban and 6.64 million rural. Unlicensed-band radio fixed wireless stood at 5.14 million, of which 3.65 million were urban and 1.49 million rural. TRAI's March snapshot had grouped 5G fixed wireless and unlicensed-band radio fixed wireless at 16.61 million. The July note's own summary line for fixed wireless access subscribers is 18.35 million.

## What did not flip

The top five broadband providers at end-July — Reliance Jio (535.12 million), Bharti Airtel (384.23 million), Vodafone Idea (129.96 million), BSNL (27.28 million), and Atria Convergence Technologies (2.47 million) — held 98.59 percent of broadband connections. Wireline tele-density was 3.36 percent overall, 8.28 percent in urban areas, and 0.57 percent in rural areas. Rural India is on the mobile and 5G-home network. It is barely on the copper-and-fibre telephone line.

Mobile number portability requests in July were 15.98 million, so the installed base is large and also restless. Active wireless subscribers on the peak visitor-location-register day were 1,204.01 million, about 93.48 percent of the wireless mobile base.

## How to use the change

Treat the March quarter as the last closed financial picture, including adjusted gross revenue of Rs 86,716 crore. Treat July as the last closed subscriber picture. Do not average them into a single "quarterly growth rate." The segment rates above are the operational signal: fibre lines are growing, and 5G home broadband is growing faster, on a base that is still about one-twentieth of the mobile broadband base.
