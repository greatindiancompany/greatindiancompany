# Which official prints are real signals about work in India

A signal about work in India is a numbered release with a denominator, a reference period, and a revision policy. Commentary about "the future of work" is not a signal. Three official series are worth reading. They do not measure the same thing, and the useful habit is to say which one you mean before you quote it.

## Provident-fund payroll: formal, covered, provisional

From April 2018 the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation has released monthly payroll estimates covering the period from September 2017. Net payroll for a month is new members joining through an Aadhaar-validated Universal Account Number, minus members who exit, plus exited members who rejoin.

For June 2025, EPFO's provisional release reported a net addition of 21.89 lakh members. The organisation described that as the highest net addition since the payroll series began. It was 9.14 percent above May 2025 and 13.46 percent above June 2024. New subscribers were about 10.62 lakh, up 12.68 percent from May 2025 and 3.61 percent from June 2024.

That is a strong formal-sector print. It is not a count of jobs created in India. It covers establishments and employees inside the EPF net. A self-employed worker, a casual labourer, and a platform worker who is not an EPF member never enter the numerator. The data are provisional because employee records keep being updated, and earlier months are revised. EPFO also notes that the estimates can include temporary employees whose contributions are not continuous, and that adding the months of a year does not mean every person in the sum was still on the rolls at year-end. The payroll page on epfindia.gov.in is the place to see those later revisions. A June headline that is not checked against the revised file is an incomplete signal.

## The household survey: participation, work, and unemployment

The Periodic Labour Force Survey, run by the National Sample Survey Office in the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, is the household picture. It publishes two concepts, and mixing them is the most common way to invent a trend.

Usual status (principal and subsidiary) looks at the year. A person can be counted as a worker on the basis of the activity that occupied them for a relatively long part of the 365 days, with subsidiary work picked up as well. Current weekly status looks at the seven days before the survey.

For July 2023–June 2024, usual status, persons aged 15 and above, the Ministry's press note reported:

- Labour force participation of 60.1 percent (men 78.8, women 41.7), up from 57.9 percent in July 2022–June 2023. Women's participation in that year rose from 37.0 percent to 41.7 percent. Men's rose from 78.5 percent to 78.8 percent.
- A worker population ratio of 58.2 percent (men 76.3, women 40.3), up from 56.0 percent a year earlier.
- An unemployment rate of 3.2 percent for men and for women. Men's rate edged down from 3.3 percent. Women's rose from 2.9 percent to 3.2 percent.

Over the longer window the same note records, rural participation rose from 50.7 percent in 2017–18 to 63.7 percent in 2023–24, and urban participation from 47.6 percent to 52.0 percent. Usual-status unemployment fell from 5.3 percent to 2.5 percent in rural areas and from 7.7 percent to 5.1 percent in urban areas.

The calendar-year 2024 press note is a different cut. On current weekly status, all-India participation was 56.2 percent in both January–December 2023 and January–December 2024. The weekly-status unemployment rate was 4.9 percent in 2024. Urban weekly-status unemployment was 6.7 percent. Those figures do not contradict the 3.2 percent usual-status rate. They are a harder test: did the person have work that week, not over the year.

A rising usual-status participation rate, driven largely by women, is the multi-year signal. A flat weekly-status participation rate in calendar 2024 is the short-horizon signal. Both can be true.

## The unorganised register: identity, not a hire

e-Shram, launched on 26 August 2021, is a self-declared register of unorganised workers, including gig, platform, and migrant workers. As on 20 August 2026 the Ministry reported 31.89 crore registrations, 54.28 percent of them women. As on 14 July 2026 the count was 31,78,31,749.

Growth in that register is a signal that more people have a Universal Account Number and can be mapped to schemes. The 20 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply listed fifteen central schemes already integrated, and linkages to the National Career Service and the Skill India Digital Hub. It is not a signal of vacancies, wages, or hours. A platform worker and a farm worker can both be on it. The register does not tell you which is which unless you use the occupation fields, and those fields are self-declared.

## What fails as a signal

A projection is not a print. NITI Aayog's 2022 report estimated 77 lakh gig workers in 2020–21 and a rise to 2.35 crore by 2029–30. That is a modelled scenario built from worker characteristics. Quoting it as this year's market size is a category error.

A coverage slogan is not a signal until you know the definition. The Ministry said on 21 November 2025 that social-security coverage had risen from about 19 percent of the workforce in 2015 to more than 64 percent in 2025. Use that sentence as the Ministry's coverage claim. Do not place it in the same chart as the PLFS unemployment rate.

The practical rule: if the sentence does not name EPFO, PLFS, or e-Shram, and does not say whether the status is usual or weekly, it is not yet a market signal. It is a prompt to go and find the release.
