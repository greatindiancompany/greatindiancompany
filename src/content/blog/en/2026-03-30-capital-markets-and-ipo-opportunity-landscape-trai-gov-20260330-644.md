# Issuance routes beside a main-board IPO

"Capital markets opportunity" is too broad to underwrite. SEBI's Annual Report 2025-26, Chapters 1 and 3, shows which routes actually took money in the last full year, and in what size. The figures are BSE and NSE data as compiled by SEBI, for April 2025 to March 2026, unless a line below says otherwise.

## Equity routes, from the public offer outward

Main-board IPOs were 109 issues and ₹1.8 lakh crore, up 8.9 per cent, median size ₹760 crore. That is the route founders mean by "the IPO." It is not the whole equity market.

The SME platform listed 257 companies and raised ₹11,587 crore, up 18.1 per cent, average size ₹45.1 crore. Three issues were between ₹5 crore and ₹10 crore, 197 between ₹10 crore and ₹25 crore, and 57 were ₹25 crore or more. For a company that cannot carry a ₹760 crore median, this is the equity route the year actually used. Offer-for-sale shares on SME deals have historically stayed low, in the 0 to 27 per cent range in SEBI's decade review. The platform is a fresh-capital market in that record.

Rights issues raised ₹46,168 crore across 139 issues, up 134.2 per cent in amount even though the count fell from 142. One issue was about 54 per cent of the money. A rights issue is available only to a company that already has holders to ask.

Preferential allotments raised ₹1,48,219 crore, up 76.3 per cent. This is a chosen-investor sale by a listed company, not a public book. Qualified institutional placements went the other way: 36 issues, ₹67,853 crore, down 50 per cent from ₹1,35,597 crore, with a higher average size of ₹1,885 crore. Chapter 3 says the QIP placement document was shortened because the issuer is already public. The route is procedurally lighter and, in this year, less used.

Follow-on public offers were a single issue of ₹88 crore. They are not, on this record, a live alternative to a QIP or a preferential deal.

Inside IPOs, the opportunity for the company and the opportunity for the selling shareholder are different products. Fresh-only IPOs raised ₹21,866 crore. Offer-for-sale-only IPOs raised ₹50,849 crore. Mixed deals raised ₹1,15,901 crore, split ₹58,166 crore fresh and ₹57,735 crore sale. A growth plan that needs cash should be matched to the fresh column. An exit should be matched to the sale column. ICDR, last amended 21 March 2026, is the eligibility test for both.

## Debt, trusts, and the social exchange

Debt issues raised ₹9,11,078 crore, down 8.4 per cent, across 1,967 issues. Private placement was 98.8 per cent. The Electronic Book Provider threshold for mandatory bidding was cut from ₹50 crore to ₹20 crore, and the platform was opened to securitised debt, REITs, small and medium REITs, and InvITs. Public debt issues were the growing minority: ₹11,343 crore, up 39.2 per cent, still 43 issues, 40 of them financial-sector. Issuers may offer extra interest or a price concession to senior citizens, women, and defence personnel on the initial allotment. The concession dies on transfer. That is a retail-debt design, not an equity IPO design.

Municipal bonds raised ₹1,756 crore in 14 issues, after one issue of ₹100 crore the year before. The base is small. The year-on-year change is the largest percentage move in this landscape, and it is a city balance-sheet product.

REITs raised ₹9,300 crore in three issues. InvITs mobilised ₹21,026 crore. Year-end net assets were ₹2.4 lakh crore for REITs and ₹6.4 lakh crore for InvITs. These are the routes for a completed rent or concession asset. SEBI's outreach went to state governments on using them for public assets, and to insurers and pension funds as buyers.

The social stock exchange listed five non-profits, which raised ₹21.9 crore, against ₹14.8 crore from six issues the year before. The minimum application for a social impact fund was cut from ₹2 lakh to ₹1,000, in line with the minimum for zero-coupon zero-principal instruments. The amounts show a narrow market. The rule change shows who SEBI is trying to let in.

ESG debt securities, as Chapter 3 defines them, add social, sustainability, and sustainability-linked bonds beside green debt. Proceeds and targets have to match the label, an independent reviewer checks that match, and a failure the debenture holders escalate can lead to early redemption. It is a use-of-proceeds market with a clawback, not a general corporate-purpose market.

## How to pick

Match the instrument to the balance sheet. Unlisted operating company with a small raise: SME platform, if ICDR's SME conditions are met. Unlisted company with a large raise or an exit: main board, and be explicit about fresh versus sale. Listed company that wants a known buyer: preferential. Listed company that wants institutions and is willing to publish a shorter placement document: QIP, which the latest year shows is open and not automatically full. Listed company that wants its own holders: rights. Operating city: municipal bond. Completed asset: REIT or InvIT. Specified social project: social stock exchange, at a scale of a few crore, not a few thousand crore.

The total primary mobilisation SEBI reports for 2025-26 is ₹13.6 lakh crore, 4.4 per cent below the year before. The opportunity is the mix inside that total, not a claim that every window is wider.
