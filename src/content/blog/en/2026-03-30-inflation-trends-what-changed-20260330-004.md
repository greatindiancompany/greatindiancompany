# Retail inflation moved up to 4.82% in August 2026

The number that changed in mid-September is the all-India retail print. On 14 September 2026 the National Statistics Office, in the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, reported that year-on-year inflation on the Consumer Price Index (base 2024=100) was **4.82% in August 2026 (provisional)**. July, marked final in the same release, was **4.45%**.

Rural inflation was 5.23% and urban inflation was 4.31%. The combined index moved from 107.95 in July to 108.74 in August. The [August CPI press release](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1789381904344_6c792dcf-8a9f-4fca-93d3-99d833bdb358_Press_Release_of_CPI_for_August_2026.pdf) is the document behind those figures.

## The climb since January

On this base, combined inflation has risen in every month of 2026 that the release prints:

| Month | Combined CPI inflation |
| --- | --- |
| January 2026 | 2.74% |
| February | 3.21% |
| March | 3.40% |
| April | 3.48% |
| May | 3.93% |
| June | 4.38% |
| July (final) | 4.45% |
| August (provisional) | 4.82% |

June is the month the series moved above 4%. The August step is smaller than the jumps in February, May, and June, and it is still a higher print. August remains provisional. July in this release is final.

## Food accounted for a large part of the latest step

The Consumer Food Price Index rose from 5.52% in July to **5.95% in August**. Rural food inflation was 6.13% and urban food inflation was 5.64%. The broader “food and beverages” division was 5.66%.

A few items moved hard in opposite directions, which is why a single food rate hides the shopping basket. Onion inflation was 48.27% in August, after 22.54% in July. Garlic was 43.60%. Tomato inflation was −31.09%, and potato inflation was −13.14%. Silver jewellery was 107.11% and gold, diamond, and platinum jewellery was 35.53%. Those jewellery rates sit in a division, “personal care, social protection and miscellaneous goods and services,” that itself printed 15.17%.

Health was 1.34%. Housing, water, electricity, gas, and other fuels was 2.61%. The retail basket did not accelerate evenly.

## The policy meeting did not yet have this print

The Monetary Policy Committee met on 3–5 August 2026, before this release. Its [resolution](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63287) said CPI inflation had increased to 4.4% in June, after remaining below the target for 16 consecutive months, and that the Q1 outturn was 30 basis points under the earlier projection. MoSPI’s combined June figure on the new base is 4.38%, which is the print the resolution rounds to 4.4%.

In that same resolution the committee projected CPI inflation for 2026–27 at 5.0%, with July–September (Q2) at 4.7% and October–December (Q3) at 5.9%. July at 4.45% and August at 4.82% are now known. September is not. The September CPI is scheduled for 12 October 2026, after the next MPC meeting on 5–7 October.

## Wholesale prices were already much higher

The same morning, the provisional Wholesale Price Index (base 2022–23) was reported at **9.92% for August**, compared with 9.78% in July. Fuel and power was 22.93%. The series is compiled by the Office of the Economic Adviser, an attached office of the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade ([about page](https://eaindustry.nic.in/about_us.asp)). The [14 September WPI press note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2309977) is a separate index from CPI. The wholesale print matters here because the retail step-up is happening while factory-gate and wholesale-market prices are already in a different range. That gap is the subject of the companion note on [wholesale prices and smaller firms](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/inflation-trends-impact-on-smes-20260330-024).

## What a reader can treat as settled

August combined CPI is 4.82%, provisional, on base 2024=100. July is 4.45%, final. Food is 5.95%. The 2026 path on this base is a steady rise from 2.74% in January. The August policy statement was written against June, and it still guides the published projection until the committee meets again.
