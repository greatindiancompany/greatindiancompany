# What official cyber records show about demand

Public documents describe work that institutions must do. They do not publish a cybersecurity market size, a share figure, or a price. The signals below are the ones those documents actually contain.

## The 2023 incident mix is a hygiene signal

CERT-In's Annual Report 2023 gives this breakup of 15,92,917 incidents handled:

| Type in the published table | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Vulnerable services | 9,41,592 |
| Unauthorized network scanning or probing | 4,47,720 |
| Virus or malicious code | 1,84,131 |
| Website defacements | 10,665 |
| Others | 6,895 |
| Website intrusion and malware propagation | 1,045 |
| Phishing | 869 |

Vulnerable services and scanning together are the bulk of that table. The report's narrative also names ransomware, distributed denial of service and data breach among types handled that year. Those three do not have their own rows in the count table, so this brief does not assign them a number.

For anyone selling or buying defensive work, the published weight sits on exposure and malicious code, alongside a much smaller counted set of phishing and website intrusion. That is a different picture from a market story built only on ransomware headlines.

The same report says Cyber Swachhta Kendra was covering about 94 percent of the subscriber base for botnet and malware notifications, and that three free bot-removal tools had a cumulative 48.88 lakh downloads through December 2023. That is a public cleaning service coordinated with ISPs and antivirus companies, not a priced product market.

## Audit capacity is a named official panel

The 2023 report says CERT-In had empanelled 176 information-security auditing organisations. The Lok Sabha reply published on 24 March 2026 says 237 security auditing organisations had been empanelled to support and audit information-security practices. The two figures are from different dates. They show the panel grew. They do not show auditor revenue.

The September 2025 MSME control document tells smaller firms they may commission a baseline audit against those controls through an empanelled organisation at least once a year, and it tells auditors to say clearly that the audit was against a minimum set.

## Shared monitoring is now a regulatory product

SEBI's August 2024 framework says compliance with a full in-house security operations centre can be onerous for smaller regulated entities because of expertise and cost. It therefore mandates BSE and NSE to set up a Market SOC, and it mandates small-size and self-certification regulated entities to be onboarded on it. Other entities may use their own SOC, a group SOC, or a third-party SOC. Functional-efficacy review is half-yearly for market infrastructure institutions and qualified entities, and yearly for the rest.

That is a demand signal written into a circular: shared monitoring for the smaller end of the securities market, and a recurring efficacy report rather than a one-time install.

## Budget lines move, and they are not a revenue forecast

In MeitY Demand No. 27 for 2026-27, Cyber Security Projects are shown at ₹210.99 crore actual in 2024-25, ₹782 crore budget estimate and ₹600 crore revised estimate in 2025-26, and ₹790 crore budget estimate in 2026-27. The CERT-In establishment line moves in a narrower band: ₹254.88 crore actual, ₹255 crore budget estimate, ₹277 crore revised estimate, and ₹269 crore budget estimate across those same columns.

A scheme line that was revised down within the year, then budgeted slightly above the previous estimate, is a funding signal. It is not evidence of how much private buyers will spend.

CERT-In's reply to Parliament still says financial loss from incidents is not maintained. Anyone using incident totals as a proxy for buyer urgency is going past the record.
