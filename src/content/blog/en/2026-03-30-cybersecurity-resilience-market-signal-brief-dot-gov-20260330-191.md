A market signal for cybersecurity resilience in India is a control you can date, name, and count without inventing a price. Sentiment, a consulting index, and a ministry homepage are not signals. This page lists series worth logging. It prints none of their values.

## Series that can move a decision

Log a point only when the underlying record is in your hands or on the public page that issued it.

- **Security certification of a product.** Whether a named piece of telecom equipment has cleared the security test required before it is connected. The National Centre for Communication Security is the Department of Telecommunications office for that test. The public shelf is [dot.gov.in](https://www.dot.gov.in/). The certificate, not the homepage, is the point.
- **Trusted source and trusted product.** Whether the vendor and the product you intend to connect are accepted under the National Security Directive on the Telecommunication Sector. A determination is a signal. A vendor brochure is not.
- **Open audit findings.** The count of security-audit findings on a telecom network that still have no closure date. The audit the licence requires is the series. A press note about an audit is not.
- **The incident clock.** For incidents your own direction says you must report, the hours from detection to the report you filed. CERT-In's clock and a telecom incident process are separate series. Do not add them into one number.
- **Late reports.** How many of those reports missed the clock in the direction you opened. The direction sets the clock. This page does not restate it.

McKinsey's India insights index was paired with an earlier draft. It is not one of these series, so it is omitted.

## What to store with each point

Date, entity or product, control, unit, and who wrote it down. "India" is not an entity. A direction word with no unit is not a point. When a point is allowed to count as a quarterly change, use the [quarter test](/blog/cybersecurity-resilience-what-changed-this-quarter-dot-gov-20260330-111).
