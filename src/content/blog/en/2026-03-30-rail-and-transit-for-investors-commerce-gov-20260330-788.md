# What an Investor Can Underwrite in Indian Rail and Metro

Underwriting here means naming a figure a ministry has published and a structure the policy already uses. It does not mean a return.

## Pool one: railway budgetary support, with an unfinished book

Gross budgetary support for rail capital in Budget Estimates 2026-27 is ₹2,78,030 crore, of which ₹79,072 crore is allocated to new tracks, according to the 1 April 2026 reply. Estimated safety-related expenditure for 2026-27 is ₹1,20,389 crore, including ₹46,189 crore for track. Those lines are disclosed separately.

The asset still being built, as on 1 April 2026, is 514 sanctioned projects, 40,000 km, about ₹8.31 lakh crore. Spend to March 2026 was ₹3,05,253 crore. Commissioned length was 12,583 km. An investor in contractors, steel, cement, or signalling is looking at a book that is sanctioned and mostly unopened, especially in doubling (6,177 km commissioned of 19,379 km) and new lines (3,361 km of 16,634 km). Cement loading of 157.17 million tonnes in 2025-26, up 4.74 percent, is the railway’s own print of construction-related freight. It is not an order book.

The dedicated freight corridors that exist can be treated as operating: Eastern, Ludhiana–Sonnagar, 1,337 km; Western, Dadri–JNPT, 1,506 km; about 443 trains a day. Dankuni–Surat cannot. The 7 August 2026 reply says the detailed project report has been taken up. There is no sanctioned cost for that corridor in the reply.

## Pool two: private terminals and wagons

Commissioned Gati Shakti terminals had drawn about ₹10,000 crore of private investment by 7 August 2026. They handled 146 million tonnes in 2025-26 against estimated capacity of 224 million tonnes a year. The underwritable facts are the capital already mobilised, the tonnes handled, and the policy terms (connectivity-land fee waiver, 24-month build, freight rebate above one million tonnes outward). The 310 in-principle approvals reported on 23 July 2026 are a pipeline, not deployed capital. Wagon schemes had about 275 special-purpose rakes, 397 general-purpose rakes, and 54 automobile rakes in operation. That is industrial rolling stock on a public network.

## Pool three: metro, through a special-purpose vehicle

Since 2014, 38 metro projects covering 1,051 km have been sanctioned at about ₹3.44 lakh crore, the 15 March 2026 PIB note says. The annual metro budget in that note is ₹29,550 crore for 2025-26. A 9 August 2025 note said ₹34,807 crore for the same year. Cite March 2026, and do not average. The Metro Rail Policy puts central and state equity into a special-purpose vehicle and, as the August 2025 note describes the policy, sets a 14 percent economic internal rate of return as a test for central assistance. That 14 percent is an eligibility hurdle. It is not a cash yield an investor has earned.

The same March note, summarising a January 2026 study from the Economic Advisory Council to the Prime Minister, reports lower missed home-loan payments in metro-served parts of Hyderabad, Bengaluru, and Delhi. Those are study findings relayed by PIB. They are a reason to read the study, not a credit enhancement.

Passenger railway yield is the figure to keep out of a metro model. The subsidy on passenger tickets was ₹60,239 crore in 2024-25. Vande Bharat Sleeper fares of ₹2.40 to ₹3.80 per passenger-kilometre sit on top of that system. Two sleeper services do not make a fare box.

The three pools are set out with their dates in the [investment brief](/blog/rail-and-transit-investment-implications-commerce-gov-20260330-228). The gaps that should sit in a risk section are in the [scenario map](/blog/rail-and-transit-risk-and-scenario-map-commerce-gov-20260330-548).

Great Indian Company leaves the investor with three pools that have numbers, and one corridor that is still a project report.
