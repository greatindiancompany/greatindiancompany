# The Housing Rulebook Still in Force for Promoters and States

The regulatory outlook for Indian real estate in September 2026 is mostly a rulebook already in force, plus one consolidated securities text and one model law that states may or may not have adopted. Predicting a new Bill that has not been introduced would be a different kind of document. This one stays with what a promoter, a lender, and a state housing department can be held to now.

MoHUA's July 2025 [FAQ](https://www.mohua.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/542144159b61c980253a694f478d3a5d.pdf) opens with a disclaimer: it is general guidance, not the ministry's final view of a specific case. The statute is the [Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2158?locale=en).

## Sale and construction of projects

Registration before marketing has been the rule since 1 May 2017. The authority decides within 30 days, and silence is deemed registration. The separate account holds seventy percent of allottee money for land and construction. It is not an escrow that waits for a regulator's signature on every withdrawal. The annual audit, due within six months of the financial year, has to test that project money stayed with the project.

Several duties are easy to under-read because they sit in provisos.

- An advance above 10 percent of the price requires an executed agreement for sale. The form of that agreement is fixed by state rules, and it cannot contradict the Act.
- Major changes to sanctioned plans need the competent authority and the written consent of two-thirds of allottees, excluding apartments held by the promoter. Each allottee has one vote, however many apartments they hold.
- Transfer of a majority of the promoter's rights needs the same two-thirds consent and the authority.
- Structural and specified defects are the promoter's liability for five years from handover. The FAQ says the promoter is to rectify them within 30 days.
- Force majeure, for extending registration, means war, flood, drought, fire, cyclone, earthquake, or another natural calamity affecting the project. A commercial slowdown is not on that list. A further extension for circumstances without the promoter's fault is capped at one year.
- If registration is revoked, the association of allottees has the first right of refusal to carry out the remaining work.
- Title and construction insurance under Section 16 takes effect only in the manner the appropriate government notifies. The FAQ does not say every state has issued that notification.
- Open parking is common area and cannot be sold. A garage, enclosed on three sides with a roof, can be sold apart from the apartment.
- Occupancy is about habitability and civic services. Completion is about the project matching the sanctioned plan. A state that issues one certificate under another name can still meet the definitions, because both use "by whatever name called."

Penalties in Chapter VIII are tied to the estimated project cost, which includes land, taxes, cess, and development charges. The rate of interest for promoter default and allottee default is the same rate, set in the rules.

## Assistance, tenancy, and listed property

PMAY-U 2.0 runs for five years, 2024 to 2029, under guidelines on the mission site. The live questions for a state are the memorandum of agreement, the city-wise demand plan, the state affordable-housing policy the ministry asks states to write, and the bar on re-sanctioning beneficiaries whose earlier PMAY-U houses were curtailed by the central committee after 31 December 2023. Households that took a central, state, or local housing benefit in the last 20 years are ineligible. A house assisted under the mission is not to be sold or rented for five years from completion.

Tenancy is not federal law by virtue of the model. Cabinet approval was on 2 June 2021. Circulation was on 7 June 2021. The July 2022 Rajya Sabha reply named four states. Anything after that date has to be checked in the state gazette.

For completed, income-producing property that is held for investors rather than sold as apartments, the securities rulebook is the SEBI REIT Regulations, 2014, in the text [last amended on 18 April 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/apr-2026/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-real-estate-investment-trusts-regulations-2014-last-amended-on-april-18-2026-_101013.html). That amendment date is the consolidation a reader can cite. This outlook does not describe the clauses the amendment changed, because the page title is not a changelist.

How the sale law and the mission were designed to differ is in the [policy-shift explainer](https://greatindiancompany.com/blog/real-estate-and-construction-policy-shift-explainer-mca-gov-20260330-161). Great Indian Company publishes the outlook as a map of duties, not as a forecast of the next session of Parliament.
