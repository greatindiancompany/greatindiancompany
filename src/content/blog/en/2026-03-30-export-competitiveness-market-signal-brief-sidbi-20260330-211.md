# Market signals in India’s merchandise trade: commodities and destinations

A single export-growth rate hides the mix. The useful signal in mid-2026 is which commodities moved in the July narrative, and which destinations moved in the August quick estimate. Those are different tables.

## July commodities, from the 13 August release

The [Commerce release of 13 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298878) named the main merchandise export drivers for July 2026 and gave levels:

- Petroleum products, up 67.64 percent, from US$ 4.13 billion in July 2025 to US$ 6.92 billion.
- Electronic goods, up 57.40 percent, from US$ 3.76 billion to US$ 5.92 billion.
- Engineering goods, up 17.71 percent, from US$ 10.40 billion to US$ 12.24 billion.
- Organic and inorganic chemicals, up 14.39 percent, from US$ 2.45 billion to US$ 2.80 billion.
- Cotton yarn, fabrics, made-ups, and handloom products, up 8.40 percent, from US$ 1.02 billion to US$ 1.11 billion.

Engineering goods were still the largest of these five. Petroleum and electronics supplied the sharpest growth rates. That is a price-and-volume mix, especially for petroleum products, and it should not be read as proof that every manufacturing line grew at the headline rate.

The same release separated the non-petroleum basket. Non-petroleum exports in July were US$ 37.32 billion, against US$ 32.86 billion a year earlier. Non-petroleum and non-gems and jewellery exports were US$ 35.00 billion, against US$ 30.47 billion. For April–July, non-petroleum exports were US$ 143.61 billion, up 12.79 percent from US$ 127.32 billion. The headline merchandise rise of 17.04 percent in April–July, to US$ 173.78 billion, is real, and it is faster than the non-petroleum line. Quoting only the larger number overstates how broad the month was.

Drugs and pharmaceuticals were on the positive list for July, but only at 0.74 percent. A sector can be strategically important and still not be the month’s growth engine.

## August destinations, from the DGCIS annex

The 15 September 2026 country table, in US$ million, is a destination signal for August, not a commodity signal. The [annex](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/sep/doc_9879_20260915_19571501.pdf) prints India’s merchandise exports at 43,811 for August 2026.

Among markets with a large change in that month:

- United States: 8,320 against 6,829 in August 2025, up 21.8 percent. For April–August the table prints a 19.8 percent share and a level of 42,790, up 6.2 percent from a year earlier. The month was stronger than the five-month pace.
- United Arab Emirates: 2,378 against 3,217, down 26.1 percent. April–August was 13,588, down 10.6 percent, a 6.3 percent share.
- Saudi Arabia: 625 against 757, down 17.5 percent.
- China: 1,840 against 1,207, up 52.3 percent. April–August was 9,614, up 38.7 percent.
- Singapore: 1,878 against 720, up 161.0 percent. April–August was 9,498, up 96.6 percent.
- United Kingdom: 1,260 against 1,142, up 10.4 percent.

The July narrative had already listed the United States, China, Singapore, Kenya, and Malaysia among destinations with a positive change in value that month. August did not repeat every one of those stories at the same rate. The UAE and Saudi readings sit next to the March 2026 RoDTEP note on West Asia shipping routes, but the annex itself does not assign a cause. Treat the drop as a destination fact. Test the freight explanation in the routing data, not in a headline.

## How to use the signal

A founder in electronics can point to the July level and the growth rate. A founder shipping to the Gulf cannot borrow that sentence.
