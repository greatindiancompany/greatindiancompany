# Three Capex Clocks in Urban India

## Stacking the schemes on one chart misstates the cycle

Capital expenditure in urban India is easy to overstate because MoHUA publishes large rupee figures for missions that spend in different ways. A household subsidy, a water-treatment plant, and a completed smart road do not peak in the same year. The published clocks are these.

## Clock one: Smart Cities, closed at the Centre

The mission was launched on 25 June 2015 for 100 cities, on an area-based model of retrofitting, redevelopment, and greenfield work in selected parts of each city, not as a whole-city rebuild. MoHUA’s page puts the central outlay at ₹48,000 crore. Financial closure was 31 March 2025. Cities had claimed ₹47,458 crore, which the page describes as 99 percent of the central share. The page also says there is no further budgetary provision, and that no new city will be taken up.

The Lok Sabha reply of 6 August 2026 gives the stock of work as reported as on 31 March 2026: 8,064 projects worth ₹1,64,811 crore taken up, 7,790 worth ₹1,56,257 crore completed, and ₹46,377 crore of central assistance utilised. The original approval ran only to 31 March 2020. Four extensions carried the final completion target to 31 March 2025. Spending after that date, where it exists, is state, city, or special purpose vehicle money on projects already taken up. It is not a new central capex cycle.

Inside that book, public-private partnership projects were 197, worth ₹9,277 crore, with 194 worth ₹9,190 crore completed. Partnership capital was real and small relative to the ₹1,64,811 crore project book.

## Clock two: AMRUT 2.0, a five-year guideline that names its last year

The first AMRUT mission, launched in June 2015, covered 500 cities. PIB’s guidelines release of 27 October 2021 sets AMRUT 2.0’s indicative outlay at ₹2,99,000 crore, including ₹22,000 crore to continue the first mission, from financial year 2021-22 through 2025-26. Central share is ₹86,760 crore, including ₹10,000 crore for those continuing projects. Funds are released 20:40:40. The third-year release depends on property-tax and user-charge reforms.

The physical objects are taps in 4,700 statutory towns and sewerage or septage in the 500 AMRUT cities. Proposed connections are 2.68 crore taps and 2.64 crore sewer or septage connections. Million-plus cities are to put 10 percent of their allocated funds through public-private partnership. Cities file a City Water Balance Plan and a City Water Action Plan. States aggregate those into a State Water Action Plan.

Financial year 2025-26 ended on 31 March 2026. This brief does not announce a successor outlay. Until MoHUA or PIB publishes one, the capex assumption stops at the guideline’s last year, plus whatever completion lag the state plans still carry.

## Clock three: PMAY is assistance, then materials, then work

The December 2024 PIB note reports ₹8,07,393 crore of investment against the first PMAY-Urban, and, for the 2.0 scheme approved on 9 August 2024, government assistance of ₹2.30 lakh crore with an investment figure of ₹10 lakh crore. Unit assistance under partnership and beneficiary-led construction is ₹2.50 lakh. That money is tied to a house for an eligible household. It becomes cement, steel, and site labour when the house is built. The same note estimates 570 lakh metric tonnes of cement, 130 lakh metric tonnes of steel, and 917 crore person-days under the first scheme, as a “so far” figure at the time of the note. Those are consumption estimates attached to a dated stock of houses, not an order book for 2026-27.

The Interest Subsidy Scheme reduces the cost of a loan. It is not a line in a municipal capital budget.

## What to watch

Watch residual Smart Cities projects in the August 2026 annex, not a fresh central sanction. Watch whether AMRUT reform conditions are what a state cites when a later instalment moves. Watch PMAY through sanctions and completions by vertical, dated, rather than through a blended “urban capex” series.
