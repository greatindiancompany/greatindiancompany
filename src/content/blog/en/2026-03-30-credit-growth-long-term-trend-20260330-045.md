# How India’s bank credit cycle slowed, then sped up

The useful long view is three dated stretches, not a smooth line.

## 2023-24 was the fast year, 2024-25 the slower one

The Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India 2024-25 measures scheduled commercial banks excluding regional rural banks. Their consolidated balance sheet grew **11.2%** in 2024-25, after **15.5%** in 2023-24. On the asset side, bank credit grew **11.5%** and investments **9.2%**. Deposits grew **11.1%**.

A merger distorts that comparison. Excluding it, bank credit grew **12.5%** in 2024-25 against **16.0%** in 2023-24. Investments grew 9.9% against 11.6%. Deposits grew **11.4%** against **13.4%**. The report’s own summary is the plain version: deposits and credit still grew in double digits in 2024-25, and both were slower than the year before. The slowdown showed up across bank groups.

Public-sector banks were **54.9%** of the consolidated balance sheet at end-March 2025, down from 55.2% a year earlier. Private-sector banks were **37.1%**, down from 37.5%. Foreign banks, small finance banks, and payments banks gained share. That is a slow shift in who holds the system, not a change of regime.

## 2025-26 turned the credit side back up

The Annual Report for 2025-26 says bank credit to the commercial sector grew **15.9%** year on year, against **10.9%** a year earlier. Credit from non-bank sources grew **13.3%**, slower than the **15.1%** of the year before. Banks, not the non-bank channel, were the faster intermediary in that year. Credit growth outpaced deposit growth, and the credit-deposit ratio rose. The report states the direction. It does not print the ratio.

The June 2026 Financial Stability Report gives the supervisory pair for the same year: credit growth **14.5%**, deposit growth **11.5%**. Term deposits continued to outpace current and savings accounts. Public-sector banks still grew credit faster than private-sector banks. Personal loans and services were the main contributors to the year’s growth, and credit growth in all four major sectors was higher in March 2026.

The fortnightly non-food series then ran ahead of that full-year supervisory rate. It was **15.9%** on 31 March 2026 and **19.1%** on 31 July 2026. The July figure belongs to 2026-27. It should not be written back into the 2025-26 average.

## Quality improved through the slowdown and the speed-up

Bad loans did not track the growth cycle one-for-one. The Trend and Progress press release put the gross non-performing asset ratio at a multi-decade low of **2.2%** at end-March 2025 and **2.1%** at end-September 2025. The June 2026 stability report put it at **1.8%** in March 2026, again described as a multi-decade low, across bank groups. The annual slippage ratio eased to **1.2%** in 2025-26. The provisioning coverage ratio was **75.6%**.

Capital moved the other way from the 2024-25 dip. The capital to risk-weighted assets ratio was **17.4%** at end-March 2025 and **17.2%** at end-September 2025. By end-March 2026 it was **17.7%**, with common equity tier 1 at **15.3%**, both described as multi-decade highs. Profitability stayed high through the slower year: return on assets **1.4%** and return on equity **13.5%** in 2024-25, then **1.3%** and **12.5%** in the first half of 2025-26. The stability report says those returns were broadly stable for the full year just ended, with public-sector banks a little stronger in the second half and foreign banks softer.

## What the arc is for

A three-year reader should hold three statements at once. Credit growth cooled in 2024-25 from a very fast 2023-24, even after the merger is removed. It picked up in 2025-26, and the non-food rate was faster still by July 2026. Loan quality and capital improved on the way through both speeds. Deposit growth did not match the latest credit pace, which is why the credit-deposit ratio rose and why funding shows up as a live question in the stability report.
