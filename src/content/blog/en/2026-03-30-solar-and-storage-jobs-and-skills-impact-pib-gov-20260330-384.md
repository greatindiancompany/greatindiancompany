# What Official Notes Count as Solar Jobs

Solar employment figures in Indian official notes are easy to stack into one large number. They are not the same statistic. One is a scheme estimate. One is a training count. One is a vendor register. The module PLI page names jobs as an objective and does not give a headcount.

## The cabinet estimate

PIB release 2010133, dated 29 February 2024, approves PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana. In the outcome section it says the scheme is estimated to create around 17 lakh direct jobs in manufacturing, logistics, supply chain, sales, installation, operations and maintenance, and other services. The sentence is an estimate attached to a scheme that was also expected to add 30 GW of residential rooftop solar and serve one crore households.

An estimate in an approval note is not a payroll census. It does not say how many of those jobs existed on 29 February 2024. It does not say they are new rather than shifted from other electrical work. It covers a wide chain, from factories to rooftop crews, so it is not an installer-only number. Anyone quoting "17 lakh solar jobs" should say it is the cabinet note's estimate for this scheme.

## The later operating counts

PIB release 2294208, dated 4 August 2026, does not repeat the 17 lakh estimate. It reports an ecosystem that can be counted on that day. The scheme had 34,219 registered vendors, of whom 29,469 were active. More than 2.32 lakh people had been trained under capacity-building programmes for the rooftop value chain. The note also says 14.8 GW had been commissioned and more than 50.06 lakh households were benefiting.

Training and employment are different. A person trained is not, on this page, a person placed in a job. An active vendor is a firm on a register, not a headcount of its staff. Adding 2.32 lakh trained people to 17 lakh estimated jobs double-counts the idea of work and mixes a forecast with a programme total.

The same note describes skills the work actually requires, which is more useful to a founder than the headline estimate. Installations run through a national portal. Subsidy is meant to reach the household within 15 days through PFMS. More than 80 distribution companies are on APIs. Vendors carry star ratings that households can see. Domestic content is checked through the DCR portal. About 23,000 third-party quality inspections had been carried out. Deemed approval applies up to 10 kW. Those are the competencies a rooftop firm has to staff: portal operations, net-metering paperwork where it still exists, electrical installation, and quality response. Thirty-two states and Union Territories had removed application and net-metering charges, so the paperwork skill is not identical in every state.

## Manufacturing jobs are still an objective, not a counted total

MNRE's PLI page lists employment generation and technological self-sufficiency among the objectives of the high-efficiency module scheme. It records awards of 8,737 MW and 39,600 MW of manufacturing capacity across two tranches. It does not state how many people those factories employ. This brief will not convert megawatts of awarded capacity into jobs with a ratio the ministry has not published.

The August 2026 rooftop note's manufacturing implication is indirect. Integration with the DCR portal means household systems under the scheme are being checked for domestic content. That is demand for compliant modules, which is a jobs channel only if factories are producing. The PLI page ties incentive cash to manufacture and sale after commissioning, for five years. Jobs at those factories follow production, which follows commissioning. The pages do not date that employment.

## How to cite this without inflating it

Use three labels.

- Estimated direct jobs for PM Surya Ghar: around 17 lakh, in the 29 February 2024 cabinet note, across the full chain.
- People trained: more than 2.32 lakh, in the 4 August 2026 note.
- Vendors: 34,219 registered and 29,469 active, in that same note.

Do not add them. Do not treat the PLI objective as a fourth headcount.
