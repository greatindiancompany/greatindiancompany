# Airport capacity is ahead of the passengers who flew

The investment question in Indian airports, as the Ministry of Civil Aviation stated it on 6 August 2026, is a gap between a stock and a flow. Passenger-handling capacity across the country’s airports is over 580 million passengers a year. Passenger traffic handled in 2025-26 was about 420 million. Minister of State Murlidhar Mohol gave both figures in one sentence of a Lok Sabha reply on airport expansion. The Airports Authority of India and other operators build terminals, runways, aprons, and cargo facilities against traffic growth, airline demand, and whether the project pays for itself. The reply does not split the 580 million or the 420 million by airport, by city, or between domestic and international.

## What the gap is

Capacity above last year’s traffic means the system, taken together, was not full in 2025-26. It does not mean every metro has spare gates, and it does not mean a regional strip with a new terminal has passengers waiting. The 420 million is airport throughput for a financial year. The Directorate General’s monthly series, passengers carried by domestic airlines, is a different count and a different calendar. January–July 2026 on that series is 984.03 lakh. Putting that seven-month airline figure next to 420 million and calling the difference “empty seats” mixes two ledgers.

The long-range line in a second reply the same day is also easy to misuse. A member asked whether the sector would handle close to 500 million passengers before 2030 and move towards 1 billion by 2047. Mohol did not confirm either number. He said Indian airports taken together are expected to handle more than 3,000 million passengers by 2046-47. An investment memo that still quotes “one billion by 2047” is quoting the question, not the answer.

## What was spent to build the stock

The same expansion reply gives the capital spending that produced the current capacity. The Airports Authority of India and private airport operators spent about ₹19,260 crore in 2023-24, ₹19,317 crore in 2024-25, and ₹17,840 crore in 2025-26, from their own resources. Three years near ₹18,000–19,000 crore, with the latest year a step down, is the recent build rate. It sits outside the ₹28,840 crore Modified UDAN outlay, which is a ten-year scheme envelope for regional routes, 100 airports from unserved strips, and 200 heliports.

A project that is “AAI capex” and a project that is “UDAN aerodrome development” can touch the same city and still be different money. Darbhanga appears on the 6 August list of major AAI works and is also one of the minister’s UDAN examples. The list does not say how many crore belong to which heading.

## What an investor can underwrite from this page

Three statements are solid enough to put in a model as official descriptions, with the date 6 August 2026 attached.

The system’s stated handling capacity exceeds the passengers it handled last year. Further capacity is still being added: 25 greenfield airports have in-principle approval, 14 of them are operational, and 6 more proposals have site clearance. Hub-and-spoke operations under the ministry’s International Aviation Hub Strategy were launched on 25 June 2026. That is a policy start, not a traffic result.

Three statements are not yet an underwriting case. The reply does not give a passenger forecast for 2026-27. It does not give an airport-level load against the 580 million. And the 3,000 million figure is an expectation for 2046-47, twenty years past the scheme window that ends in 2035-36.
