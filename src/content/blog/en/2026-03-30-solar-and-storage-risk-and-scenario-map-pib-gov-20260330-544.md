# Risks Already Written Into India's Solar and Storage Schemes

These risks are not hypothetical market stories. They are consequences of rules and outcomes that MNRE and PIB have already published. Each one has a document, a date, and a wrong way to model it.

## Price risk inside a fixed battery budget

PIB release 2118325 of 3 April 2025 records what happened when battery prices fell. The viability-gap amount was cut from Rs 96 lakh per MWh to Rs 46 lakh per MWh or 30 percent of capital cost, whichever is lower. The 2024-25 provision for the first slice of 1,000 MWh was cut from Rs 96 crore to Rs 46 crore. Scheme capacity rose from 4,000 MWh to 13,200 MWh without raising the approved allocation of Rs 3,760 crore.

The scenario already observed is: cheaper equipment, a smaller grant per unit, and a larger megawatt-hour headline inside the same rupees. A further price fall would, if the same rule were applied again, point toward a still lower grant or a still larger megawatt-hour cover. This brief does not predict the next revision. It says the scheme has already shown that the per-unit grant is not a fixed tariff.

The second observed outcome is delay at the first milestone. No project achieved financial closure in 2024-25, so the 10 percent slice was not paid and expenditure was nil. The scenario "grant approved, therefore cash received" is the one the reply falsifies. Cash starts at financial closure, then commercial operation, then three annual slices. A project that never closes receives nothing under this schedule, whatever the cabinet date.

## Commissioning risk on the module incentive

MNRE's PLI page pays the module incentive for five years after commissioning, on manufacture and sale. Tranche I awards of 8,737 MW date from late 2021. Tranche II awards of 39,600 MW date from April 2023. The page updated in August 2023 does not report that the five-year cash window has opened for those lines.

The risk is a shifted window, not a cancelled outlay. If commissioning moves later, the five sales years move later. A lender who amortises Rs 24,000 crore across the award year is using the outlay as if it were a construction grant. The page describes a production incentive. SECI's allocation of around Rs 13,937.535 crore to the eleven tranche-II bidders is still described as payable over five years after commissioning.

## Eligibility risk on the cell list

PIB release 2082901 of 10 December 2024 creates a binary that does not soften with a good story. From 1 June 2026, covered projects use List-II cells. Projects whose last bid-submission date was before that order keep an exemption even if they are commissioned later. A bid submitted after the order, commissioned after 1 June 2026, does not get that exemption on the text of the note.

The scenario that hurts is a developer who priced a project on imported or unlisted cells and then finds the bid date is on the wrong side of 10 December 2024. Thin-film modules from integrated plants are the compliance route the note itself names. There is no third route in that release.

## Household-scheme risk is operational

PIB release 2294208 of 4 August 2026 shows a scheme that is paying: Rs 28,024 crore in subsidy through direct benefit transfer, and a designed path of subsidy within 15 days. It also shows dependence on distribution companies. More than 80 of them are on APIs. Thirty-two states and Union Territories have removed application and net-metering charges, which leaves a set of states where that relief is not reported. A rooftop firm that assumes every utility has waived charges is extending the note past what it says.

Vendor standing is a second operational risk the note makes visible. Households see star ratings. About 23,000 third-party inspections had been done. A firm that is registered but not active — the note's own split is 34,219 registered and 29,469 active — is not the same commercial position.

## How to keep a scenario honest

Name the rule that would have to change, and the date of the note that states today's rule. Do not attach a new rupee outcome the note does not contain. The battery case is the model: the government published both the price change and the non-payment, and a reader can stop there.
