# Where India's digital rails are still stuck

Execution problems are the steps between a working national system and a person who can use it. India's digital public infrastructure has working national systems. The official record also shows where the last steps are unfinished. Four gaps are concrete enough to manage against. A fifth is a measurement gap that blocks anyone from even seeing the bottleneck.

## Identity enrolment has not finished in specific states

The bottleneck is not the existence of Aadhaar. The Press Information Bureau's 29 July 2026 account says more than 144 crore IDs have been generated. UIDAI's saturation report as on 30 June 2026 is more precise and less flattering. Estimated live assignments are 1,34,76,52,046 and live penetration is 94.59 percent. Raw assignments are higher, at 1,44,80,05,767, because the live column adjusts for estimated deaths.

The unfinished work sits in the rows. Nagaland's live penetration is 62.41 percent: 14,31,599 estimated live assignments against a projected population of 22,94,000. Arunachal Pradesh is 77.77 percent. Manipur is 81.92 percent. Meghalaya is 84.00 percent. Sikkim is 83.68 percent. Ladakh is 84.26 percent. Bihar is 88.29 percent, which is a smaller rate gap on a much larger base: the projected population is 13,22,65,000 and the live estimate is 11,67,82,847. Gujarat is 89.74 percent.

Any workflow that stops when authentication fails will stop disproportionately in those places. The execution task is enrolment and update of addresses and biometrics, which UIDAI's method itself says are the fragile points: the state count follows the address on the record, and the person may not be domiciled there. A saturation drive that does not follow UIDAI's definitions will "finish" on paper and remain unfinished in the live column.

This is also a bottleneck for every other rail that treats Aadhaar as a solved dependency. DigiLocker registrations described as Aadhaar-enabled, eSign, and welfare authentication inherit the map.

## The fibre count and the usable count are far apart

Digital execution in rural areas is still a civil-works problem. As of June 2026, the 29 July PIB account says 2.21 lakh gram panchayats had been made service-ready under BharatNet. During that same month, points of presence were operational at 80,472 gram panchayats. The second number is the one a school, a CSC, or a clinic can use. The first number is a project status.

Until those figures move closer together, a national UPI volume of 24,508.96 million transactions in August 2026 does not describe the gram panchayat where the point of presence is not operational. The bottleneck is not the payment message format. It is the site that is service-ready in the project report and dark in the operational count.

Common Service Centres are how the system routes around that gap, and they are themselves an execution stock that has to be staffed. On 30 June 2026, 4,07,122 CSCs were functional at gram panchayat level. That is a large network. It is not the same as 2.21 lakh service-ready panchayats each having a working digital counter, and the releases do not claim they do.

## Seller onboarding is still a field operation in two cities

The Open Network for Digital Commerce can report scale and an onboarding bottleneck at the same time. The Lok Sabha reply of 4 August 2026 says cumulative orders crossed 500 million as of July 2026, with over 500 orders a minute, about 2 lakh or more active retail merchants, and about 10 lakh or more service providers in mobility and logistics. About 60 percent of the retail merchants are described as small sellers.

The same reply says the dedicated kirana effort, DigiDukaan, is operating in Hyderabad and Jaipur. Field staff, distributors, and outreach are how small shops get on. Training recorded in the reply is over 3,000 hours of virtual sessions and over 200 hours of technical sessions, with over 50,000 participants, plus handbooks in 14 languages. That is a real curriculum. It is also evidence that the network specification does not onboard a shop by itself.

The approved money for this work is finite: ₹277.35 crore under the MSME Trade Enablement and Marketing initiative for 2024-25 through 2026-27. Execution risk here is ordinary programme risk. The window ends. The field model has been described for two cities. The reply does not say the same density of field support exists elsewhere.

There is a further bottleneck the ministry states openly: ONDC does not keep a network-wide, individually attributed seller count, because sellers sit with network participants and can move. A district official who wants to know which local shops are actually transacting cannot get that answer from the network company. The data sit with the seller apps. That is an execution problem for public oversight, not only for merchants.

## Health identity is far ahead of the facility registry

As of 20 July 2026 the health ministry reported 94.87 crore ABHA IDs, 5.36 lakh facilities on the Health Facility Registry, and 10.09 lakh professionals on the Healthcare Professionals Registry. The ID count and the facility count are not in the same units, so a ratio of IDs per facility would be a rhetorical device rather than a workload. The operational point is simpler. Creating an ABHA is a different job from verifying a hospital, clinic, pharmacy, or blood bank onto the registry, and from getting that facility's software through the sandbox and the web application security audit the ministry requires.

The reply says there is no central store of health records and that exchange depends on consent and on standards. A large ID base with a thinner verified-facility base means the person can be identified into a system the local provider may not yet be able to answer. Integrations that do exist, including PM-JAY, Nikshay, and named state systems, show the path. They are listed as examples, not as a claim that every facility is done.

The third-party evaluation from June to August 2025 is summarised qualitatively. It is not a bottleneck score. It should not be used to declare the execution problem solved, because the reply does not publish the waiting-time numbers it alludes to.

## Document types are not the same as usable documents

DigiLocker, as on 31 July 2026, offered 5,437 document types or services, of which 4,776 were from state governments and 661 from the Centre, with more than 72.43 crore registered users. The state share of document types shows that the onboarding bottleneck has, in one sense, moved to states, and that states have listed a great many types. A listed type is not evidence that a resident can pull this year's certificate from this department without a counter visit. The parliamentary answers do not publish success rates for document fetches. The 29 July Lok Sabha answer does publish a stock of more than 936.03 crore documents accessible, which shows the wallet is not empty. The execution question left open is the long tail of state departments that are counted in the 4,776 and still fail a real request.

MeitY also told the Lok Sabha that an IIPA evaluation of the electronic-governance scheme in November 2025 found improved access and less time in service delivery. The findings as printed are qualitative. They do not identify which departments inside the 4,776 are the slow ones.

## The literacy programme is no longer a tool you can deploy

PMGDISHA trained 6.39 crore people and concluded on 31 March 2024, according to the 29 July 2026 PIB account. Where the bottleneck is a person who cannot complete a consent or a document pull, the flagship literacy scheme is not available to restart. Later answers point to iGOT courses for government staff and to CSC assistance for residents. Those are different instruments. Planning as if PMGDISHA were still an open tap will stall.

## What to manage first

If the goal is a resident completing a digital step, the order suggested by the documents is physical and local before it is architectural. Operational BharatNet points of presence, then Aadhaar live coverage in the states the June 2026 report names, then a staffed CSC where self-service fails, then the specific onboarding programmes that still look like fieldwork: DigiDukaan's two cities, health facilities awaiting audit, state document types that do not resolve. The national transaction records show that the core systems are past the prototype stage. They are not the bottleneck the tables are pointing at.
