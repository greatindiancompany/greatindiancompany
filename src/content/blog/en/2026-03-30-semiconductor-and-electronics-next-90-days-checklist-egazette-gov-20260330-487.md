From 24 September 2026, ninety days runs to 23 December 2026. The useful work in that window is to watch a short list of official pages for documents that do not yet exist in the record used here, or for a status line that was deliberately left open in July. This is a checklist of those pages. It is not a forecast of what they will say.

## 1. Look for a Semicon 2.0 notification, not another summary of the Cabinet note

The [Cabinet decision of 15 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784) sets the outlay at ₹1,27,500 crore and names six pillars, including machines and materials. The release does not publish the claim percentage, the eligible expenditure, or an application portal for those new pillars. The document that would change an operator's model is a MeitY notification or a guideline posted after 15 July.

Check the Ministry's scheme pages and [ism.gov.in](https://www.ism.gov.in/) for a new scheme title that cites Semicon 2.0 and carries a gazette number. A fresh PIB restatement of the 15 July pillars, without a notification number, does not change the claim formula. Until a notification appears, the live formulas remain the Design Linked Incentive gazette of 21 December 2021, SPECS, PLI 2.0 for IT hardware, and the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme.

## 2. See whether the fourth production start is named

On 15 July 2026 the Cabinet note said Micron, Kaynes, and CG Semi had started commercial production, and that one more unit was expected to start in 2026. It did not name the fourth. On 5 May 2026 the earlier release had said two projects had started commercial shipments and two more were expected soon. The July text raised the named count to three and still withheld the next name.

A PIB release or a parliamentary reply in this 90-day window is the place that name would surface. Guessing it from the construction calendar — Morigaon, Jewar, Surat, or another site — is not supported by either release. If no name appears by 23 December, the accurate sentence remains the July one: three named plants in commercial production, and a fourth expected in 2026 without a public name as of 15 July.

## 3. Read the ECMS window amendment before assuming a segment is closed

The [Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme page](https://www.meity.gov.in/offerings/schemes-and-services/details/electronics-component-manufacturing-scheme-UTM1IjMtQWa) lists an amendment extending the application window. The original text, in the 26 April 2025 PIB note, opened applications on 1 May 2025 for three months on sub-assemblies and bare components, and for two years on the supply-chain and capital-equipment segment.

Inside this 90-day window the original two-year segment is still inside its first text: two years from 1 May 2025 runs past December 2026. The three-month segments are a different question. Their original close fell in 2025, and only the extension amendment can say whether they are open now, and until when. Download that amendment from the MeitY page. Do not infer the new date from the existence of the word "extension".

## 4. Re-read the design scheme's window with C-DAC's page, not from memory

The Design Linked Incentive opened applications for three years from 1 January 2022, with extension reserved to the Minister. Twenty-four projects have since been approved for financial support, which shows the scheme has been operating. It does not, by itself, tell a new applicant that the window is still open on a given day in late 2026. The check is the [ISM design-scheme page](https://ism.gov.in/design-linked-incentive) and any guideline or public notice C-DAC has posted after the December 2022 guidelines. The ceilings, if the window is open, are unchanged in the gazette: ₹15 crore on product design and ₹30 crore on deployment.

## 5. Use the next parliamentary reply as a status revision, and keep the old PDF

The latest ministerial status in this set is Lok Sabha unstarred question 483, answered on [22 July 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU483_2k5xlV.pdf): 12 projects, six states, about ₹1.64 lakh crore, around 32,000 direct jobs depending on automation, three plants in commercial production. The winter session can produce a later annex. If it does, replace the status sentence and keep the July PDF, because training counts and employment wording have already moved between February and July 2026. A later reply is an update. It does not erase the earlier wording, and a model that mixed the two dates will not reconcile.

## 6. Treat ism.gov.in as the project file Parliament named

The July reply says the details of the 12 projects are at ism.gov.in. If that page gains a project table with investment, capacity, and a production-start date, prefer it to secondary summaries for those fields. If the page still points back to PIB releases, the Cabinet notes of 29 February 2024, 12 August 2025, 5 May 2026, and 15 July 2026 remain the approval record. A change in the count of projects, away from 12, would be the signal that the manufacturing book itself has moved. A change in wording about Semicon 2.0 guidelines would be the signal that the new outlay has an implementing text.
