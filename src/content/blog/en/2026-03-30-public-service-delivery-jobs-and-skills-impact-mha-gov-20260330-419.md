# The service-delivery jobs official notes actually name

Citizen-service reform creates a few kinds of work that official notes name directly, and a much larger cloud of "digital employment" that those notes do not attribute to UMANG or e-District. This brief stays with the named work.

## The counter: village-level entrepreneurs

The [6 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295635) says Common Service Centres offer government and business services through village-level entrepreneurs, as the last-mile channel in rural areas. As of May 2026 it records 5.01 lakh functional centres, 3.91 lakh of them at gram panchayat level, delivering over 800 services. That is a count of centres, not a headcount of people, wages, or hours. A PIB research note of 12 August 2026 records more than 5.16 lakh centres as of June 2026. Use the date on the sentence. Either way, the job the government describes is assisted delivery: a local operator helping a resident complete a service the resident may not finish alone.

The CPGRAMS portal adds a commercial detail to that role. It says the government does not charge a fee to file a grievance, and that money the public pays when filing goes to the CSC. Assisted access can be a paid counter even when the government channel is free. Operators and labour readers should keep those two prices separate.

## The literacy scheme that has closed

The same August 2026 reply treats digital literacy as a completed input, not an open-ended hiring plan. Pradhan Mantri Gramin Digital Saksharta Abhiyan trained over 6.39 crore persons against a target of 6 crore, and the reply says the scheme concluded on 31 March 2024. In Tamil Nadu it records more than 14 lakh candidates trained, including 64,813 in Dharmapuri. People who can use a phone are a precondition for self-serve portals. The scheme's closure means new literacy volume has to be read from later programmes, not from an assumption that PMGDISHA is still enrolling.

The reply also names the National Institute of Electronics and Information Technology: digital literacy and skill courses, including Awareness in Computer Concepts and Course on Computer Concepts, through 56 centres and over 9,000 partner institutes, with 31.92 lakh or more candidates trained in the last three financial years. That is training throughput. It is not a placement rate into government service desks.

## Credentials that travel with the worker

Skill certificates are themselves a delivery problem. A DigiLocker note on the National Academic Depository says that after the earlier tripartite arrangement ended, the Ministry of Education's notification of 18 March 2020 placed NAD on DigiLocker, with the University Grants Commission as nodal agency. The same note says NSDC and NCVT provide digital skill certificates through DigiLocker, alongside state and central education boards. A certificate a worker can pull from an issuer, rather than carry as a scan, is the skills-side use of the same issued-document rule that applies to a driving licence.

## The office skill Sevottam tried to build

DARPG's [Sevottam page](https://www.darpg.gov.in/relatedlinks/sevottam) describes a scheme to strengthen state administrative training institutes and central training institutions, run in training institutes in 10 states during the 12th Five Year Plan period of 2012-2017, extended to 31 March 2020 and then to 31 March 2021. The page says the scheme is under review in the department. The capability module of Sevottam is explicit: service delivery needs trained staff, not only a portal. The page does not publish a current trainee count, so this brief will not invent one. The citizen-charter tradition DARPG dates to 24 May 1997 still requires standards, time limits, and a redress avenue. Staff who can meet a published time limit are the job behind that charter.
