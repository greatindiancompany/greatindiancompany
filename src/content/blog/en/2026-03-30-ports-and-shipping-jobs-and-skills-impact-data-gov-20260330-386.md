# Jobs and skills around India's ports, yards, and coastal districts

Maritime employment in India is several labour markets that share a coastline. A rating on a merchant ship, a welder in a yard, a crane operator on a berth, and a worker on a recycling plot are not one "blue economy job." The public record is precise about training institutions and thin about how many people a new berth will hire. This brief stays with the counts the ministry and the Press Information Bureau have published.

## The coast is the labour shed

The Sagarmala page says about 18 percent of India's population lives in 72 coastal districts, which it describes as 12 percent of the mainland. Coastal community development is one of the programme's five pillars, alongside port modernisation, connectivity, port-led industry, and coastal shipping. The page points to fisheries, maritime tourism, cruise and lighthouse work, and skill programmes run with the Sagarmala skill effort, centres of excellence in maritime and shipbuilding, and convergence with the rural skills scheme. It says 75 lighthouses have been developed for visitors and that 205 were identified under Maritime India Vision 2030. Tourism jobs and seafaring jobs should not be added into one headline.

## Seafarers have a university; yard trades have ITIs

Indian Maritime University is a central university under the ministry, set up on 14 November 2008 and headquartered in Chennai. It absorbed seven older institutes, including the maritime academies and marine engineering colleges in Mumbai, Kolkata, and Visakhapatnam, and the National Ship Design and Research Centre. That is the officer-and-research track: navigation, engineering, maritime law, dangerous cargo, and design. The Directorate General of Shipping, an attached office since September 1949 with headquarters in Mumbai, remains the executive office for merchant shipping. A university seat and a certificate of competency are not the same document.

Shipyard trades are being tied to industrial training institutes. The 24 July 2026 Lok Sabha reply says 11 shipyards have signed memoranda with 34 institutes across eight coastal states, using a 150-hour on-the-job model, and that 727 trainees had been trained in those yards by the date of the reply. A trainer programme has run at two institutes in West Bengal through the Centre of Excellence in Maritime and Shipbuilding. A preliminary skill-gap study covered nine yards. The reply says 92,697 candidates were trained, skilled, or upskilled in the three years it covers. It also notes a plan of implementation with the Korea International Cooperation Agency for shipbuilding skills. These are training counts. They are not a placement rate.

The India Ship Technology Centre is the design and research bet. The 27 December 2025 note places it under the maritime university. The July 2026 reply says it has been established as a section 8 company, covering design, research, software, and skills. Design skill is the constraint behind the output figures in that same reply: UN-cited output of 57,637 gross tonnage in 2025 against a capacity aim of about 4.5 million gross tonnage a year, which the December note dates to 2047.

The reply uses an employment multiplier of 6.4 for shipbuilding. That is the minister's figure in the reply, not a calculation redone here, and it does not say how many direct jobs a given contract creates.

## Recycling and port operations are separate sheds

Alang-Sosiya in Gujarat is the recycling labour market. The shipping division says nearly 98 percent of Indian ship recycling happens there, across 153 plots, with 115 described as Hong Kong Convention compliant. Safety and compliance work at those plots is a skills issue of its own, under the Ship Recycling Act, 2019. It is not the same course as a maritime university semester.

Port operating skills show up indirectly, in the indicators the Ports Wing watches: turnaround, berth output, detention, and dwell. Those improve when equipment and planning improve, not only when a training batch graduates. A concessionaire's hiring plan is not published on the ministry's skill page.
