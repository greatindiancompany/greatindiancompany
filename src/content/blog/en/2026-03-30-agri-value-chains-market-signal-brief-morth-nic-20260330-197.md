# MSP, sown area, and the e-NAM tape

A market signal in Indian agriculture is easy to fake by adding three numbers that do not share a unit. The support price is rupees per quintal for a season that is about to buy. Sown area is hectares of a crop still in the field. e-NAM trade is a cumulative stock of auctions since 2016. Each one tells a reader something. None of them is a spot price for tomorrow.

## The support price is a floor with a history of buying

On 13 May 2026 the Cabinet set kharif MSPs for marketing season 2026-27. Common paddy is Rs 2,441 a quintal, up Rs 72. Grade A paddy is Rs 2,461. Tur is Rs 8,450, up Rs 450 from Rs 8,000, a margin of 54 per cent over cost. Moong is Rs 8,780, up Rs 12, and the release gives it the highest margin, 61 per cent. Urad is Rs 8,200, up Rs 400. Maize is Rs 2,410, against Rs 2,400 a year earlier, with a margin of 56 per cent. Sunflower seed took the largest absolute rise, Rs 622, to Rs 8,343. Medium-staple cotton rose Rs 557, to Rs 8,267.

The release is explicit that the margin is measured over cost, and that cost includes paid-out expenses plus the imputed value of family labour. A 50 per cent margin over that cost is the policy floor announced in the Budget for 2018-19. It is not a forecast of the mandi price.

The same note is also a procurement record. From 2014-15 to 2025-26 the government bought 8,418 lakh metric tonnes of paddy and paid paddy growers Rs 16.08 lakh crore. For 14 kharif crops together, procurement was 8,746 lakh metric tonnes and payments were Rs 18.99 lakh crore. Paddy dominates that history. A higher MSP for nigerseed or sesamum is a price on paper until a buyer, public or private, takes the lot.

## Area is the crop the price has not met yet

The Crop Weather Watch Group report published on 8 September 2026 gives area as on 4 September. It can be set beside the May price list. It cannot be read as the effect of that price list. Sowing decisions also follow rain, seed, and last year's price.

Rice area was 421.82 lakh hectares, down 16.37 lakh hectares from the same week a year earlier, and still above the normal of 412.00. The common-paddy MSP rose Rs 72. Both facts are official. The report does not say the MSP caused the area change.

Pulses moved the other way on some lines that also received larger MSP increases. Tur area was 45.66 lakh hectares, up 0.72 and above its normal of 44.31, beside an MSP rise of Rs 450. Urad area was 25.15 lakh hectares, up 2.68, and still below its normal of 29.60, beside an MSP rise of Rs 400. Moong, which received only Rs 12 more and the richest margin over cost, saw area fall 1.04 lakh hectares to 33.19, below its normal of 35.48. A reader who wanted "MSP up, area up" will find it in urad and tur, and will not find it in moong or rice. That is the useful part of reading the two tables together.

Cotton area was 109.17 lakh hectares against a normal of 125.51, while the MSP rose Rs 557. Maize area was 91.09 lakh hectares, down 2.99 on the year, against an MSP that rose by Rs 10. The area report is a progress report. It becomes a supply number only when the Directorate of Economics and Statistics publishes an advance estimate for 2026-27. The standing tonne estimate is still the 2025-26 Third Advance Estimate of 27 May 2026.

## e-NAM is a tape of past auctions, measured from 2016

The electronic National Agriculture Market is the nearest thing to a national trade tape, and it is a cumulative tape. The Lok Sabha reply of 17 March 2026 says that from inception through February 2026 the platform traded 13.22 crore metric tonnes worth Rs 4,82,350 crore. That rupee figure is not 2025-26 turnover. A PIB note of 13 April 2026 describes the same cumulative idea in round numbers: trade value of Rs 4.84 lakh crore from 2016 to March 2026, and a rise in that cumulative stock from Rs 3.19 lakh crore in 2024 to Rs 4.84 lakh crore in 2026.

As on 28 February 2026 the registers held 1.80 crore farmers, 2.72 lakh traders, and 4,724 FPOs, across 1,656 mandis. The April note adds that the mobile app carried free price information for 247 commodities as of December 2025, and that the portal's dashboard shows commodity-wise volumes and mandi prices. A founder who wants today's price opens that dashboard. A founder who quotes Rs 4.82 lakh crore as this year's trade has misread the stock.

Sale proceeds are meant to reach the farmer's bank account. That is a design fact in the March reply. It does not tell you the farmer's share of the consumer price.

## How to use the three without adding them

Keep three columns. Column one is the MSP the public buyer is authorised to pay this marketing season, crop by crop. Column two is hectares sown for the crop that will meet that season, with the normal beside the year-on-year change. Column three is the e-NAM cumulative, used only to see whether a mandi is on the platform and what the live dashboard says. When a later advance estimate arrives, it replaces column two as the supply signal. Until then, hectares are the signal.
