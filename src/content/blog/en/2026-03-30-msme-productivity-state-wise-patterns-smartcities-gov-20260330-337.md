# How to read state patterns in MSME productivity

National MSME averages are a poor map of India. The official sources do publish a way to go below the national line. They do not publish, in the notes used here, a ranked list of states by output per worker. This brief stops where those notes stop.

## What ASUSE is built to show

MoSPI's ASUSE 2025 press note, issued through the Press Information Bureau on 24 March 2026, says annual estimates of the survey's indicators are produced at state and Union Territory level and at all-India level. The sampling design was revised so that districts within a state are the basic strata, which lets participating states generate annual estimates at district level. Quarterly estimates are at all-India level. The headline counts in that note — 7.92 crore establishments, about 12.81 crore workers, gross value added per worker of ₹1,56,539 — are all-India.

A state pattern is therefore a table on mospi.gov.in or the e-Sankhyiki catalogue, not a sentence that applies the national 4.54 percent rise in current-price value added per worker to every state. The survey also excludes construction and covers unincorporated establishments, so a state's factory sector and its Udyam register will not reproduce the ASUSE state total.

The reference period is January–December 2025, after an October–September year in ASUSE 2023-24. A state time series has to respect that break before anyone calls a change a trend.

## What RAMP shows instead

Raising and Accelerating MSME Performance is the Ministry's World Bank-supported programme for markets, finance, and technology, run with the states. The Ministry's 26 June 2026 note says Systematic Investment Plans from all 36 states and Union Territories were evaluated, and that 398 proposals worth ₹3,211.75 crore were approved. As on June 2026, initiatives under the scheme had reached more than 55 lakh MSMEs. The same note says the government had achieved 50 percent of the programme target for claiming reimbursement on completed deliverables.

That is an implementation geography: every state and Union Territory filed a plan, a subset of proposals was approved, and reimbursement lags completion. It is not a productivity league table. The RAMP portal describes the programme as a central sector scheme across 2022–27 and states a programme size of ₹6,062 crore. Older Ministry notes have said ₹6,000 crore over five years. Quote the page you are looking at.

State plans are also where Trade Receivables Discounting System onboarding, technology centres, and green or cluster projects show up as local work. A state briefing that skips the approved proposal and quotes only the national ASUSE average has not used the state instrument the Ministry built.

## A workable state read

Pull the state's ASUSE indicators from MoSPI when the factsheet is posted. Pull the state's approved RAMP proposals and reimbursement status from the Ministry. Pull Udyam counts from the Ministry dashboard if the question is formalisation. Label each column with its universe. SIDBI's state credit picture, where SIDBI publishes one, belongs in a fourth column.
