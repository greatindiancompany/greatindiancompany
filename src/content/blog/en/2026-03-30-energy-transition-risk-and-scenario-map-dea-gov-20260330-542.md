# Risks And Scenarios For India's Energy Transition

India's energy transition has a small set of structural scenarios. They differ by whether wires and storage catch up with solar, whether demand growth stays strong, and whether distribution companies can pay. They are not probability-weighted forecasts, and this page does not assign them percentages. They are a map so a risk register stops listing "policy risk" as if it were one thing.

## Five ways the system can unfold

Transmission catches up. Corridors and bays arrive in step with renewable zones. Midday solar is absorbed across a wider footprint. Curtailment stays an exception. Coal runs less in the afternoon and remains important in the evening until storage thickens. This is the scenario most capacity targets quietly assume.

Transmission lags. Generation in a few renewable-heavy states keeps outrunning local and interstate evacuation. Curtailment and delayed commercial dates become normal. Developers carry idle assets. The national capacity number still rises, which hides the local failure. Ministry of Power connection data, not the renewable award list, is how you would notice this early.

Demand stays strong and coal generation stays large. Even a fast renewable build fails to shrink coal's energy share quickly, because new air-conditioning, industry, and transport add units that solar does not fully cover. Political pressure to keep plants and mines working remains high. Investors who modelled an early coal exit take residual-value risk. Renewable projects take midday-price risk but not a collapse in the need for electricity.

Storage scales. Batteries and pumped hydro move solar into the evening. The value of plain midday energy falls relative to firmed energy. Coal's flexibility premium narrows. Projects that cannot shift their output need contracts that still want their original shape.

Distribution finances worsen. Obligations and tariffs diverge. Generators wait longer to be paid. Open access grows only where industrial buyers can leave the utility. In this scenario the technology works and the receivable does not. Scheme design at the renewable ministry cannot repair a utility that does not collect.

## What the scenarios share

Every path still has to pass through land, a bay, a buyer, and an hour of the day. State patterns matter: a western solar state and an eastern coal state are not living the same scenario in the same year. Rule changes on access, deviation, and module eligibility can move a project from one path to another without moving the national target.

## How to use the map

Pick the scenario your contract assumes. Write the two signals that would tell you it is the other one: curtailment versus commissioning of the named corridor, payment days versus tariff orders, coal loading in the evening versus storage actually commissioned. Leave the probabilities blank until you have evidence you can cite. A scenario with a fake precision is less useful than a scenario with a signal.
