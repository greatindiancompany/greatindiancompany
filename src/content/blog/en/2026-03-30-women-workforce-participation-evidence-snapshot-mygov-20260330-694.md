# The official snapshot of women’s work in India

These are the levels a reader can repeat without leaving the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, the Ministry of Labour and Employment, the Ministry of Women and Child Development, and PIB. Each figure below carries the definition that makes it true. A figure without that definition is a different fact.

## Participation, work and unemployment in 2025

The PLFS Annual Report for January–December 2025, released on 27 March 2026, gives usual status (principal activity plus subsidiary economic activity) for persons aged 15 and above:

- Female labour force participation: 40.0 percent. Male: 79.1 percent. All persons: 59.3 percent.
- Rural women: 45.9 percent. Urban women: 27.7 percent.
- Female worker population ratio: 38.8 percent. Male: 76.6 percent.
- Female unemployment: 3.1 percent, the same as male. Rural women: 2.1 percent. Urban women: 6.4 percent.
- Women employed, using MoHFW population projections: 20.0 crore, out of 61.6 crore workers.

On the current weekly status for the same year, female participation was 34.3 percent (rural 38.3, urban 25.9). The weekly rate uses the seven days before the interview. Usual status uses 365 days and includes subsidiary work of 30 days or more.

MoSPI states that the sample was revamped in January 2025 and that these annual estimates may not be strictly comparable with earlier PLFS reports. On the previous July–June design, female usual-status participation was 23.3 percent in 2017–18 and 41.7 percent in 2023–24. Rural women in 2023–24 were at 47.6 percent and urban women at 28.0 percent. That series is the history. The 40.0 percent is the 2025 level on the new design, which the press note calls stable against 2024.

## The latest quarter, so the snapshot is not only annual

In April–June 2026, on the weekly status, female participation was 33.2 percent, against 34.7 percent in January–March 2026. Figure 3 of the 10 August 2026 press note places rural women at 37.2 percent and urban women at 25.0 percent. The female worker population ratio was 31.3 percent. Urban female unemployment was 8.7 percent. Women employed in the quarter: 16.4 crore. In June 2026 alone, female weekly participation was 32.7 percent.

## Job type and earnings

Among women workers in usual status in 2025, 64.2 percent were self-employed and 18.2 percent were in regular wage or salaried work, against 66.5 percent and 16.6 percent in the press note’s 2024 comparison. Rural women workers: 70.7 percent self-employed, 9.3 percent regular wage, 20.0 percent casual. Urban women workers: 40.4 percent self-employed, 50.9 percent regular wage, 8.7 percent casual.

Women’s average earnings in the press note: regular wage ₹18,353 in 2025, from ₹17,126; self-employment ₹6,374, from ₹5,861; casual labour other than public works ₹315, from ₹299. The units are those in the annual report’s earnings table. The levels are not interchangeable.

Among women outside the labour force, 44.4 percent cited child care or personal commitments in home-making. Among persons aged 15–59 with formal vocational or technical training, 51.4 percent of women were in the workforce, against 83.3 percent of men. Only 4.2 percent of persons aged 15–59 reported such training.

## Time, payroll and the unorganised register

Time Use Survey, January–December 2024, persons aged 6 and above: women participants spent 289 minutes a day on unpaid domestic services and men 88. Participation in that activity was 81.5 percent for women and 27.1 percent for men. Women participants spent 137 minutes on unpaid caregiving and men 75. Women aged 15–59 spent 305 minutes on unpaid domestic services, against 315 in 2019.

EPFO: 26.9 lakh net female subscribers added in 2024–25. e-Shram: 31.89 crore registrations, 54.28 percent women, in the five-year note, with eligibility that excludes EPFO members, ESIC members and income-tax payers. Palna: 11,395 Anganwadi-cum-creches approved across 34 States and Union Territories as of March 2025, against a stated 2024–25 target of 17,000.

Maternity benefit, as MoLE describes the 2017 amendment: 26 weeks of paid leave where the woman has fewer than two surviving children, 12 weeks where she has two or more, and a creche duty at 50 or more employees. An Internal Committee under the sexual harassment law is mandatory at 10 or more employees.

## How to quote this page

Quote a cell with its status, its age, its year, and its design. “Forty percent of Indian women work” drops all four. The sentence the table supports is: in calendar 2025, 40.0 percent of women aged 15 and above were in the labour force on usual status, 38.8 percent were employed, and 20.0 crore women were employed on that definition.
