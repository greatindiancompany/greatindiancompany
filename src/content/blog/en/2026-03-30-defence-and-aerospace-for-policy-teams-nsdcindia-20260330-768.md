# A policy map of India's defence production and commercial space rules

## Brief the instrument, not the slogan

"Atmanirbhar defence" is a programme name. The operable texts are narrower, and they sit in different departments. A policy note that merges them will assign the wrong office the wrong failure. The map below is the public division of labour as the notices describe it.

## Who tells the buyer to stop importing

Positive indigenisation lists come from two places. A PIB factsheet through May 2026 counts five lists from the Department of Military Affairs and five from the Department of Defence Production, together covering 5,521 items. On 18 August 2026 the Department of Defence Production notified a sixth list of 405 items. Service-driven lists and production-department lists should stay labelled. The August list's consequence is procurement from Indian industry after successful development, through Make procedures and in-house work, with MSMEs participating.

SRIJAN is the offer platform, not the legal rule. It has been up since August 2020. Mixing "items offered", "items notified", and "items indigenised" will overstate progress. The August release itself separates more than 33,000 items offered from more than 15,700 indigenised.

## Who sets ownership and who sets buying rules

DPIIT's Press Note 4 of 2020 sets foreign equity: 100 percent cap, automatic to 74 percent for new industrial licences, government route beyond that on a modern-technology test or recorded reasons, and a tighter 49 percent path for companies that are not new licensees. Security clearance and a national-security review sit in the same note. Industrial licences are considered with Defence and External Affairs.

Buying rules are a Ministry of Defence text. A PIB note of 20 November 2025 describes the Defence Acquisition Procedure 2020, with first priority to Buy (Indian-IDDM), and the Defence Procurement Manual 2025, effective 1 November 2025, for revenue purchases of about Rs 1 lakh crore. FDI policy does not award a contract. Acquisition procedure does not clear a foreign shareholder.

## Who controls the border

DGFT publishes the SCOMET control list and keeps the updated file on its site. The Department of Defence Production counts defence export authorisations: 1,762 in FY 2024-25. DGFT's strategic-trade handbook points munitions-list licensing to the Department's procedure. A policy review of "export promotion" that looks only at the Rs 23,622 crore export total, and not at authorisation capacity and the control list, will miss the constraint on the next shipment.

The export total also needs its split. Private firms contributed Rs 15,233 crore and defence public sector undertakings Rs 8,389 crore in FY 2024-25. The growth that year was on the public-sector export line. Production, by the November note, is still about 77 percent public-sector. Promotion policy and production policy are not moving the same share.

## Who governs commercial space

The Department of Space's Indian Space Policy 2023 assigns IN-SPACe to authorise and promote non-government space activity, ISRO to research and development, and NewSpace India Limited to commercialise platforms paid for with public funds. Defence FDI and defence export rules do not appoint IN-SPACe, and the space policy does not notify a positive indigenisation list. Joint briefs should say where the handoff is, especially for a firm that builds both a satellite and a defence component.

## What a quarterly policy check can honestly say

It can say whether a new list was notified, whether capital outlay was used (Rs 1.86 lakh crore at revised estimates for FY 2025-26, fully used), and whether the latest production and export releases have replaced Rs 1,50,590 crore and Rs 23,622 crore. It can compare those results with the stated 2029 aims of Rs 3 lakh crore of production and Rs 50,000 crore of exports without converting the aims into a trajectory. It should not brief a global rank, a classified programme, or a jobs total these pages do not contain.
