Ninety days is long enough to name the controls an entity in India already owes, and too short to invent a new national policy. This checklist is work on assets, clocks, and owners. It is not a claim that any of these items is unfinished in your organisation. Empty cells stay empty.

The Department of Telecommunications site, [dot.gov.in](https://www.dot.gov.in/), is where you open telecom instruments. CERT-In directions are a different site. Do not tick a telecom box with an incident advisory, or the reverse. Nomura research pages were on an earlier draft of this file and are omitted. They do not complete a row.

## Days 1 to 30: the duties you can open

- Write the name of each instrument that applies: telecom licence security conditions, Telecommunications (Telecom Cyber Security) Rules, 2024, if you are a telecommunication entity, the CERT-In direction that names you, and any sector-regulator cyber direction.
- For each one, copy the reporting or testing clock from the document. Leave the cell blank if you have not opened it. Do not fill it from memory.
- List network elements you plan to connect in the next two quarters. Mark which ones need a trusted-product check or a security certificate before connection.

## Days 31 to 60: ten assets

Pick ten systems that are in production. For each, write the owner, where the logs are, the last security test or audit date, and the person who would file an incident tonight. A missing name is a finding. Do not hire in the abstract. The roles are on the [jobs page](/blog/cybersecurity-resilience-jobs-and-skills-impact-dot-gov-20260330-391).

## Days 61 to 90: one drill and the open findings

- Walk one incident from detection to the report the direction describes. Record the hours. The hours are yours. This page does not supply a benchmark hour.
- List audit findings that are still open. Give each a named owner and a next action inside the remaining days.
- Note which stall consumed the drill: a missing certificate, a vendor status, a log you could not retrieve, or an empty roster. That classification is the [bottleneck review](/blog/cybersecurity-resilience-execution-bottleneck-review-dot-gov-20260330-591).

Put the same ten-asset log on the weekly cadence in the [operator guide](/blog/cybersecurity-resilience-for-founders-and-operators-dot-gov-20260330-711). If a cell needs a kind of record you do not have, the [evidence page](/blog/cybersecurity-resilience-evidence-snapshot-dot-gov-20260330-671) says what the record looks like.
