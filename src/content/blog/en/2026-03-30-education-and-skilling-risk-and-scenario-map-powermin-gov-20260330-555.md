A scenario is useful when it is tied to a fact that has already happened once, so you can recognise it if it happens again. The four below are not assigned probabilities. They are the stalls already visible in official school, survey, budget, and scheme records.

## Retention stays weak while early grades look solved

UDISE+ 2024-25 shows a school system that holds children early and loses them later. Foundational retention was 98.9%. Secondary-stage retention was 47.2%, up from 45.6% the year before. Secondary dropout fell from 10.9% to 8.2% and remains the highest stage dropout in that note. Transition from middle to secondary was 86.6%.

The stall looks like this: preparatory dropout, already 2.3%, keeps falling, computer access keeps rising from 64.7%, and public communication treats that as the school problem being over. Meanwhile a cohort still fails to complete the secondary stage, which is Grades 9–12 in the policy structure, and the 2030 goal of universal gross enrolment through secondary is missed in the last years. The National Education Policy's own older figures, 56.5% gross enrolment in Grades 11–12, were an early version of the same warning.

What would argue against this stall is a published rise in secondary retention and in the middle-to-secondary transition, state by state, not a further fall in preparatory dropout alone.

## Certificates multiply, and jobs evidence does not

Youth unemployment on the usual-status PLFS was 10.2% in 2023-24 for ages 15–29, down from 17.8% in 2017-18 and still far above the 3.2% rate for everyone aged 15 and above. Over a similar span the skill ministry says vocationally trained youth rose from 7.1% to 26.1%. PMKVY has certified a very large cumulative number, over 1.64 crore candidates up to 31 December 2025 across its versions, and PMKVY 4.0 alone had trained 27.08 lakh people as on 7 December 2025.

The stall is a growing stock of certificates beside an outcome field the current scheme no longer fills. Placements were tracked for short-term training in PMKVY 1.0 to 3.0. They are not tracked the same way in PMKVY 4.0. If employers cannot see a credible outcome, they discount the certificate, public money keeps paying for training, and the youth unemployment rate moves for reasons that have little to do with the scheme. Apprenticeship is the partial counter-scenario: an employer is on the contract. Even there, completions are much smaller than engagements. In 2024-25, 2.33 lakh apprentices completed final assessment, against multi-lakh engagement in the year.

## The revised estimate becomes the real budget

In 2025-26 the pattern was already in the books. Samagra Shiksha went from a budget estimate of ₹41,250 crore to a revised ₹38,000 crore. PM SHRI went from ₹7,500 crore to ₹4,500 crore. The skill ministry's net budget estimate of ₹6,100.10 crore was revised to ₹2,703.54 crore. Its centrally sponsored schemes went from ₹3,049.90 crore to ₹367 crore.

The 2026-27 budget puts the numbers back: Samagra Shiksha ₹42,100 crore, PM SHRI ₹7,500 crore, skill ministry net ₹9,885.80 crore, PM-SETU ₹6,140.50 crore. The stall is a repeat of the revision. Partners who hired trainers and ordered equipment against the budget estimate spend the year unpaid or under-paid. The political announcement stays intact because the budget-estimate figure was real on the day of the speech. The risk is not hypothetical. It is the previous year's column.

A variant that is easy to miss: higher education's central sector schemes were revised upward in 2025-26, while the sponsored line was cut. A stall in one column can sit next to a spend in another. "Education spending was protected" can be true of one demand and false of the scheme you are in.

## ITI money waits on a vehicle that is not formed

PM-SETU was launched on 4 October 2025 to upgrade 1,000 government ITIs and five trainer institutes. The 1 April 2026 reply said release for upgradation is contingent on a special purpose vehicle, and that state proposals had not yet reached the national steering committee. The preparatory markers, clusters, committees, budget heads, tenders, show a system doing paperwork.

The stall through 2026-27 is that the ₹6,140.50 crore estimate remains largely unreleased because vehicles and proposals lag, while the public story continues to cite 1,000 ITIs and a hub-and-spoke model. The network behind that upgrade is much larger: 14,688 ITIs in the ministry's later count, against 9,776 in 2014. Upgrading a thousand government institutes can still matter. It cannot modernise institutes that are not in the proposal. A further risk is geographic: the five National Skill Training Institutes named for capacity augmentation are in five cities. Trainer quality outside those hubs does not automatically move.

## How to use the map

Pick the scenario that matches the decision. A school foundation should watch secondary retention, not the ministry's gross enrolment sentence alone. A training investor should ask whether revenue survives a revised estimate, and whether any outcome besides enrolment is published. An industry partner on ITIs should ask whether the special purpose vehicle exists. None of these require a probability. They require the next official column, which will either repeat the stall or break it.
