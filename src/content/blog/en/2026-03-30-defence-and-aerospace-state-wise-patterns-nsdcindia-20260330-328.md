# Two defence corridors, eleven nodes, and two investment snapshots

## The map that has stayed stable

India has two Defence Industrial Corridors notified by the Ministry of Defence. One is the Uttar Pradesh Defence Industrial Corridor. The other is the Tamil Nadu Defence Industrial Corridor. A written reply in the Lok Sabha on 4 February 2022, carried as PIB PRID 1795541, names the nodes and the nodal agencies.

Uttar Pradesh has six nodes: Aligarh, Agra, Chitrakoot, Jhansi, Kanpur, and Lucknow. The nodal agency is the Uttar Pradesh Expressways Industrial Development Authority. Tamil Nadu has five nodes: Chennai, Coimbatore, Hosur, Salem, and Tiruchirappalli. The nodal agency is the Tamil Nadu Industrial Development Corporation.

The reply says the nodes were chosen for an end-to-end aerospace and defence ecosystem covering design, engineering, and manufacturing. The stated aim was to attract about Rs 10,000 crore of investment in each corridor. That aim is not a sum already banked.

## The 2022 investment picture, labelled as 2022

As information then received from the state governments, UPEIDA had signed 62 memoranda with an indicated potential of about Rs 8,638 crore. Of those, 25 proposals, about Rs 2,527 crore, had been finalised and land allotted. TIDCO had arrangements, including memoranda, for a potential Rs 11,153 crore from 40 industries. The reply totals 81 memoranda across both corridors, 30 of them with MSMEs, and says investment had come from anchor firms, MSMEs, foreign original-equipment makers, and startups.

It also records two state policies: the Uttar Pradesh Defence and Aerospace Unit and Employment Promotion Policy of 2018, and the Tamil Nadu Aerospace and Defence Industrial Policy of 2019. Incentives named there include stamp duty, land cost, and electricity-tax relief, plus roads, drainage, water, and power. Under the offset policy, the reply assigns a higher multiplier of 2.0 for investment in the corridors.

The same reply says there was then no proposal for a defence industrial corridor in Bihar. It does not survey every other state. Absence from this reply is not a ban on defence factories elsewhere. Hindustan Aeronautics, shipyards, and other public producers already sit outside these eleven nodes. The corridors are an investment geography, not a map of every defence job.

## A later combined figure, without a state split

A PIB note of 20 November 2025 says that, as of October 2025, the two corridors together had attracted investments worth over Rs 9,145 crore, with 289 memoranda signed and Rs 66,423 crore in potential opportunities.

That is a newer official snapshot. It is also a combined one. The note does not say how much of the Rs 9,145 crore sits in Uttar Pradesh and how much in Tamil Nadu. Do not add it to the 2022 potentials, and do not divide it in half. "Investments attracted" and "potential" are different words inside the same sentence. Rs 9,145 crore is the figure the note treats as attracted. Rs 66,423 crore is potential.

The February 2022 aim of about Rs 10,000 crore in each corridor, and the October 2025 combined investment of over Rs 9,145 crore, can both be true because they measure different things on different dates. A site-selection memo should quote the date beside the rupee.

## What a state team can decide from this

The durable public facts are the eleven nodes, the two nodal agencies, and the existence of a later combined investment total. Land status, a company's incentive, and a fresh memorandum belong to UPEIDA, TIDCO, or a newer Ministry reply. Great Indian Company does not convert a 2022 memorandum into a 2026 factory.
