## Sort capital by legal status

Corridor investment in India is readable only after it is sorted into commissioned assets, sanctioned programmes with published spend, private money already mobilised, and proposals whose project reports are still being written. Mixing those buckets produces a capex story the budget documents do not support.

## Commissioned rail, and the next rail proposal

The Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor (Ludhiana–Sonnagar, 1,337 km) and the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor (Dadri–JNPT, 1,506 km) have been commissioned, according to the Railways Minister's Rajya Sabha reply on 7 August 2026. That is sunk public capital now returning train paths. About 443 trains a day were operating on the two corridors.

The same reply says a new corridor from Dankuni to Surat was announced in the Union Budget 2026 and that its detailed project report is being prepared and updated. The 1 February 2026 ministerial briefing gave the length as 2,052 km and the sum as nearly ₹1.20 lakh crore, and said the line would tie into the Western corridor for west-coast ports. Until a sanction reply repeats those figures as an approved estimate, treat ₹1.20 lakh crore as the briefing's number for a proposed corridor, and treat the August reply as confirmation that report work is underway.

On 19 March 2025 the ministry was explicit about three other reports — East Coast (Kharagpur–Vijayawada), East-West (Palghar–Bhusawal–Nagpur–Kharagpur–Dankuni, plus Rajkharsawan–Kalipahari–Andal), and North-South (Vijayawada–Nagpur–Itarsi). They were under examination. None had been sanctioned. The reply listed the tests: technical feasibility, financial and economic viability, traffic demand, and availability of funds. A later budget line for Dankuni–Surat does not, by itself, retire that sentence for the other two alignments.

## Private capital already on the railway

The Gati Shakti Cargo Terminal policy is the clearest private cheque. By 7 August 2026 it had mobilised about ₹10,000 crore, commissioned 142 terminals, and supported an estimated 224 million tonnes a year of handling capacity. Freight handled at those terminals in 2025-26 was 146 million tonnes. Wagon schemes sit beside that: about 275 rakes of special-purpose wagons, 397 rakes of general-purpose wagons, and about 54 automobile rakes owned by industry were operational.

These cheques buy terminals and wagons. They do not buy a new dedicated corridor.

## Roads and ports

Bharatmala Pariyojana, approved in 2017 for 34,800 km, is the road ministry's corridor programme, including port and coastal roads. On 18 December 2024 the ministry told the Rajya Sabha that 26,425 km had been awarded and 18,714 km constructed as on 31 October 2024, and that NHAI expenditure under Bharatmala was ₹4.72 lakh crore up to 30 November 2024. Under the port and coastal connectivity roads category, 18 projects covering 424 km had been awarded and 189 km constructed, in coastal states including Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and West Bengal.

Sagarmala, under the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, is the port-led programme. A 27 March 2025 note says about 839 projects estimated at ₹5.79 lakh crore had been identified, and that 272 projects accounting for about ₹1.41 lakh crore had been completed as of 19 March 2025. Funding is a stack: public-private partnerships where feasible, internal and extra-budgetary resources of port bodies, grant-in-aid for lower-return social projects, and equity from Sagarmala Development Company Limited, set up in August 2016. Sagarmala 2.0 is described with budgetary support of ₹40,000 crore, aimed at leveraging ₹12 lakh crore of investment over the next decade. The leverage figure is the programme's aim.

## Implication

An investment memo can underwrite commissioned corridor capacity, operating terminals, awarded highway kilometres, and completed Sagarmala projects with the figures above. It can watch Dankuni–Surat, the three un-sanctioned reports as of March 2025, and the Sagarmala 2.0 leverage aim as future decisions. The Ministry of Railways has already said that dedicated freight corridors are highly capital intensive and that sanction depends on viability and funds.
