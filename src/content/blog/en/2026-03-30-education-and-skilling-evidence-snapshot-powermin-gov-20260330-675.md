India publishes several serious education and labour series. They answer different questions. The usual failure is not that the series are secret. It is that a paragraph adds a school census, a college survey, a household labour survey, and a scheme portal into one "people reached" number. This is a field guide to keeping them apart.

## UDISE+ counts schools

The Unified District Information System for Education Plus is the school census of the Department of School Education and Literacy. The latest national note used here is for 2024-25, released on 28 August 2025. It reports teachers, pupil-teacher ratios, dropout, retention, transition, gross enrolment, and facilities, on the policy's stage structure: foundational, preparatory, middle, and secondary.

Gross enrolment is not retention. In that note, secondary-stage gross enrolment was 68.5% and secondary-stage retention was 47.2%. Both can be cited. They cannot be averaged. Dropout is a third concept: 8.2% at secondary, 3.5% at middle, 2.3% at preparatory. A falling dropout rate does not tell you the share of a cohort that finishes the stage. Retention is closer to that question.

Facilities are shares of schools, not of students. Computer access in 64.7% of schools does not mean 64.7% of children used a computer. Girls' enrolment at 48.3% is a student share. Female teachers at 54.2% is a staff share. The pupil-teacher ratios, 10, 13, 17, and 21 across the four stages, are national. They do not cancel single-teacher schools, which the same note says fell by around 6%.

The unit is the school return. A child in a recognised school should be in it. A child in a training centre that is not a school will not be.

## AISHE counts higher-education institutions that reply

The All India Survey on Higher Education is a web return from universities, colleges, and stand-alone institutions. The Ministry's 8 July 2026 release covers 2022-23 and 2023-24. The gross enrolment ratio uses the projected population aged 18–23 from the 2011 census. It was 30 in 2023-24. That denominator is a projection, not a new census count of 18-year-olds. If the projection is off, the ratio is off, even when enrolment headcount is carefully filled in.

Headcount and ratio will not always move together. Enrolment rose from 4.46 crore to 4.50 crore between the two latest years, and the ratio rose from 29.5 to 30. Small moves are compatible with a large base.

The Ministry's own survey note says participation is voluntary, data are self-reported, validations exist, and large-survey limits can affect the result. In 2023-24, 59,533 of 64,756 registered institutions responded, a rate the Ministry puts above 90% for both published years. Non-response is a real, bounded gap. It is not a reason to throw the series out. It is a reason not to treat a college's absence as zero students without checking.

Do not add AISHE's 4.50 crore to a UDISE enrolment total. Many higher-secondary students are still in schools. Adding the two double-counts the ages where school and college definitions can overlap, and it mixes a census with a voluntary survey.

## PLFS counts households

The Periodic Labour Force Survey is a household sample, not an administrative register of trainees. Usual status, principal plus subsidiary, is the concept behind the annual rates cited by the Directorate General of Employment for 2017-18 to 2023-24: unemployment of 3.2% for ages 15 and above, 10.2% for ages 15–29, worker population ratios by education level. Current weekly status is a different reference period in the same survey. A quarterly urban print and an annual usual-status print can diverge without either being wrong.

A vocationally trained share of youth, 26.1% in 2023-24 in the skill ministry's telling, is a person-level characteristic from this family of labour statistics. It is not a count of PMKVY certificates. One young person can hold a certificate and still be outside the "vocationally trained" definition the survey uses, or the reverse, depending on the question asked. Read the survey's definition before you treat the two as the same stock.

## Scheme portals count scheme events

The Skill India Digital Hub and ministry replies count candidates trained, apprentices engaged, and funds used inside a named scheme. PMKVY 4.0's 27.08 lakh trained, as on 7 December 2025, is a scheme count. The cumulative "over 1.64 crore" certified under PMKVY since inception, up to 31 December 2025, adds earlier versions. Those versions tracked placements for short-term training. Version 4.0 does not, in the Ministry's account. A time series of "placement rate" that crosses that break is not a time series.

Apprentices engaged, 51.30 lakh from 2016-17 to 31 December 2025, are contracts. Final assessments completed, 2.33 lakh in 2024-25, are a different event. Budget estimates are a fourth object. PM-SETU's ₹6,140.50 crore for 2026-27 is an estimate. The previous year's sponsored-scheme revised estimate of ₹367 crore is what a preparatory year released. None of these is a student in UDISE or a worker in PLFS.

## A sentence that stays honest

Name the series, the year, the unit, and the "as on" date. Say "secondary-stage retention in UDISE+ 2024-25 was 47.2%" or "AISHE gross enrolment for ages 18–23 was 30 in 2023-24." Do not say "India's education coverage is" and then a sum. The National Education Policy's targets, 50% higher-education enrolment by 2035 and universal school enrolment by 2030, are aims in a policy PDF. They become evidence only when a later UDISE or AISHE table is placed next to them, with the definition unchanged.
