# The Telecom Act, Right of Way, and Digital Bharat Nidhi

India's broadband policy is easier to follow as three instruments with three jobs. The statute sets the powers. The right-of-way rules decide whether a fibre crew can enter a road. Digital Bharat Nidhi pays for networks the market does not build on its own. National Broadband Mission 2.0 is the timetable laid on top of those instruments.

## The statute

The Telecommunications Act, 2023 (Act 44 of 2023) consolidates the law on telecom services, networks, and spectrum assignment. Press Information Bureau notes on the first rules under the Act describe two chapters that matter for broadband builders.

Section 24 renames the Universal Service Obligation Fund as Digital Bharat Nidhi and keeps it under the Central Government. Sections 11 to 15 are the right-of-way chapter: permission to lay a network on public property, permission on other property, and a non-discriminatory grant. The Department of Telecommunications (DoT) writes the rules. The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) still recommends on spectrum price, caps, and rollout. Those are different desks.

## The fund

PIB's note of 30 August 2024 records the Telecommunications (Administration of Digital Bharat Nidhi) Rules, 2024, published as G.S.R. 530(E). The draft had been out from 4 July 2024 for a 30-day consultation. The rules set the administrator's powers, the criteria for schemes, and how implementers are chosen. PIB describes the fund's purpose as affordable service in rural and remote areas, access to information, and a narrower digital divide. BharatNet, mobile towers in uncovered areas, and the submarine cables to the islands are the kind of scheme this fund is built to carry. They are not licence-fee trivia.

## The permission

PIB's 13 February 2025 note states that the Telecommunications Act, 2023 and the Telecommunications (Right of Way) Rules, 2024, effective 1 January 2025, introduced uniform right-of-way charges across the country. The same note says National Broadband Mission 2.0 starts on 1 April 2025. A later parliamentary reply, published by PIB on 12 February 2026, says 33 of 36 states and union territories had implemented those rules. Tamil Nadu and West Bengal are named among those where compliance was still pending. The national average disposal time is given as 30.4 days, down from 455 days. Tamil Nadu's average in that reply is about 85 days.

Uniform charges on paper, and a state portal that still takes about three times as long, are both part of the policy picture. The rule does not finish the permission.

## The timetable

The Union Minister of Communications unveiled National Broadband Mission 2.0 on 17 January 2025. PIB's February 2025 note says the mission starts on 1 April 2025. The 12 February 2026 reply lists seven 2030 targets, including optical fibre with 95 percent uptime in 2.7 lakh villages, broadband at 90 percent of anchor institutions, a national average fixed-broadband download speed of 100 Mbps, and rural internet subscribers of 60 per 100 population. The right-of-way disposal target is the one the minister described as already met.

Read the mission as a scoreboard for the Act, the fund, and the permission rules. It does not replace any of them.
