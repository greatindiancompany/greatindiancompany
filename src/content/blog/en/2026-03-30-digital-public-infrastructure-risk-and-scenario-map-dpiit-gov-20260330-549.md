# Risks the official record already shows on India's digital rails

A risk map that invents probabilities is not a map. The useful version stays inside what publishers have already disclosed: where identity coverage is thin, where a national average misleads, where a consent system is forbidden to hold data and can still be misused at the edges, where fibre is service-ready but not operational, and where the privacy statute arrives later than the rails.

The scenarios below are reading frames for those facts. They are not forecasts, and they do not add up to 100 percent.

## Identity exclusion is already measured

UIDAI's saturation report as on 30 June 2026 estimates national live Aadhaar penetration at 94.59 percent: 1,34,76,52,046 live assignments against a projected population of 1,42,46,96,577. The same report puts Nagaland at 62.41 percent, Arunachal Pradesh at 77.77 percent, Manipur at 81.92 percent, Meghalaya at 84.00 percent, Sikkim at 83.68 percent, and Bihar at 88.29 percent. Gujarat is 89.74 percent. These are not speculative failure modes. They are the coverage prior for any service that requires Aadhaar authentication.

The report's own notes add a second risk, which is misreading success. Holders are counted by the address on the Aadhaar, not by domicile. Live figures are adjusted for estimated deaths using Registrar General death rates. That is why Kerala can show 105.02 percent and Lakshadweep 108.48 percent. A programme dashboard that celebrates percentages over 100, or that manages to the national 94.59 percent, will mis-state both the surplus and the shortfall.

Scenario A is simply the continuation of this table. Services that are digital-only keep working for most of the population and keep failing a large share of people in the lowest states, and a large absolute number of people in big states whose rate looks merely "a bit low." Nothing new has to break for this scenario. The June 2026 report is enough.

## Measurement risk: the wrong stock gets quoted

Several official numbers are true and easy to abuse.

UPI's August 2026 product-statistics row is 24,508.96 million transactions and ₹29,82,355.95 crore. May and July of 2026 were higher on value. A headline that says the rail set a record, without saying whether it means volume or value, is a measurement error with consequences for anyone planning liquidity or reconciliation around "the peak."

DigiLocker has at least three public stocks in the July and August 2026 parliamentary record: over 71.66 crore users and more than 936.03 crore accessible documents (Lok Sabha, 29 July), and more than 72.43 crore users with about 72.86 crore document accesses over three years (Rajya Sabha, as on 31 July). Adding the document stock to the access flow double-counts the system.

Account Aggregator figures diverge by column. Sahamati's July 2026 dashboard shows 11.68 million accounts linked that month, 44.84 million consents fulfilled, and 343.78 million data deliveries. Fulfilled consents, in Sahamati's definition, are not all consent requests and not all fetches. A risk report that quotes a single "AA transactions" number has left the source.

ONDC's July 2026 parliamentary figure is over 500 million cumulative orders, not a rupee gross merchandise value. The ministry also says it does not hold a seller-level census. An inclusion claim that needs a state-wise MSME count cannot be audited from that reply.

Scenario B is a reporting failure rather than a system outage. Decisions get made on the national UPI value peak, the DigiLocker document stock, or the ONDC order count, each used as if it were revenue, coverage, or a monthly flow. The underlying systems can be healthy while the decision is wrong.

## Consent and data: the design mitigates one risk and leaves others

The Reserve Bank's Account Aggregator directions of 28 November 2025 are written as risk controls. Financial information must not reside with the aggregator. The aggregator must not support transactions, must not see customer credentials, must not outsource the aggregation business, and must not share information without explicit consent. The consent artefact has to name purpose, recipients, and expiry. If the aggregator's statement disagrees with the provider's books, the provider governs.

Those rules reduce the risk of a large warehouse of bank statements sitting inside the intermediary. They do not, by themselves, remove the risk that a customer approves a broad purpose, that a regulated financial information user misuses a legitimate pull, or that a firm outside the regulatory perimeter tries to collect the same data by another route. The directions say a financial information user must be regulated by a financial-sector regulator. That perimeter is the control. It is also the place a risk review should look, because the public dashboard does not show complaints or rejected consents.

Health states a parallel control. The 24 July 2026 reply says there is no centralised repository of health data under the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, that exchange follows patient consent, and that applications pass a sandbox and a web application security audit. The residual risk is the same shape: many holders of data at the edge, rather than one holder in the centre. The reply does not publish a breach count. This map does not invent one.

The wider legal control is late relative to the rails. G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025 left most operative sections of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 to commence eighteen months after publication, which falls in May 2027. The Data Protection Board itself was established from the November 2025 gazette date. Scenario C is a timing gap: population-scale sharing continues under sector rules such as the Account Aggregator directions and the health-mission design, while the general statute's main duties are still on the appointed future date. That is a legal sequencing risk the gazette itself creates. It is not evidence that current sector rules are vacant.

## Connectivity: the outage that is a stock, not an incident

The Press Information Bureau's 29 July 2026 account says that as of June 2026, 2.21 lakh gram panchayats were service-ready under BharatNet, and that during June 2026 points of presence were operational at 80,472 gram panchayats. The risk is not a hypothetical national shutdown of UPI. August 2026 volume says the national system processed 24,508.96 million transactions. The risk is that "service-ready" is being read as "usable this month." For the gram panchayats in the difference between those two counts, the digital rail is a distant system.

Common Service Centres are the documented mitigation: 4,07,122 functional at gram panchayat level on 30 June 2026, including 70,069 operated by women. Assisted service reduces exclusion only where the centre is open and the entrepreneur can complete the transaction. The releases do not report uptime per centre.

PMGDISHA, which trained 6.39 crore people, concluded on 31 March 2024. A skills risk follows from a closed scheme: the literacy programme is not an active countermeasure unless a successor is named. The 2022-23 survey quoted beside it found high phone ownership. Ownership was never the same as the ability to manage a consent artefact.

## How to use the map

| Scenario | What would be true | What already supports it | What it does not claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Uneven identity | Authentication-gated services keep excluding people where live penetration is low | UIDAI, 30 June 2026 | That the national rate will fall |
| B. Misread statistics | Operators act on the wrong stock or the wrong month | The gaps between UPI volume and value, DigiLocker metrics, AA columns, ONDC orders versus value | That the publishers falsified the tables |
| C. Law arrives after the rail | General data-protection duties start later than sector rails | G.S.R. 843(E); Account Aggregator directions already in force | That sector rules are empty in the meantime |
| D. Local connectivity lags the national volume | Rural facilities remain unusable where points of presence are not operational | BharatNet's two June 2026 counts | A prediction of a national UPI outage |

Reviewers who need incident rates, fraud counts, or app-level concentration will not find them in this set of sources. NPCI publishes fraud-awareness material for customers; this brief does not turn that material into a loss number. The risks above are the ones a public document will actually support in a committee.
