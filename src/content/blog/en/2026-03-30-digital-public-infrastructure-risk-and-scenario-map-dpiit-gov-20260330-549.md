# India DPI Risks That Follow from the Published Design

## Risks named from the design, not from an incident log

The Press Information Bureau research note of 6 March 2026 does not publish a breach count, a fraud rate, or a downtime series for India's digital public infrastructure. This brief does not add those. The points below follow from facts the note does state. They are reading points. They are not scenarios with probabilities, and the 23 September 2026 update does not turn them into forecasts.

## Access is wide and still incomplete

The note says 85.5 percent of households own at least one smartphone. It says 5G service is available in 99.9 percent of districts and covers 85 percent of the population. Wireless subscribers stood at 125.87 crore at the end of December 2025, and 5.18 lakh 5G base stations were installed as of that month.

The complement is the household and population share not in those figures. A service that assumes a smartphone and a live connection leaves that complement out. The note does not count those people, and it does not say how many public services still require an in-person step.

## Identity and documents concentrate trust

More than 144 crore Aadhaar numbers had been generated as of March 2026, and the note records over 2,707 crore Aadhaar authentications in 2024-25. Those checks run through one national identity system. DigiLocker, with 67.63 crore users as of 5 March 2026 and over 950 crore documents issued, is described as consent-based. Consent is the control the note names. The note does not describe withdrawal of consent, or how often a share is disputed.

## Payments concentration is a volume fact

UPI at 81 percent of domestic retail payment volume is a concentration fact. The note also says UPI is live in eight countries: the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, France, Mauritius, and Qatar. Because that share is on one rail, the published design has a single-rail dependency for most of the stated retail volume, plus the named corridors. The note does not describe an outage, a fraud rate, or a corridor limit.

## Data sharing is voluntary and still large

The Department of Financial Services page says joining an account aggregator is voluntary and that data moves only with explicit consent. As of 31 March 2026 it still records 284.6 million linked accounts and more than 2.88 billion accounts enabled to share, with the progress figures credited to Sahamati. Voluntary design and a large enabled stock can both be true. The page does not give a misuse count.

## What would turn a risk into a reported event

A later official note would need an as-of date and a unit: failed authentications, disputed consents, UPI downtime, or a cross-border corridor paused. None of those units appear in the March note.
