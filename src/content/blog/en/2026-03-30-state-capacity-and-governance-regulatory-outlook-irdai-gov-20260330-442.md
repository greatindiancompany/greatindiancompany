# Release Rules That Now Govern State Capital Finance

State capacity is being regulated less by a new inspectorate than by the conditions attached to money and to published scores. Three rule-sets are in force as of this update: the Department of Expenditure's capital-loan guidelines, DARPG's NeSDA 2025 assessment, and the CPGRAMS grievance clock. A firm that waits for a sector regulator's circular will miss the rules that decide whether a state can pay and whether a service counts as delivered.

## Conditions before a capital rupee moves

The Public Finance (States) Division already approves state borrowing under Article 293(3) of the Constitution and enforces the fiscal path recommended by Finance Commissions, in coordination with the Reserve Bank. SASCI sits on top of that ceiling. The 27 March 2026 guidelines make the extra loan conditional.

Before any component is available, the finance department certifies full compliance with the official names and branding of Centrally Sponsored Schemes, and files utilization for earlier SASCI releases on PFMS in Form 12-B. The second installment of untied funds then adds a harder set: 75 percent use of the first installment, closure of every Single Nodal Agency account for those schemes, return of unspent central share and interest to the Consolidated Fund of India, and a complete upload of projects sanctioned from 2020-21 to 2024-25. Part IV adds an external examiner. States submit public-finance IT reform reports to the Comptroller and Auditor General by 15 December 2026. The CAG recommends releases to the Department of Expenditure by 31 December 2026. The incentive is Rs 50 crore a component, inside a Rs 4,000 crore part, and only for reforms undertaken during 2026-27.

These are release rules. A vendor, a contractor or a municipal project inside the five priority sectors a state is told to choose will feel them as timing. A department outside those five sectors will feel them as exclusion, unless the state spends the incentive, once earned, on a capital work in another sector. The guideline allows that use of proceeds. It does not allow every department to originate the reform.

## Rules for what counts as an online service

The NeSDA 2025 portal, launched with nodal officers on 25 May 2026, is the rule-set for service maturity. PIB's 28 May 2026 note lists the parameters governments are asked to submit against: accessibility, content availability, ease of use, information security and privacy, end service delivery, integrated service delivery, status and request tracking, open government data, e-participation, and use of emerging technologies. Fifty-nine mandatory services are proposed for every state and UT. City portals are inside the frame. Data come from the governments. DARPG reviews them. A portal that cannot show tracking or a privacy practice is exposed by the parameter list itself.

Right-to-service laws are the state-level twin, and they are not yet wired to the central grievance system. A Lok Sabha reply published on 10 December 2025 says DARPG is working with state Right to Service commissions on mandatory-service saturation and on time-bound delivery, and that there is at present no API integration between CPGRAMS and state RTS systems. The National Centre for Good Governance has a study underway on how those state frameworks actually meet timelines. Until that link exists, a citizen can have a statutory service clock in the state and a separate grievance file at the Centre.

## The grievance clock

CPGRAMS is the 24x7 portal for grievances to public authorities, connected to central ministries and to states, with an appeal if the complainant rates the closure as poor. DARPG's June 2026 report, the fiftieth on central ministries, puts the average disposal time in those ministries for 2026 at 14 days. The report belongs to a ten-step reform whose stated aims are quality of disposal and shorter timelines. Secretary-level reviews have their own module, live since 14 June 2025. By 30 June 2026, 377 such meetings had been held. June itself saw 1,96,133 cases received, 1,99,968 redressed and 78,793 pending, plus 32,269 appeals received and 21,753 appeals pending.

The outlook from these documents is plain. More of the capital loan clears only after a certificate. More of the service catalogue will be scored on a published parameter list that includes security, tracking and integration. Grievance performance is monthly, indexed, and still split from state right-to-service systems. None of that is an insurance or market-conduct circular. It is the regulatory surface of state capacity.
