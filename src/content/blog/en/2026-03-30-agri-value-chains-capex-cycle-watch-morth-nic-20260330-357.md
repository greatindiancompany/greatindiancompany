# Three capex ledgers behind the agri chain

Capital spending in India's agri chain is recorded in at least three public ledgers, and they do not add up to one capex number. One is a bank loan with an interest subvention. One is a grant-in-aid for an integrated cold chain. One is private investment that a production-linked incentive later pays against. A fourth, older ledger counts godowns. Keeping them apart is the whole of this brief.

## The loan ledger

The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund has run since July 2020. Banks and financial institutions lend. The Centre pays a 3 per cent a year interest subvention and supports a credit guarantee, through CGTMSE and NABSanrakshan, for loans up to Rs 2 crore. The August 2025 ministry note caps the lending rate at 9 per cent, limits the subvention to seven years and to the first Rs 2 crore of a larger loan, and describes the scheme as operational from 2020-21 to 2032-33. It also says disbursement of new loans was to be complete by the end of 2025-26. Interest support on loans already made can therefore outlive the disbursement window.

The sanctioned-loan stock, in a Lok Sabha reply covering the period to 26 January 2026, was Rs 80,224.15 crore across 1,50,431 projects, mobilising investment of Rs 1,27,508 crore. A reply as on 15 July 2026 uses a different label: cumulative capital investment leveraged of Rs 1,61,537.22 crore, of which Rs 50,332.66 crore is farm-gate storage and logistics. The July figure is not "the January loan stock plus a quarterly sanction." The words on the two replies are not the same, so the arithmetic between them is not a flow.

Composition is available for an earlier date. As of 30 June 2025 the ministry counted 30,202 custom hiring centres, 22,827 processing units, 15,982 warehouses, 3,703 sorting and grading units, and 2,454 cold stores. The cold stores carried Rs 8,258 crore of sanctions. On that date the count of projects was led by machinery-hiring centres, not by refrigerated rooms. Anyone watching "cold chain capex" inside AIF should ask for a later composition before treating June 2025 as the current mix.

PM-KUSUM solar pumps sit in the same ledger only at the margin. Components B and C, included from September 2020, had Rs 29.20 crore sanctioned for 921 projects up to 26 January 2026.

## The grant ledger

The Ministry of Food Processing Industries runs a separate cold-chain programme under Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana. As of 30 June 2026 it had approved 409 projects, sanctioned grant-in-aid of Rs 3,089.08 crore, and released Rs 2,540.89 crore. Completed projects numbered 311 and had created 9.236 lakh metric tonnes of cold storage. Ninety-eight projects were still ongoing. Releases to project agencies were Rs 113.52 crore in 2024-25 and Rs 125.71 crore in 2025-26.

That grant stock is small next to the loan stock, and small next to the national cold-storage capacity the same parliamentary answer cites from the Department of Agriculture: 402.87 lakh metric tonnes as on 31 January 2026. The interesting capex fact is the gap in scale, not a claim that the grant is failing. Most of the country's cold storage was not built by this component.

The August 2025 agriculture note records a different storage scheme, Agricultural Marketing Infrastructure, as of 30 June 2025: 49,796 storage projects in 27 states, capacity of 982.94 lakh metric tonnes, and subsidy released of Rs 4,829.37 crore. Those are godowns. They dwarf the 9.236 lakh metric tonnes of PMKSY cold chain. A capex watcher who says "storage" has to say which of these two ledgers.

PMKSY's umbrella outlay was Rs 6,520 crore for the period up to 2025-26, with 1,607 projects approved till 31 December 2025. The cold-chain counts above are one component, and they were still being reported after that finance-cycle sentence.

## The incentive ledger

The production-linked incentive for food processing has an outlay of Rs 10,900 crore for 2021-22 to 2026-27. As on 31 December 2025, 168 approved applicants had invested about Rs 9,207 crore and created around 35 lakh metric tonnes a year of processing and preservation capacity. Incentive disbursed was Rs 2,714.79 crore. The capex is the Rs 9,207 crore. The public flow is the incentive. The outlay is the ceiling the scheme was given, and it is not a statement that Rs 10,900 crore has been spent.

The incentive requires the manufacturing chain, including primary processing, to be in India. That is a location rule for the capex, not a utilisation rate.

## What to watch next, without inventing a cycle

The live questions are dated and narrow. Has AIF loan disbursement in fact stopped at the end of 2025-26, as the August 2025 note said it would, or does a later reply show new sanctions? The July 2026 reply is a stock, so it does not answer that. How many of the 98 ongoing cold-chain projects have been commissioned since 30 June 2026, and has the released grant moved past Rs 2,540.89 crore? Has the PLI investment stock moved past Rs 9,207 crore since 31 December 2025, inside a scheme window that runs to 2026-27? Those three questions are the capex cycle. A single "agri infra spend" figure is not in any of these documents.
