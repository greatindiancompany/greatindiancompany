# Where cold-chain capital is being spent

The cold-chain spend that the Ministry of Food Processing Industries can account for is a grant book, not a private-capex survey. As on 30 June 2026 the Integrated Cold Chain and Value Addition Infrastructure scheme, a component of Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana, had 409 approved projects. Grants-in-aid sanctioned were ₹3,089.08 crore. Of that, ₹2,540.89 crore had been released. Projects completed and operational were 311. Projects still ongoing were 98. The completed projects account for 9.236 lakh tonnes of cold storage capacity.

The cash that moved in the latest two years is much smaller than the cumulative sanction. The Ministry released ₹113.52 crore to project agencies in 2024-25 and ₹125.71 crore in 2025-26. A capex cycle watched only through those annual releases looks quiet. A cycle watched through the stock of sanctions looks like a ₹3,089 crore programme with about ₹548 crore still unreleased and 98 projects unfinished. Both readings are in the same Lok Sabha replies. They describe different things: flow versus stock.

The subsidy rule on new projects is 35 percent of eligible project cost in general areas and 50 percent in difficult areas, including the North-Eastern states, and for projects promoted by SC or ST entrepreneurs, FPOs, and self-help groups. The cap is ₹10 crore for a cold-chain project. The same 35/50 pattern and the same ₹10 crore cap apply to the Agro Processing Cluster scheme and to standalone projects under the other-grants component; integrated projects under that component can go to ₹15 crore. The scheme is demand-driven. Approval follows an expression of interest. It is not a state-wise entitlement.

## What the tonne figure leaves out

The 9.236 lakh tonnes is cold storage created by completed projects under this one scheme. It is not India’s entire cold-storage stock. The Ministry told Parliament it has the Department of Agriculture’s wider capacity figures, and it pointed to a NABCONS study from 2022 for state-wise post-harvest loss rates. This note does not reprint those loss percentages, because they belong to that study’s tables rather than to the June 2026 project count. A 2025 evaluation, by the Academy of Management Studies, is cited by the Ministry for a qualitative result: commissioned projects reduced post-harvest losses and added processing and preservation capacity. The evaluation is not a new tonne total. The tonne total to use for this scheme remains 9.236 lakh tonnes on the completed projects.

State rows in the 6 August 2026 reply show how uneven the 409 are. Uttarakhand had 30 approved and 27 completed. Gujarat had 29 and 25. Tamil Nadu had 26 approved and 15 completed, so a larger unfinished share. Uttar Pradesh had 26 and 21. Punjab had 25 and 21. Haryana had 23 and 18. A founder siting a packhouse should read the unfinished count, not only the approved count. Tamil Nadu’s 11 ongoing projects are a different pipeline from Uttarakhand’s 3.
