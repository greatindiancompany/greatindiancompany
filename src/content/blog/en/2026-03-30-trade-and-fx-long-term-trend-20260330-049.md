# India's trade balance over the last full year, and what this year has added

The useful long window for India's trade is the last completed financial year, read beside the opening months of 2026-27. Merchandise exports barely rose in 2025-26. Services did the export growth. The goods deficit widened anyway. The new year has continued that shape at a faster export pace, with reserves higher than at the end of March.

## The completed year: goods flat, services up, the goods gap wider

The Commerce ministry's trade release of 15 April 2026 is the full-year statement. For April–March 2025-26:

| | 2025-26 | 2024-25 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Merchandise exports | US$441.78 billion | US$437.70 billion |
| Merchandise imports | US$774.98 billion | US$721.20 billion |
| Merchandise trade deficit | US$333.19 billion | US$283.50 billion |
| Services exports | US$418.31 billion | US$387.55 billion |
| Services imports | US$204.42 billion | US$198.72 billion |
| Services trade surplus | US$213.89 billion | US$188.84 billion |
| Goods and services exports | US$860.09 billion | US$825.26 billion |
| Goods and services imports | US$979.40 billion | US$919.92 billion |
| Overall trade balance | −US$119.30 billion | −US$94.66 billion |

Merchandise exports rose 0.93 per cent. Combined exports rose 4.22 per cent. Services exports are estimated to have risen 7.94 per cent. Non-petroleum merchandise exports were US$387.88 billion, up 3.62 per cent from US$374.32 billion. Non-petroleum, non-gems and jewellery exports were US$359.67 billion, against US$344.50 billion.

The release marks March 2026 services as an estimate, because RBI services data then ran through February, and it says 2024-25 and April–December 2025 were revised on a pro-rata basis using quarterly balance-of-payments data. The year is official. The services line inside it still carries that estimation note.

A later PIB note, on 18 August 2026, describes combined merchandise and services exports in 2025-26 as a record **US$863.1 billion**, including merchandise exports of **US$441.8 billion**. The merchandise figure matches the April release once it is rounded. The combined figure does not match US$860.09 billion. Both documents are on pib.gov.in. This note uses the April release when it needs the full table, and it does not average the two combined totals.

## Software is the multi-year services engine the survey can name

The RBI survey released on 18 September 2026 covers software and IT-enabled exports in 2025-26, a narrower set than all services. Exports excluding overseas commercial presence were **US$221.4 billion**, up 8.2 per cent from US$204.7 billion in 2024-25. Computer services were US$153.4 billion, a 69.3 per cent share. IT services alone were US$147.0 billion. BPO services were US$56.0 billion. Engineering services were US$12.0 billion.

Including delivery by foreign affiliates of Indian companies, software exports were US$239.3 billion, up 9.5 per cent. Affiliate business in the local overseas market rose from US$13.9 billion to US$17.9 billion.

That survey is the official long view of software and IT-enabled exports. Total services exports in the Commerce year were US$418.31 billion. Software at US$221.4 billion is just over half of that Commerce services-export line. The two compilations differ in coverage and timing, so the comparison is a scale check.

## MSME-related goods became a larger share through 2024-25

PIB, reporting a Lok Sabha reply on 11 December 2025, said MSME-related products rose from 45.74 per cent of merchandise exports in 2023-24 to **48.55 per cent in 2024-25**, in US dollar terms. That is the latest share in that PIB note. It predates the 2025-26 full-year trade release and should not be applied to the US$441.78 billion.

## 2026-27 has added speed, and a still-wide goods gap

April–June 2026, on the RBI basis, already shows goods exports of US$132.0 billion and a goods deficit of US$86.1 billion, against US$68.9 billion a year earlier. Net services receipts were US$51.6 billion. The current account deficit was US$4.2 billion, 0.5 per cent of GDP.

Commerce's April–July quick estimates show merchandise exports of US$173.78 billion, up 17.04 per cent from US$148.48 billion, and a merchandise deficit of US$118.60 billion, against US$96.66 billion. DGCIS provisional figures extend that to April–August: merchandise exports US$215,912.45 million, up 17.85 per cent, and imports US$363,000.93 million, up 18.21 per cent.

The long pattern is intact. Export growth in the new year is faster than the 0.93 per cent merchandise pace of 2025-26. Import growth is at least as fast. The services surplus remains the offset, not a closed goods account.

## Reserves are higher than at the close of March

As on 11 September 2026, reserves were US$780,782 million. That is US$89,674 million above end-March 2026 and US$77,816 million above a year earlier. The path was not a straight line. In April–June, reserves fell US$8.1 billion on a payments basis and US$22.5 billion once a US$14.4 billion valuation loss is included. July's payments-basis overall surplus was US$20.8 billion. The weekly stock can move by several billion dollars, as it did between 4 September (US$785,706 million) and 11 September.

Over a year, the stock is up. Over a quarter, valuation and capital flows can dominate the trade balance. That is the long-term fact the weekly headline tends to hide.
